## Introduction

Ise Grand Shrine (Ise Jingu) in Ise City, Mie Prefecture, is hailed as "Japan's Chief Guardian Deity" and attracts over 8 million visitors annually, making it one of Japan's foremost spiritual power spots. Its official name is simply "Jingu," and it encompasses a total of 125 shrines and facilities. A unique tradition, the **Shikinen Sengu**, has continued for over 1,300 years, involving the complete reconstruction of all shrine buildings every 20 years. This article provides a comprehensive overview on a single page, covering its history, pilgrimage order, model itineraries, surrounding spots, recommended power stones for your visit, and frequently asked questions.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins of Ise Grand Shrine

According to the "Nihon Shoki" (The Chronicles of Japan), approximately 2,000 years ago during the reign of Emperor Suinin, Imperial Princess Yamatohime-no-mikoto journeyed throughout the land seeking a suitable place to enshrine Amaterasu Omikami. Her search led her to the banks of the Isuzu River, which became the origin of Naiku. Geku was established about 500 years later, during Emperor Yuryaku's reign, when Toyouke Omikami, the deity of food and provisions, was invited from Tanba Province and enshrined there.

The **Shikinen Sengu**, established by Emperor Tenmu, is a magnificent ritual held every 20 years, involving the complete rebuilding of all shrine structures and the renewal of sacred treasures. This unparalleled ceremony symbolizes the passing down of traditional techniques and the eternal youth of the deities. During the Edo period, "O-Ise Mairi" (pilgrimage to Ise) became a cherished aspiration for common people, with records showing millions of pilgrims visiting annually.

> Source: Official Jingu Administration materials, "Nihon Shoki," various folklore sources.

## <span id="benefit"></span>Blessings and Benefits

- **National prosperity and family well-being**
- **Good fortune and warding off evil**
- **Abundant harvests and industrial prosperity** (especially Geku)

Traditionally, Ise Grand Shrine is said to be less a place for making wishes and more a place for expressing daily gratitude. It's positioned as a sacred site primarily for offering thanks for the gift of life itself, rather than for seeking personal desires.

## <span id="order"></span>Official Pilgrimage Order: Geku to Naiku

Following the tradition of "**Geku Sentsai**" (visiting Geku first), you should visit Geku, and then Naiku.

### Geku (Toyouke Daijingu)

- Deity of clothing, food, housing, and industry
- Easily accessible, about a 5-minute walk from Ise-shi Station
- The order is Omotesando (main approach) → Kaguraden (hall for sacred music and dance) → Goshogu (main sanctuary) → Betsugu (auxiliary shrines, such as Taganomiya)

### Naiku (Kotaijingu)

- The central shrine dedicated to Amaterasu Omikami
- Crossing **Ujibashi Bridge** marks the transition from daily life to the sacred realm
- The tradition of purifying one's mind and body at the **Mitarashiba of the Isuzu River**
- The approach path is right-hand traffic (Geku is left-hand traffic)

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Pilgrimage Etiquette

1.  **Bow once before passing through the torii gate.**
2.  **Do not walk in the center of the path** (it's reserved for the deities).
3.  **At the chozuya (purification font), purify your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, wash your left hand again, and then cleanse the dipper.**
4.  **Two bows, two claps, one bow** (common to both shrines).
5.  **Keep personal wishes modest, focusing primarily on gratitude.**

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Power Spots

### Sarutahiko Shrine

About a 15-minute walk from Naiku. Many people visit this shrine, dedicated to "**Michihiraki no Kami**" (Deity of Guiding Paths), when they are uncertain about their direction in life or career.

### Futami Okitama Shrine (Meoto Iwa)

Famous for the **Meoto Iwa (Wedded Rocks)** floating in Ise Bay. Traditionally, it was a place for "**misogi**" (ritual purification) before the Ise pilgrimage. Originally, one was supposed to visit here before going to Ise Grand Shrine.

### Tsukiyomi-no-miya and Yamatohime-no-miya

Auxiliary shrines of Naiku, these tranquil sanctuaries enshrine **Tsukuyomi-no-mikoto**, who appears in mythology, and **Yamatohime-no-mikoto**, who established Ise Grand Shrine.

### Okage Yokocho and Oharai-machi

An essential area to enjoy after your visit. Tea and mochi at **Akafuku Honten** after worship are a classic Ise experience.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is it okay to visit only Geku or Naiku, not both?**

A. While "Ryogu Sanpai" (visiting both shrines) is the traditional custom, if time is limited, visiting one is perfectly fine. However, local custom, following "Geku Sentsai" (Geku first), suggests visiting Geku first if you can only choose one.

**Q2. Is early morning pilgrimage truly recommended?**

A. Yes, absolutely! Early mornings between 5 and 7 AM have fewer visitors, allowing you to experience the purest atmosphere of the Isuzu River. The birdsong in the sacred forest at this time is also truly exceptional.

**Q3. Is photography allowed?**

A. Photography is prohibited from the stone steps of the Goshogu (main sanctuary) upwards. It's allowed up to the front of the torii gate. The shrine's forest, Ujibashi Bridge, and the Isuzu River are permissible for photography.

**Q4. Are there any dress code requirements?**

A. There are no strict rules, but as it's a sacred place, it's customary to avoid overly revealing attire. If you wish to participate in the Mikakiuchi Sanpai (special inner sanctuary visit), men should wear suits, and women should wear jackets and non-revealing clothing.

**Q5. Are omamori (charms) and goshuin (red seal stamps) available at both shrines?**

A. Yes. Both Geku and Naiku offer goshuin and omamori. Visiting both and collecting both goshuin is a common tradition for an Ise pilgrimage.

### Common Misconceptions

- ❌ "You must not make personal wishes." → ✅ It's not forbidden. However, the ancient tradition is "gratitude first, and wishes second."
- ❌ "Goshuin are only needed from Naiku." → ✅ Both shrines issue goshuin. Collecting both is a standard part of an Ise pilgrimage.
- ❌ "The Shikinen Sengu year brings the most blessings." → ✅ The Sengu year is crowded, but blessings do not increase or decrease. The essence of Ise is that "it welcomes you consistently, at any time."
