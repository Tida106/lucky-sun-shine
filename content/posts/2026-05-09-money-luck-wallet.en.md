## Introduction

Many people claim that their money luck changed after they switched wallets. This sentiment has been shared across cultures and throughout history. In Japan, a wallet is traditionally seen as the closest "home" for your money. The idea is that if this "home" is comfortable, money will stay longer; if it's messy, money will quickly leave. This belief forms the core of Japan's traditional view on money luck. This article provides a comprehensive guide to choosing a wallet with money luck in mind, covering colors, materials, styles, replacement timing, usage, storage, disposal methods, and frequently asked questions, all in one place.

## <span id="basics"></span>Basic Principles of Wallet Selection

Traditionally, three main elements are considered vital when choosing a wallet:

1.  **Color**: Determines the theme of your relationship with money.
2.  **Material**: Affects its texture and energetic compatibility.
3.  **Shape**: Influences the "comfort" for your money.

Additionally, the timing of replacement and daily usage combine to foster your wallet's relationship with your money luck. This concept is widely shared in the worlds of Feng Shui, fortune-telling, and good luck charms.

> **Please Note**: The effects on money luck are cultural and symbolic. They are not scientifically guaranteed, and simply changing your wallet won't automatically increase your income. A modern approach is to use it as a "mindset switch."

## <span id="color"></span>Choosing by Color

Color is the first choice that sets your wallet's theme. Choose according to your wishes.

### Yellow and Gold Tones (Colors that Attract)

These are the classic colors for money luck, traditionally known as "the colors that attract money."

-   **Yamabuki-iro (Bright Golden Yellow)**: A traditional color symbolizing prosperity and abundance.
-   **Gold**: For those who want to maximize their energy; popular among entrepreneurs and business owners.
-   **Cream Yellow**: For gentle money luck; popular among women.

However, it's also said that "flashy yellow can lead to wasteful spending." Therefore, many people opt for a calm color scheme, such as a beige interior.

### Brown and Camel Tones (Colors that Save)

Considered "the color that saves money," symbolizing the **energy of the earth**.

-   Recommended for those prone to overspending.
-   Offers a sophisticated impression and suits business settings.
-   Pairs excellently with leather materials that develop a rich patina over time.

If you aim to both "attract and save," a bicolor wallet with yellow and brown is also an option.

### Black (Color that Protects)

"The color that protects existing money." Ideal for those who want to prevent their current assets from flowing out and manage their money strategically.

-   For those who want to be protective.
-   For those who want to carry a sophisticated wallet for a long time.
-   Popular among business owners and investors.

### Green, White, and Pink

-   **Green**: For "saving and healthy money luck," suitable for practical individuals.
-   **White**: For "purification and reset," ideal for new beginnings.
-   **Pink**: For "money luck derived from connections and relationships," popular among women.

### Colors Traditionally Advised Against

-   **Red**: Said to evoke "deficits" (though it symbolizes passion, it requires caution from a money luck perspective).
-   **Blue**: Said to "flow away like water" (a color associated with wasteful spending).

However, these are traditional interpretations, and some believe that carrying a color you genuinely like leads to greater personal satisfaction.

## <span id="shape"></span>Choosing by Style

### Long Wallet

-   Allows banknotes to be stored without folding.
-   Often described as "a comfortable home for money to reside in."
-   Often most recommended in Feng Shui.
-   Offers high storage capacity for cards and coins.

### Bi-fold Wallet

-   Compact and easy to carry.
-   It's said to "bend money," and is often avoided in traditional money luck theories.
-   However, it's not an issue if you prioritize a **modern lifestyle**.
-   Convenient for fitting into suit pockets.

### Mini and Tri-fold Wallets

-   The mainstream choice in the cashless era.
-   A style that prioritizes "ease of use" over traditional money luck theories.
-   Perfect for those who primarily use cards and e-money.
-   Assumes minimal banknote storage.

If you "value tradition," choose a long wallet. If you prioritize "modern convenience," a mini wallet is suitable—this approach strikes a practical balance.

## <span id="timing"></span>Timing for Replacement

### Spring Wallet (Haru-Saifu)

-   Purchased anew around **January to March**.
-   A pun where "haru" (spring) also means "to expand" or "swell," implying a wallet full of money.
-   The most popular time of year for replacement.
-   The period from Risshun (around February 4th) to Rikka (around May 5th) is considered the "Spring Wallet" season.

### Autumn Wallet (Minori-Saifu)

-   Around **September to October**.
-   A good luck charm associated with "harvest = abundance."
-   A popular time, on par with the Spring Wallet period.

### Tora-no-hi, Mi-no-hi, and Tensha-nichi

-   **Tora-no-hi (Day of the Tiger)**: Symbolizes "traveling a thousand miles and returning a thousand miles," meaning money that leaves will come back.
-   **Mi-no-hi (Day of the Snake, especially Kinoe-Mi-no-hi)**: The envoy of Benzaiten, a day associated with the god of money luck.
-   **Tensha-nichi (Heavenly Forgiveness Day)**: The most auspicious day, occurring only a few times a year, ideal for new purchases or starting to use things.
-   **Ichiryu Manbai-bi (One Grain Ten Thousandfold Day)**: Also favorable for investments and starting new ventures.

It is believed that acquiring or starting to use a new wallet on a day when these auspicious days overlap will strengthen your money luck even further.

### Estimated Lifespan

The lifespan of a wallet is said to be approximately **1,000 days (about 3 years)**. Even before then, it's said that money luck escapes if your wallet becomes damaged, so it's good to regularly check its condition.

-   Scuffing on corners
-   Malfunctioning fasteners
-   Overstuffed and out of shape
-   Fading interior color

If these signs start to bother you, it's considered a signal to replace your wallet.

## <span id="start"></span>Starting Rituals (Seed Money and Resting)

Before you start using a new wallet, there's a ritual of "letting it rest."

### Letting it Rest for 9 Days

-   Place about **three times your usual amount of money (seed money)** into the new wallet.
-   Leave it there for **nine days** in a quiet place (a north-facing drawer is ideal).
-   Start using it actively after nine days.

This ritual is based on the traditional idea of teaching the wallet, "This is the normal amount of money to have inside." While there are no strict rules for the amount of seed money, it's generally best to use new banknotes.

### Transferring from an Old Wallet

When transferring money from an old wallet to a new one, tradition suggests aligning the direction of the banknotes and prioritizing new bills. It's preferred not to transfer old bills or receipts, but to simply make a fresh start.

## <span id="usage"></span>Daily Usage

To make your wallet an effective tool for money luck, small daily habits are considered important.

-   **Don't accumulate receipts**: It's said to make money "uncomfortable." Tidy them up at least once a week.
-   **Align banknotes**: It's believed that if the portraits are facing downward and aligned, money will "reside comfortably."
-   **Separate coin purse and bill compartment**: Keeping them together can make the wallet heavy, which is said to dull your money luck.
-   **Don't put it directly into pockets**: Prevents distortion and deterioration.
-   **Don't handle banknotes roughly at the register**: Handle them carefully when taking them out and putting them back.
-   **Reduce unnecessary cards and loyalty cards**: An overstuffed wallet is said to hinder money luck.

## <span id="storage"></span>Feng Shui Storage Location

Traditionally, the storage location for your wallet when not in use has also been highly valued.

### Recommended Locations

-   **North-facing drawer**: North is the direction of "water," where money is said to grow peacefully.
-   **Dark and quiet place**: Inside a drawer away from direct sunlight.
-   **Tidy and organized place**: Keep the surroundings clean as well.

### Locations to Avoid

-   **Left unattended in the entryway**: Money luck is said to escape.
-   **Kitchen or near water sources**: Fire and water are considered enemies of money luck.
-   **Near TVs or electronic devices**: Feng Shui dislikes the influence of electromagnetic waves.
-   **Directly on the floor**: Considered "treating money disrespectfully."

## <span id="dispose"></span>Disposing of and Honoring Old Wallets

In Japan, it's traditional to return a long-used wallet with gratitude once its role is complete. It's considered proper etiquette to avoid discarding it with regular trash.

### Honoring at a Shrine

-   Bring it to **Dondo-yaki** (a ritual bonfire around January 15th).
-   Some shrines accept them at their **Kofuda Nosho** (old talisman return points) (please check beforehand).
-   Some shrines hold a **Wallet Memorial Ceremony** once a year.

### Practices You Can Do at Home

Minimum practices if returning it is difficult:

1.  **Wrap it in white paper or cloth.**
2.  **Sprinkle a pinch of salt on it.**
3.  **Express gratitude, saying "Thank you very much."**
4.  **Dispose of it with burnable trash, separate from other garbage.**

It's important to have a feeling of "saying goodbye" rather than "throwing away." For detailed disposal methods of good luck charms, please also refer to the [Complete Guide to Choosing Good Luck Items](/blog/lucky-items-guide/).

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Which is better, a Spring Wallet or an Autumn Wallet?**

A. Both are auspicious times, and **there is no superior or inferior choice**. Traditionally, a cycle of "buying a new wallet in spring and using it for a year, then again in spring" has been established. However, it's practical to align it with your own lifestyle (start of the fiscal year, moving, job change, and so on).

**Q2. Does a borrowed wallet or a used wallet reduce money luck?**

A. Generally, a **personal wallet is considered for the owner only**. It's traditionally advised to avoid receiving someone else's wallet as a hand-me-down, as you might "acquire another person's money luck habits." However, a **new gift** from family or a partner is perfectly fine. Used wallets sold commercially are also often not recommended from a money luck perspective.

**Q3. Is it good to change wallet colors every year?**

A. **It's perfectly fine.** If you choose with a theme in mind, such as "this year, I'll focus on saving with black" or "this year, I'll attract money with yellow," every time you see your wallet, you'll be reminded of your goal. Using a consistent theme for multiple years is also a good choice.

**Q4. How do I choose a wallet in the cashless era?**

A. If you have a lifestyle where you **hardly use cash**, a mini wallet or card case is perfectly fine. The era is moving towards prioritizing **ease of use** over traditional money luck theories. However, it's a good idea to leave enough space for a small amount of cash and a lucky charm, so you won't be inconvenienced in a pinch.

**Q5. Can I put a power stone in my wallet?**

A. Yes. Many people place small pieces of **money luck stones (citrine, rutilated quartz, pyrite, and tiger's eye)** in their wallets. It's a common way to carry them as protective stones. For more details, you can refer to [The Meaning and Effects of Citrine](/blog/citrine/) and [The Meaning and Effects of Rutilated Quartz](/blog/rutilated-quartz/).

**Q6. What does "aligning banknotes" mean?**

A. It means **the state where the portraits (faces of people) are aligned in the same direction.** Furthermore, traditionally, it is believed that if the **portraits are facing downward**, "money will reside comfortably and be less likely to leave the wallet." This small habit of tidying banknotes every time you take them out is said to foster awareness of money luck.
