The etymology comes from the Amazon River in South America. However, the actual main sources are **Russia and Brazil**, with very little production in the Amazon River basin itself. The deep blue-green variety known as "Russian Amazonite" is considered the highest quality.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Lore and Historical and Cultural Background

Its gemstone lore speaks of "**Hope, Courage, and Self-Expression**."

In ancient Egypt, Amazonite was used as **decorative plaques engraved with the "Book of the Dead,"** serving as an amulet to support the king's eternal journey. In Greek mythology, the Amazonian warrior women were a tribe comprised solely of females, legendary for their bravery, even said to have removed their right breasts to better wield a bow and arrow. Nineteenth-century adventurers brought back blue-green stones believed to be found in the Amazon River basin, naming them after these warrior women. It has been passed down as a stone that connects **the courage to venture into uncharted territories** with **the power to express one's own voice.**

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What is the origin of its Japanese name, Tengaseki (天河石)?**

A. It originates from its Chinese name, "**Tianhe Shi**" (天河石), and comes from its **blue-green and white striped pattern** reminiscent of the Milky Way (galaxy) in the night sky. In the Japanese gem market, both its Japanese and Western names are used.

**Q2. What makes Russian Amazonite different?**

A. Amazonite from the Ural Mountains in Russia is considered the highest quality, prized for its beautiful **deep blue-green coloration and striking contrast with white streaks**. Brazilian Amazonite tends to have brighter, paler hues and is generally more affordable.

**Q3. I heard it's good for electromagnetic wave protection.**

A. While Amazonite is spoken of as a "**stone that neutralizes electromagnetic waves,**" this **is not scientifically proven**. Many people place it on their desks during PC work as a charm or a source of comfort.

**Q4. I heard it's good for anxiety. What are its effects?**

A. Culturally, it has been spoken of since ancient times as a "**stone that bestows hope and courage,**" but it has no medical therapeutic effects for anxiety disorders. It's recommended to use it as an amulet or for a change of mood, in conjunction with medical treatment.

**Q5. Are there other stones for self-expression?**

A. Aquamarine, Turquoise, Lapis Lazuli, and Sodalite are classic choices. For more details, please refer to the "Relationships" chapter in the [Comprehensive Guide to Power Stones by Purpose](/blog/luck-powerstones-complete-guide/).
