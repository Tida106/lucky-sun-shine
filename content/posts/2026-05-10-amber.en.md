## Introduction

Amber is an organic gemstone formed from **fossilized tree resin tens of millions of years ago**. In ancient Greece, amber was called **"elektron"** because it generated static electricity when rubbed, and this is the etymological root of the English word "electricity." Revered since ancient times as the **"Stone of the Sun,"** it's a rare gem that holds Earth's ancient history within, cherished worldwide as a talisman for health, longevity, and child protection. This article compiles **mineral data, historical background, reputed effects, FAQs, and an editor's note**.

Approximately **80% of the world's amber** originates from the Baltic Sea. **Blue amber** from the Dominican Republic is a rare variety that fluoresces blue under ultraviolet light. **"Inclusion amber,"** containing ancient insects or plant fragments, holds special significance both academically and artistically.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Lore and Historical and Cultural Background

Its gemstone lore includes "**Sun, Healing, and Ancient Wisdom**."

In Greek mythology, it's said that amber was formed from the tears of the Heliades, sisters who mourned the loss of their brother Phaeton, son of the sun god. During the reign of Roman Emperor Nero, large quantities of amber were imported from the Baltic Sea. In the Baltic countries of Northern Europe, it's known as the **"Tears of the Sun"** and the **"Tears of the Mermaids."** In Russia, Catherine the Great's Palace features the **"Amber Room,"** a world-heritage-level chamber with walls entirely covered in amber. In Europe, it has long been used as a talisman in **children's pacifiers and infant necklaces** to alleviate teething discomfort and night crying.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is Amber a "Stone"?**

A. Strictly speaking, it's not a mineral but a "**fossilized organic material (resin)**." However, in gemology, it's traditionally classified alongside organic gemstones like jet, pearl, coral, and ivory. Culturally, it's a beloved power stone.

**Q2. How can you tell the difference between plastic imitations and genuine amber?**

A. There are several ways to check: ① It floats in saltwater (genuine amber floats), ② It emits a pine resin scent when touched with a hot needle, and ③ It fluoresces under UV light. The most reliable method is to purchase from a **specialist store with a certificate of authenticity**.

**Q3. Can I buy inclusion amber?**

A. Yes. There is a wide range of inclusion amber available on the market, from **a few thousand yen to several million yen**. Amber from Myanmar can contain insects from the Cretaceous period, dating back 100 million years, and is highly valued academically.

**Q4. Can it be used as a pacifier for children?**

A. While traditionally used as infant necklaces in Europe, **it is not recommended in modern times due to the risk of choking and suffocation**. It is safer to display it as an accessory in a room, or for parents to wear it as they watch over their children.

**Q5. Are there other healing stones?**

A. Rose Quartz, Amethyst, and Aventurine are popular choices. For more details, refer to the healing and health section of our [Complete Guide to Power Stones by Purpose](/blog/luck-powerstones-complete-guide/).
