## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineralogical Data

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Stone Name | Amethyst / Purple Quartz |
| Japanese Name | Murasaki Suisho (Purple Crystal) |
| Mineral Species | Quartz |
| Chemical Composition | SiO₂ (contains trace amounts of iron) |
| Crystal System | Trigonal |
| Main Origins | Brazil, Uruguay, Zambia, Russia, Madagascar |
| Mohs Hardness | 7 |
| Specific Gravity | 2.65 |
| Color | Pale purple to deep reddish-purple (Violet to Purple) |
| Corresponding Chakras | 6th (Third Eye) / 7th (Crown) |

Amethyst gets its purple color from trace amounts of iron within quartz, which is then exposed to natural radiation underground. This is why **prolonged exposure to UV light can cause its color to fade, making it more transparent** — this is Amethyst's biggest weakness. Conversely, Citrine is formed when Amethyst is heated, causing a change in the iron's state that turns it yellow (most commercially available Citrine is heat-treated Amethyst).

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Language and Historical/Cultural Background

Its gemstone language includes "**True Love, Intuition, and Healing**."

Its name comes from the Greek "a-" (not) + "methystos" (to intoxicate) = meaning "**not intoxicated**." Greek mythology tells a tale where Dionysus, the god of wine, transformed the maiden Amethyst into a crystal statue, and his tears of wine dyed it purple.

In Ancient Rome, it was crafted into goblets and rings, symbolizing temperance and sincerity. In medieval Europe, it was known as the "**Bishop's Stone**" and was invariably used in the rings of high-ranking Catholic clergy (a custom that continues today). In Japan, purple has been considered a noble color since ancient times, holding the highest rank in Prince Shotoku's Twelve Level Cap and Rank System.

> Source: British Museum "Gems and Their Histories," GIA Amethyst Reference, and others.

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care

Recommended methods:

-   **Clear Quartz Chips** — The safest and suitable for all stones
-   **White Sage Smoke** — 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient
-   **Moonlight Bath** — Place it by a window three days before and after a full moon

Methods to avoid:

-   **Prolonged Direct Sunlight** — Causes fading (especially pale Amethyst can lose color in a few months)
-   **Directly Burying in Coarse Salt** — Can cause surface dullness and scratches

For daily care, simply wipe it gently with a soft cloth. If it gets sweaty or oily, rinse it quickly with lukewarm water and wipe it dry immediately.

## <span id="fake"></span>How to Spot Fakes and Treated Stones

-   **Heat-treated** Amethyst turns into Citrine, so "orange-tinted purple" might indicate heating.
-   **Dyed glass** has unnaturally uniform color without variation.
-   Stones with extreme transparency and absolutely no inclusions might be synthetic quartz.
-   **Coldness** — Natural stones feel colder than glass.
-   Purchasing from a reliable specialty store with an appraisal certificate provides peace of mind.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. I've seen stones that are a mix of Amethyst and Citrine. What are those?**

A. That's a stone also known as "**Ametrine**." Famous from Bolivia, it features a purple and yellow gradient. Since Amethyst and Citrine both belong to the quartz family, this combination can occur naturally.

**Q2. Is it okay to place Amethyst in the bedroom?**

A. Traditionally, it's actually **highly recommended**. There's a long-standing cultural practice worldwide of placing it by the bedside as a stone that supports peaceful sleep and intuition. However, avoid placing it in a window where morning sun directly shines, as UV light can cause fading.

**Q3. What color of Amethyst is best?**

A. Culturally, **deep reddish-purple** is considered the highest quality, but it's best to choose based on your preference. Pale colors are beautiful when light passes through them in the morning, while darker colors show deep richness under artificial lighting.

**Q4. Should I start with a cluster (geode) or a bracelet?**

A. If you want to carry it with you, go for a **bracelet**; if you want to place it in a space, choose a **cluster**. Many people use both, cleansing their bracelet on top of a cluster at night — that's a common way to use them.

**Q5. I was born in February, but I already have another birthstone. Should I still get Amethyst?**

A. Whether you "should" is a personal decision, but it's indeed considered compatible as a birthstone. If you already have another stone, adding Amethyst as a complementary piece is a natural choice.
