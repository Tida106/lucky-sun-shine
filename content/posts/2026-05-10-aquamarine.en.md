## Introduction

Aquamarine, a clear blue-green stone, gets its name from the Latin *aqua marina*, meaning "**water of the sea**." Ancient Roman sailors carried it as a good luck charm for their voyages, and in medieval Europe, it was an indispensable part of wedding ceremonies as "**the stone of marital harmony**." It belongs to the beryl family, just like emerald, and is cherished worldwide as the birthstone for March. ## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Lore, History, and Cultural Background

Its stone lore includes "**peace, communication, and happy marriage**."

Ancient Roman sailors, wishing for safe voyages and returns, offered aquamarine as a **tribute to Neptune, the Roman god of the sea**. In medieval Europe, there was a custom of giving it as "**the morning jewel gifted from husband to wife on the morning after the wedding**," hoping for marital harmony. Even today, it remains popular in Europe and the Americas for engagement rings and mother's rings. **High-quality, intensely blue aquamarines are known as "Santa Maria,"** named after a Brazilian mine.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What's the difference between heat-treated and natural aquamarine?**

A. Most aquamarines on the market are **heat-treated to enhance their blue color**, which is a standard industry practice. Greenish rough stones are heated to achieve a clearer, more vibrant blue. High-quality, completely untreated pieces are rare and expensive.

**Q2. What is "Santa Maria"?**

A. "Santa Maria" is the common name for **aquamarines with a particularly intense and clear blue color** produced from the Santa Maria de Itabira mine in Brazil. This mine is now depleted, but similarly deep blue aquamarines can also be found from African sources.

**Q3. Is it appropriate to give as a wedding charm?**

A. Yes, absolutely! This stone has historically been chosen as a gift, symbolizing "**marital harmony and happy marriage**." It's perfect for milestones like engagements, weddings, and silver anniversaries.

**Q4. Are there other birthstones for March?**

A. **Bloodstone (Heliotrope)** is also known as a traditional March birthstone. Some people choose between aquamarine and bloodstone based on their personality or preference, while others even own both!

**Q5. Are there other stones for marital bliss and finding a partner?**

A. Rose Quartz, Rhodochrosite, and Pink Tourmaline are popular choices. For more details, please refer to the chapter on marital luck in our [Comprehensive Power Stone Guide by Purpose](/blog/luck-powerstones-complete-guide/).
