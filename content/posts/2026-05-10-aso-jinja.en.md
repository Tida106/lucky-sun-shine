## Introduction

Aso Shrine stands at the foot of **Mount Aso**, an active volcano with one of the world's largest calderas, and is the **Ichinomiya of Higo Province**. Its main deity is **Takeiwatatsu no Mikoto**, a grandson of Emperor Jimmu, along with his family, the **Aso Twelve Gods**. As the ancestral deities of regional development, they have been revered for over 2,300 years, making this one of Kyushu's most ancient and prominent shrines. The **2016 Kumamoto Earthquake** caused the collapse of its Romon Gate and Haiden (worship hall), but **the Romon Gate was fully restored in December 2023**, once again welcoming worshippers from all over Japan. This article brings together the shrine's **history, Romon Gate restoration, the Takasago Pine, FAQ, and an editor's note** all on one page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

According to shrine tradition, Aso Shrine was founded in **282 BCE (the 9th year of Emperor Korei's reign)**. Its history begins with the pioneering myth of **Takeiwatatsu no Mikoto**, a grandson of Emperor Jimmu, who descended upon Aso and, according to legend, "kicked out the water from the caldera" at the site of Tateno, draining the huge lake that filled the caldera to create arable land. Since then, Aso Shrine, dedicated to Takeiwatatsu no Mikoto and the Aso Twelve Gods of his family, has been revered as the collective guardian deity of the entire Aso region.

From the medieval period onward, the Aso clan successively inherited the position of Daiguji (chief priest), establishing their own power as the Aso Daiguji family during the Sengoku period. The current shrine buildings from the Edo period were rebuilt in the Tenpo era of the 1840s. The Romon Gate was a magnificent architectural work, counted as one of **"Japan's Three Great Romon Gates"**, but it collapsed in the 2016 Kumamoto Earthquake. After seven years of restoration work, it was fully restored in 2023.

## <span id="romon"></span>One of Japan's Three Great Romon Gates and Its Restoration from the Earthquake

Aso Shrine's **Romon Gate** is one of **"Japan's Three Great Romon Gates,"** alongside those of **Iwashimizu Hachimangu Shrine** in Kyoto and **Hakozakigu Shrine** in Fukuoka. This magnificent two-storied gate, standing approximately 21 meters tall, was built in 1849 and is designated as an Important Cultural Property of Japan.

In **April 2016, the Kumamoto Earthquake** caused devastating damage, leading to the collapse of the Romon Gate, Haiden, and parts of the Shinden (main sanctuary). After **seven years of extensive restoration work**, the **Romon Gate was fully restored in December 2023**, returning almost to its original appearance from the time of its construction. It garnered national attention as a symbolic example of cultural property restoration, with approximately 12,000 salvaged parts meticulously repaired one by one.

## <span id="takasago"></span>The Matchmaking Takasago Pine

The **"Takasago Pine"** within the grounds of Aso Shrine has long been revered as a sacred tree for **matchmaking and fostering love**. The tradition states that **men walk around the pine from the left side, and women from the right**, while making a wish, to be blessed with a good partner. This practice is still observed by many visitors, especially younger ones.

Furthermore, it is believed that if you gently stroke the **"Gankake-ishi" (Wish-granting Stone)** within the shrine grounds while making a wish, your wish will come true. This makes Aso Shrine a cherished place for both matchmaking and the fulfillment of heartfelt desires.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

By Train: Approximately a 15-minute walk from JR Hohi Main Line "Miyaji Station."

By Car: Approximately 1 hour from Kumamoto IC on the Kyushu Expressway, or approximately 50 minutes from Mashiki Kumamoto Airport IC.

**Parking**: Free dedicated shrine parking available (approx. 40 spaces). During crowded times, paid parking in the Monzenmachi area is also available.

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Season and Time

The shrine is open for visits year-round. **Spring after the controlled burns (April-May)** when new green shoots emerge, **summer with blooming gentians in the grasslands (July-August)**, and **autumn foliage (October-November)** are particularly beautiful times. Please check in advance for any restrictions on entering the Nakadake crater due to volcanic gas warnings.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Etiquette

1.  Bow before passing through the Torii gate and Romon Gate.
2.  The center of the Sando (main approach path) is for the deities; please walk along the sides.
3.  At the Temizuya (water purification pavilion), cleanse your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, cleanse your left hand again, and finally clean the ladle.
4.  Perform **"Nihai Nihakushu Ippai" (two bows, two claps, one bow)**.
5.  When circling the Takasago Pine, please be considerate and do not obstruct other worshippers.
6.  Do not enter areas where restoration work is still underway.

If you are unsure about basic worship etiquette, please also refer to the [Complete Guide to Basic Shrine Visiting Etiquette](/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Has the restoration from the earthquake been completed?**

A. The Romon Gate was fully restored in December 2023, and major construction on the main buildings is largely complete. Some minor detail work may still be ongoing, so please check the official website for the latest information.

**Q2. How do I circle the Takasago Pine?**

A. Traditionally, **men circle the pine from the left, and women from the right**, while silently making a wish. There may be a queue, so please be courteous and yield to other worshippers.

**Q3. Can I visit the Mount Aso crater?**

A. Access to the Nakadake crater is subject to change based on **volcanic gas concentration and crater activity**. Please check the Aso Volcano Disaster Prevention Council's website for the latest conditions on the day of your visit. It is recommended that **individuals with respiratory conditions** refrain from visiting even if regulations are lifted.

**Q4. Where can I get a Goshuin (shrine stamp)?**

A. You can receive a Goshuin at the **Juyosho (shrine office / reception for amulets, etc.)** located to the right after entering the Romon Gate. Limited edition Goshuin may be available during certain periods to commemorate the restoration, so please inquire during your visit.

**Q5. Is the water from Kami no Izumi (Sacred Spring) potable?**

A. The **Kami no Izumi (Sacred Spring)** that wells up on the shrine grounds has long been considered sacred water. However, for official information regarding its potability and water quality testing, it's best to confirm directly with the shrine office. Traditionally, it is used for purifying hands and face.
