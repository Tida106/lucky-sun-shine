## Introduction

April's birthstone, **Diamond**, is the hardest mineral on Earth, truly the "**King of Gems**"! Its everlasting sparkle is the ultimate symbol of "**eternal love, an unyielding spirit, and purity**," making it an absolute classic for wedding and engagement rings. Because of its rather high price tag, **Quartz (Crystal)** has been cherished as an alternative birthstone in Japan for a long time. It's a stone that perfectly blends **sparkle and strength**, just right for those born in April, marking the start of a new academic year and spring!

## What are April Birthstones?

April's main birthstone is **Diamond**, with **Quartz (Crystal)** traditionally recognized as an alternative. In the **2021 birthstone revisions**, **Morganite** was also added!

## Meanings and Stone Lore

The stone lore for Diamond speaks of "**Eternal Bond, Unyielding Spirit, Purity, and Cleansing**." Its name comes from the Greek word "**adamas**," meaning "**unconquerable**," reflecting its incredible hardness (Mohs scale 10), which means it **cannot be shattered by anything**.

> **Please Note**: The effects of power stones are cultural and symbolic. They are not medically or scientifically guaranteed.

In ancient India, it was believed that diamonds were born from "**tears of the gods**" or "**fallen lightning bolts**," making them amulets for warriors. During medieval Europe, they adorned **crowns and scepters**, and from modern times onward, they became the **go-to for engagement rings**, symbolizing "eternal love." On the other hand, its alternative, Quartz, is known as the "**Master Crystal**" or "**all-purpose stone**," celebrated for its purifying and amplifying properties. It shares a common symbolism with diamonds: **transparent purity**.

## Fun Facts and Origins

-   **Etymology**: From the Greek word *adamas* (unconquerable).
-   **Mohs Hardness**: 10 (the highest hardness), but still vulnerable to impact.
-   **The 4 Cs**: Its value is determined by Carat (weight), Color, Clarity, and Cut.
-   **Origin in India**: India was the first source in the 4th century BCE, later followed by Brazil and South Africa as major producers.
-   **History of Engagement Rings**: Became a global standard with De Beers' "**A Diamond is Forever**" campaign in 1947.
-   **Quartz**: In Japan, **Kofu, Yamanashi Prefecture**, has been a historic center for quartz polishing and continues as a local industry today.
-   **Master Crystal**: Quartz is believed to "**amplify the energy of other stones**," making it a versatile stone that forms the core of many combinations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What if diamonds are too expensive for me?**

A. **Quartz (Crystal)** is recognized as a traditional alternative birthstone. **Morganite**, added in 2021, is also popular as an April birthstone at a more affordable price. You can find Quartz starting from **around 2,000 yen**, offering a lovely presence as a birth month amulet.

**Q2. Which stones pair well with diamonds?**

A. Classics include **[Quartz](/blog/clear-quartz/)** (amplifies as it's also colorless and transparent), **[Rose Quartz](/blog/rose-quartz-meaning/)** (harmony with love), **[Morganite](/blog/morganite/)** (another April birthstone), and **[Aquamarine](/blog/aquamarine/)** (often combined as an engagement stone).

**Q3. If diamonds are "unyielding," does that mean they are also resistant to impact?**

A. **Hardness (resistance to scratching) and toughness (resistance to breaking) are different things**! While diamonds have a Mohs hardness of 10 and are incredibly scratch-resistant, they do have a cleavage property, meaning they can split if a **strong impact is applied in a specific direction**. Bumping your ring or overusing an ultrasonic cleaner can sometimes cause them to chip or break.

**Q4. Can Quartz really be an alternative to diamonds?**

A. While their prices are worlds apart, they share common symbolism like "**colorless transparency, purity, and cleansing**." Since ancient times, **Quartz was believed to be "ice that never melts**," and it has been widely accepted as an April birthstone in Japan. It definitely holds enough value as a protective amulet!

**Q5. Is it okay to wear diamonds every day?**

A. Yes, absolutely! However, because **rings can accumulate soap, detergents, and cosmetics**, I recommend cleaning them about once a month with a **mild detergent and a soft toothbrush**. Getting into the habit of taking them off during housework or exercise will help maintain their beauty for a long time.
