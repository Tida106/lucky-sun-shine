## Introduction

The August birthstone **Peridot** is a gem perfect for the height of summer, known as the **"Evening Emerald" or "Sun of the Night"** due to its bright yellowish-green glow. It is said to have been cherished by Cleopatra in ancient Egypt, and has long been beloved in Japan as an August birthstone, symbolizing **"marital harmony, hope, and warding off evil."** With **Sardonyx** (traditional) and **Spinel** (added in 2021) also officially recognized, August offers a rich array of choices!

## Fun Facts and Origins

-   **Etymology**: Several theories exist, with the Arabic word *faridat* (gem) being the most likely origin.
-   **Mohs Hardness**: 6.5 to 7.
-   **"Sun of the Night"**: Named for its property of shining brightly even by candlelight.
-   **Cleopatra's Stone**: In the 20th century, mineralogists pointed out that some of the emeralds Cleopatra loved were actually peridot.
-   **Meteorite Peridot**: Peridot of cosmic origin is found in **pallasite meteorites**, gaining popularity as an extraterrestrial birthstone.
-   **Sardonyx**: Used in ancient Rome for **seals**, with patterns carved along the red and white bands to create impressions.
-   **Spinel**: The "Black Prince's Ruby" in the British Crown Jewels is **actually a spinel**, reflecting its long history of being confused with ruby.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What is the difference between Peridot and Emerald?**

A. They are **different minerals**. Peridot is the gem name for the mineral **olivine**, while emerald is **beryl**. Peridot is a **bright yellowish-green**, while emerald is a **deep green**. Peridot is also generally more affordable.

**Q2. What stones pair well with Peridot?**

A. **[Clear Quartz](/blog/clear-quartz/)** (amplification), **[Citrine](/blog/citrine/)** (another yellow-hued sun stone), **[Aventurine](/blog/aventurine/)** (healing with other green stones), and **[Rose Quartz](/blog/rose-quartz-meaning/)** (harmony with love) are classic pairings.

**Q3. Is Spinel really indistinguishable from Ruby?**

A. It's difficult to distinguish them with the naked eye; **even professionals rely on gem identification reports**. Spinel has a **single crystal system (octahedral)**, while ruby has a **trigonal system (hexagonal prism)**. Accurate identification is possible by measuring their refractive indices.

**Q4. Is it true that Peridot is fragile?**

A. Yes. While its Mohs hardness is 6.5 to 7, its **toughness (resistance to breaking)** is relatively low, making it prone to cracking from sudden temperature changes and impacts. It's best to avoid **ultrasonic and steam cleaners** and to remove it before housework or exercise.

**Q5. What is the difference between Sardonyx and Onyx?**

A. **Onyx is a black-banded agate**, while **Sardonyx is a red and white banded agate**, so their colors are completely different. Mineralogically, both are banded varieties of **chalcedony**. In ancient times, both were referred to as "onyx."
