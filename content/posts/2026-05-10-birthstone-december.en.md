## Introduction

December offers one of the most generous selections of any month, with **four officially recognized birthstones: Turquoise, Tanzanite, Lapis Lazuli, and Zircon**. 

Each brings its own legendary charm: **Turquoise as the stone of the sky and the traveler's guardian, Tanzanite as the 20th-century "Stone of the New Era," Lapis Lazuli as a sacred fragment of heaven, and Zircon with its brilliant, diamond-rivalling sparkle**. All four share stunning shades of blue and cool tones that reflect the crisp winter air. They make deep, mystical companions for anyone born in the closing month of the year!

## What Are the December Birthstones?

Historically, **Turquoise, Lapis Lazuli, and Zircon** have long been celebrated as traditional December birthstones. **Tanzanite** officially joined the global lineup after Tiffany and Co. named and introduced it to the world in the late 1960s.

## Meanings and Gemstone Lore

Here is a closer look at the traditional meanings behind each December gem:

- **Turquoise**: **"Success, Prosperity, Traveler's Protection."** Revered by Ancient Egyptians, Native Americans, and Tibetans as a stone that captures the spirit and color of the open sky.
- **Tanzanite**: **"Transformation, New Beginnings, Spirituality."** Discovered near Mount Kilimanjaro in 1967 and celebrated by Tiffany and Co. as the "Stone of the 20th Century."
- **Lapis Lazuli**: **"Truth, Wisdom, Celestial Power."** A royal gem famously inlaid around the eyes of King Tutankhamun's golden funerary mask.
- **Zircon**: **"Authentic Radiance, Grounded Confidence."** A natural, ancient gemstone whose dazzling refractive index rivals that of diamond.

## Fun Facts and Origins

- **The Name "Turquoise"**: Comes from the French phrase *pierre turquoise*, meaning "Turkish stone," because the gem first reached European markets through trade routes across Turkey.
- **Persian Turquoise**: Mined in modern-day Iran, fine Persian turquoise has historically set the standard for pure, robin's-egg blue with minimal veining.
- **Tanzanite's Single Source**: Found exclusively in a small mining patch near the foothills of **Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania**—nowhere else on Earth!
- **Named by Tiffany and Co.**: Introduced in 1969 by Tiffany, which declared it the most exciting new gem discovery in 2,000 years.
- **Lapis Lazuli**: Valued higher than gold in ancient times, lapis was ground down to create **ultramarine**, the most expensive blue pigment used by Renaissance master painters.
- **Zircon**: A completely natural mineral with brilliant optical fire; Australian zircon crystals have been dated at **4.4 billion years old**, making them among the oldest fragments of Earth's crust ever discovered.
- **Natural Zircon vs. Cubic Zirconia**: Cubic zirconia is an inexpensive lab-created synthetic stone, whereas **Zircon is a genuine, naturally occurring gemstone** with centuries of history.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Which December birthstone should I choose?**

A. **All four are officially recognized December birthstones!** A popular way to decide is by personal intention: choose **Turquoise** for protection and courage during travel, **Tanzanite** for personal reinvention and milestones, **Lapis Lazuli** for study, focus, and ancient wisdom, or **Zircon** for unmatched clarity and light.

**Q2. Which crystals pair well with Tanzanite?**

Tanzanite pairs beautifully with **[Clear Quartz](/blog/clear-quartz/)** to amplify its clarity, **[Amethyst](/blog/amethyst/)** to enhance spiritual intuition, **[Lapis Lazuli](/blog/lapis-lazuli/)** for unified December harmony, and **[Moonstone](/blog/moonstone/)** to support emotional ease during life changes.

**Q3. Is natural Zircon the same as Cubic Zirconia?**

A. **No, they are completely different.** Natural Zircon is a genuine earth-mined gemstone—in fact, one of the oldest minerals on Earth. Cubic zirconia is an artificial, man-made crystal produced in laboratories. Despite their similar names, their geological value and rarity are entirely separate.

**Q4. Does Turquoise change color over time?**

A. Yes, natural, untreated turquoise is porous and can absorb oils, perspiration, cosmetics, and lotions, which can gradually shift its color from sky blue to greenish tones. Some people treasure this gradual change as personal character, but if you prefer to preserve the original blue, wipe your jewelry with a soft, dry cloth right after wearing it.

**Q5. Why is Tanzanite called a "one-generation gemstone"?**

A. Because its only known commercial deposit is concentrated in a tiny strip of land in northern Tanzania. Geologists estimate that these mines could be exhausted within decades, leading many collectors to describe it as "a thousand times rarer than diamond."
