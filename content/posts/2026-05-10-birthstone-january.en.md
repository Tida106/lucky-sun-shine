## What is a Birthstone?

So, what exactly are birthstones? The tradition of assigning specific gemstones to each month is believed to have originated in 18th-century Poland, eventually spreading worldwide. In Japan, the Japan Jewelers' Association established a modern birthstone list in 1958, which underwent a **significant revision in 2021 after about 60 years**, adding new stone varieties. For January, **Garnet** has consistently been the main birthstone through the ages, and recently, **Rhodonite** was added as a new option.

## Meaning and Gemstone Lore

Garnet's gemstone lore speaks of "**fruition, victory, passion, and true love**." Its name originates from the Latin word "**granatum**," meaning pomegranate seed, inspired by its crystal structure resembling pomegranate arils. From ornaments worn by ancient Egyptian royalty to Roman soldiers' seals and Crusader amulets—it has been cherished across epochs as a "**crystal of effort and sacrifice**."

## Fun Facts and Origins

- **Etymology**: From Latin *granatum* (pomegranate seed). It was called *granatus* in Medieval Latin, which evolved into the English word "garnet."
- **Mohs Hardness**: 6.5 to 7.5, a durable hardness for everyday wear.
- **Variety**: Garnet is a collective name for a group of **about 20 different minerals**, including Pyrope, Almandine, Spessartine, Grossular, and Andradite.
- **Green Garnet**: Rare varieties like "**Tsavorite**" and "**Demantoid**" possess a sparkle entirely different from the deep red.
- **Bohemian Garnet**: Small, deep red garnets from the Czech Republic (formerly Bohemia) sparked a huge craze in 19th-century Europe.
- **Warrior's Stone**: It is said that Crusader soldiers **embedded garnets in their shields** to seek protection on bloody battlefields. How amazing is that?
