## Introduction

June is truly blessed when it comes to gemstones, offering three officially recognized birthstones: **Moonstone, Pearl, and Alexandrite**! This makes June one of the months with the **widest variety of choices**. Celebrated worldwide for the tradition of the "June Bride," June places **Moonstone and Pearl**—symbols of gentle femininity and fresh beginnings—front and center. Their soft, milky luminescence glows beautifully against the gentle light of early summer.

## What Are June's Birthstones?

Traditionally, **Pearl** has held the main spot, with **Moonstone** standing alongside it, and **Alexandrite** cherished as a classic, prestigious addition. Following the **2021 gemstone revision** in Japan, all three gems are now officially recognized side by side.

## Meanings and Gemstone Messages

Here is a closer look at the symbolic messages behind each stone:

- **Moonstone**: "**Intuition, Femininity, and New Beginnings**." Believed to be an embodiment of Diana, the goddess of the moon, ancient lore says its inner glow shifts along with the lunar phases.
- **Pearl**: "**Health, Longevity, and Purity**." Famous for the ancient Roman legend of Cleopatra dissolving a pearl in vinegar, and celebrated globally after **Kokichi Mikimoto invented cultured pearls in 1893** in Japan.
- **Alexandrite**: "**Transformation and King of Birthstones**." Renowned for changing color from **green in daylight to red under incandescent light**, earning it the title of "the stone with two faces: day and night."

## Trivia and History

- **The June Bride**: Named after **Juno**, the ancient Roman goddess of marriage and childbirth, who oversees the month of June.
- **Cultured Pearls**: Kokichi Mikimoto succeeded in culturing the world's first hemispherical pearl in Japan in 1893, later being hailed by the British Royal Family as the "Pearl King."
- **Moonstone's Adularescence**: A mesmerizing optical phenomenon caused by alternating feldspar layers scattering light to create a floating **blue and white glow**.
- **Alexandrite's Discovery**: Unearthed in the Ural Mountains of Russia in 1830 and named in honor of the future Tsar, **Alexander II**.
- **The "Alexandrite Effect"**: The stone shifts from bluish-green under natural daylight to reddish-purple under incandescent light due to chromium impurities in its crystal structure.
- **Etymology of Pearl**: Derived from the Latin word *perna*, referring to a leg-shaped bivalve mollusk, which eventually evolved into the English word "pearl."

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How should I wear pearls for formal occasions?**

A. For **weddings and celebrations**, a **single-strand white Akoya pearl necklace** is standard. For **memorials and funerals**, a **single-strand white necklace (or black Tahitian pearls)** is appropriate. A length of **around 16 inches (choker length)** is considered the classic standard. Avoid double-strand necklaces at funerals, as doubling strands traditionally symbolizes "multiplying misfortune."

**Q2. What is the difference between Moonstone and Pearl?**

A. **Moonstone is a mineral** (a variety of feldspar), whereas **pearl is an organic gem** produced by living mollusks. While both share a dreamy white tone, moonstone is famous for its floating, inner blue-white sheen (**adularescence**), while pearls showcase a luminous, rainbow-like **surface luster (orient)**.

**Q3. Why is Alexandrite so expensive?**

A. Natural alexandrite that exhibits a striking **daytime green to nighttime red color change** is exceptionally rare in the gem world. Because fine-quality rough material is so scarce, it can command higher prices per carat than diamonds of similar size, earning it legendary status among gem collectors.

**Q4. Which crystals pair well with Moonstone?**

A. Excellent pairings include **[Clear Quartz](/blog/clear-quartz/)** (energy amplification), **[Rose Quartz](/blog/rose-quartz-meaning/)** (harmonizing unconditional love), **[Aquamarine](/blog/aquamarine/)** (the deep connection between the sea and the moon), and **[Labradorite](/blog/labradorite/)** (a kindred member of the feldspar family).

**Q5. Can pearls last for decades?**

A. Yes! With thoughtful care, pearls can easily last for **decades and across multiple generations**. Always wipe away perspiration and cosmetics with a soft cloth after wearing, store them flat away from direct sunlight, and protect them from perfumes and hairsprays. Treated with love, they become treasured family heirlooms passed from mother to daughter.
