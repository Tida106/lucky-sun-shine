## Introduction

The primary birthstone for March is **Aquamarine**, alongside its classical alternative, **Bloodstone**. Cherished since ancient times as the **"stone of serene seas and happy marriage protected by the sea goddess,"** Aquamarine was carried by ancient sailors as a protective talisman. With its **pure, soothing light-blue glow**, it is the perfect gem for anyone born in March as the refreshing breeze of spring begins to arrive!

## What Are the March Birthstones?

While **Aquamarine** has long been beloved as the main birthstone for March, **Bloodstone** has traditionally stood beside it as a classic counterpart. In addition, **Coral and Iolite** were officially added during birthstone revisions in 2021.

## Meaning and Symbolism

The core meanings associated with Aquamarine are **"serenity, wisdom, a happy marriage, and honest communication."** Its name comes from the Latin **"aqua marina," meaning "water of the sea."** Legend tells that these gemstones washed ashore after slipping from the treasure chest of Aphrodite, the goddess of the sea.

> **Note**: The spiritual and emotional properties of gemstones are cultural and symbolic traditions. They are not intended as medical or scientific guarantees.

In ancient Rome, Aquamarine served as **a protective amulet for sailors**, while in medieval Europe, it was gifted at engagements and weddings as a **"stone of marriage."** Resembling the soothing, never-ending rhythm of ocean waves, it symbolizes an **"unbroken, calm relationship."** Even today, it is frequently chosen as an accent stone for wedding and anniversary rings to invite a lifetime of marital bliss. 

Meanwhile, **Bloodstone**, with its distinctive dark green body scattered with crimson spots, carries the legendary lore of **"green jasper marked by the blood of Christ."** Across history, it has been treasured as a powerful talisman of courage and physical vitality.

## Trivia and Origins

- **Etymology**: Derived from the Latin *aqua marina* ("sea water").
- **Mohs Hardness**: 7.5 to 8, making it durable and part of the beryl mineral family.
- **Sister to the Emerald**: Aquamarine and **Emerald are both beryls**. Trace amounts of iron produce Aquamarine's serene blue, while chromium gives Emerald its deep green!
- **Sailor's Amulet**: Ancient Roman mariners carried it to **calm stormy seas**, and naval officers during the Napoleonic era kept it close during voyages.
- **Royal Elegance**: Queen Elizabeth II famously owned a striking tiara and jewelry suite featuring exceptionally large Aquamarines.
- **Standardized in 1912**: The American National Retail Jewelers Association officially standardized Aquamarine as the March birthstone in 1912, though its European popularity stretches back to the Middle Ages.
- **Bloodstone's Lore**: Also known as **heliotrope**, it was worn by warriors in ancient Babylon and Egypt, believed to stop bleeding on the battlefield and instill unbreakable courage.

**Q3. What is the difference between Aquamarine and Blue Topaz?**

A. While they can look remarkably similar, **they are entirely different minerals**. Aquamarine belongs to the **beryl family** (hardness 7.5 to 8), whereas Blue Topaz is a **topaz mineral** (hardness 8). Aquamarine typically shows a softer, slightly greenish-blue hue, while Blue Topaz often features a more vibrant, saturated blue. To learn more, read our article on [Blue Topaz Meaning and Benefits](/blog/blue-topaz/).

**Q5. What if I don't love green or dark stones like Bloodstone?**
