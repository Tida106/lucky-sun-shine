## Introduction

Enoshima Shrine is an ancient shrine that considers the entire **Enoshima Island**, floating in the sea off Shonan, as its sacred precinct. It comprises three sub-shrines within the island: **Hetsunomiya**, **Nakatsunomiya**, and **Okutsunomiya**, which collectively enshrine the **Munakata Three Goddesses**. Historically, it has been revered as **"Enoshima Benzaiten"** and is a maritime sacred site counted among **Japan's Three Great Benzaiten**, alongside Aki no Miyajima Island and Omi no Chikubushima Island. This article consolidates information on its **history, the Three Goddesses, the recommended order for visiting the three sub-shrines, FAQs, and an editor's note** into a single page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

According to shrine tradition, Enoshima Shrine's origin traces back to the 13th year of Emperor Kinmei (552 AD), when the Munakata Three Goddesses were invited and enshrined in a rock cave on the southern part of the island (Enoshima Iwaya Caves) by imperial command. It is a historically significant spiritual site, with records indicating that renowned ascetic practitioners such as En no Gyōja, Kūkai, and Ennin trained on Enoshima.

During the Kamakura period, Minamoto no Yoritomo commanded Mongaku to enshrine Benzaiten, and the shrine garnered reverence from the samurai government. In the Edo period, it sparked a popular pilgrimage travel boom among commoners known as "Enoshima-mōde" and was repeatedly depicted in ukiyo-e by artists like Hokusai and Hiroshige, becoming a popular place of worship. Due to the separation of Shinto and Buddhism during the Meiji era, the Buddhist-style Benzaiten worship was transformed into Shinto, leading to the current system with the Munakata Three Goddesses as the main deities.

## <span id="threeshrines"></span>Characteristics of Each of the Three Sub-Shrines

| Shrine Name | Enshrined Deity | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Hetsunomiya** (Lower Shrine) | Tagitsuhime-no-Mikoto | The first shrine near the entrance, where the Hōandō Hall (housing Benzaiten statues) is located. |
| **Nakatsunomiya** (Middle Shrine) | Ichikishimahime-no-Mikoto | Features a beautifully vermilion-lacquered shrine building, known for blessings related to female beauty. |
| **Okutsunomiya** (Main Shrine) | Tagorihime-no-Mikoto | Located at the farthest end of Enoshima, near Ryūgū (Wadatsumi-no-Miya) and featuring the "Eight-Direction Staring Turtle" painting. |

Visiting the three shrines in the order of **Hetsunomiya → Nakatsunomiya → Okutsunomiya** is the traditional route.

## <span id="benten"></span>Japan's Three Great Benzaiten and Happi Benzaiten and Myōon Benzaiten

Enoshima Shrine is one of **Japan's Three Great Benzaiten**, alongside **Itsukushima Shrine** in Aki no Miyajima and **Hogonji Temple** on Omi no Chikubushima Island. Within the Hetsunomiya precinct, the **Hōandō Hall** houses two precious Benzaiten statues: **Happi Benzaiten (Eight-Armed Benzaiten)** and **Myōon Benzaiten (Naked Benzaiten)**.

-   **Happi Benzaiten**: An eight-armed Benzaiten, each holding a weapon, known for good fortune in competition.
-   **Myōon Benzaiten**: A naked Benzaiten playing a biwa, guardian of music and performing arts.

You can pay an admission fee to worship them, and pilgrims aspiring to artistic pursuits visit continuously.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Visiting Points

-   The traditional order of worship is **Hetsunomiya → Nakatsunomiya → Okutsunomiya**.
-   Separate admission fee is required for **Happi Benzaiten and Myōon Benzaiten in Hōandō Hall**.
-   Look up at the ceiling painting of the **"Eight-Direction Staring Turtle"** in Okutsunomiya.
-   Experience the legend of the Five-Headed Dragon at **Ryūgū (Wadatsumi-no-Miya)**, a tale passed down on Enoshima.
-   Consider using the **Enoshima Escar (paid)** as there are many stairs.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

By train: Approximately 15 minutes walk from **Katase-Enoshima Station** on the Odakyu Enoshima Line, about 20 minutes walk from **Enoshima Station** on the Enoden Line, and about 20 minutes walk from **Shonan-Enoshima Station** on the Shonan Monorail.

**Parking**: Several paid parking lots are available on and around the island. Public transportation is recommended, especially on weekends when parking lots are almost certainly full.

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Season and Time

Open for worship year-round. **May to October** offers beautiful views of the sea and blue sky, and **sunset** provides a spectacular silhouette of Mt. Fuji from the Sea Candle. **Early mornings in winter** are less crowded, allowing for a more peaceful visit. Expect crowds on weekends and national holidays.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Etiquette

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities; walk on the sides.
3.  Purify your left hand, then right hand, then mouth, then left hand, and finally the dipper at the chozuya (water ablution pavilion).
4.  Perform **two bows, two claps, and one bow** at the main shrine.
5.  Photography is prohibited inside the Hōandō Hall.
6.  Be considerate of other worshippers when on stairs.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. In what order should I visit the three sub-shrines?**

A. Traditionally, the order is **Hetsunomiya (near the entrance) → Nakatsunomiya → Okutsunomiya (farthest end)**. This is also a natural route geographically. It takes about 30 to 40 minutes one way on foot, which can be shortened by using the Enoshima Escar.

**Q2. Can I see the Benzaiten statues in Hōandō Hall at any time?**

A. Yes, you can view them during regular visiting hours by paying an admission fee (200 yen for adults). Seeing both the **Happi Benzaiten and Myōon Benzaiten** is a major highlight of Enoshima Shrine.

**Q3. What is the Enoshima Escar?**

A. It is a **paid escalator** designed to help visitors ascend the steep stairs on the island. It has three sections, and a full-section ticket costs 360 yen for adults. It is popular among those who find stairs challenging or are visiting with children. The descent is by stairs or on foot only.

**Q4. Is Enoshima Shrine famous for matchmaking blessings?**

A. The Munakata Three Goddesses are considered beautiful deities, and **Nakatsunomiya** in particular attracts many visitors praying for female beauty and matchmaking. Many worshippers also entrust their wishes to the **Musubi no Ki (Matchmaking Tree)** and **ema (votive tablets)** within the precincts.

**Q5. Can I go to the Enoshima Iwaya Caves?**

A. Yes, if you continue past Okutsunomiya, you will reach the **Enoshima Iwaya Caves**. This is the birthplace of Enoshima Shrine, and the admission fee is 500 yen. Inside the cave, you can have a mystical experience **navigating through the dim passages with a candle in hand**.
