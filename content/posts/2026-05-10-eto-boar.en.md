## Introduction

The Boar (Inoshishi) is the twelfth and final animal sign in the Chinese zodiac. Just like the saying "rushing in like a wild boar" suggests, the image of a boar charging straight ahead has made it a symbol of **bravery, directness, purity, and good health.** In Buddhism, the Boar is considered an attendant of Marici, the goddess of war, and was deeply revered by warriors as a messenger who brings victory by remaining unseen by enemies. The stone that resonates most deeply with those born in the Year of the Boar is **Hematite**, which boasts a rich, metallic luster due to its iron content. Also known as the "**blood stone**," it has been used since ancient times as a warrior's shield and serves as a protective stone that healthily supports the Boar's charging power.

## Personality and Fortune for Those Born in the Year of the Boar

Those born in the Year of the Boar are considered the most "**straightforward**" of all the zodiac signs. They are known for their purity, disliking lies and manipulation, and for their courage to charge ahead without hesitation once they've set their minds on something. Their resilience to confront challenges head-on and their refusal to back down easily are often said to be unmatched among the twelve zodiac signs.

In their careers, they tend to excel in fields that require a **direct path to results**, such as entrepreneurs, pioneers, technical professionals, artisans, athletes, and on-site leaders. The Boar zodiac sign is also a symbol of "**stamina**," and it's said that many born under this sign possess strong physical and mental resilience, making them well-suited for long hours and physically demanding work.

However, their straightforward nature can sometimes manifest as **stubbornness, tunnel vision that makes them unaware of their surroundings, and a tendency for conflict in relationships.** The habit of not being able to stop once they start is a trait that those born in the Year of the Boar are often advised to consciously moderate.

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Boar is associated with the element of "Water" and governs the hours between 9 PM and 11 PM, the time when the day quietly transitions into sleep. **When the sun has completely set, and preparations for the next day begin**—though it might seem like a symbol of rest, it's actually considered a profound time when energy is accumulated for the coming year and future generations. This is said to be the very source of the Boar's charging power.

## Why Hematite is a Perfect Match

Hematite is a mineral known as red iron ore. When polished, it shines with a silvery-black metallic luster, but if you scratch it, it produces a red powder, leading to its name the "**blood stone**" (Haima = blood). In ancient Rome, warriors wore it as shields and amulets, believing it to be a stone that "deflects enemy blades" and "protects the blood flowing through the body."

The primary reason Hematite resonates with those born in the Year of the Boar is that it's known as a "**grounding stone**" (a stone that helps you feel rooted). While Boars excel in their charging power, they are also prone to recklessness. Hematite is said to help channel that momentum into "**earth-rooted strength**." The gravitas of the boar and iron are said to deeply resonate in terms of energy direction.

Furthermore, Hematite has been traditionally known as a symbolic stone governing "**blood and health**," beautifully aligning with the Boar, which is a zodiac sign associated with "**good health and protection from illness**." It's said that in some regions, there's a custom of displaying a boar figurine and Hematite together for the family, as a traditional charm to support the health luck of those born in the Year of the Boar.

## Complementary Stones to Carry

- **Garnet**: This deep red gemstone, resembling a pomegranate, is believed to govern victory and safe return. It's said to transform the Boar's charge into "**stamina to finish the race**." As a fellow red-toned stone, it's thought to have a particularly strong affinity with the Boar.
- **Smoky Quartz**: A brown variety of quartz and a prime grounding stone. Alongside Hematite, it imparts earth-rooted strength and is said to help "**release fatigue into the ground**" after a vigorous charge.
- **Carnelian**: An orange-red stone of action and passion. It's believed to further refine the Boar's momentum, serving as a companion stone that guides their energy not towards recklessness, but towards **progress in the right direction**.

- **Clothing Colors**: Deep red, black, and gray. Deep, rich color schemes that complement Hematite's metallic luster are said to enhance the Boar's dignified presence.
- **Wallets**: Black, brown, and red tones. They are said to be well-suited for long-term use, and using a long wallet to keep bills straight, rather than a bi-fold, is believed to align most naturally with the Boar's characteristics.
- **Places of Worship**: Go'o Shrine in Kyoto (famous for its狛イノシシ—boar guardian statues—instead of guardian dogs), Kennin-ji Zenkyoan Temple in Kyoto (dedicated to Marici), Yushima Seido Tenmangu and Marishiten Tokudaiji Temple in Tokyo, and others. The custom of eating "**Inoko mochi**" (boar child rice cakes) is considered a tradition most harmonious with the Year of the Boar, as it prays for good health and protection from illness.
- **Habit**: A 5-minute daily grounding meditation focusing on "**deep breathing and sole awareness**." It's said that by consciously cultivating the feeling of stomping the ground like a boar, the Boar's charging power can be healthily maintained.

## Years of Bad Luck, Milestones, and Stone Usage

For those born in the Year of the Boar, the year of their own zodiac sign, occurring once every 12 years (known as *Toshio-otoko* or *Toshionna*), is called the "**Year of Reckoning**" or "Culmination Year," marking the grand finale of the zodiac cycle. It's considered a milestone year where the path they've charged down thus far comes together, shaping the direction for the next 12 years. Many are said to acquire a new Hematite during this time.

During their *Hon-yaku* (main unlucky year, e.g., 42 for men and 33 for women by traditional Japanese counting), their charging power is prone to manifesting as "**overwork**" or "**conflict**." It's traditionally recommended to use Smoky Quartz alongside it to supplement "**the courage to pause**" and "**the awareness to listen to one's body**." It's also suggested to approach this year with a conscious effort to undergo health check-ups and medical examinations.

The direct opposite zodiac sign for those born in the Year of the Boar is the **Year of the Snake (Mi-doshi)**, and this year is said to be one where they might easily be swayed by deep insights and strategic moves. During this year, it's favored to use Hematite as a base and reaffirm sincere convictions with Garnet.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone in my *Toshio-otoko* or *Toshionna* year (my zodiac year)?**
A. The Year of the Boar is considered a 'culmination year,' and many people acquire a new Hematite to serve as a companion for the next 12 years. It's common to cleanse old stones with gratitude and use them as secondary companions.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. For those born in the Year of the Boar, it's often a good match to use different stones depending on the situation, such as for challenges or health. It's favored to combine the main Hematite with other red-toned stones like Garnet or Carnelian.

**Q. Is it bad to carry stones of other zodiac signs?**
A. It's considered perfectly fine. In fact, complementary combinations have been traditionally recommended since ancient times, such as using the Rabbit's Rose Quartz to supplement the Boar's occasional lack of 'flexibility,' or the Snake's Amethyst for 'deeper insight.'

**Q. Can I bring Hematite into the bath or hot spring?**
A. Since Hematite contains iron, it's said that prolonged exposure to water or hot spring minerals can cause discoloration or rust. It's generally recommended to remove it before bathing for peace of mind.
