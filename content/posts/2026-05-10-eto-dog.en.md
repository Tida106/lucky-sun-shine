## Introduction

The "Dog" (Inu) is the eleventh sign in the Japanese Zodiac (Jūnishi). Symbolizing the loyal nature of a dog guarding its home and following its master, it has long been associated with **loyalty, honesty, friendship, and justice**. Dogs have been known since ancient times for their easy deliveries, and the tradition of the **Inu no Hi belly band (Obi-iwai)** is still widely observed by pregnant women in Japan today. Reliable guardians of the home – those born in the Year of the Dog truly resonate with **Garnet**, a stone that, with its deep red glow, embodies "bonds" and "victory." Believed to have been used as an amulet by warriors since ancient times, Garnet is said to watch over those who walk an honest path until the very end.

## Personality and Fortune of Those Born in the Year of the Dog

Those born in the Year of the Dog are considered among the most "**trustworthy**" of the twelve zodiac signs. They are known for their honesty, always keeping promises, and seeing through what they've started until the very end. It's often said that once they open their hearts to someone, they pour deep affection into that relationship, often building long-lasting bonds.

In their careers, they are said to truly excel in fields where **trust directly translates into results**, such as policing, firefighting, self-defense forces, healthcare, welfare, specialized professions, inheriting family businesses, and long-term consulting roles that require ongoing support. Many are willing to "**get their hands dirty**" on the front lines, and they are often highly regarded as dependable figures who quietly support their organizations and colleagues.

However, this very honesty can sometimes manifest as "**stubbornness**," "**shyness**," or a "**rigid sense of justice that sees everything in black and white**." They also tend to keep "barking" at anyone they perceive as an enemy, so learning to balance their distinctions between friend and foe is said to be a key challenge for those born in the Year of the Dog.

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Dog is associated with the element of "Earth" and governs the hours of 7 to 9 PM, the twilight time when people are hurrying home. The **dog that guards the home** – a symbol of protection at the end of the day. This deep affection for family and friends is said to be the very core of the Dog's fortune.

## Why Garnet is a Perfect Match

Garnet, with its deep crimson crystals resembling pomegranate seeds, is said to have been worn by warriors in ancient Egypt and Rome who wished for "**victory and safe return**." In the biblical story of Noah's Ark, a Garnet was said to have illuminated the dark voyage, earning it titles like "**stone of promise**" and "**stone that reunites separated people**."

The primary reason Garnet resonates so well with those born in the Year of the Dog is that it is known as the "**stone of bonds and honesty**." The symbolism of a dog protecting its home and obeying its master beautifully aligns with Garnet's lore of promises and return. This makes it the most natural support for the "**unwavering affection**" that Dog-year individuals possess.

Furthermore, Garnet is also known as the "**stone that brings long-standing efforts to fruition**." Considering this, Garnet is a stone that watches over the steady efforts of Dog-year individuals, helping them reach the fruition of "**welcoming new life or achievements**," much like the Inu no Hi belly band. When you think about it that way, the compatibility between the Year of the Dog and Garnet is said to be particularly rich in narrative among all the zodiac signs.

## Complementary Stones to Carry Together

-   **Rose Quartz**: This cherry-blossom-pink stone of love and harmony is said to gently balance the Dog-year individual's tendency to see things in black and white, bestowing the "**strength to not make enemies**." It is also often used in combination for safe childbirth prayers.
-   **Lapis Lazuli**: Known as the 'King's Stone' or a 'Fragment of Heaven,' it is said to add a "**broad perspective**" to the Dog's honesty, helping them judge matters with a long-term view, not just based on immediate justice.
-   **Onyx**: This black stone, said to sever evil, symbolizes enhanced protection, much like a guard dog. It is considered the most natural stone for situations involving the protection of one's home and family.

## Good Fortune Actions for Those Born in the Year of the Dog

-   **Clothing Colors**: Deep red, brown, beige. Traditionally, color schemes combining Garnet hues with earthy tones are favored, and comfortable attire is said to enhance the Dog's charm.
-   **Wallet**: Beige, brown, or red tones. A wallet that is used for a long time and lovingly repaired as it wears is said to resonate most naturally with the Dog, becoming a "**treasured wallet**."
-   **Places of Worship**: Suiten-gū in Tokyo (the head shrine for safe childbirth prayers), Naritasan Shinshō-ji in Chiba, Fushimi Inari-Taisha in Kyoto, and Mitsumine Shrine, which reveres dogs as divine messengers. Visiting during **Inu no Hi** has been chosen for safe childbirth prayers since ancient times and is believed to strengthen the protection for those born in the Year of the Dog.
-   **Auspicious Day Rhythm**: **Inu no Hi** (Day of the Dog) is considered a gentle auspicious day that occurs every 12 days. It is often chosen for family milestones (like wedding anniversaries or birthdays), greetings, or as the starting day for habits one wishes to continue long-term.
-   **Habit**: A weekly habit of sending a short message to family and friends, asking "**Are you doing well, as always?**" This act is said to keep the Dog-year individual's inherent nature of "being on the protective side" healthy and vibrant.

## Using Stones During Challenging Years and Milestones

For those born in the Year of the Dog, their **Toshio or Toshionna** year (the year of their birth zodiac sign, occurring every 12 years) is sometimes called the "**Year When Trust Takes Shape**." It's considered a milestone where long-standing efforts in work and family life culminate in a significant achievement. Many are said to acquire a new Garnet during this time, imbuing the stone with a sense of gratitude.

During their *Yakudoshi* (challenging or unlucky years, traditionally around ages 42 for men and 33 for women by traditional Japanese counting), it's believed they are prone to exhaustion from taking on too much responsibility and obligation. Traditionally, it's recommended to use Rose Quartz alongside Garnet to supplement the "**courage to say no**" and the "**strength to avoid making enemies**." Consciously practicing relying on family and friends is also suggested for these times.

The zodiac sign directly opposite the Dog is the **Dragon (Tatsu)**. During a Dragon year, Dog-year individuals are said to be susceptible to being swayed by grand schemes or flamboyant personalities. In such years, it's favored to center on Garnet and use Lapis Lazuli to reaffirm one's own convictions.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone in my Toshio/Toshionna year?**
A. The Year of the Dog is considered a 'Year When Trust Takes Shape,' so many people welcome a new Garnet. Traditionally, old stones are purified with gratitude and kept as family amulets.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. It's often preferred to combine your main Garnet with protective stones like Rose Quartz or Onyx. For Dog-year individuals, layering stones with a focus on 'family protection' is said to be particularly suitable.

**Q. Is it bad to carry stones from other zodiac signs?**
A. Not at all! In fact, to broaden their perspective, Dog-year individuals have traditionally been encouraged to combine stones from other zodiac signs, such as Lapis Lazuli for the Dragon or Citrine for the Monkey.

**Q. Can I carry Garnet with the Inu no Hi belly band?**
A. In some regions, there's a tradition of tucking Rose Quartz or Garnet into the belly band for safe childbirth prayers, and examples of this being combined with Inu no Hi shrine visits are reported. It's not just for the pregnant mother; family members sometimes carry it on her behalf.
