The "Dragon" (Tatsu) is the fifth of the twelve Chinese zodiac signs and is the **only mythical sacred creature** among them. Dragons soar through the heavens, command water, and have been revered as symbols of emperors across East Asia. Those born in the Year of the Dragon have long been said to be individuals destined to carry **a grand fate**. The sacred ultramarine stone, **lapis lazuli**, used by ancient kings for crowns and talismans, resonates most deeply with these Dragon-born individuals. Its deep blue, like the sky captured on earth, is said to be the very color of the heavens where dragons reside.

## Personality and Fortune of those Born in the Year of the Dragon

Those born in the Year of the Dragon are said to possess a unique presence among the twelve zodiac signs. Many exhibit an "extraordinary aura" from childhood and often rise to prominence as leaders or expressive individuals.

In terms of career, they are believed to have a strong connection to **large-scale endeavors**, such as venture founders, artists, professions that put them in the spotlight, or fields dealing with spirituality and religion. They possess both the strength to forge their own path and the charisma to lead others. They might often feel stifled by an average way of life.

However, if this "Dragon" energy is mishandled, it can manifest as self-righteousness or an inflated ego. **While strong in solitude, they can also easily withdraw into it** — this has been noted as a shadowed aspect of Dragon-born individuals since ancient times.

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Dragon is associated with the element of "Earth" and governs the hours between 7 AM and 9 AM, when the morning sun gains its peak momentum. It symbolizes the **moment of ascending from earth to heaven**, and it is often said that many Dragon-born individuals achieve significant breakthroughs in the latter half of their lives.

## Why Lapis Lazuli is a Perfect Match

Lapis lazuli has been treasured since the Mesopotamian civilization, approximately 6000 years ago, and was even inlaid into the eyes of Tutankhamun's golden mask, earning it the title of the "King's Stone." In ancient Egypt, it was called a "**Fragment of Heaven**" and is said to have been used by priests in rituals to gain wisdom.

The primary reason why lapis lazuli deeply resonates with those born in the Year of the Dragon is that it is a **"stone that simultaneously governs spirituality and governance."** Just as the Dragon is a spiritual creature that commands water and a symbol of the emperor, lapis lazuli has been used as a stone that connects the two aspects of "priest" and "king." This stone is said to most naturally support the balance of spirituality and leadership that Dragon-born individuals possess.

Furthermore, lapis lazuli is described as a "**stone that illuminates one's life purpose.**" Those born in the Year of the Dragon are often said to grapple with the profound question of "what was I born for?" As a guiding stone that helps attract those answers, it is considered the most suitable guardian stone for the Dragon year, a milestone that occurs only once every twelve years.

For those born in the Year of the Dragon, their **birth year** (every twelve years) is called the "**Year of Breakthrough**," unique among all zodiac signs.
