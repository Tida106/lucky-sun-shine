## Introduction

The "Horse (Uma)" is the seventh of the twelve Chinese zodiac signs, presiding over noon, the exact point where yang energy reaches its peak within the zodiac cycle. Since ancient times, horses have been considered sacred animals ridden by deities. Shrines even keep **sacred horses (shinme)**, and the custom of *Ema* (votive tablets) originated from horse worship. For those born in the Year of the Horse, the stone that resonates most deeply with their true nature is **Carnelian**, whose fiery vermilion color embodies passion itself. Known as "the stone of action and victory" beloved by Napoleon, it is said to continuously ignite the soul of the swift-moving Horse.

## Personality and Fortune of Those Born in the Year of the Horse

Those born in the Year of the Horse are considered remarkably bright, sociable, and quick-moving among the twelve zodiac signs. Many tend to act before thinking things through, and it's often said they pave their way with spontaneous energy.

In terms of work, they are said to excel in fields where "running," "moving," and "meeting people" directly lead to results, such as sales, sports, entertainment, and travel or transportation-related industries. Their agility is said to be unparalleled among the twelve zodiac signs, and they are adept at quickly seizing connections and opportunities.

However, this momentum can sometimes manifest as "fickleness" or "a lack of sustained effort." They might start fast but tend to lose momentum over long distances—a tendency those born in the Year of the Horse are advised to consciously address.

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Horse most strongly embodies the energy of "fire," governing the hours from 11 AM to 1 PM, when the sun races across the zenith. **The Midday Horse**—an existence most directly connected to the yang energy among all animals—and that brilliance is said to be the core of the Horse-born's charm.

## Why Carnelian is a Good Match

Carnelian is a type of chalcedony that shines from orange to red, historically used as an amulet for warriors in ancient Egypt and as a seal stone in Roman times. The single stone Napoleon brought back from his Egyptian campaign and always carried into battle is still known today as the "**Carnelian of Victory**."

The primary reason Carnelian resonates with those born in the Year of the Horse is that it is known as the "**stone that transforms action into results**." While the Horse zodiac sign has the power to start running, it can sometimes tend to just run without achieving anything concrete. Carnelian is said to bestow "**the passion to run through to the end**" and supplement the endurance needed to reach the goal without giving up.

Additionally, the symbols of horse and fire have been deeply intertwined since ancient times. The Horse, which governs the energy of fire, and the vermilion Carnelian, which seems to crystallize fire—both resonate as "Fire and Fire" in terms of Yin and Yang and the Five Elements, amplifying the inherent energy of those born in the Year of the Horse.

*   **Clothing Colors**: Vermilion, Red, Orange, Turquoise Blue. The contrasting colors of "fire and sky" are said to attract luck for the Horse-born, and it is traditionally favored to incorporate a touch of vermilion in a shirt or scarf.
*   **Wallet**: Red or Yellow hues. The use of a "**circulating wallet**" that doesn't keep bills for long is compatible with the Year of the Horse, and a shorter replacement cycle (1-2 years) is also preferred.

*   **Habit**: Light exercise every morning (walking or stretching is fine). "**Moving the body to circulate energy**" is an essential practice for the Horse zodiac, and a lack of exercise is said to directly lead to stagnation of luck.

## Years of Calamity, Milestones, and Stone Usage

For those born in the Year of the Horse, their **zodiac year** (Toshi Otoko/Toshi Onna), which occurs once every 12 years, is called the "**Year of Sprinting**." It is suitable for new challenges, job changes, moves, and other actions involving significant movement. Many people acquire new Carnelian and carry Turquoise alongside it as a travel amulet.

During the main unlucky year (yakudoshi), such as 42 for men and 33 for women by traditional Japanese counting, Horse-born individuals tend to rush ahead with too much momentum, potentially leading to injuries, accidents, or interpersonal conflicts. It is traditional to use Tiger Eye in conjunction to supplement "**the courage to pause**," and a lifestyle that consciously creates days of rest is recommended.

The zodiac opposite to the Horse is the **Year of the Rat**, which can be a year prone to being swayed by nocturnal rhythms and intricate calculations. In this year, with Carnelian as the base, it's recommended to balance the flow of wealth with Citrine and avoid excessive late nights.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone in my zodiac year?**
A. The Horse year is considered a year that provides a tailwind for new beginnings, and a new Carnelian is said to become your companion for the next year. It's also common to cleanse old stones with gratitude and use them as a secondary stone for travel.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. The Horse zodiac sign has good compatibility with using different stones for various situations like movement, competition, and relationships, so it's preferred to have "one main stone plus two to three types for specific scenes."

**Q. Is it bad to carry stones of other zodiac signs?**
A. It is not considered a problem. In fact, complementary ways of carrying stones have been traditionally recommended since ancient times, such as supplementing the Horse's often-lacking "endurance" with the Ox's Agate, or "depth of insight" with the Serpent's Amethyst.

**Q. I heard Carnelian is too strong and can make one restless. Is that true?**
A. It is said to be a stone with strong fire energy, and some people may feel overly stimulated by it. If you feel uncomfortable, it's recommended to remove it temporarily and let it rest through moonlight bathing or running water.
