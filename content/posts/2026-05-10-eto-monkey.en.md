## Introduction

The zodiac sign "Monkey" (申, Saru) is the ninth of the twelve Chinese zodiac animals. Derived from the image of the clever monkey, excelling in intelligence, resourcefulness, and the ability to imitate and learn, it has been a symbol of **dexterity, sociability, optimism, and talent**. Shrines where monkeys are revered as messengers of the gods, such as **Sannō Gongen** at Mount Hiei and Hiyoshi Taisha Shrine, have traditionally been seen as symbols of warding off evil and misfortune due to the pun "Ma ga saru" (evil departs, or "monkey" - saru). The stone that resonates most with the true nature of those born in the Year of the Monkey is **Citrine**, which emits a golden glow as if crystallized from sunlight. While Citrine is also considered the guardian stone for those born in the Year of the Rat (子), it is said that for the Year of the Monkey, it carries the unique nuance of "**luck that transforms talent into business opportunities**."

## Personality and Fortune of Those Born in the Year of the Monkey

Those born in the Year of the Monkey are considered one of the quickest-witted and most broadly interested zodiac signs. Their speed in learning new things, their ability to imitate others and make skills their own, and their sociability in reading a room and livening up the atmosphere are all said to be among the best of the twelve zodiac signs.

In terms of career, they are said to excel in **fields that require both intellect and social skills**, such as planning, marketing, engineering, education, journalism, and entertainment. They also have high aptitude for multitasking, and many are said to be adept at running multiple projects simultaneously.

However, their dexterity can also manifest as a tendency towards "**fickleness**" or "spreading themselves **wide and shallow**." Moving on to the next thing before deeply exploring one area, and consequently, struggling to develop true expertise — this is often cited as a habit that those born in the Year of the Monkey should consciously manage.

Among the twelve zodiac animals, the Monkey is associated with the element of "Metal" (金, Kin), and governs the hours between 3 PM and 5 PM, when the day's activities move towards completion and fruition. It symbolizes the time for **gathering results towards evening**, and its essence of luck lies in resourcefulness and efficiency – sowing seeds in the morning and reaping in the afternoon.

## Why Citrine is a Good Match

Citrine is known as "Yellow Quartz" and was used as an amulet for merchants in ancient Rome. In medieval Europe, it was placed near cash registers and ledgers as the "Merchant's Stone." In Japan, too, it is popular as a quintessential stone for financial luck and is favored as a gift, being a stone that holds the light of the sun.

> **Note**: The "effects" of powerstones are cultural and symbolic. They do not guarantee medical or scientific efficacy.

The primary reason Citrine resonates with those born in the Year of the Monkey is that Citrine is the stone that **"transforms talent into business opportunities."** While the Monkey sign possesses abundant talent and knowledge, individuals are often said to stumble at the stage of "monetizing" or "materializing" their talents. Citrine is believed to act as a golden bridge, translating wisdom into tangible results, thereby supporting the biggest challenge for those born in the Year of the Monkey.

Furthermore, because the Monkey sign is associated with the element of "Metal" (金, Kin), it naturally resonates with Citrine's golden color in terms of Yin-Yang and the Five Elements philosophy. While the Year of the Rat, which also has Citrine as its guardian stone, is associated with "accumulating" wealth, the Year of the Monkey is linked to wealth that is "**activated and grown**." It is said that the same stone showing different facets depending on the zodiac sign reveals the profound depth of powerstone culture.

## Compatible Stones to Carry Alongside

-   **Aquamarine**: The birthstone for March, embodying the blue of the sea. It is said to enhance the clarity of communication for sociable individuals born in the Year of the Monkey, helping them engage in dialogues that go beyond mere chat to reveal true feelings and essence.
-   **Fluorite**: A gradient stone ranging from purple to green, known as the "**Stone of Genius**." It is believed to support concentration and logical thinking, helping to reorient the often scattered nature of the Monkey sign towards "**deep exploration**."
-   **Tiger's Eye**: A golden stone for decision-making and warding off evil. It is said to grant the insight to discern "which option to choose *now*" from among a wide array of possibilities.

## Auspicious Actions for Those Born in the Year of the Monkey

-   **Clothing Colors**: Yellow, white, and gray. Traditionally, a color scheme is favored where white hues, symbolizing intellect, form the base, with citrine-yellow used as an accent color.
-   **Wallet**: Yellow or gold tones. For the Monkey sign, using money actively, or "**circulating**" it, is more compatible than letting bills sit idle. The habit of keeping the wallet slim by organizing point cards and such is also favored.
-   **Pilgrimage Sites**: Shrines where monkeys are revered as divine messengers, such as Hiyoshi Taisha in Shiga, Hie Jinja in Tokyo, and Zenkōji in Nagano, as well as the main shrine of Sannō Sōhongū Hiyoshi Taisha in Kyoto. **Monkey Days** (申の日), especially **Kōshin Days** (庚申の日), have traditionally been considered the most powerful days for warding off evil.
-   **Auspicious Day Rhythm**: "**Kōshin Day**" occurs only once every 60 days, and the custom of "Kōshin-machi" (Kōshin Vigil), staying up all night, was widespread until the Edo period. For those born in the Year of the Monkey, these are said to be six powerful days each year for their own protection against misfortune.
-   **Habit**: A 5-minute habit before bed: jotting down "**what you learned today**" in a single line. This practice is believed to transform the wide-ranging experiences of the Monkey sign into "intellectual assets."

## Unlucky Years, Milestone Years, and How to Use Stones

The year of being in one's **Eto (zodiac animal year)**, sometimes called "nen-otoko" or "nen-onna", is referred to as "a **year for talents to bloom**." It is said to be suitable for actions that transform intellect into profit, such as starting a new business, changing jobs, or turning a side hustle into a main profession, and many people acquire new Citrine during this time.

In a **year of major misfortune** (Hon-yaku, e.g., 42 for men and 33 for women by traditional Japanese counting), dexterity is said to sometimes backfire, leading to a tendency to "**overextend and lose momentum**." It is traditionally advised to use Fluorite in conjunction with other stones to supplement the awareness of "**narrowing down**" and "**deepening**" one's focus, and some choose to spend this year by declining new projects.

The zodiac opposite to the Year of the Monkey is the **Year of the Tiger (寅, Tora)**, which is said to be a year when one can easily be swayed by brave and straightforward energy. During such a year, it is favored to use Citrine as a base, supplemented by Tiger's Eye to support direct and decisive actions.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone in my zodiac year (Eto year)?**
A. The Year of the Monkey is considered a milestone for talents to bloom, and many people welcome new Citrine. It's common practice to cleanse older stones with gratitude and use them as secondary pieces on a desk or in a wallet.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. Since the Monkey sign often encounters many learning and social situations, it is favored to have one main stone and two to three compatible stones, using them selectively depending on the occasion.

**Q. Isn't it contradictory for both the Year of the Rat and the Year of the Monkey to have Citrine as their guardian stone?**
A. It is believed that even the same stone can have different symbolic directions depending on the zodiac sign. The Year of the Rat is associated with "accumulating wealth," while the Year of the Monkey is linked to "activating wealth." Sharing the same guardian stone is said to not be uncommon among the twelve zodiac animals.

**Q. Citrine comes in various shades, which one is best for the Year of the Monkey?**
A. For those born in the Year of the Monkey, vibrant shades ranging from "**bright lemon yellow to orangey hues**" are generally recommended as they reflect an active disposition. Deeper Madeira Citrine is sometimes said to be more suitable for the Year of the Rat, but it is believed that you can choose based on your personal preference.
