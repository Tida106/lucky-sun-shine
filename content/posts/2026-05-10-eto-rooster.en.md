## Introduction

The "Rooster" (Tori) is the tenth sign of the Chinese Zodiac, and its depiction as a rooster announcing the dawn has long symbolized **meticulousness, foresight, and honesty**. Furthermore, due to the pun "torikomu" (which means "to take in" or "to capture"), the Rooster is also considered a zodiac sign for business prosperity. Every November, during the **Tori-no-Ichi (Rooster Market)**, the custom of "raking in good fortune" with a kumade (rake) is still cherished. The stone that resonates most deeply with Rooster-born individuals is **Sodalite**, with its deep indigo-blue hue and striking white matrix. Known as "**the stone that bridges logic and intuition**," it quietly supports the true nature of Rooster-born individuals, who possess both meticulousness and foresight.

## Personality and Fortune of Rooster-Born Individuals

Rooster-born individuals are considered the most skilled among the twelve zodiac signs in the "**power to organize and arrange**." Their ability to plan tasks, their integrity in meeting deadlines, and their careful attention to detail are said to be unparalleled.

In their careers, they are said to excel in fields requiring **accuracy and responsibility**, such as accounting, finance, administration, editing, quality control, public administration, and specialized professions. Also, true to the symbol of the "rooster announcing the dawn," they often possess **foresight to read one step ahead of others**, detecting trends and market changes early on.

However, their meticulousness can sometimes manifest as a tendency towards "**perfectionism**" or "**being too strict with others**." Demanding 100% from themselves and others can lead to friction and exhaustion—a habit that Rooster-born individuals are told they should consciously let go of.

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Rooster is associated with the element of "Metal" and governs the hours of 5 PM to 7 PM, the time that concludes the day. This symbolizes the time to **organize the harvest and prepare for tomorrow**, aligning with the Rooster's nature of both reflection and foresight.

## Why Sodalite Is a Good Match

Sodalite is a relatively new stone, discovered in Greenland in the early 19th century. Yet, its beautiful indigo-blue color quickly earned it recognition as "**the stone that bridges thought and intuition**." It closely resembles Lapis Lazuli, but can be distinguished by the white matrix running through it instead of golden flecks.

The primary reason Sodalite resonates with Rooster-born individuals is that it is "**a stone that keeps logic calm and clear, while also preventing intuition from being clouded**." Rooster individuals need both logic and intuition, but if they lean too much towards meticulousness, their intuition can dull, and if they rely too much on intuition, their plans can fall apart. Sodalite, as a bridging stone that balances these two extremes, is said to most naturally support the true nature of the Rooster.

Additionally, Sodalite is also known as "**the stone that discerns truth**," and its values deeply resonate with the Year of the Rooster, which symbolizes honesty. The belief that continuing honest work eventually "takes in good fortune" for the Rooster, and Sodalite's sincere indigo blue are said to harmonize beautifully.

## Compatible Stones to Carry Alongside

-   **Lapis Lazuli**: The king's stone, known as a "fragment of heaven." While sharing Sodalite's indigo hue, it is said to govern formality and life's direction, encouraging Rooster-born individuals in milestone decisions.
-   **Tiger's Eye**: The golden stone of decision-making and warding off evil. It plays a role in translating foresight-based predictions into "decisions to act now," connecting the Rooster's meticulousness to action.
-   **Citrine**: The golden stone of business prosperity. Like the kumade rake at Tori-no-Ichi, it symbolizes "**taking in good fortune**" and is said to be a companion stone that translates the Rooster's organizational power into financial luck.

## Lucky Actions for Rooster-Born Individuals

-   **Clothing Colors**: Indigo, navy, white. A color scheme based on Sodalite's hues, accented with white for cleanliness, is traditionally favored and considered compatible with the Rooster sign in business settings.
-   **Wallet**: Navy, black, or white. Using a long wallet to neatly organize bills aligns most naturally with the Rooster's nature, and the habit of not accumulating receipts is also said to support financial luck.
-   **Places of Worship**: Washi Shrine in Tokyo (origin of Tori-no-Ichi), Ohtori Grand Shrine in Osaka, Yasaka Shrine in Kyoto, and others. On "**Tori no Hi**" (Rooster Day) during "Tori no Tsuki" (November), Tori-no-Ichi markets are held, considered the strongest auspicious days (1 to 3 times a year) for those born in the Year of the Rooster.
-   **Auspicious Day Rhythm**: "**Tori no Hi**" (Rooster Day) is considered suitable for business prosperity, connecting with new clients, and organizing. Many people choose this day to review the contents of their desks and wallets.
-   **Habit**: The practice of noting down just three items for "**tomorrow's to-do list**" at the end of the day. It's said to refine foresight and prevent excessive meticulousness, making it the most suitable evening routine for the Rooster sign.

## Unlucky Years, Milestone Years, and Stone Usage

The year of your zodiac animal (every 12 years), often referred to as "**Toshi-otoko**" or "**Toshi-onna**" (zodiac year for men and women respectively), is sometimes called the "**Year of Taking In**." It's considered a milestone year for actions that "take in good fortune," such as new clients, new businesses, or new titles, and many people purchase a new Sodalite stone.

During the year of "**Hon-Yaku**" (major unlucky year, e.g., age 42 for men, 33 for women by traditional Japanese counting), meticulousness can easily spiral into perfectionism. It's traditionally recommended to use Tiger's Eye concurrently to supplement "**the courage to release at 80% completion**," and a tolerant approach towards oneself and others is advised.

The zodiac opposite of the Rooster is the **Rabbit (Usagi)**, and this year is said to be one where you might easily be swayed by its gentle flexibility. During this year, it's favored to use Sodalite as a foundation and Lapis Lazuli to reaffirm your own core.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone during my zodiac year?**
A. The Rooster year is considered the "Year of Taking In," and many people welcome a new Sodalite. It's common practice to cleanse old stones with gratitude and keep them as secondary amulets near your desk or ledger.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. It's favored to combine the main Sodalite with prosperity-oriented stones like Lapis Lazuli or Citrine. The Rooster sign is said to be suited for arranging stones neatly, as an extension of their organizational skills.

**Q. Is it bad to carry stones of other zodiac signs?**
A. It's said to be fine. In situations where perfectionism strongly emerges, carrying stones that complement traits you lack, such as Rabbit's Rose Quartz for gentleness, has been traditionally recommended.

**Q. Can I use a kumade bought at Tori-no-Ichi with my stone?**
A. The kumade is a symbol of "taking in good fortune," and Sodalite is a symbol of "discerning the right fortune." Their combination is traditionally considered highly compatible. The custom of placing the kumade at the entrance and the stone in a wallet or on a desk has been passed down for a long time.
