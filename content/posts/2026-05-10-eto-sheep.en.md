## Introduction

The "Sheep" (Hitsuji) is the eighth sign in the Chinese zodiac, and its image of sheep living in a flock has long symbolized **harmony, receptivity, artistry, and family bonds**. The character '未' (mì) itself represents the "stage before fruit ripens," suggesting a softness that is not yet fully formed, encompassing the potential for future maturity. The protective stone that resonates most deeply with those born in the Year of the Sheep is **Rose Quartz**, with its pale, cherry-blossom pink hue symbolizing love and harmony. While it's a prominent stone for love and relationships, often associated with the Year of the Rabbit, the Year of the Sheep uniquely emphasizes "**love for the flock and family**" rather than just "individual love."

## Personality and Fortune of Those Born in the Year of the Sheep

Those born in the Year of the Sheep are considered the most gentle and accommodating among the twelve zodiac signs. They are said to possess a nature that avoids conflict and prioritizes harmony with those around them. Being excellent listeners who attentively hear out everyone, they often play a "**social lubricant**" role in their family, workplace, or community.

In their careers, they are said to excel in fields where **beauty and gentleness directly contribute to results**, such as design, music, culinary arts, fashion, welfare, and childcare. Much like wool being spun into fabric, many born in the Year of the Sheep cherish the steady accumulation of diligent handwork.

However, a potential challenge is their tendency to bend their own opinions too much in valuing harmony, or to become exhausted by taking on too much without being able to say no. While appearing similar to those born in the Year of the Rabbit, the Sheep sign's unique challenge is said to be their greater susceptibility to bearing the **weight of their "flock" and "family."**

Among the twelve zodiac signs, the Sheep is associated with the element of "Earth" and governs the hours between 1 PM and 3 PM, when the sun begins to set. The essence of those born in the Year of the Sheep is said to be the gentle calmness of **"ripening time"** — like the afternoon light that sweetens fruit.

## Why Rose Quartz is a Perfect Match

From ancient times, Rose Quartz has been known as "the stone of Aphrodite, the goddess of love and beauty." It has been cherished as a prominent stone for love and relationships, presiding over family harmony, marital bliss, and parent-child bonds. In Japan, it is often chosen for wedding and birth celebrations, symbolizing a warm embrace of human connections.

The primary reason Rose Quartz deeply resonates with those born in the Year of the Sheep is its role as **the stone that governs "flock love."** It is said to possess a frequency that softly embraces not only one-on-one romantic relationships but also family, relatives, and the entire community, aligning most naturally with the inherent nature of sheep living in a flock.

Furthermore, those born in the Year of the Sheep are also considered a zodiac sign prone to "self-sacrifice." Rose Quartz has historically been spoken of as **a stone to reclaim "self-love,"** and it is said to act as a mirror, supporting them in maintaining a balance where they cherish their flock without exhausting themselves.

## Complementary Stones to Carry

- **Aquamarine**: The March birthstone, carrying the blue of the sea. It is said to keep conversations with family and friends pure and to help articulate difficult truths, thus being spoken of as a stone that helps resolve the "unspoken words" of those born in the Year of the Sheep.
- **Kunzite**: A pale pink stone of "unconditional love." Symbolizing maternal and devoted love, it is said to guide the hearts of those born in the Year of the Sheep, who support their family and surroundings, toward fulfillment rather than depletion.
- **Morganite**: A rare, cherry-blossom pink beryl. Spoken of as a stone that attracts a **"life partner,"** it is considered the most delicate and beautiful love lesson stone for those born in the Year of the Sheep who cherish their family.

## Good Fortune Actions for the Year of the Sheep

- **Clothing Colors**: Pastel pink, ivory, and lavender. Soft and bright color schemes are said to draw out the harmonious energy of the Sheep. The texture of materials is also important, favoring linen, cotton, and cashmere over stiff fabrics like denim.
- **Wallet**: Pink or light beige. A long wallet, used to carefully arrange bills, is considered suitable for the Sheep. Many are also said to keep family amulets tucked inside.
- **Shrine Visits**: Shrines symbolizing flocks and families, such as Kasuga Taisha in Nara and Matsuo Taisha in Kyoto. Visiting as a family is said to be more compatible with the Year of the Sheep than individual visits.
- **Auspicious Day Rhythm**: The "**Day of the Sheep**" is considered suitable for meticulous handiwork, household chores, and family events. The practice of cherishing family milestones, such as celebrating small anniversaries for weddings or children's birthdays, is favored.
- **Habit**: The daily practice of verbally expressing "thank you" to family and loved ones. This action is said to amplify the harmonious energy of those born in the Year of the Sheep the most.

## Auspicious and Milestone Years and Stone Usage

For those born in the Year of the Sheep, their own zodiac year (the "Year of the Sheep" itself, occurring once every 12 years) is considered a turning point where their position within their family and flock changes. "**Family phase transitions**" such as marriage, childbirth, relocation, or parental care are likely to occur, and many are said to acquire a new Rose Quartz to wish for a renewal of family fortune.

During the years of misfortune (Yakudoshi, such as 42 for men and 33 for women by traditional Japanese reckoning), it is a period when one is prone to physical and mental exhaustion from taking on too many requests from others. The traditional way to navigate this is said to be by using Kunzite in conjunction to supplement "**the courage to say no**" and "**self-love**."

The zodiac sign opposite to the Sheep is the **Ox (Ushi)**, and this year is said to be one where those born in the Year of the Sheep may be easily swayed by responsibilities and persistence. During an Ox year, it is advised to use Aquamarine in addition to Rose Quartz and to spend more time engaging in dialogue with family.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I buy a new stone in my zodiac year (Year of the Sheep)?**
A. The Year of the Sheep is considered a significant turning point where family phase transitions are likely to occur, and many people choose to welcome a new Rose Quartz. Traditionally, older stones are cleansed with gratitude and sometimes passed on to family members.

**Q. Should I only carry one zodiac stone?**
A. A combination of the main Rose Quartz layered with other pink-hued love stones like Kunzite or Morganite is favored. For the Year of the Sheep, layering stones is said to create a gentle and harmonious balance.

**Q. Is it bad to carry stones from other zodiac signs?**
A. It is not considered an issue. In situations where you want to assert yourself while maintaining a harmonious spirit, it has traditionally been recommended to carry stones that complement qualities you might lack, such as Tiger's Eye (Tiger's stone) to enhance decisiveness.

**Q. Both the Rabbit and Sheep have Rose Quartz as their guardian stone. Is there a difference?**
A. The Rabbit symbolizes "individual gentleness," while the Sheep symbolizes "gentleness for the flock and family," so their symbolic directions differ. Even with the same stone, for the Year of the Sheep, uses that embrace the entire household are preferred, such as placing a single stone in a family amulet box.
