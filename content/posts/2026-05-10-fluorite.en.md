## Introduction

Fluorite's English name, *Fluorite*, originates from the Latin word "**fluere (to flow)**." Its history as a flux in iron smelting due to its low melting point, used since ancient times, is the source of its name. In fact, the chemical element "**Fluorine**" itself was named after this very stone!

The property of **fluorescing when exposed to ultraviolet light** (fluorescence) was also named by scholars observing fluorite. Known as a "mysterious glowing stone," it was crafted into cups in ancient Rome and is said to have been used as a research tool by alchemists in medieval Europe. Today, it's called the "**Stone of Genius**" and the "**Stone of Concentration**," making it one of the most popular stones among researchers, students, and creators.

## Stone Words and Meaning

Fluorite's stone words are "**Concentration, Academics, and Mental Clarity**." As an aid to concentration, known as the "Stone of Genius," it has been cherished worldwide for a long time.

In ancient Rome, purple cups called "**Murrhine**," brought from Persia, are believed to have been made of fluorite, which Emperor Nero reportedly spent vast sums to collect. Due to its mysterious fluorescent properties, medieval alchemists considered it a "**mineral that enhances intellect**" and displayed it in their laboratories.

The phenomenon of "**Fluorescence**," which gave origin to the term itself, is a rare example where this stone's name is enshrined in academic terminology. As a "**symbol of academia and intellect**," it remains a top choice in today's spiritual market as a charm for qualification exams, academic tests, and research.

## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Natural fluorite naturally has small cracks and color variations inside.
-   While non-fluorescent fluorite exists, extremely uniform, highlighter-like colors may indicate dyed products or glass.
-   Due to its low Mohs hardness of 4, fine scratches on the surface can be one of the "signs of authenticity."
-   Purchasing with a certificate of authenticity from a trusted specialty store is recommended. Please also refer to our [Guide to Spotting Fake and Artificial Stones](/blog/spot-fake-stones/).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Does Fluorite fade?**
A. Yes. Colors like purple and green can fade with strong UV exposure. Avoid placing it in direct sunlight for extended periods.

A. **8-10mm** is standard. However, due to its low hardness, those who do desk work where their wrist might rub frequently might find smaller beads last longer.

**Q. How is Rainbow Fluorite different?**
A. A type of fluorite with multiple layers of colors such as purple, green, and colorless within a single stone is called "Rainbow Fluorite." It's highly popular as a symbol of balance and integration, and its price is usually a bit higher.

**Q. I want to give it as a gift to a student preparing for exams. What color is best?**
