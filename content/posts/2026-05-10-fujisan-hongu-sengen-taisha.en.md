## Introduction

Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine is the **head shrine of approximately 1,300 Sengen Shrines** nationwide, revering the sacred **Mount Fuji as its primary deity**. It forms a grand sphere of worship with two main shrine buildings: the main hall located in Fujinomiya City and the **Okumiya (Inner Shrine) at the summit of Mount Fuji (3,776m above sea level)**. In 2013, it was registered as a component of the World Cultural Heritage site "Mount Fuji – Sacred Place and Source of Artistic Inspiration." This article compiles **its history, the legend of Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto, Wakutama Pond, pilgrimage to the Okumiya, FAQs, and an editor's note** all on one page for you!

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

From ancient times, Mount Fuji has been revered and feared as an **"erupting active volcano."** It's said that the origin of Sengen worship dates back to the 11th Emperor Suinin's reign, when rituals were performed to appease Mount Fuji's eruptions by enshrining **Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto**. During the reign of the 12th Emperor Keiko, Prince Yamato Takeru ascended the mountain, and in the Heian period, **Sakanoue no Tamuramaro built the shrine buildings at the current location**. In 1604, Tokugawa Ieyasu rebuilt the main hall in the unique two-story **"Sengen-zukuri" architectural style**, which remains today as a National Important Cultural Property.

**All areas of Mount Fuji above the 8th station are considered part of the Sengen Taisha Shrine grounds**, a remarkably rare ownership status officially recognized by a shogunate decree in 1779.

> Source: Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine Official Documents, *Kojiki*, *Nihon Shoki*, and various historical materials.

## <span id="sakuya"></span>The Legend of Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto

The primary deity, **Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto**, is a mountain goddess renowned for her beauty, likened to that of a cherry blossom. The legend goes that after conceiving a child with Ninigi no Mikoto (the deity who descended from Heaven), her chastity was questioned. To prove her purity, she declared, **"If this child is truly divine, it will be born safely in fire,"** and then gave birth to three divine children amidst flames. From this legend, she is revered as the **goddess of safe childbirth, fertility, and protection from fire**.

It is also said that the Japanese word for cherry blossom, *sakura*, derives from "Sakuya," making her a foundational figure in the enduring connection between cherry blossoms, Mount Fuji, and their status as symbols of Japan.

## <span id="okumiya"></span>About the Mount Fuji Summit Okumiya (Inner Shrine)

At the summit of Mount Fuji, you'll find **Kusushi Shrine (affiliated with Kitaguchi Hongu Sengen Jinja Shrine) and the Sengen Taisha Okumiya**.

- **Opening Period**: Only during the climbing season, from around July 10th to September 10th.
- **Access**: Via Mount Fuji climbing trails (Yoshida, Subashiri, Gotemba, and Fujinomiya routes).
- **Okumiya Goshuin** (shrine seal stamp) is available only to those who have made the pilgrimage to the summit.
- **The Mount Fuji summit is considered part of Sengen Taisha Shrine's grounds** (from the 8th station upwards).

## <span id="wakutama"></span>Misogi (Purification Ritual) at Wakutama Pond

**Wakutama Pond** is a sacred spring where Mount Fuji's snowmelt water gushes forth after many years. Designated as a **Special Natural Monument**, this divine area sees a remarkable **2.4 cubic meters of water per second** flowing out! Traditionally, pilgrims would perform **misogi (a purification ritual) here before ascending Mount Fuji**, and even today, many visitors come to collect its sacred water to take home.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Approximately a 10-minute walk from **Fujinomiya Station** on the JR Minobu Line. If you're coming by car, it's about a 15-minute drive from **Shin-Fuji IC** on the Shin-Tomei Expressway, or about 20 minutes from **Fuji IC** on the Tomei Expressway.

**Parking**: Available (150 spaces, first 30 minutes free).

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Etiquette

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate or main gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities, so please walk along the sides.
3.  At the *temizuya* (water ablution pavilion), purify your left hand, then your right hand, rinse your mouth, cleanse your left hand again, and then rinse the dipper.
4.  Perform **two bows, two claps, and one bow** (*nihai nihakushu ippai*).
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, and focus on expressing gratitude.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. When is the best time for prayers for safe childbirth?**

A. Traditionally, the **Day of the Dog in the fifth month of pregnancy** is recommended. Pregnant women from all over Japan visit to pray for safe childbirth, seeking blessings from Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto. No advance reservation is needed; you can register at the shrine office at any time.

**Q2. Who can receive the Goshuin (shrine seal stamp) at the Okumiya on Mount Fuji's summit?**

A. **Only pilgrims who have ascended to the summit**. The Goshuin is offered at the Okumiya on Mount Fuji's summit exclusively during the climbing season, typically from July 10th to September 10th. After descending, you can also receive a "Summit Pilgrimage Commemoration" Goshuin at the main shrine.

**Q3. Is the water from Wakutama Pond drinkable?**

A. The water from Wakutama Pond is considered **"sacred water" and can be taken home**. There's a designated spot within the shrine grounds for collecting it. However, as it's raw water, it's recommended to boil it at home before drinking.

**Q4. When is the best time to see the cherry blossoms?**

A. **Late March to early April**. The combination of the Sakura no Baba (Cherry Blossom Horse Riding Grounds) in front of the main hall and Mount Fuji is a classic photo composition. There are also years when **evening cherry blossom illuminations** are held.

**Q5. There are Sengen Shrines all over Japan, but is this the head shrine?**

A. Yes, that's right! **Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha Shrine is indeed the head shrine of approximately 1,300 Sengen Shrines nationwide**. Famous shrines like Kawaguchi Asama Shrine and Kitaguchi Hongu Fuji Sengen Jinja Shrine (Yamanashi Prefecture) are also part of this main lineage.
