## Introduction

Hieizan Enryakuji Temple is the **head temple of the Tendai sect**, encompassing the entire **Mount Hiei** (848m above sea level) that straddles the border between Kyoto and Shiga Prefectures. It was founded in **788 (Enryaku 7)** by Dengyo Daishi **Saicho**. Many famous monks who later established their own independent sects, such as **Honen, Shinran, Eisai, Dogen, Nichiren, and Ippen**, trained here in their youth. Because of this, it is known as "the **Mother Mountain of Japanese Buddhism**." In 1994, it was registered as a World Cultural Heritage site as part of "**Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto**," making it one of Japan's most crucial sacred Buddhist sites. This article covers its **history, the Three Pagodas tour, the Eternal Flame, FAQs, and an editorial note**, all in one page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

The history of Enryakuji Temple began when **Saicho** entered Mount Hiei at the age of 19 and built a small hermitage called "**Ichijoshikan-in**" (later Konpon Chudo) in **788**. Saicho traveled to Tang China as a Japanese envoy in 804, where he studied Tendai doctrine, Esoteric Buddhism, Zen, and Vinaya (monastic rules) before returning to Japan and founding the **Tendai sect** in 806.

From the Heian period onwards, Enryakuji Temple gained immense influence as the "**Mother Mountain of Japanese Buddhism**," nurturing many famous monks in their youth who would later establish their own sects, such as **Honen (Jodo-shu), Shinran (Jodo Shinshu), Eisai (Rinzai Zen), Dogen (Soto Zen), Nichiren (Nichiren-shu), and Ippen (Ji-shu)**.

In **1571, the entire mountain was burned to ashes during Oda Nobunaga's attack**, but it was later restored with aid from Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu. Even after the separation of Shinto and Buddhism and religious control during the Meiji era, it continued as the head temple of Tendai Buddhism and was registered as a World Cultural Heritage site in 1994. The "**Eternal Flame**" has continued to burn without interruption for over 1200 years.

## <span id="threetowers"></span>About the Three Pagodas (Todo, Saito, and Yokawa)

Enryakuji Temple features a "**Three Pagodas and Sixteen Valleys**" structure, where its three main areas – **Todo, Saito, and Yokawa** – are spread out across the vast Mount Hiei.

| Area | Central Building | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Todo** | Konpon Chudo | The birthplace of Enryakuji Temple and the central area most visited by tourists |
| **Saito** | Shakado | A quiet training ground associated with Saicho, the Ni-nai-do Hall is a highlight |
| **Yokawa** | Yokawa Chudo | The innermost training ground, associated with Genshin, Shinran, and Nichiren |

The three areas are quite separate, so you can explore them on foot in half a day to a full day, or in a single day by using a car or shuttle bus.

## <span id="houtou"></span>Konpon Chudo and the Eternal Flame

The **Konpon Chudo** in Todo is the main hall of Enryakuji Temple and is designated as a **National Treasure**. In front of the Yakushi Nyorai (Bhaisajyaguru) statue enshrined in the center, an "**Eternal Flame**" has been burning continuously for over **1200 years**. It is believed that this flame has been protected and maintained since it was first lit by Saicho himself.

The Japanese word "**yudan**" (油断, meaning carelessness or negligence) is said to have originated from a warning against "cutting off the oil supply" (油を断つ) to this eternal flame, serving as a powerful admonition for practitioners even today. **As of 2026, Konpon Chudo is undergoing major renovations, which happen only once every 60 years or so**, but worship and visitation are still possible.

## <span id="kaihogyo"></span>Sennichi Kaihogyo (The Thousand-Day Circumambulation)

Among Enryakuji Temple's ascetic practices, the most famous is the "**Sennichi Kaihogyo**" or "Thousand-Day Circumambulation." This severe training involves traversing over 260 sacred sites on Mount Hiei for a total of **1000 days** over seven years. Especially in the final year, the "**Do-iri**" practice of **nine days of fasting, abstinence from water, sleeplessness, and prostration** is considered to push human limits.

Those who achieve this are called "**Dai-gyoman Dai-Ajari**," and only 13 individuals have done so in the post-war period. This practice symbolizes the profound spirituality that still underpins Enryakuji Temple.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Visiting Points

- Experience the full scope of Enryakuji Temple by visiting the **Three Pagodas: Todo, Saito, and Yokawa**.
- Pray quietly before the **Eternal Flame** in Konpon Chudo.
- Utilize the **shuttle bus and driveways** for efficient sightseeing.
- Note that during winter (December to March), there may be snow and closed sections, so check in advance.
- Don't miss the **Sakamoto Satobo group** (monks' quarters) at the foot of the mountain.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train and Cable Car:
- From Kyoto side: Take the Eizan Electric Railway from Keihan "**Demachiyanagi Station**" to the cable car and ropeway up to the mountain.
- From Shiga side: Take the Sakamoto Cable from JR Kosei Line "**Hieizan Sakamoto Station**" or Keihan "**Sakamoto Hieizanguchi Station**" to Enryakuji Station.

Bus: From JR Kyoto Station, take the **Hieizan Drive Bus** for about 60 to 90 minutes.

Car: Via Hieizan Driveway (toll road) from Kyoto-Higashi IC or Otsu IC.

**Parking**: Paid parking is available in each area of the driveway.

## <span id="season"></span>Best Time and Season to Visit

**Spring for fresh greenery (May) and autumn for vibrant foliage (late October to mid-November)** are particularly beautiful. Due to its high altitude, it's cool even in summer, making it a good escape from the intense heat of Kyoto city. During **winter (December to March)**, some sections may be closed or have shorter visiting hours, so check the official website before your visit.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Temple Etiquette

1.  Bow before passing through the main gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the Buddhas; walk along the sides.
3.  Inside the halls, remove your hat and refrain from loud conversations.
4.  When visiting, **gassho (pressing palms together) and a bow** are standard (do not clap your hands like at a Shinto shrine).
5.  Photography is often prohibited inside the halls; please follow the signs.
6.  Remember that it is a mountain of ascetic practice and maintain a quiet and respectful demeanor.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can I visit all Three Pagodas in one day?**

A. Yes, it's possible in one day if you use the shuttle bus or your own car. If you plan to walk everywhere, it will take **half a day to a full day**. The areas are separated by mountain paths, so plan with plenty of time.

**Q2. How much is the admission fee?**

A. The "**Three Pagodas Tour Ticket**" is 1,000 yen for adults (as of 2026). This allows admission to all major halls in Todo, Saito, and Yokawa. Driveway tolls are separate.

**Q3. Can I still visit Konpon Chudo during the renovation?**

A. Yes! Although it's undergoing major renovations (which happen only once every 60 years or so), it's a rare and valuable opportunity to **see the restoration work from scaffolding built over the roof**. Worship to the main deity is also continuing.

**Q4. Can I see the Sennichi Kaihogyo?**

A. You might coincidentally encounter a practitioner on the paths during their training, but please be respectful and quiet so as not to disturb their practice. It is good manners not to talk to them or take photos if you see them.

**Q5. What should I wear?**

A. Since it's a mountain area, **comfortable walking shoes and a jacket or outer layer** are essential. The halls are scattered throughout the mountains, and you'll be walking more than you might expect. In winter, dress warmly, and even in summer, be prepared for cooler temperatures in the mornings and evenings.
