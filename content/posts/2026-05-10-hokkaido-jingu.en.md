Hokkaido Jingu Shrine was established by the Meiji government to oversee the development of Hokkaido, and it's truly **Hokkaido's grand guardian shrine**. Nestled in a **beautiful forest of about 180,000 square meters, right next to Maruyama Park** in Sapporo's Chuo Ward, this powerful shrine has watched over Hokkaido's progress for 150 years. With the **"Three Kami of Frontier Development" and Emperor Meiji** as its main deities, it's widely known as a protector for those embarking on new journeys. ## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

In September of Meiji 2 (1869), by imperial edict of Emperor Meiji, the shrine was established in Tokyo with the Three Kami of Frontier Development enshrined, designated as **"Hokkaido's grand guardian."** Initially a temporary structure, it was **relocated to its current site in Maruyama** in 1871. In 1964, **Emperor Meiji** was additionally enshrined, and the shrine's name was changed from "Sapporo Jinja" to "**Hokkaido Jingu**."

The **"Three Kami of Frontier Development"** served as a spiritual anchor for settlers from various parts of Honshu, guiding the development and prosperity of Hokkaido. The annual grand festival (June 14-16), commonly known as the **"Sapporo Festival,"** sees over 100 floats and portable shrines parade through central Sapporo, making it known as Hokkaido's largest festival.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

About a 15-minute walk from **Maruyama Koen Station** on the Tozai Subway Line. By car, it's about 60 minutes from New Chitose Airport and about 15 minutes from Sapporo Station. During the **Sapporo Festival in June**, traffic regulations are enforced on surrounding roads.

**Parking**: Available (free, 240 spaces).

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Etiquette

1.  Bow before passing through the torii gate or main gate
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities, so walk along the sides
3.  Purify yourself at the chozuya (water purification pavilion): left hand, then right hand, rinse mouth, rinse left hand again, and finally cleanse the dipper
4.  **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once**
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, focusing primarily on gratitude

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What exactly are the "Three Kami of Frontier Development"?**

A. They are: ①**Okunitama no Kami** — The deity of Hokkaido's land, ②**Onamuchi no Kami** — The deity of nation-building (Okuninushi no Kami of Izumo Taisha), and ③**Sukunahikona no Kami** — A smaller deity of medicine and sake brewing. All three are believed to possess the **power to develop land**, becoming the spiritual backbone of Hokkaido's frontier development.

**Q2. What is "Hangan-sama"?**

A. It's a **baked mochi** sold at the **Rokkatei Jingu Chaya tea house** along the approach path, a specialty named after the legend that Minamoto no Yoshitsune (Hangan) crossed over to Ezo (the old name for Hokkaido). It's a unique Hokkaido treat, sometimes even **offered for free exclusively to shrine visitors**.

**Q3. When is the best time to see the cherry blossoms?**

A. The best time is in **early May**. Spring arrives about a month later than in Honshu. You can enjoy Hokkaido's late-blooming cherry blossoms as three varieties—**Ezo-yamazakura, Somei Yoshino, and Yaezakura**—bloom in succession.

**Q4. How crowded is it for Hatsumode (first shrine visit of the New Year)?**

A. From pre-dawn to noon on New Year's Day, it's **Hokkaido's busiest Hatsumode spot**, with over 700,000 visitors. If you want to avoid the crowds, **January 3rd or later** is recommended.

**Q5. When is the Sapporo Festival?**

A. Annually from **June 14-16**. It's Hokkaido's largest festival, with over 100 floats and portable shrines parading through central Sapporo. Numerous food stalls line the streets, and locals affectionately know it as the festival that heralds the **"beginning of Sapporo's summer."**
