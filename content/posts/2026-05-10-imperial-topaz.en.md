## Introduction

The name Imperial Topaz is said to originate from a legend where Emperor Alexander I of Russia in the 19th century was so captivated by the pink-orange topaz from the Ural Mountains that he decreed "only the Emperor (imperial) may own it." It was quite literally called "the Emperor's Topaz," and for a long time, only royalty and nobles could possess this magnificent gemstone.

Today, its primary source is Ouro Preto in Minas Gerais, Brazil. This ancient city, meaning "black gold" in Portuguese, flourished from gold mining in the 18th century, and Imperial Topaz, discovered as a byproduct, entered the global gem market. Its sherry-like amber to peach-pink glow continues to be cherished today, carrying special significance as a stone symbolizing "success, dignity, and confidence."

## Symbolism and Meaning

Imperial Topaz's symbolism includes "**Emperor's Radiance, Success, and Confidence**." It has been cherished worldwide for a long time as the "Stone of Success and Dignity" beloved by Russian Emperors.

The history of topaz itself dates back to ancient Egypt and Rome, where it was revered as the "**Stone of Ra, the Sun God**" and a "**Stone of Good Fortune**." Pliny, the Roman naturalist, wrote that its name originated from stones found on Topazos Island in the Red Sea, though it's also said these were actually peridot.

The "**Imperial**" designation became established in 19th-century Russia, when highly reddish topaz gathered for the imperial family began to be fashioned into jewelry as "the Emperor's Topaz." Even today, with its primary source shifted to Ouro Preto, Brazil, only individual stones exhibiting sherry to orange-pink hues are permitted to bear the "Imperial" name, making it a highly branded stone.

As a warm-toned stone corresponding to the 3rd Chakra (Solar Plexus), it is cherished for the theme of "**taking charge of your own life**."

## Color Variations

Imperial Topaz is a stone whose value can change significantly with even slight variations in color.

-   **Sherry Color (Reddish-brown)**: The most classic color, cherished by Russian emperors.
-   **Peach Pink**: The most popular color in today's market.
-   **Golden Orange**: Also known as "**Champagne Topaz**," this is the color associated with Ra, the Sun God.
-   **Red-Pink**: Rare. The highest rank that can be designated "**Imperial**" on a gemological certificate.
-   **Sherry Pink Pleochroism**: A rare item whose color changes depending on the viewing direction, popular among collectors.

## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Many heat-treated and radiation-treated items are circulated in the market, labeled as "Imperial Topaz." Always check a gemological certificate to confirm if it's natural color (untreated).
-   If it's only labeled "Sherry Topaz," there are cases where it doesn't qualify as true Imperial Topaz.
-   There are instances where heat-treated citrine (yellow quartz) is misrepresented as "topaz," so always confirm the mineral name.
-   Purchasing from a reputable specialty store with a gemological certificate provides peace of mind. Please also refer to our [Guide to Spotting Fake and Artificial Stones](/blog/spot-fake-stones/).
