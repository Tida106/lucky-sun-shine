## Introduction

The English name *Iolite* for Iolite (Cordierite) is said to originate from the Greek word "**ios (violet)**," referring to its beautiful blue-violet color reminiscent of a violet flower. Mineralogically, it's known as "**Cordierite**," named after **Pierre Cordier**, the French mineralogist who studied this stone in 1813.

The most famous legend tells of **Viking sailors using it as a "Sun Stone" to navigate like a compass**. Iolite exhibits strong **pleochroism**, meaning its color changes depending on the viewing angle. It's believed that by holding a thin slice of iolite to their eye, Vikings could determine the sun's position even on cloudy days by detecting polarized light. From this episode, known as the "**Viking Compass**," Iolite has been cherished ever since as the "**Stone that Shows the Way**" for people at life's crossroads.

Iolite's stone powers are "**Compass, Path, and Vision**." As the Viking Compass, the "Stone that Shows the Way," it has been loved worldwide for a long time.

Viking sailors are said to have held thinly polished iolite to their eyes to discern the sun's position amidst the dense fog and overcast skies of the North Sea. This is because the stone possesses the property of **selectively filtering light like a polarizing filter**, and the validity of this theory is still discussed in modern science. The symbolism of "a stone that clearly shows the path forward" was born from this.

As a deep violet-blue stone corresponding to the 6th Chakra (Third Eye), Iolite is favored today for themes of "**life direction**" and "**clarifying one's vision**." It is particularly chosen as an amulet for situations requiring **choice**, such as career changes, relocation, further education, or starting a business.

## Enjoying Pleochroism

Iolite's most distinctive feature is its strong **pleochroism**, meaning its color changes when you rotate it.

-   **When viewed from the front**: Deep violet-blue (violet color). This is considered its most beautiful orientation.
-   **When viewed from the side**: Pale blue to pale yellow.
-   **When viewed from directly above**: Gray to nearly colorless.

For high-quality stones, jewelers choose the orientation that brings out the most beautiful blue when cutting. For beads, the varying color depending on the bead's orientation is also a "**sign of authenticity**." ## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   A major characteristic of natural Iolite is that its **color reliably changes when you alter the viewing angle**. Beads with a completely fixed color may be glass or dyed products.
-   Historically, it was sometimes sold as "Water Sapphire." While it might be confused with sapphire, their price ranges differ significantly, so be cautious.
-   While similar in color to Tanzanite, Iolite can be distinguished by the appearance of yellow to gray hues.
-   For peace of mind, purchase from a trusted specialty store with items accompanied by a gemological certificate. Please also refer to the [Guide to Spotting Fake and Artificial Stones](/blog/spot-fake-stones/).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. How do Iolite and Tanzanite differ?**
A. Both are violet-blue, but [Tanzanite](/blog/tanzanite/) is a zoisite from Tanzania, while Iolite is a cordierite. Tanzanite has a deeper color and is more expensive. Iolite is definitively different due to its pleochroism, which allows you to **see yellow hues**.

**Q. What bead size is recommended for bracelets?**
A. **8 to 10mm** is standard for enjoying the color change. The larger the bead, the more distinct the pleochroism.

**Q. What if it's labeled "Water Sapphire"?**
A. This is an old or popular name for Iolite. It's different from sapphire (corundum), so make sure the price point is appropriate.

**Q. Is it suitable as an amulet for job searching?**
