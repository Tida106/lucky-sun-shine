## Introduction

Itsukushima Shrine, nestled on Miyajima Island (also known as Itsukushima) in the Seto Inland Sea, is an ancient shrine renowned worldwide for its **vermilion Great Torii Gate floating on the sea**. Originally constructed as its current over-water sanctuary by Taira no Kiyomori in the Heian period, it was designated a **World Cultural Heritage site in 1996** as a truly unique sacred place where **its scenery dramatically transforms with the ebb and flow of the tides**. It is also one of Japan's Three Most Scenic Views and Hiroshima Prefecture's largest power spot, attracting over 3 million visitors annually. This article combines **history, a tidal guide, FAQs, and editorial notes** into one comprehensive page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

Miyajima has long been revered as **"the island where deities are worshipped and honored" (Itsuki Matsuru Shima)**, with the entire island considered a sacred precinct. Itsukushima Shrine is said to have been founded in 593 AD (the first year of Emperor Suiko's reign) by Saeki no Kuramoto.

After Taira no Kiyomori was appointed governor of Aki Province in 1146, he undertook extensive construction work, hoping for the prosperity of the Taira clan. In 1168, the **current over-water sanctuary** was completed. Precious cultural assets such as the Heike Nokyo (Sutras copied by the Taira clan) and armor were handed down. Even after the rise of the Minamoto clan, Minamoto no Yoritomo, Ashikaga Takauji, and Toyotomi Hideyoshi visited the shrine. Mori Motonari also prayed for victory there in the **Battle of Miyajima** in 1555. Despite repeated damage and reconstruction due to typhoons and lightning strikes, it is an astonishing shrine that has **maintained its over-water structure for approximately 850 years**.

The main deities are the **Munakata Three Goddesses**, goddesses of maritime protection and safe journeys. In mythology, they are said to be **three sisters born from the pledge (Ukei) between Amaterasu Omikami and Susanoo-no-Mikoto**, and they transmit the "Munakata faith" along with Munakata Taisha Shrine in Fukuoka.

## <span id="highlight"></span>Highlights and Must-Sees

-   **Great Torii Gate floating on the sea** (16.6m tall; current gate rebuilt in 1875, major renovations completed in 2022)
-   **Main Hall (Honden), Prayer Hall (Haiden), and Corridors** (National Treasure)
-   **High Stage (Takamori)** (one of Japan's Three Great Stages)
-   **Noh Stage** (Important Cultural Property, Japan's only Noh stage built over the sea)
-   **Sori-bashi Bridge (Curved Bridge)**
-   **Mount Misen** (the highest peak on the island, accessible by ropeway)

## <span id="tide"></span>Tides and Visiting Tips

The greatest feature of Itsukushima Shrine is how **its scenery dramatically changes with the tides**.

-   **High Tide** (Tidal level 250cm or more) — The Great Torii Gate and shrine buildings appear to float on the sea, creating a breathtaking view.
-   **Low Tide** (Tidal level 100cm or less) — You can walk to the Great Torii Gate, ideal for a closer visit.
-   **Twilight to Night Illumination** (Sunset until 11 PM) — The torii gate glows enchantingly.

It is essential to check the **Japan Meteorological Agency's tidal chart** or the official website before your visit. High tide and low tide each occur twice a day.

## <span id="access"></span>Access Information

From JR Hiroshima Station, take the Sanyo Main Line to **Miyajimaguchi Station** (approximately 25 minutes). From Miyajimaguchi, take a ferry to Miyajima Pier (approximately 10 minutes), then walk about 12 minutes to the shrine buildings.

**Parking**: There is almost no parking available on Miyajima Island, so please use a **parking lot on the Miyajimaguchi side** and cross by ferry.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Etiquette

1.  Bow before passing through the torii gate or temple gate.
2.  The center of the path is for the deities; walk along the sides.
3.  Purify yourself at the chozuya (water purification fountain): left hand, then right hand, rinse mouth, left hand, then cleanse the ladle.
4.  **Bow twice, clap hands twice, and bow once.**
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, focusing on gratitude.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What is the current status of the Great Torii Gate?**

A. **Extensive repair work was completed between 2019 and 2022**, and it can now be seen in its beautiful vermilion state. While some might have wanted to see it with scaffolding during renovations, it has now been fully restored to its original appearance.

**Q2. What is the best time to walk to the Torii Gate?**

A. You can walk to the base of the torii gate at **low tide (when the tidal level is 100cm or less)**. It is recommended to check the official website and the Japan Meteorological Agency's tidal chart to plan your visit accordingly.

**Q3. Are there deer?**

A. Yes. Many **wild deer** inhabit Miyajima. They are cherished as messengers of the deities, but feeding them is prohibited. Be careful, as they may try to snatch food from your bags.

**Q4. How long does it take to hike Mount Misen?**

A. You can take the ropeway close to the summit (Momijidani Station to Shishiiwa Station, approximately 30 minutes). From Shishiiwa Station to the summit of Mount Misen is about a 30-minute walk on a mountain path. The panoramic view of the Seto Inland Sea is exceptional.

**Q5. Is it possible to hold Shichi-Go-San or weddings here?**

A. Yes. Shinto weddings held in the over-water sanctuary are incredibly popular, with requests for **"sea weddings"** pouring in from all over Japan. Advance reservations are essential.

## <span id="team-view"></span>Our Editorial Team's Perspective: Visit It as a "Tidal Sanctuary," Not Just a "Tourist Spot"

Itsukushima Shrine is a World Heritage site and one of Japan's Three Most Scenic Views — inevitably attracting many tourists, with much of the focus often on eating and shopping in the arcade. While that is certainly enjoyable, our editorial team would like to offer a suggestion. Simply having the idea of **"staying according to the tides"** will deepen your experience of this sacred place by two levels.

### Why a Half-Day Isn't Enough, and You Should Visit for a Full Day

| Time Slot | What You Can Experience |
|---|---|
| 6 AM - 8 AM | Low tide at the torii, deserted paths |
| 9 AM - 12 PM | Tourist time, shopping street, souvenirs |
| 1 PM - 3 PM | Take the Mount Misen ropeway to the summit, stunning views of the Seto Inland Sea |
| 4 PM - 6 PM | The shrine buildings floating on the sea at high tide, evening scenery |
| 7 PM - 10 PM | The Great Torii Gate illuminated, a privilege for overnight guests |

Since **high tide and low tide each occur twice a day**, you need a **minimum of 10 hours** to witness both. If you treat it as a "quick side trip" for a day visit, you will only experience **less than half** of Itsukushima Shrine's true charm. Our editorial team strongly recommends **staying overnight on Miyajima** if possible.

### Itsukushima Shrine is Especially Recommended for People Who:

-   Are deeply moved by **ocean, water, and tidal landscapes**.
-   Have a lifestyle that allows them to **"slowly wait for changes in scenery"** without being rushed.

Conversely, for those who want to "tour efficiently in a short time" or "quickly collect a list of blessings," the charm of Itsukushima Shrine will be halved. The key is to understand its nature as a **"shrine that changes time"** and approach it accordingly.
