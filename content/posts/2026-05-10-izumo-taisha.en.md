## Introduction

Izumo Grand Shrine (Izumo Oyashiro) is nationally renowned as the "**Deity of Matchmaking and Connections**." Its Main Hall is said to date back to the Age of Myths, and ancient texts like "Kuchizusami" from the Heian period describe it with the phrase "**Kumota, Wani, Kyosan**" – suggesting that Izumo Grand Shrine's Main Hall was then the tallest building in Japan, surpassing even the Great Buddha Hall of Todai-ji Temple and the Daigoku-den of the Kyoto Imperial Palace. It is truly a shrine of immense ancient heritage.

This article provides a comprehensive guide to **its founding history, worship etiquette, strategies for visiting during Kamiari-zuki, nearby sacred sites to explore, compatible crystals, and frequently asked questions**. Whether you are visiting for the first time or seeking deeper knowledge on a repeat visit, feel free to use it as your definitive guide.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Mythology

Okuninushi-no-Okami, the main deity of Izumo Grand Shrine, is the central figure in the Japanese myth of "**Kuni-yuzuri**," or the "Transfer of the Land." He was entrusted by the heavenly deities with the creation of the earthly realm, and later ceded the land during the Tenson Korin (descent of the heavenly grandchild). It is said that the magnificent palace built by the heavenly deities in return became the origin of Izumo Grand Shrine.

The Main Hall during the Heian period is believed to have stood at **around 48 meters** (according to various theories). In 2000, three massive pillars, each 3 meters in diameter, were excavated within the shrine grounds, archaeologically proving the existence of such a colossal main hall.

The shrine gained its national reputation as the "**Deity of Fate-Forging**" due to the traditional custom of **"Kamiari-zuki" (the Month with Gods)** in the tenth month of the lunar calendar. During this time, deities from all over Japan are said to gather in Izumo to hold "Kamuhakari" (Divine Conference) and forge connections between people. While other regions of Japan call October "Kannazuki" (the Month without Gods) because the deities are absent, Izumo alone calls it Kamiari-zuki.

## <span id="highlights"></span>Highlights

### Honden (Main Hall) (National Treasure, Taisha-zukuri style)

The current Main Hall, rebuilt in 1744, is a prime example of the **Taisha-zukuri style**, the oldest form of Japanese shrine architecture, standing 24 meters tall. The Honden is usually not open to the public, but special viewings are occasionally held.

### Kaguraden's Grand Shimenawa

Measuring **13.6 meters** long and weighing **5.2 tons**, the Kaguraden's grand Shimenawa (sacred straw rope) is one of the largest in Japan and a prominent symbol of Izumo Grand Shrine. Many visitors take photos here. Throwing coins into the shimenawa is considered bad manners and is prohibited.

### Seidamari Grand Torii Gate

This large wooden torii gate marks the starting point of the approach to the shrine. It is traditional to bow once before passing through the gate.

### Musubi-no-Goshinzō (Deity of Connections Statue)

This statue depicts the moment Okuninushi-no-Okami received the **Sakimitama Kushimitama (the Spirit of Fortune and the Mysterious Spirit)** and became the "Deity of Fate-Forging." It is one of the highlights of a visit.

### Soga-no-Yashiro Shrine

Located directly behind the Main Hall, this subordinate shrine is dedicated to Susanoo-no-Mikoto. Visiting **Soga-no-Yashiro as well as the Main Hall** is known as the authentic way of worship among serious visitors.

## <span id="manners"></span>Worship Etiquette (Unique Two Bows, Four Claps, One Bow)

A key characteristic of Izumo Grand Shrine is its unique etiquette of "**two bows, four claps, one bow**." This is **two claps more** than the standard "two bows, two claps, one bow" used at most other shrines.

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate.
2.  Keep to the **left side of the approach path** (the center is reserved for the deities).
3.  At the temizuya (purification font), cleanse your left hand, then your right hand, then your mouth (receiving water with your hand), then your left hand again, and finally turn the ladle upright to rinse its handle.
4.  At the Haiden (worship hall) front, offer your prayers with **two bows, four claps, and one bow**.
5.  Keep personal wishes discreet; first, express gratitude.
6.  It is recommended to extend your visit to Soga-no-Yashiro Shrine after the Main Hall.
7.  Do not throw offertory coins into the shimenawa.

> ※ On May 14th, the "Reisai" (Annual Grand Festival), and on other specific festival days, an even more reverent ritual of **two bows, eight claps, and one bow** is performed.

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Seasons and Worship Strategies

### Kamiari-zuki (Tenth month of the lunar calendar, generally November in the modern calendar)

This is the **absolute best timing**. It is the period when deities from all over Japan gather in Izumo, and a series of rituals, starting with the Kami-mukae-shinji (God-Welcoming Ritual) at Inasa-no-Hama Beach, are exclusive to Kamiari-zuki. Be prepared for crowds, but this is when Izumo Grand Shrine's energy is at its peak.

### Early Morning (around 7:00 AM)

This is an **ideal time to visit** year-round. There are fewer people, allowing for quiet worship in the pure air. The sensation of hearing only the crunch of gravel underfoot cannot be experienced amidst the afternoon crowds.

### After the Rain

Surprisingly, visiting the shrine grounds after the rain is also recommended. The lush green of the trees stands out beautifully, and with fewer tour buses, there are also fewer people.

### Times to Avoid

-   January 1st to 3rd (massive crowds for Hatsumode, the first shrine visit of the New Year)
-   Major holidays (Golden Week and Obon)
-   Around November 23rd, the "Kenkokusai" (Harvest Festival)

## <span id="access"></span>Access

-   **Train**: From JR Izumoshi Station, take the Ichibata Bus for about 25 minutes and alight at "Izumo Grand Shrine" stop, which is right next to the shrine. Alternatively, take the Ichibata Electric Railway to "Izumotaisha-mae" Station and walk for about 7 minutes.
-   **Car**: Approximately 15 minutes from Izumo IC on the Sanin Expressway.
-   **Parking**: Free shrine parking is available (385 spaces). Early morning arrival is recommended during Kamiari-zuki and the New Year.
-   **Airport**: Approximately 30-40 minutes by car or bus from Izumo Enmusubi Airport.

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Spots You Might Want to Visit

-   **Inasa-no-Hama Beach** — The location for the "Kami-mukae-shinji" (God-Welcoming Ritual) during Kamiari-zuki. It's a 5-minute taxi ride from Izumo Grand Shrine. It is a well-known local custom to take a handful of sand from the beach and exchange it for sand from Soga-no-Yashiro Shrine at Izumo Grand Shrine.
-   **Hinomisaki Shrine** — Located west of Izumo Grand Shrine, this vermilion-lacquered shrine overlooks the Sea of Japan and enshrines Susanoo's sibling deity, Amaterasu.
-   **Shimane Museum of Ancient Izumo History** — Right next to Izumo Grand Shrine. The actual exhibition of the excavated giant pillars is a must-see.
-   **Yaegaki Shrine** — Famous for its "Kagami-no-ike" (Mirror Pond) fortune-telling for relationships. About a 30-minute drive from Izumo Grand Shrine.
-   **Susa Shrine** — An ancient shrine said to be the last place where Susanoo resided. Popular among power spot enthusiasts.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Should I visit Izumo Grand Shrine or Ise Grand Shrine first?**

A. There is no strict rule. Traditionally, the order of **Ise Grand Shrine → Izumo Grand Shrine** is preferred (visiting the heavenly deities first, then the deity who created the earthly realm). However, it's perfectly fine to visit whichever one you feel called to first.

**Q2. Is it okay to visit alone?**

A. Many say that **worshipping alone allows for greater focus**. Praying for connections is also perfectly fine to do alone. Visiting during the quiet early morning hours is recommended.

**Q3. How do I get a Goshuin (shrine seal stamp) at Izumo Grand Shrine?**

A. Goshuin can be obtained at the Shamusho (shrine office) to the left of the Haiden. During crowded periods like Kamiari-zuki, there may be a wait of several tens of minutes. Original Izumo Grand Shrine Goshuincho (Goshuin notebooks) are also available for purchase.

**Q4. How long does a visit take?**

A. To thoroughly visit the Haiden, Honden, Soga-no-Yashiro, and Kaguraden from the parking lot, **about 60 to 90 minutes** is a good estimate. If you want to explore in detail or take many photos, allow 2 hours to be safe.

**Q5. Are there any dress code requirements?**

A. There are no strict rules, but modest attire that shows little skin is recommended as a sign of respect for the sacred grounds. It's best to avoid sandals, tracksuits, and shorts. During colder seasons, dress warmly, as the shrine grounds can be quite breezy and colder than you might expect.

**Q6. How do I draw an Omikuji (fortune slip)?**

A. A unique characteristic of Izumo Grand Shrine's Omikuji is that they **do not have "good or bad luck" categories** like Daikichi (great luck) or Kyo (bad luck). Instead, they contain teachings from Okuninushi-no-Okami, and the custom is to keep them in mind and take them home.
