## Introduction

Jade is a green stone known as "**Gyoku**" (玉) in the East, revered more highly than gold in China. In Japan, it has been used since the Jomon period for **Magatama and Taishu beads**, with Jade from Itoigawa being unearthed from ruins over 7,000 years old. As a symbol of important values like "virtue, prosperity, and longevity," it's one of the most beloved crystals in East Asia. This article covers its **mineral data, historical background, reputed effects, FAQs, and an editor's note**.

There are two mineralogical types of stone called "Jade," and **Jadeite (hard jade), especially from Myanmar, is considered the highest quality.** Itoigawa in Japan is home to the world's oldest Jade processing culture. In 2016, the Mineralogical Society of Japan selected Jade as the "**National Stone of Japan**."

In ancient China, "**Yu**" (玉) referred to Nephrite, and it was only after the Qing Dynasty that **Jadeite began to be prized as "Feicui" (翡翠)**.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Meanings, History, and Cultural Background

Its stone meanings are "**Longevity, Prosperity, and Virtue**."

In ancient China, it was said that "**Jade possesses five virtues**" (benevolence, righteousness, wisdom, courage, and integrity), and it was the highest-ranking stone used for imperial and aristocratic adornments and burial items. In Japan, **Taishu and Magatama beads** were crafted from Itoigawa Jade since the Jomon period, and these have been unearthed from various archaeological sites, including Izumo Taisha. Guatemalan Jadeite was also sacred in Maya and Aztec civilizations, making it a rare mineral considered the "**highest stone**" independently in **both the Eastern and Central American hemispheres**.

> Source: Mineralogical Society of Japan, GIA Jade Reference, various archaeological documents.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What are "A Jade," "B Jade," and "C Jade"?**

A. These are **industry terms indicating the treatment level** of Jadeite. A refers to untreated natural jade, B to resin-impregnated jade, C to dyed jade, and B+C to impregnated and dyed jade. If you wish to purchase authentic natural Jade, please look for **"A Jade," "untreated," and "with a gemological certificate."**

**Q2. Is Lavender Jade dyed?**

A. **Natural lavender (purple) Jade** does exist, but many dyed products are also circulated in the market. It's best to check a gemological certificate to confirm if the color is natural or treated.

**Q3. Can I buy Itoigawa Jade from Japan?**

A. Yes, absolutely! You can purchase domestically produced Jade at specialty stores within Itoigawa City or at fairs organized by Niigata Prefecture. The charm lies in being able to enjoy Japan's **"National Stone"** firsthand.

**Q4. Are "Yu" and "Feicui" the same in China?**

A. Strictly speaking, they are different. In ancient China, "**Yu**" (玉) referred to **Nephrite (soft jade)**. It was only during the Qing Dynasty (from the 18th century onward) that **Jadeite (hard jade)** from Myanmar began to be highly prized as "Feicui" (翡翠).

**Q5. Are there other stones for prosperity and virtue?**
