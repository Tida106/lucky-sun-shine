## Introduction

Kasuga Taisha Shrine is an ancient shrine connected to the Fujiwara clan, set against the backdrop of the **Kasugayama Primeval Forest**, a **250-hectare pristine forest** spreading east of Nara Park. The **approximately 3,000 stone and hanging lanterns** that light up its grounds create a spectacle unparalleled anywhere else in the world. It is a unique sacred precinct where **deer**, considered messengers of the gods, roam freely. In 1998, it was registered as a World Cultural Heritage site as part of the **"Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara."** This article compiles its **history, visiting tips, Meoto Daikokusha Shrine, FAQs, and an editor's note** into a single page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

When the capital was moved to Heijo-kyo in 710, the ancestral deity of the Fujiwara clan, **Takemikazuchi no Mikoto**, was **invited from Kashima Jingu Shrine in Hitachi Province, riding a white deer.** This legend is why the deer in Nara Park are cherished and protected as "messengers of the gods." In 768, by imperial decree of Empress Shotoku, four main shrine buildings were constructed at the current location. Since then, the tradition of **Shikinen Zotai (rebuilding the shrine every 20 years)** has continued to this day, giving it a history as distinguished as Ise Jingu Shrine.

From the Heian period onwards, it became a symbol of power as the **tutelary shrine of the Fujiwara clan**, flourishing as a religious center of Nara alongside the "Nanto Shichidaiji" (Seven Great Temples of Nanto).

## <span id="meoto"></span>Meoto Daikokusha Shrine for Matchmaking

**Meoto Daikokusha Shrine**, a subsidiary shrine within the Kasuga Taisha grounds, is a sacred place for matchmaking, being the only shrine in Japan to enshrine **a married couple of Daikoku deities.** The **heart-shaped *Ema* (votive tablets) for "Mizu-urani" (water fortune-telling)** are very popular; when you float the heart in water, a message about matchmaking appears. It's always bustling with worshippers praying for good relationships and marital harmony.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- Treat the deer as **messengers of the gods**; only feed them deer crackers.
- Experience **Mizu-urani (water fortune-telling) and *shamoji* (rice paddle) *Ema*** at Meoto Daikokusha Shrine.
- The **Mantoro Lantern Festival** (Setsubun and August 14-15) sees approximately 3,000 lanterns lit simultaneously.
- Tour the grounds by taking the **Wakamiya Jūgo-sha Meguri (Pilgrimage of the Fifteen Wakamiya Shrines).**
- The view of the vermilion corridor and lanterns is most beautiful **around dusk**.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

From JR or Kintetsu "**Nara**" Station, take the city loop bus for about 10 minutes and get off at "**Kasuga Taisha Honden**" (Kasuga Taisha Main Sanctuary) bus stop. It's right there. If walking, it's about a 25-minute walk from Nara Station (you can pass Todai-ji Temple and Kofuku-ji Temple along the way).

**Parking**: Available (paid, 100 spaces). Public transport is recommended, as it tends to fill up on weekends and national holidays.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1.  Bow once before passing through the *torii* gate or main gate.
2.  The center of the *sando* (path to the shrine) is for the deities; walk along the sides.
3.  At the *temizuya* (water purification pavilion), purify your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, purify your left hand again, and finally rinse the ladle.
4.  **Two bows, two claps, one bow**.
5.  Keep personal wishes modest; focus on gratitude.
6.  Only feed deer **deer crackers**; other sweets and bread are forbidden.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. When can I see the Mantoro Lantern Festival?**

A. It is held twice a year, during **Setsubun (February)** and **Obon (August 14-15)**. The mystical sight of approximately 3,000 lanterns lit simultaneously is Kasuga Taisha Shrine's greatest highlight. A **special admission ticket** may be required.

**Q2. How does the water fortune-telling work?**

A. It involves floating a **heart-shaped white paper**, received at Meoto Daikokusha Shrine, in the spring water. **Fortune-telling characters will then appear** on the paper. It's a popular spot for praying for good relationships and love.

**Q3. How should I interact with the deer?**

A. Treat them with respect as **messengers of the gods**. The rules are: **do not feed them anything other than deer crackers**, **do not chase them**, and **do not kick them**. It's a famous Nara sight when deer bow back if you bow to them.

**Q4. Are there any lanterns donated by individuals?**

A. Yes. **Many of the stone lanterns were donated by individuals and organizations from the Heian to Edo periods**, with the oldest dating back to 1136 (Heian period). **Donations are still accepted today**, and the names of donors for hanging lanterns in the corridors are inscribed for contributions starting from several hundred thousand yen.

**Q5. How many types of *Goshuin* (red stamps) are available?**

A. In addition to the *Goshuin* from the main shrine, *Goshuin* are also available at **Wakamiya Shrine, Meoto Daikokusha Shrine, Mizuya Shrine, and the fifteen Wakamiya shrines**, among others. It is a popular shrine among collectors.
