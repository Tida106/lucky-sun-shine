## Introduction

Kirishima Jingu is a venerable shrine that stands at the foot of the sacred **Kirishima Mountain Range**, representing Southern Kyushu. It enshrines **Ninigi-no-Mikoto**, the main character of the **Tenson Korin (Descent of the Heavenly Grandson)** myth, and serves as a distant worship site for **Mount Takachiho**, the mythical stage itself. It's also famous as the destination for **Sakamoto Ryoma and Oryo's first honeymoon in Japan**, and its **vermilion-lacquered main hall was designated a National Treasure in 2022**. It's a sacred precinct with a grand scale, offering views of the Kirishima Mountain Range and Sakurajima. In this article, I'll put together the shrine's **history, the Tenson Korin myth, Sakamoto Ryoma's story, FAQs, and an editor's note** all on one page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

According to shrine tradition, Kirishima Jingu was founded during **Emperor Kinmei's reign (6th century)**. Initially, it was enshrined between **Mount Takachiho** and **Mount Hinotsunemine** in the Kirishima Mountain Range. However, due to repeated damage from volcanic eruptions, it was relocated multiple times, finally settling in its current location in the **15th century**.

The current shrine buildings were donated by **Shimazu Yoshitaka, the 4th Lord of the Satsuma Domain, in 1715**. It's renowned as a masterpiece of architecture, embodying the vermilion and polychromatic styles of Kagoshima, and its **Main Hall, Heiden, and Haiden were designated National Treasures in 2022**. Since the Meiji era, it has gathered nationwide reverence as a Kanpei Taisha (Grand Imperial Shrine), attracting approximately 1.5 million worshippers annually.

## <span id="myth"></span>The Tenson Korin Myth and Mount Takachiho

According to the "Kojiki" and "Nihon Shoki," **Ninigi-no-Mikoto**, the grandson of **Amaterasu Omikami**, descended from **Takamagahara (the High Plain of Heaven)** to the earthly realm, carrying the Three Sacred Treasures. It's said that he landed on the summit of **Mount Takachiho** (elevation 1,574m), the highest peak in the Kirishima Mountain Range.

An anecdote remains that **"Ama-no-Sakaboko" (Heavenly Reverse Spear)** stands thrust into the summit of Mount Takachiho, and **Sakamoto Ryoma and Oryo pulled it out and laughed**. Kirishima Jingu stands as a **distant worship site for Mount Takachiho**, connecting myth and reality. For those who genuinely want to experience the world of myth, it's possible to hike to Mount Takachiho from the former site of Kirishima Jingu (for experienced hikers).

## <span id="building"></span>The National Treasure Shrine Buildings

The **Main Hall, Heiden, and Haiden** of Kirishima Jingu were **designated National Treasures in 2022**. The gorgeous architectural style, built in 1715 with **vermilion lacquer, gold leaf, and richly colored carvings**, is also known as **"Nikkō of the West."**

Especially noteworthy are the **flower and bird paintings on the coffered ceiling** and the **dragon carvings on the pillars** inside, making it a representative example of early modern shrine architecture in Kyushu. The excellent state of preservation of the entire building was also a key factor in its National Treasure designation.

## <span id="ryoma"></span>Sakamoto Ryoma and Oryo's Honeymoon

In **1866 (Keiō 2)**, Sakamoto Ryoma, who was injured in the Teradaya Incident, embarked on a recuperative journey in Satsuma with his new wife, **Oryo**, also taking advantage of the hot springs. This journey is remembered as **"Japan's first honeymoon,"** and it's said that the couple climbed the Kirishima Mountain Range and **also visited Kirishima Jingu**.

The anecdote of them pulling out the Ama-no-Sakaboko on Mount Takachiho's summit and laughing together is recorded in a letter Ryoma sent to his sister Otome. Today, tourist routes tracing **"Ryoma and Oryo's Footsteps"** are maintained around Kirishima Jingu.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train: From JR Nippō Main Line **"Kirishima Jingu Station,"** take the Iwasaki Bus for about 10 minutes and alight at "Kirishima Jingu."

Car: Approximately 30 minutes from Kyushu Expressway **"Mizobe-Kagoshima Airport IC,"** or about 60 minutes from Higashi Kyushu Expressway "Miyakonojo IC."

**Parking**: Available within the shrine grounds (free, approx. 500 spaces).

## <span id="season"></span>Best Times to Visit

**Cherry blossoms in spring (late March to early April) and autumn foliage (November)** are especially beautiful. **Hatsumode (first shrine visit of the New Year)** draws some of the largest crowds in Southern Kyushu. Visiting on a clear morning when the **Kirishima Mountain Range** is visible makes it easier to experience the mythological worldview.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Visiting Manners

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate.
2.  The center of the path is for the deities; walk along the sides.
3.  At the chozuya (purification font), cleanse your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, cleanse your left hand again, and then cleanse the dipper.
4.  **Two bows, two claps, one bow**.
5.  When photographing the decorations of the National Treasure shrine buildings, please be mindful of others.
6.  When touching the sacred tree, do so quietly and don't linger.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. When was it designated a National Treasure?**

A. The Main Hall, Heiden, and Haiden were collectively designated National Treasures in **February 2022**. This was due to the high evaluation of the excellent preservation state and architectural value of the vermilion-lacquered shrine buildings, which were built in 1715.

**Q2. What about hiking to Mount Takachiho?**

A. There's a climbing trail from the former site of Kirishima Jingu, leading to a serious **hike of about 3 hours to the summit (5-6 hours round trip)**. There may be entry restrictions due to volcanic gas or weather, so please check information with the Kirishima Mountain Nature Guide Club, etc., beforehand.

**Q3. Can I trace Sakamoto Ryoma's footsteps?**

A. Centered around Kirishima Onsenkyo, the **"Ryoma and Oryo Honeymoon Road"** is maintained, allowing you to visit sites like the remains of Shiohira Onsen, where they stayed. Walking maps are available at the tourist information center.

**Q4. Where can I get a Goshuin (shrine stamp)?**

A. You can get one at the **Juyosho (shrine office for offerings and charms)** in front of the Haiden (Worship Hall). There are sometimes **limited edition Goshuin commemorating the National Treasure designation**, so please check when you visit.

**Q5. What about the effects of eruptions and volcanic gas?**

A. While the Kirishima Mountain Range is an active volcano, Kirishima Jingu itself is in a safe location. However, volcanic activity from Shinmoedake and Sakurajima can sometimes affect surrounding tourism, so please check the Japan Meteorological Agency's volcanic activity information.
