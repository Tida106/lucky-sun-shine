Hello everyone! Sun-chan here! Kumano Hongu Taisha Shrine, nestled in Hongu-cho, Tanabe City, Wakayama Prefecture, stands as the **central pillar of the Kumano Sanzan (Three Grand Shrines of Kumano)**. For over a millennium, it has been revered as a **"Land of Rebirth" and "a Place for a Life Reset,"** drawing pilgrims from emperors to common folk in what was known as the "**Ant Procession to Kumano**" due to the sheer numbers. In 2004, it was registered as a World Heritage site under the designation "**Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range**." In this article, I'll bring you all the essential information, including its **history, key pilgrimage points, the Yatagarasu legend, FAQs, and a postscript**, all in one convenient guide.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

Kumano faith revolves around **"prayer transcending life and death,"** drawing immense numbers of pilgrims, known as the "**Ant Procession to Kumano**," since the Heian period. Historical records show over 100 **imperial pilgrimages (Kumano Goko)** by retired emperors and cloistered emperors alone, with Emperor Shirakawa visiting 9 times, Emperor Go-Shirakawa 34 times, and Emperor Go-Toba 28 times—truly a sacred site repeatedly visited by the era's most powerful figures!

The original shrine buildings were located at **Oyunohara**, at the confluence of the Kumano River, Otonashi River, and Iwata River. However, they were tragically swept away in the **Great Flood of 1889 (Meiji 22)**. The four shrine buildings that miraculously survived were then relocated to the current elevated site. At the original shrine location of **Oyunohara**, now stands **Japan's tallest torii gate (33.9m high)**, which remains a revered and special sacred area to this day.

## <span id="yatagarasu"></span>The Legend of Yatagarasu (the Three-Legged Crow)

The divine messenger of Kumano is **Yatagarasu, a three-legged crow**. According to the 'Nihon Shoki' (The Chronicles of Japan), Yatagarasu was sent by the gods to guide Emperor Jimmu during his eastern expedition from Kumano to Yamato. As a symbol of **"showing the right path when one is lost in life,"** many people still visit today to pray for guidance in their careers or job changes.

The **three-legged crow** on the emblem of the Japan national football (soccer) team actually originates from this very Yatagarasu! And guess what? The goshuin (red stamp) you receive will even feature a Yatagarasu stamp. How cool is that?!

## <span id="point"></span>Key Pilgrimage Points

- Traditionally, pilgrims **walk the Kumano Kodo Nakahechi Route** to reach the shrine.
- After visiting Hongu Taisha, be sure to **stop by Oyunohara** (about a 10-minute walk).
- Combine your visit with **Kumano Hayatama Taisha and Kumano Nachi Taisha** for the "**Kumano Sanzan Pilgrimage**."
- Perform a ritual purification (misogi) at **Tsubo-yu in Yunomine Onsen** (a World Heritage site).
- You can even **mail letters from the Yatagarasu Post Box**!

## <span id="access"></span>Access

From JR Kii-Tanabe Station, take a Ryujin Bus or Meiko Bus for about two hours and get off at "**Hongu Taisha-mae**." The shrine is right there! If you're driving, it's about a 1 hour and 40-minute drive from the Hanwa Expressway's Nanki-Tanabe Interchange. There are also bus routes from **Shingu Station and Kii-Katsuura Station**.

**Parking**: Free parking is available (150 spaces).

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Etiquette

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate or main gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities; please walk along the sides.
3.  At the chozuya (water ablution pavilion), purify your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, clean your left hand again, and then rinse the ladle.
4.  Perform **two bows, two claps, and one bow** (the traditional Shinto prayer).
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, focusing primarily on gratitude.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. What is the best way to visit the Kumano Sanzan?**

A. Historically, the traditional order is Hongu → Shingu → Nachi. If starting from Shingu Station, it's Hayatama → Nachi → Hongu; if from Kii-Tanabe Station, it's Hongu → Shingu → Nachi. If you want to visit all three in one day, a car or rental car is almost essential!

**Q2. I want to walk the Kumano Kodo, but is it okay for beginners?**

A. Absolutely! The classic beginner-friendly course is from **Hosshinmon-Oji to Kumano Hongu Taisha** (about 7 km, 3 hours). It's a gentle route with charming stone paths, so even hiking novices can enjoy it with peace of mind.

**Q3. What is there at Oyunohara?**

A. It's a vast sacred area where Japan's largest torii gate (33.9m high) stands on the site of the original shrine. There are no shrine buildings, but it's said that the energy of this land, where Kumano Hongu Taisha originally stood, is truly special. It's about a 10-minute walk from Hongu Taisha.

**Q4. What does 'Land of Rebirth' mean?**

A. In Kumano faith, it is believed that "the soul is reborn anew after completing the Kumano pilgrimage," and it's positioned as a sacred place where sins, impurities, and sufferings are reset. Traditionally, people visit this place at "life's turning points when they wish to let go of the past."

**Q5. How many types of Goshuin are there?**

A. The standard goshuin features a Yatagarasu stamp. You can receive goshuin at Kumano Hongu Taisha, Oyunohara, and Udasha. Collecting all three completes your Hongu pilgrimage!
