## Introduction

Labradorite is a relatively new stone, discovered by missionaries in 1770 on the Labrador Peninsula in eastern Canada. It exhibits a unique phenomenon called **labradorescence**, where iridescent flashes of light shimmer as the angle of light changes. This is said to be caused by the interference of light within its thin layered structure.

Inuit legends tell that long ago, the aurora borealis was trapped within rocks. A brave warrior struck the rocks with a spear, releasing the aurora into the night sky, but some light remained, becoming Labradorite. This stone, with its mystical discovery tale, is still regarded today as the "**stone that encapsulates the aurora of the far north**," a prime symbol of spirituality and intuition.

## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Natural stones naturally exhibit color variations, subtle inclusions, and small cracks.
-   If the transparency and color are too uniform, it might be glass, an artificial stone, or a dyed stone.
-   Stones labeled "Rainbow" with excessively strong iridescence might be synthetic.
-   The more popular a stone like Labradorite is, the more likely fakes are to circulate. Purchasing with a certification from a trusted specialty store offers peace of mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. What is the difference between Labradorite and Moonstone?**
A. Both are feldspar-group minerals and appear similar, but Labradorite is characterized by its iridescent flashes called "labradorescence," while Moonstone exhibits a soft, white to blue glow called "adularescence" or "schiller." In terms of properties, Labradorite is associated more with intuition and psychic abilities, while Moonstone is linked to lunar rhythms and feminine energy.

**Q. At angles where the light isn't visible, it just looks like a plain stone...?**
A. Labradorite is unique in that it only shows its flash in specific directions relative to the angle of light incidence. This is part of its charm! Try tilting it slowly in your palm to find the angle where the iridescent colors appear.

**Q. Is it okay to wear it when I sleep?**
A. Traditionally, it's recommended to remove it before sleeping and place it on your bedside table. Since it corresponds to the Third Eye, sensitive individuals might find their dreams become too vivid.

**Q. Is it okay for children to carry it?**
A. Carrying it as a charm is generally fine, but please be careful about strong impacts, as it has cleavage and is prone to breaking.
