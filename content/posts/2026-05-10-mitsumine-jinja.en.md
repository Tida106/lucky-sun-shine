## Introduction

Mitsumine Shrine, nestled in the Chichibu mountains at an elevation of about 1,100 meters, stands as one of Kanto's foremost power spots. It's renowned for its unique **Mitsu-Torii (Triple Torii) gate**, the awe-inspiring sea of clouds viewed from its heights, and its special **'Oin-sama' (sacred wolf) worship**. Legend has it that its origins trace back to Prince Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto, who enshrined Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto during his eastern expedition. Today, it remains a sacred site of mountain worship, drawing numerous visitors even on weekdays, all eager to bask in the **powerful spiritual energy ('Ki')** unique to the Kanto region. In this article, we've compiled everything you need to know, from its **history, Oin-sama worship, and the Mitsu-Torii gate, to FAQs and an editor's note**, all on one page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

According to shrine tradition, the shrine's origins trace back to **Prince Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto**. During his eastern expedition, upon crossing the Usui Pass and arriving in Chichibu, he gazed upon the three peaks of Mt. Shiraiwa, Mt. Myohogake, and Mt. Kumotori. Recalling the creation myth of Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto, he enshrined these two deities there. The name "Mitsumine" (Three Peaks) is derived from this very event.

From the medieval period onwards, it flourished as a sacred site for **Shugendo** (mountain asceticism), becoming a training ground for ascetics. Legends tell of **wolves guiding and protecting** monks who trained in the mountains, leading to the veneration of wolves as divine messengers (Kenzoku). During the Edo period, the "Mitsumine-ko" (Mitsumine faith association) spread nationwide, and 'Oin-sama' amulets became widely recognized throughout the Kanto region as the most powerful talismans for **protection against theft, fire, and evil spirits**.

> Source: Mitsumine Shrine official documents and various Chichibu regional historical materials.

## <span id="okuinusama"></span>About Oin-sama (Sacred Wolf) Worship

The most distinctive feature of Mitsumine Shrine is that its divine messengers are **wolves, known as Oin-sama**. Throughout the shrine grounds, you'll find **wolf guardian statues (Komainu)** – the open-mouthed 'Agyo' and closed-mouthed 'Ungyo' figures.

It is believed that enshrining an 'Oin-sama' amulet at home **wards off theft, fire, and evil possessions**, a belief that has garnered fervent devotion throughout the Kanto region since the Edo period. The ritual of "**Gokenzoku Haishaku**," where worshippers "borrow" Oin-sama as a divine attendant, is a unique prayer service at Mitsumine Shrine that continues to see endless applications even today.

> **Note**: The "efficacy" of amulets and charms holds religious and cultural significance.

## <span id="highlight"></span>The Mitsu-Torii Gate and Highlights of the Grounds

-   **Mitsu-Torii (Triple Torii Gate)** — A rare form featuring a central torii gate flanked by two smaller ones. Only a few shrines nationwide possess this unique design.
-   **Zuishin-mon Gate** — An impressive vermillion-lacquered gate.
-   **Main Hall and Worship Hall (Honden and Haiden)** — Built in Kansei 12 (1800), featuring richly colored carvings.
-   **Sea of Clouds from the Yoai-den (Distant Worship Hall)** — A spectacular viewpoint where a sea of clouds often spreads out in the early morning.
-   **Sacred Tree (Goshinboku)** — A large cedar tree in front of the worship hall, where visitors can touch it to receive spiritual energy.
-   **Kounkaku** — A shukubo (temple lodging) and hot spring facility.

## <span id="okumiya"></span>About the Okumiya (Inner Shrine)

The Okumiya (Inner Shrine), located about an hour's walk from the main shrine at the **summit of Mt. Myohogake (1,329m elevation)**, requires proper hiking gear for a full-fledged mountain pilgrimage. It's recommended for those in good physical condition, as it involves chains and steep ascents. However, the breathtaking panoramic views of the Chichibu mountain range from the summit's small shrine and the profound tranquility not found at the main shrine are said to be truly exceptional.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Visiting Tips

-   Don't forget to bow once before passing through the Mitsu-Torii Gate.
-   Many visitors take a deep breath in front of the sacred tree to receive its spiritual energy.
-   The "**Kimamori**" amulet is now offered in its regular colors, following the discontinuation of the special white Kimamori (the white Kimamori ceased distribution after 2018).
-   For Okumiya worship, bring hiking gear. Be cautious of snow and ice during winter.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train: From Seibu Chichibu Line "**Seibu Chichibu Station**" or Chichibu Railway "**Mitsumineguchi Station**," take a Seibu Kanko Bus for approximately 75 minutes and alight at "Mitsumine Shrine."

Car: Approximately 2 hours from "Hanazono Interchange" on the Kan-Etsu Expressway. Drive carefully as there are continuous mountain curves.

Parking: Paid parking available (approx. 300 spaces for standard cars).

## <span id="season"></span>Best Time to Visit

**Spring to Autumn** is recommended. Especially if you're aiming for the **sea of clouds in the early morning**, visit on a chilly morning in spring or autumn. During winter, be cautious when driving on mountain roads due to snow and ice. The scenery is particularly beautiful during the fresh green season (May) and autumn foliage (late October to November).

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1.  Bow once before passing through the Mitsu-Torii Gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities; walk along the edges.
3.  At the chozuya (water purification pavilion), purify your left hand, then your right hand, then your mouth, then your left hand again, and finally rinse the dipper.
4.  Two bows, two claps, one bow.
5.  When touching the sacred tree, do so quietly and do not linger.
6.  Take your trash with you and avoid speaking loudly in the mountains.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is the white Kimamori amulet still available?**

A. The **white Kimamori amulet**, which used to be distributed only on the first day of each month, was discontinued after June 2018 as a measure to mitigate traffic congestion on surrounding roads. Currently, the **Kimamori** in its regular colors is available year-round.

**Q2. How should I enshrine the Oin-sama amulet?**

A. It's generally enshrined in a high place in your home (on a kamidana, if you have one), facing east or south. Amulets received through "**Gokenzoku Haishaku**" should be returned after one year and a new one borrowed.

**Q3. How long does it take to reach the Okumiya?**

A. From the main shrine to the **Okumiya on Mt. Myohogake summit**, it takes approximately **1 to 1.5 hours one way** on foot. Hiking gear (trekking shoes, rain gear, water, headlamp) is essential due to chain sections and steep ascents.

**Q4. Is staying at Kounkaku recommended?**

A. Mitsumine Shrine's shukubo, **Kounkaku**, includes hot springs and allows for early morning worship. Staying overnight is best if you're aiming to see the sea of clouds. It's a precious opportunity to experience the crisp morning air of the shrine.

**Q5. Can I visit during winter?**

A. The shrine itself is open for worship year-round, but be cautious of snow and ice due to its **1,100m elevation**. Studless tires are essential, and it's safer to refrain from visiting the Okumiya during winter.
