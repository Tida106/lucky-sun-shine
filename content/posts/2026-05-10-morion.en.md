## Introduction

Morion is a type of quartz so deeply black that it's almost opaque to light, even darker than smoky quartz. Authentic morion is formed when natural radiation from the Earth's crust slowly transforms the crystal structure of quartz over long periods, staining it jet black. It's a relatively rare find.

In ancient China, it was known as "**Black Dragon Stone**" or "**Stone of Xuanwu**" and was used in Feng Shui to ward off misfortune by placing it in weak directions of a home. In Japan, it has rapidly gained recognition as the "**strongest protection stone**" in recent years and is strongly supported by people who feel intense negativity related to land, buildings, or relationships. A slight reddish-brown transparency, barely visible in its deep blackness, is sometimes considered proof of genuine morion.

## Stone Words and Meaning

Morion (Black Quartz) is said to be a stone symbolizing "**the strongest evil repellent, spiritual guardian, and protection**". As a jet-black quartz praised as the "strongest protection stone," it has been cherished worldwide for a long time.

Ancient Morion artifacts have been unearthed from Celtic ruins, and in Europe, it's said to have been used by shamans in rituals as the "**Necromancer's Stone**" or "**Stone for Speaking with the Dead**". In contrast, in China, Taiwan, and Tibet, it's traditionally believed to have been used as a boundary stone for temples, serving to seal off evil spirits and negative energies.

In modern Japan, its uses have expanded to include placing it in the four corners of a new home if one feels a connection to past land issues, carrying it as an amulet during times of intense jealousy in relationships, or wearing it for protection by individuals with strong spiritual sensitivities. It's sometimes described as "**the last stronghold when facing negativity that no other stone can match**".

## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Natural stones typically have natural variations in color, subtle inclusions, and small fissures.
-   If the transparency and color are too uniform, it might be glass, an artificial stone, or a dyed stone.
-   When held up to strong light, genuine natural morion often shows a slight reddish-brown to dark brown transparency. A stone that is completely opaque and jet black might be glass or artificially irradiated.
-   Morion (Black Quartz) is a popular stone, making it more prone to fakes. Purchasing from a reputable specialized store with an authenticity certificate is recommended for peace of mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. What is the difference between Morion and Smoky Quartz?**
A. Both are colored quartz mineralogically, but while smoky quartz has a translucent, smoky hue, morion possesses a deep, almost jet-black intensity. The general distinction is that stones so deeply colored they are almost opaque to light are called morion.

**Q. I feel my body becoming heavy when I wear Morion.**
A. As it is a stone that offers strong protection and grounding, sensitive individuals might feel a sense of heaviness. If the discomfort is strong, it's recommended to switch to placing it by your bedside or on your desk, and to wear it for shorter durations.

**Q. How many Morion stones are needed if placed at the entrance?**
A. In Feng Shui, it's often recommended to place one in each of the four corners of a room, or four in total for the entire house. Some schools of thought also suggest that even a single rough stone placed on the floor at the entrance is sufficient.

**Q. How often should I purify it?**
A. Since it's believed to absorb negative energies, it's generally recommended to purify it with clear quartz chips or sage at least once a week, if possible.
