## Introduction

Munakata Taisha Shrine, located in the Munakata region of northern Fukuoka Prefecture, is revered as the **guardian deity of maritime traffic**. Since ancient times, it has been an esteemed old shrine, attracting the worship of the imperial court since the Yamato sovereignty era, as a site for national rituals praying for **safe trade with the continent**. It comprises three shrines: **Hetsugu (on the mainland), Nakatsugu (on Oshima Island), and Okitsugu (on Okinoshima Island)**. Okinoshima is a sacred island, a special divine realm known as the **"Island Where Gods Dwell,"** where general entry is strictly forbidden. In 2017, it was registered as a World Cultural Heritage site as "**Okinoshima Island and Related Sites in the Munakata Region, an Island Where Gods Dwell**." This article brings together information on its **history, the Three Munakata Goddesses, the three shrines, the Shinpokan (Sacred Treasure Museum), FAQs, and an editor's note** all on one page for you!

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

Munakata Taisha is one of Japan's oldest shrines, documented in the 'Kojiki' and 'Nihon Shoki' (ancient historical texts). Its origin is said to be when the **Three Goddesses**, born from the sacred pledge (Ukei) between **Amaterasu Omikami and Susanoo-no-Mikoto**, were commanded by Amaterasu Omikami to protect the "**Ummi no Nakka-michi**" (sea route to the continent) and descended to the land of Munakata.

From ancient times, it was highly valued as a site for national rituals praying for the safety of **diplomacy and trade with the continent and the Korean Peninsula**. From the **4th to the 9th century**, **large-scale rituals** were conducted on Okinoshima, led by the imperial court. The 80,000 ritual artifacts unearthed (many designated as National Treasures) are known as the "**Shosoin of the Sea**," valuable heritage that tells the story of international exchange during that period.

In July 2017, it was registered as a **UNESCO World Cultural Heritage** site. The strict taboos of Okinoshima (women are forbidden and general entry is prohibited) and the excellent preservation of the ritual artifacts were recognized as a globally unparalleled marine ritual culture.

## <span id="sanjoshin"></span>About the Three Munakata Goddesses

The **Three Munakata Goddesses** enshrined at Munakata Taisha are each considered guardian deities of different sea areas.

| Goddess | Location Enshrined | Symbol or Other Name |
|---|---|---|
| **Tagorihime-no-kami** | Okitsugu Shrine (Okinoshima Island) | Offshore sea routes, eldest daughter |
| **Tagitsuhime-no-kami** | Nakatsugu Shrine (Oshima Island) | Middle sea routes, second daughter |
| **Ichikishimahime-no-kami** | Hetsugu Shrine (mainland) | Coastal guardian, youngest daughter, syncretized with Benzaiten |

**Ichikishimahime-no-kami** was syncretized with **Benzaiten** after the introduction of Buddhism and is also enshrined as the main deity in **Itsukushima Shrine (Miyajima, Hiroshima, and others)** across Japan.

## <span id="okinoshima"></span>Okinoshima, the Island Where Gods Dwell, and World Heritage

**Okinoshima** is an **island about 4 km in circumference** floating in the Genkai Sea, approximately 60 km from the mainland, and the **island itself is considered a sacred deity**. Since ancient times, it has been known as "**Oiwazusama**" (the Silent One), and strict taboos are still observed today: absolutely nothing seen or heard on the island must be spoken about outside, **women are forbidden**, and **general entry is prohibited**.

Even after its World Heritage designation in 2017, **entry for tourism purposes is absolutely forbidden**, and with the exception of rare instances once every few decades, no one other than Shinto priests is permitted to land. Worshiping remotely across the sea from the **Okitsugu Remote Worship Site** on Oshima Island is the only method available to general visitors.

## <span id="houmotsukan"></span>Shinpokan (The Shosoin of the Sea)

The **Shinpokan (Sacred Treasure Museum)** within the Hetsugu Shrine grounds houses and displays **approximately 80,000 ritual artifacts** unearthed from Okinoshima. Among these, **about 80,000 items are designated National Treasures**, a concentration of National Treasures at a single shrine that is rare throughout Japan.

Gold rings, Persian glass vessels, Tang Dynasty sancai pottery, Nara period mirrors, and other evidence of international exchange from the **4th to the 9th century** are all gathered here. This is why it is called the "**Shosoin of the Sea**," and it is one of the core reasons for its World Heritage designation.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Points for Your Visit

-   After visiting **Hetsugu Shrine**, explore the national treasure ritual artifacts at the **Shinpokan**.
-   Stand on the ancient ritual ground at **Takamiya Saijo**.
-   If possible, take a ferry to Oshima Island and visit **Nakatsugu Shrine and the Okitsugu Remote Worship Site**.
-   **Okinoshima Island is inaccessible**, so worship remotely from Oshima Island.
-   It receives particularly deep reverence from the **aviation, shipping, and automotive industries**.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train and bus: From JR Kagoshima Main Line's "**Togo Station**," take a Nishitetsu bus for about 12-15 minutes and get off at "Munakata Taisha-mae."

Car: Approximately 20 minutes from Kyushu Expressway's "**Koga Interchange**" or about 30 minutes from Wakamiya Interchange.

**Ferry to Oshima Island**: Approximately **25 minutes by municipal ferry (or about 15 minutes by passenger boat)** from Konominato Port. 6-7 services per day.

**Parking**: Free parking available at Hetsugu Shrine (approx. 800 spaces).

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Seasons and Times

Open for visits year-round. **Spring to autumn** offers mild weather and is suitable for trips to Oshima Island. During **winter, the Genkai Sea** can be rough, posing a risk of ferry cancellations during stormy weather, so if you plan to visit Oshima Island, it's safer to choose a clear day. **Early mornings** have fewer visitors, allowing you to explore the Shinpokan at a leisurely pace.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Pilgrimage Etiquette

1.  Bow before passing through the torii gate.
2.  The center of the approach is for the deities, so walk along the sides.
3.  At the chozuya (water purification pavilion), purify your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, clean your left hand again, and finally cleanse the dipper.
4.  **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once.**
5.  Photography is prohibited inside the Shinpokan, and please refrain from loud conversations.
6.  Understanding and respect for Okinoshima's taboos (women are forbidden and general entry is prohibited).

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is entry to Okinoshima absolutely forbidden?**

A. Yes, **general entry is completely forbidden**, and even after its World Heritage designation, tourist visits are not permitted. Participation by the general public in the annual ritual by Shinto priests (the **Local Grand Festival** on May 27) was also abolished after 2018. Worshipping remotely from the **Okitsugu Remote Worship Site** on Oshima Island is the only method.

**Q2. Do I need to book the Oshima ferry?**

A. Reservations are not usually required for passenger services, but **if you are taking a vehicle, reservations are essential**. It can get crowded during tourist seasons and weekends, so please check the timetable for the municipal ferries "**Oshima**" and "**Shiokaze**" in advance.

**Q3. What is the admission fee for the Shinpokan?**

A. **Adults 800 yen, high school and university students 500 yen, elementary and junior high school students 400 yen** (as of 2026). It's a precious opportunity to see the collection of National Treasures unearthed from Okinoshima up close.

**Q4. What is Takamiya Saijo?**

A. It's an **ancient ritual site** located at the back of the Hetsugu Shrine grounds, an **Iwasaka (sacred rock enclosure)** believed to be the spot where Ichikishimahime-no-kami descended. It's a sacred area that preserves the original form of rituals even older than the main shrine building, with few visitors and a tranquil atmosphere.

**Q5. Are Benzaiten and Ichikishimahime-no-kami the same?**

A. After the introduction of Buddhism, **Ichikishimahime-no-kami** was syncretized with **Benzaiten** (regarded as a single deity incorporating both Shinto and Buddhist elements). Many Benzaiten cults throughout Japan enshrine Ichikishimahime-no-kami as their deity, and she has a sisterly relationship with **Enoshima Shrine and Itsukushima Shrine**.
