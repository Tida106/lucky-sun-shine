## Introduction

Obsidian is a **natural glass** formed from rapidly cooled volcanic lava. In ancient Maya and Aztec civilizations, it was revered as a sacred "**Smoking Mirror**" used by priests for divination. During Japan's Jomon period, arrowheads and blades crafted from obsidian were widely traded. With its sharp fracture and jet-black luster, Obsidian holds a unique presence as **"the stone that cuts through truth" and "the mirror that reflects the past."** This article summarizes its **mineral data, historical background, commonly attributed effects, FAQs, and an editor's note.**

Obsidian is an **amorphous natural glass without a crystal structure**, and it fractures cleanly like glass with a conchoidal break. Japan's Wada Pass, Kozushima Island, and Hokkaido are **among the leading producing areas in Japan**, and archaeological evidence confirms traces of trade by Jomon people.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Language and Historical and Cultural Background

Its stone language includes "**Truth, Protection from Negative Energy, and Release of the Past**."

In ancient Aztec civilization, obsidian mirrors were symbols of "**Tezcatlipoca, the Smoking Mirror god,**" and priests used the images reflected in them for divination. In Mayan civilization, it was treated as a sacred tool, a ceremonial knife called "**Chac Mool,**" used to touch the essence of life. The obsidian mirror used by John Dee (Queen Elizabeth I's magician in the 16th century) is still on display at the British Museum today. In Japan, obsidian has been referred to as "**a fragment of the night sky**" since ancient times, and it has been spoken of as a stone for warding off negative energy.

> Source: GIA Obsidian Reference, various archaeological and anthropological documents.

**Q1. Can Obsidian feel "heavy"?**

**Q2. What is the difference between Obsidian and Onyx?**

A. **Onyx is a crystalline chalcedony, while Obsidian is an amorphous natural glass.** Although their colors are similar, they are entirely different mineralogically. Obsidian is more prone to fracturing and is strongly associated with introspection and the release of the past.

**Q3. Is the rainbow in Rainbow Obsidian real?**

A. Yes, it is. It's a natural iridescence caused by **light interference** from microscopic air bubbles or thin layers of minerals. Its mystical appearance, where a rainbow emerges depending on the angle of light, is said to symbolize gently illuminating past pains.

**Q4. Do Japanese-produced obsidian stones have special meaning?**

A. Obsidian from Hokkaido, Nagano, and Kozushima Island holds historical value as **evidence of trade during the Jomon period.** Many people report feeling "energy connected to ancient Japan" when carrying it as an amulet.
