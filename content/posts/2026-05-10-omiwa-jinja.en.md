## Introduction

Omiwa Shrine (pronounced O-miwa Jinja) is one of Japan's oldest shrines, unique for having **Mount Miwa (Miwa-yama) itself as its sacred body**. It has no main sanctuary (honden) and has preserved an **ancient form of Shinto worship** for over 1500 years, where visitors directly revere Mount Miwa from behind the worship hall (haiden). **Omononushi-no-Okami**, who appears in the ancient chronicles *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*, is enshrined as the principal deity. The shrine has been revered as a deity of sake brewing, matchmaking, and medicine, making it a hidden yet powerful spiritual spot in Nara. This article covers **Mount Miwa worship, its history, methods for sacred mountain ascent, FAQs, and an editor's note**, all in one convenient guide.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

The ancient chronicles *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* tell us that **Omononushi-no-Okami** appeared from beyond the sea to help Okuninushi-no-Kami build the nation, and wished to **enshrine himself on Mount Miwa**. During the reign of Emperor Sujin (from 97 BCE), **Otataneko** was appointed as the chief priest to appease Omononushi's spirit, which is considered the origin of the shrine's current rituals.

Omiwa Shrine, often referred to as **"the shrine of shrines"** or **"Japan's oldest shrine,"** maintains an ancient form of worship where no sanctuary is built, and **Mount Miwa itself is revered as the sacred body from afar**. This practice represents the most primordial aspect of Shinto. Under the name Miwa Myojin, the shrine has long been revered as the supreme guardian of sake brewing, agriculture, and medicine.

> Source: Omiwa Shrine Official Documents, *Kojiki*, *Nihon Shoki*, and various ethnological materials.

## <span id="miwayama"></span>Characteristics of Mount Miwa Worship

- **No Main Sanctuary (Honden)** — Worshipers directly revere Mount Miwa from behind the worship hall.
- **The Entire Mount Miwa is the Sacred Body** — All trees, rocks, and water within the mountain are considered sacred ground.
- **Forbidden Area Before the Meiji Era** — General hiking was strictly prohibited.
- **Currently, "Tohhai" (Sacred Mountain Ascent) is permitted** — After registering at Sai Shrine, you enter the mountain wearing a white sash (tasuki).

## <span id="highlight"></span>Highlights and Must-Sees

- **Worship Hall (Haiden)** (Important Cultural Property) — Constructed in Kanbun 4 (1664) by Tokugawa Ietsuna.
- **Miwa Torii** (Important Cultural Property) — A rare form consisting of three torii gates joined together.
- **Mi-no-Kami Sugi (Sacred Snake Cedar)** — A sacred tree where a snake (mi), an incarnation of Omononushi-no-Okami, is said to reside.
- **Nade Usagi (Petting Rabbit)** — A charm for recovery from illness.
- **Sai Shrine (Medicinal Well)** — Sacred water believed to be effective against all ailments.

## <span id="climb"></span>How to Ascend Mount Miwa (Tohhai)

Mount Miwa is accessible for **Tohhai (sacred mountain ascent)** after registering **at Sai Shrine** (it is a religious ritual, not general mountaineering).

- **Registration Hours**: 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM (Descent by 4:00 PM)
- **Entry Fee**: 300 yen
- **What to Bring**: A white sash (tasuki, provided at registration), water, hiking boots.
- **Time Required**: 2 to 3 hours round trip.
- **Photography, eating, drinking, and private conversation are prohibited** — This is strictly for worship.
- **Mount Miwa itself is a forbidden area**, not treated as a general hiking trail.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Tips for Your Visit

- Worship at Omiwa Shrine's main worship hall, then get sacred water from Sai Shrine's Medicinal Well.
- If you plan to ascend the mountain, **comfortable clothing and hiking boots** are essential.
- It's customary to **offer eggs to the Mi-no-Kami Sugi** (offerings available within the shrine grounds).
- The formal approach is to walk along the approach path starting from the **Grand Torii (32m high)**.
- Sake brewers have a tradition of taking home **sugidama (cedar balls, indicating freshly brewed sake)**.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Worship Etiquette

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate or main temple gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities; walk along the sides.
3.  At the chozuya (purification font), purify your left hand, then right hand, then mouth, then left hand again, and finally the ladle.
4.  **Two bows, two claps, one bow** (the standard Shinto worship procedure).
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, focus on gratitude.
6.  During the sacred ascent of Mount Miwa, **absolutely no photography, eating, drinking, private conversation, or recording is permitted**.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. I want to try ascending Mount Miwa (Tohhai), is it okay for beginners?**

A. The mountain is 467m high, and the round trip takes 2 to 3 hours. It's considered an intermediate level for general hiking. **Hiking boots, water, and proper physical condition management** are essential, and **entry is prohibited during rain or stormy weather**. Please follow the instructions at the reception and approach it with a mindset of sacred worship.

**Q2. I often hear it referred to as "Japan's oldest shrine," is that true?**

A. The more accurate expression is **"one of Japan's oldest shrines."** It preserves the **ancient form of worship where there is no main sanctuary and the mountain itself is the sacred body**, representing the earliest layer of Shinto before shrine architectural styles were established. Alongside other shrines that also claim to be among Japan's oldest (such as Isonokami Jingu), it conveys the primordial form of Shinto to this day.

**Q3. Why are eggs offered to the Mi-no-Kami Sugi?**

A. The *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* relate that Omononushi-no-Okami **appears in the form of a white snake (mi)**. It's an ancient custom to offer **eggs and sake**, which are favored by snakes, at the foot of the Mi-no-Kami Sugi.

**Q4. What is the origin of the Nade Usagi (Petting Rabbit)?**

A. The **Petting Rabbit statue** in front of the worship hall originates from the "Usagi no Hi Mairi" (Rabbit Day Pilgrimage) held at Omiwa Shrine. It's a very popular statue among visitors, believed to have **divine efficacy for recovery from illness if you pet the same part of the statue as your ailment**.

**Q5. What about the deity of sake?**

A. Omononushi-no-Okami is the **guardian deity of sake brewing**. Throughout the shrine grounds, you'll see **sugidama (cedar balls, signifying freshly brewed sake)** donated by sake breweries from all over Japan. Every November, sake breweries nationwide hold their **"Sake Brewing Safety Prayer Festival"** at Omiwa Shrine.
