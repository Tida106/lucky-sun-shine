## Introduction

Peridot is a gemstone known for its vibrant yellowish-green sparkle. In ancient Egypt, it was mined on St. John's Island in the Red Sea and was known as the **"Gem of the Sun."** It's also famous for Cleopatra's beloved collection, which she believed to be emeralds, but later research revealed many were actually peridot. It's cherished as the August birthstone and the commemorative stone for 16th wedding anniversaries. ## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Lore and Historical/Cultural Background

Its stone lore includes "**Sun, Hope, and Marital Harmony.**"

Mining on St. John's Island (present-day Zabargad Island) in ancient Egypt began around 1500 BCE, and peridot was widely used in **pharaohs' adornments.** In medieval Europe, peridot brought back by the Crusaders was often mistaken for emeralds. Modern research has revealed that many of the green gems adorning the **"Shrine of the Three Kings"** in Cologne Cathedral were, in fact, peridot. Known for its vivid yellowish-green color, which led to it being described as **"shining even at night,"** it's spoken of as a stone of hope and positivity.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Difference from Emerald?**

A. Emeralds are blue-green beryl, while peridots are yellowish-green olivine. The main difference is that **"peridot is a bright green with yellow undertones, and emerald is a deep green with blue undertones."** Many of Cleopatra's collection, which she referred to as "emeralds," were actually identified as peridot by modern appraisal.

**Q2. What is meteorite-origin peridot?**

A. Some peridot crystals are of cosmic origin, found within **pallasite meteorites (stony-iron meteorites).** They are rare and expensive but popular among enthusiasts as stones embodying the romance of space.

**Q3. I heard it's good as a marital charm.**

A. Peridot has long been known as the "**Stone of Marital Harmony**," symbolizing support for sincere relationships by warding off jealousy and doubt. As it's also the commemorative stone for 16th wedding anniversaries, it makes an excellent gift for significant milestones.

**Q4. Is it effective against rivals or jealousy?**

A. Peridot is described as the **"stone that cultivates your inner core,"** symbolizing a way to soften the habit of becoming negative by comparing yourself to others. It's not a stone for competing with others, but rather one that encourages you to **focus on your own path.**

**Q5. Are there other stones for hope and positivity?**
