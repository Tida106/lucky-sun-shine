## Introduction

Pyrite is a crystal that's affectionately known as "**Fool's Gold**"! Why? Well, during the Gold Rush, its dazzling appearance so closely mimicked real gold that prospectors often mistook it and happily carried it home, only to realize their 'gold' was actually pyrite! Its scientific name actually comes from the Greek word 'pyr,' meaning 'fire.' And it totally makes sense, because back in ancient Greece and Rome, people used to strike it with a hammer to create sparks! So, it was a practical **flint stone** for everyday life.

You know, when pyrite grows naturally into perfect cubes or dodecahedrons, they're so incredibly precise that they look like they've been carved by human hands! They're definitely show-stoppers in any museum mineral display. And get this! In the Inca Empire, they even polished pyrite into flat plates and used them as **priestly mirrors**, believing they could reflect the future or even the spirit world! So, it's been cherished since ancient times as a symbol of both wealth and wisdom.

## How to Spot Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Natural stones naturally have color variations and tiny inclusions or cracks – that's their unique charm!
-   If the transparency and color are too uniform, it might be glass, an artificial stone, or a dyed stone.
-   Brass fakes sometimes circulate cheaply, marketed as "pyripite" or "cluster-style." You can often tell by checking its weight, magnetism, and the precision of its cube faces.
-   The more popular a stone like Pyrite (Iron Pyrite) is, the more likely you are to find fakes. So, for peace of mind, it's always safest to buy from trusted specialty stores that offer appraisal certificates!

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. How to distinguish between Pyrite and Gold?**
A. Pyrite is much harder and will actually break and sparkle when you hit it with a hammer (while gold is soft and just bends!). As for color, real gold has a strong, warm yellow hue, but pyrite has more of a slightly greenish, brassy tone. Also, pyrite is super reflective and will give off sharp flashes of light when you change its angle!

**Q. It has turned black and rusted. Can I restore it?**
A. Oh no! Once the surface sulfidation starts, it can't be fully reversed. The most important thing is to wipe it gently with a dry cloth as soon as possible and store it in a spot with low humidity. Sometimes, a soft cloth with a tiny bit of baking soda can help bring back some of its shine!

**Q. Can I keep it in my wallet?**
A. It's a super popular way to keep it as a financial luck charm! But because there's a risk of it rusting from sweat and humidity, I totally recommend putting it in a silk or thick little pouch, and then placing it in the driest part of your wallet.

**Q. Can I place it in the bathroom or near water?**
A. Oh, absolutely not! This stone will definitely rust with water and humidity, so please don't put it in the bathroom, washroom, or kitchen. The golden rule is to keep it in dry spots like your living room or study.
