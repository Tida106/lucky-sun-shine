## Introduction

Sodalite is a sodium-rich silicate mineral discovered in 1811 within Greenland's **Ilimaussaq Complex**. For a while after its discovery, it was treated mainly as an academic sample. However, in 1891, a massive deposit was found in Ontario, Canada, and it's said that the stone captivated **Princess Louise (British royal princess)**, who was visiting Canada at the time, leading to its rapid surge in popularity among European courts.

Its deep navy blue base, interwoven with striking white calcite streaks, creates a unique appearance reminiscent of a night sky. It's well-known for often being confused with Lapis Lazuli. Because it gained prominence after mineralogy was established as a science in the 19th century, Sodalite is now referred to as the "**Stone of Thought**" and the "**Scholar's Stone.**" It's cherished by researchers, students, and professionals for its ability to support logic, intellect, and calmness.

## Stone Properties and Meaning

Sodalite is said to symbolize "**Logic, Academia, and Conviction.**" As the "Stone of Thought and Conviction" ideal for a scholar's desk, it has been beloved worldwide for a long time.

Sodalite is also known as "**Princess Blue.**" This name originates from an anecdote that in 1891, when Princess Louise visited Canada, she was deeply moved by the beauty of the Sodalite deposit in Bancroft, Ontario. She brought a large quantity of raw stones back to England and used them to decorate her palace.

In a spiritual context, the blue wavelength is believed to correspond simultaneously with the "**Throat Chakra (communication)**" and the "**Third Eye Chakra (intuition and thought).**" It has therefore been positioned as a stone symbolizing "**the ability to organize thoughts and express them clearly.**" It is a stone long supported by those in professions that involve "thinking and conveying," such as students preparing for exams, business professionals who give many presentations, researchers, and writers.

## How to Identify Fakes and Artificial Stones

-   Natural stones inherently display color variations, minor inclusions, and cracks. This is their natural state.
-   If the transparency or color saturation is too uniform, it might be glass, an artificial stone, or a dyed stone.
-   Sodalite is sometimes sold as a substitute for Lapis Lazuli, so check for white streaks and the absence of golden flecks (pyrite).
-   The more popular the stone, the more likely counterfeits are to appear. Purchasing from a trusted specialty store with a certificate of authenticity offers peace of mind.
