## Introduction

Sugilite is a relatively new purple mineral discovered in 1944 by Japanese geologist **Ken-ichi Sugi** on Iwagi Island, Ochi District, Ehime Prefecture (Seto Inland Sea), Japan. It was named "**Sugilite**" after its discoverer. This makes it a rare stone, as a mineral discovered in Japan later became one of the World's Three Great Healing Stones (alongside [Larimar](/blog/larimar/) and [Charoite](/blog/charoite/)), loved by people all over the world.

However, it only gained widespread recognition as a gemstone after large, deep purple deposits were discovered in the **Wessels Mine, South Africa**, in the 1970s. Japanese-sourced sugilite typically has a lighter color and is considered a rare specimen; the majority of sugilite on the market today comes from South Africa. Its long-standing support from healers and therapists worldwide as a "**stone of deep healing**" is also attributed to its purple hue, a color culturally associated with "**nobility, spirituality, and sacredness**" since ancient times.

## Symbolism and Meaning

Sugilite is said to be a stone that symbolizes "**purple healing, spirituality, and protection from negative energy**." Counted as one of the World's Three Great Healing Stones, it has been revered in the healing community, especially for its ability to offer "**healing that reaches the depths of the heart**" and for possessing "**energies of love and compassion**."

The color purple, known as "**royal purple**," was reserved for emperors in ancient Rome, and has symbolized suffering and redemption in Christianity, and wisdom and compassion in Buddhism. Sugilite's deep purple color carries on this symbolic tradition, sometimes described as a stone that embodies "**sacredness on Earth**."

Furthermore, its reputation in the global healing industry surged in the latter half of the 20th century after world-renowned healer Melody referred to Sugilite as an "**emissary of love**" in her writings. Many enthusiasts feel a sense of fateful connection in the very story of a stone named after a Japanese geologist being called a "stone of love" and circulated on the other side of the world.

## How to Spot Fakes and Synthetics

- Natural stones naturally exhibit color variations, subtle inclusions, and small fissures.
- If the transparency or color saturation is too uniform, it might be glass, a synthetic stone, or a dyed stone.
- Since Sugilite is rare and expensive, dyed agate or glass are sometimes sold as "Sugilite."
- Purchasing with an authenticity certificate and from a reputable specialty store offers peace of mind. For more details, refer to [How to Spot Fake and Synthetic Power Stones](/blog/spot-fake-stones/).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Why is Sugilite so expensive?**
A. It is attributed to the limited dark purple veins in South Africa's Wessels Mine and the naturally low yield. It is said that supply cannot keep up with demand, leading to a yearly increase in its market price.

**Q2. What is the difference between Sugilite and Amethyst?**
A. [Amethyst](/blog/amethyst/) is a type of quartz (silicon dioxide) with a translucent purple color, while Sugilite is an opaque to semi-translucent deep purple, making them entirely different minerals. Symbolically, Amethyst is associated with "mental stability and intuition," whereas Sugilite is known for "deep healing and spirituality," indicating distinct properties.

**Q3. Is it okay for non-healers to own Sugilite?**
A. Absolutely, it's considered fine! "Healing stone" doesn't mean it's exclusively for healers, but rather refers to a "stone believed to possess strong healing properties." It is widely cherished as a talisman for daily stress relief.

**Q4. Should I choose a lighter or darker colored Sugilite?**
A. Generally, darker colors are highly valued and more expensive, but for beginners, starting with a lighter lavender hue is also recommended. It is said to offer easy visual healing effects as well.

**Q5. Can I use salt for purification?**
A. With a Mohs hardness of 5.5 to 6.5, which is relatively low, prolonged salt baths may damage the surface luster. Therefore, purification using clear quartz crystal chips or sage is recommended.
