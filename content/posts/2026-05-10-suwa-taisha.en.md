## Introduction

Suwa Grand Shrine is Shinano Province's Ichinomiya (chief shrine of a province), comprising **four distinct shrines: Kamisha Honmiya (Suwa City), Kamisha Maemiya (Chino City), Shimosha Akimiya, and Shimosha Harumiya (Shimosuwa Town)**. These four shrines are spread across Lake Suwa, and the "Suwa Grand Shrine Four Shrines Pilgrimage" (Suwa Taisha Yonsha Mairi), which connects them, has been cherished as a traditional style of worship for centuries. The main enshrined deities are **Take Minakata no Kami** and his consort **Yasaka Tome no Kami**. Legend has it that Take Minakata no Kami, who retreated to Suwa during the Kuniyuzuri Myth (Transfer of the Land Myth) from the Kojiki (Records of Ancient Matters), settled there, governing Suwa and being revered as a deity of valor and agriculture. The **Onbashira Festival, held every seven years**, is a grand festival renowned throughout Japan. In this article, we'll condense everything from its **history and the characteristics of the four shrines to the Onbashira Festival and FAQs** into one comprehensive page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origin

Suwa Grand Shrine is one of Japan's oldest shrines, mentioned in both the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki (Chronicles of Japan). Legend has it that its origin lies in the Kuniyuzuri Myth (Transfer of the Land Myth), where **Take Minakata no Kami**, the son of **Okuninushi no Kami** from Izumo, was defeated in a contest of strength by **Takemikazuchi no Kami** (the main deity of Kasuga Grand Shrine) and retreated to Suwa in Shinano. Since then, he vowed never to leave Suwa and has been enshrined in the sacred area overlooking Lake Suwa.

In the medieval period, it was deeply revered as a deity of war by warlords, with Takeda Shingen dedicating his "Suwa Hosshō Kabuto" (Suwa Buddhist Law Helmet) and showing profound respect. A major characteristic is that it doesn't have a main hall (honden); instead, Kamisha enshrines **Mount Moriya (Moriya-san)** and Shimosha enshrines a **giant ichii (Japanese yew) tree** as their goshintai (sacred objects), preserving an ancient form of worship.

## <span id="fourshrines"></span>Characteristics of Each of the Four Shrines

| Shrine Name | Location | Main Deities | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kamisha Honmiya** | Suwa City, Nakasu Miyama | Take Minakata no Kami | The largest of the four shrines, with the Nunobashi Bridge and Yonkyakumon Gate as highlights. |
| **Kamisha Maemiya** | Chino City, Miyagawa | Yasaka Tome no Kami | The birthplace of Suwa worship, featuring the Omuro-sha shrine and the clear stream of Suiga. |
| **Shimosha Akimiya** | Shimosuwa Town, Takei | Take Minakata no Kami and Yasaka Tome no Kami | Home to the approx. 800-year-old Neiri no Sugi (Root-Sleeping Cedar) and the Kagura-den (Sacred Dance Hall). |
| **Shimosha Harumiya** | Shimosuwa Town, Harumiya | Take Minakata no Kami and Yasaka Tome no Kami | Known for the Manji Stone Buddha and the Gebabashi Bridge. |

At Kamisha and Shimosha, there is a tradition of "Senja-sai" (Sacred Relocation Festival), where the sacred spirits (goreitai) move between the two. It is said that Yasaka Tome no Kami travels to Shimosha in February and to Kamisha in August.

## <span id="onbashira"></span>About the Onbashira Festival

The **Onbashira Festival (Onbashira-sai)** is Suwa Grand Shrine's biggest festival, held once every seven years (in the years of the Tiger and Monkey). It involves manually pulling and erecting 16 giant fir trees, each over 150 years old, from the mountains to the four corners of each shrine's precincts. The "Kiotoshi" (Tree Drop), where participants ride the giant trees down steep slopes, is famous nationwide, and this grand festival brings together approximately 200,000 parishioners working as one. The next festival is scheduled for **2028 (Reiwa 10, the Year of the Monkey)**.

The onbashira pillars are erected at the four corners of each shrine's precincts and serve as a sacred boundary (kekkai) for seven years. Many visitors touch them to gain vitality.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Points for Visiting

-   If possible, it is formal to visit all four shrines with the **"Suwa Grand Shrine Four Shrines Pilgrimage."** You can receive a **"Four Shrines Pilgrimage commemorative item"** at the Juyosho (shrine office for charms).
-   Many worshippers touch the onbashira pillars to receive vitality (please follow shrine office etiquette).
-   The **Onbashira Festival year (Tiger or Monkey)** is a nationally observed event, so expect significant crowds!
-   Goshuin (red seal stamps) are available at each of the four shrines, and dedicated goshuin-cho (stamp books) are also available.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

By car, it's 15-30 minutes from Suwa Interchange on the Chuo Expressway to each shrine. By train, the nearest stations are JR Chuo Line's "**Kamisuwa Station**," "**Shimosuwa Station**," and "**Chino Station**."

**Parking**: Free parking is available at each shrine. Traffic restrictions will be in place around the shrines during the Onbashira Festival year.

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Seasons and Times

You can visit year-round. **Spring to autumn** are ideal for the Four Shrines Pilgrimage due to easier walking conditions, and during the **winter Omiwatari season (January-February)**, you can enjoy the special scenery of Lake Suwa. ## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Shrine Etiquette

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate or main gate.
2.  The center of the path is for the deities; please walk along the sides.
3.  At the Chozuya (water purification pavilion), cleanse your left hand, then right hand, rinse your mouth, cleanse your left hand again, and then rinse the dipper.
4.  **Two Bows, Two Claps, One Bow** (Nihai Nihakushu Ippai - the standard method for Suwa Grand Shrine).
5.  Keep personal wishes modest, focusing primarily on gratitude.
6.  When touching the onbashira pillars, please be considerate not to obstruct other worshippers.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is it possible to visit all four shrines in one day?**

A. Yes, if you have a car, it's possible to visit all four shrines in one day. However, if you're walking or using public transport, it's more realistic to split it over two days. The Kamisha shrines (Honmiya and Maemiya) and Shimosha shrines (Akimiya and Harumiya) are separated by Lake Suwa, so planning your route from Kamisha to Shimosha, or vice versa, will make your visit smoother.

**Q2. When is the Onbashira Festival held?**

A. Once every seven years in the years of the Tiger and Monkey. The next festival is scheduled for **2028 (Reiwa 10, the Year of the Monkey)**. It consists of two parts: "Yamade" (mountain procession) in April and "Satobiki" (village procession) in May, with different dates for Kamisha and Shimosha.

**Q3. Where can I get a Goshuin?**

A. You can get goshuin at the Juyosho (shrine office for charms) of each of the four shrines. **Dedicated Suwa Grand Shrine goshuin-cho (stamp books)** are also available, and a commemorative item is presented upon completing the Four Shrines Pilgrimage.

**Q4. Should I visit Kamisha or Shimosha first?**

A. There are no strict rules, but many visitors choose to tour them in geographical order or follow the traditional flow from Kamisha (the birthplace of Suwa worship) to Shimosha. The order of **Maemiya → Honmiya → Akimiya → Harumiya** is considered traditional.

**Q5. What is the Goshintai (sacred object)?**

A. At Kamisha, **Mount Moriya** is considered the goshintai, and at Shimosha, the sacred **ichii (Japanese yew) tree**. They preserve an ancient form of worship without a main hall (honden). It's a rare shrine that doesn't have a "honden" within its precincts!
