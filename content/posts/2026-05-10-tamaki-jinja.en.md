Tamaki Shrine is an ancient shrine nestled near the summit of Mount Tamaki, at an elevation of **1,076 meters**, in Totsukawa Village, southern Nara Prefecture. It's revered as the **inner sanctuary of Kumano Sanzan**. Because of its challenging access, it has been said since ancient times that **"only those who are called can reach this shrine."** Yet, it continues to draw pilgrims from all over Japan as one of **Japan's most powerful spiritual spots**—a truly rare and sacred place, don't you think? You'll find the awe-inspiring **Jindai Sugi cedar, estimated to be 3,000 years old**, and the **Tamaishi-sha (Iwakura)** at the summit of Mount Tamaki, preserving the ambiance of a primitive sacred site. In this article, I'll cover its **history, the "Called Shrine" legend, the Jindai Sugi cedar, Tamaishi-sha, FAQs, and a special postscript**, all in one convenient guide for you!

## <span id="history"></span>History and its Position as Kumano Sanzan's Inner Sanctuary

Let's learn about the rich history of Tamaki Shrine and why it's considered the inner sanctuary of Kumano Sanzan!

According to shrine tradition, Tamaki Shrine was founded in **37 BCE (the 61st year of Emperor Sujin's reign)**. The legend says that Japan's first emperor, Emperor Jimmu, on his eastern expedition, journeyed up the Totsukawa River and offered sacred jewels on Mount Tamaki, praying for military success. Isn't that fascinating?

From the Heian period onwards, Mount Tamaki flourished as a **sacred site for Shugendo**, a unique Japanese ascetic practice. It became recognized as the **inner sanctuary** for the **three grand shrines of Kumano: Kumano Hongu Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha, and Kumano Nachi Taisha**. It's also renowned as an important lodging point on the **Omine Okugake Trail**, a spiritual path forming the core of Shugendo practitioners' training routes. In **2004, it was registered as a component of the UNESCO World Cultural Heritage site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range."** How amazing is that?

## <span id="yobareru"></span>The Legend of 'Only the Called Can Reach'

Now, let me tell you about the captivating legend: **"Only those who are called can reach this shrine."** This isn't just a metaphor; it truly reflects how challenging it is to access the shrine!

-   The mountain roads feature continuous **hairpin bends where it's difficult for cars to pass each other**.
-   **Public transportation is virtually nonexistent**, so you can only get there by car.
-   During **winter (December to March)**, heavy snow and ice often lead to **road closures**.
-   It's common for people to have to turn back due to **bad weather, feeling unwell, or car troubles**, even if they had planned their trip carefully.

There are countless stories of people who tried to visit but couldn't make it, or those who finally reached it after several attempts! This creates a unique sense of accomplishment and a feeling that **"simply being able to reach it is a blessing"** among those who visit.

## <span id="sugi"></span>Jindai Sugi Cedar and the Giant Trees on the Shrine Grounds

One of the biggest highlights on the shrine grounds is the **"Jindai Sugi" cedar, estimated to be about 3,000 years old**! With a massive trunk circumference of 11 meters and a height of approximately 24 meters, its presence is absolutely overwhelming. Just as its name suggests—meaning "cedar from the age of the gods"—it has been revered as a sacred tree that has watched over people since mythological times. Isn't that incredible?

You'll also find other magnificent giant trees like the **"Meoto Sugi" (Couple Cedar), "Tachiki Sugi" (Standing Cedar), "Iware Sugi," "O Sugi" (Great Cedar), and "Heiwa no Sugi" (Peace Cedar)**, making the entire precinct feel like a **"sanctuary of giant trees."** Several of these trees have even been selected for the **Forestry Agency's "100 Giants of the Forest" list**!

## <span id="tamaishi"></span>Tamaishi-sha (Iwakura at Mount Tamaki's Summit)

After visiting the main shrine, you can hike up the mountain trail for about 20 minutes to reach **"Tamaishi-sha,"** located near the summit of Mount Tamaki. This is an extremely primitive form of worship, where there's no shrine building; instead, **exposed white sacred stones (Iwakura)** are worshipped as the deity itself. It's so ancient, you can truly feel the history!

It's called the **"shrine without a hall, the shrine without a shimenawa rope,"** and is believed to preserve an even older form of worship than the main shrine. It's a tradition for visitors to make sure to walk up to Tamaishi-sha after their main shrine visit.

## <span id="point"></span>Pilgrimage Tips

Here are some important tips for your pilgrimage to Tamaki Shrine!

-   Always extend your visit to **Tamaishi-sha (at the summit)** after your main shrine visit.
-   During **winter (December to March)**, road closures and freezing conditions often make it impossible to visit, so please be careful.
-   The mountain roads have **difficult-to-pass sections** with tight turns, so drivers who are not confident should be very cautious.
-   If your physical condition or the weather isn't perfect, it's also important to have the **courage to turn back**. Your safety comes first!
-   It's said to be especially auspicious to include Tamaki Shrine at the end (or beginning) of your **Kumano Sanzan** pilgrimage.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Let's talk about how to get to this special place!

**By Car**: Approximately **30 minutes** from Totsukawa Onsen, **2 hours and 30 minutes** from Gojo, and **2 hours** from Shingu (Kumano Hongu Taisha). Be prepared for continuous **hairpin mountain roads where it's difficult for cars to pass each other**.

**Public Transportation**: Reaching the shrine by **bus or train is nearly impossible**. The local Totsukawa Village bus has extremely few services, so realistically, a rental car or taxi is essential.

**Parking**: There's a shrine parking lot available (free, approx. 50 spaces). It's closed in winter. **From the parking lot to the main shrine, it's a 15- to 20-minute walk.**

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Pilgrimage Manners

Here are some basic pilgrimage manners to help you show respect at the shrine!

1.  Bow once before passing through the torii gate.
2.  The center of the approach path is for the deities, so please walk along the edges.
3.  At the temizuya (water purification basin), purify your left hand, then your right hand, rinse your mouth, cleanse your left hand again, and finally clean the ladle.
4.  Perform the **two bows, two claps, and one bow** ritual.
5.  When touching sacred trees like the Jindai Sugi cedar and other giant trees, please do so quietly and don't linger for too long.
6.  At Tamaishi-sha, please do not take any stones home, and take photos quietly and respectfully.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Got questions? I've got answers! Here are some Frequently Asked Questions about Tamaki Shrine.

**Q1. Is visiting during winter really impossible?**

A. During **December to March**, **forest roads are often closed** due to freezing and heavy snow, making pilgrimage difficult. Even with studless tires, please **always check road conditions** with the Totsukawa Village Office or the shrine before heading out in winter. Safety first!

**Q2. What does 'only the called can go' mean?**

A. This is both a metaphor and a reality! It's been said since ancient times because **last-minute changes to plans** often occur, preventing people from reaching the shrine. Conversely, there are many stories of people who **"surprisingly reached it without any trouble,"** which have become popular tales among pilgrims.

**Q3. How long does it take to reach Tamaishi-sha?**

A. It's about a **20-minute walk** from the main shrine. There's a steep mountain path along the way, so please wear comfortable shoes and clothes that allow easy movement. For those with good stamina, the total visit time including the main shrine would be around 2 hours.

**Q4. What's the pilgrimage route from Kumano Hongu Taisha?**

A. It's about a **2-hour drive** from Kumano Hongu Taisha. The traditional order of pilgrimage is to visit the **Kumano Sanzan (Hongu → Hayatama → Nachi)** and then visit Tamaki Shrine **last, as the inner sanctuary**. However, more people are choosing to visit in the reverse order too!

**Q5. Are there any Shukubo (temple lodgings) or accommodations?**

A. The shrine's own **"Tamaki Shrine Sanrosho"** offers accommodation (reservations required, please inquire at the shrine office). Staying overnight is very convenient for early morning visits or for trying to see the sea of clouds. You can also enjoy meals at the shrine!
