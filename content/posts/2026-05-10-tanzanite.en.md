## Introduction

Tanzanite is a rare gem considered one of the greatest gemstone discoveries of the 20th century, found **exclusively in 1967 in the Merelani Hills of northern Tanzania (at the foothills of Mount Kilimanjaro)**. The bluish-purple crystals, accidentally discovered by Maasai pastoralists, caught the eye of geologists. Tiffany and Company, the renowned New York jeweler, then named it 'Tanzanite' (from 'Tanzania' the country and '-ite' for stone) and commercially launched it, making it famous worldwide.

Tiffany famously promoted it as "**the most exciting blue gemstone discovery since sapphire**," and it reportedly spread across the global market within just a few years. Due to its geographical rarity, being mined solely in Tanzania, it's known as the "**New Time Stone of the 20th Century**" and the "**Sapphire of the Next Generation**." It's also recognized as a gemstone that will eventually be depleted. Mineralogically, it's a blue variety of zoisite. The common modern practice involves heat treatment to transform its natural brownish hue into a vibrant bluish-purple.

## Stone Symbolism and Meaning

Tanzanite is said to symbolize "**transformation, spirituality, and a new life**." Given its recent discovery in 1967, it resonated with the "**New Age movement**" of the late 20th century and has been enthusiastically embraced by spiritual enthusiasts as a "**stone heralding the arrival of a new era**."

From a color symbolism perspective, its unique blend of blue and purple is believed to represent a "**bridge between the earthly (blue) and the heavenly (purple)**." It has been used in meditation and healing practices to simultaneously activate the Third Eye (6th Chakra) and Crown (7th Chakra).

Furthermore, Tanzanite possesses a rare property called **pleochroism**, where rotating the crystal reveals three distinct colors: blue, violet, and reddish-purple. This characteristic is believed to contribute to its symbolism as a "**stone that changes your perspective on life**" and "**a stone that offers multifaceted viewpoints**." According to Maasai tradition, blue is a sacred color symbolizing "**the birth of new life**," and the stone is said to be given to women as a blessing after childbirth.

## Origin and Rarity

Tanzanite is exclusively found in one location worldwide: the Merelani Mine. According to on-site surveys, it's predicted to be largely depleted around 2050. To protect the industry, the Tanzanian government has nationalized the area around the mine and tightened export regulations on unprocessed rough stones. The rarity, meaning "**no more will ever be produced beyond what currently exists**," is considered one of the factors supporting the recent price increase.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Will Tanzanite really be depleted?**
A. Based on the Merelani Mine's reserves, it's generally estimated that it will be largely depleted around 2050. It's suggested that current yields might hold "antique" value in the future.

**Q2. Why is it heat-treated?**
A. Raw Tanzanite typically has a brownish-to-greenish hue. Heat treatment (around 400-500°C or 750-930°F) transforms it into its vibrant bluish-purple color. This is a standard industry practice and does not diminish its value.

**Q3. How is it different from [Sapphire](/blog/lapis-lazuli/)?**
A. Sapphire is corundum (Mohs hardness 9), while Tanzanite is zoisite (Mohs hardness 6.5-7); they are distinct minerals both chemically and in terms of hardness. Tanzanite exhibits strong pleochroism, with its color changing depending on the viewing angle. Sapphire is generally considered more durable for everyday wear.

**Q4. Is it truly a December birthstone?**
A. Yes. In 2002, the American Gem Trade Association added Tanzanite alongside turquoise and zircon as a December birthstone. It's a relatively recent addition.

**Q5. Is it suitable for use in rings?**
A. With a Mohs hardness of 6.5-7, it's somewhat fragile and also has cleavage. Therefore, while it can be used in rings, it's generally recommended for special occasion jewelry rather than everyday wear, or for use in pendants and earrings.
