The name 'Turquoise' (トルコ石 in Japanese literally means 'Turkish Stone') comes from the belief that it reached Europe via Turkey, although its primary source was actually Iran (formerly Persia). It is also designated as a **December birthstone**.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Properties and Historical and Cultural Background

Its stone properties are "**Travel Protection, Friendship, and Success**."

In ancient Egypt, it was used as **ornaments for royalty** from around 3000 BCE, and it is even inlaid in Tutankhamun's golden mask. Among Native Americans (Navajo and Zuni tribes), it was revered as a "**piece of the sky**," with a tradition of attaching it to warriors' bows to pray for success in hunting and battle. Along the Silk Road, traveling merchants wore it, and even today, many travelers wear turquoise before boarding an airplane.

> Source: GIA Turquoise Reference, various archaeological and ethnological documents.

## <span id="care"></span>Purification Methods and Care

-   **Clear quartz chips, white sage, and moonlight bathing** — Your reliable trio.
-   **Avoid salt, running water, and direct sunlight** — May cause discoloration or deterioration.
-   **Also vulnerable to sweat and cosmetics** — Reduce skin contact time by putting it on and taking it off carefully.
-   **Avoid chemical substances** — Be careful with perfume, disinfectants, and nail polish remover.

Turquoise is a delicate stone with a Mohs hardness of 5-6, making it **relatively soft and vulnerable to moisture and chemicals** due to its hydrous mineral nature. Gentle care determines the lifespan of the stone.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Are products labeled "treated" or "reconstructed" genuine?**

A. Most turquoise undergoes **resin impregnation and wax impregnation treatments**. This is a standard process to reinforce the brittle natural stone. High-quality, completely untreated turquoise is rare and expensive. It's realistic to choose based on price and intended use.

**Q2. What is Howlite Turquoise?**

A. It's an **imitation product made by dyeing white howlite blue**, and it is actually a different mineral. While it's affordable and vividly colored, it's important to understand that it's a turquoise substitute when purchasing.

**Q3. Is it better to have no matrix (black or brown webbing)?**

A. This is a matter of preference. While uniform blue without webbing is more expensive, **the matrix also has its own unique charm**. In Native American jewelry, turquoise with matrix is often considered traditional and authentic.

**Q4. My turquoise has changed color.**

A. **Yellowing and greening** can occur due to sweat, skin oils, and UV exposure. Many people appreciate this as a "stone that grows with its owner," but if you want to avoid discoloration, make it a habit to wipe it dry after wearing.

**Q5. Are there other stones for travel protection?**

A. Aquamarine, Malachite, and Sugilite are popular choices. For more details, please refer to the Travel and Road Safety section of our [Complete Guide to Crystals by Purpose](/blog/luck-powerstones-complete-guide/).
