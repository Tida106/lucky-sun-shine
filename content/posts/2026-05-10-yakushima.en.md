## Introduction

Yakushima is an island with a circumference of approximately 132 km, floating in the East China Sea, about 60 km south of the Kyushu mainland. At its center stands **Mount Miyanoura (1,936m above sea level), Kyushu's highest peak**, and it is known as the **"Ocean Alps"** due to its vertical distribution of vegetation, ranging from subtropical to subarctic zones. From the **Jomon Sugi, said to be 7,200 years old**, to the moss-covered primeval forest of **Shiratani Unsuikyo** (the real-world inspiration for the film *Princess Mononoke*), and **Nagata Inaka Beach** where sea turtles lay their eggs—the entire island is one of the ultimate natural power spots. It was registered as **Japan's first World Natural Heritage site in 1993**. This article comprehensively covers its **geology, World Heritage status, Jomon Sugi, Shiratani Unsuikyo, Yaku Shrine, FAQs, and a postscript** on a single page.

## <span id="nature"></span>Yakushima's Nature and World Natural Heritage

In **December 1993**, Yakushima was registered as **Japan's first World Natural Heritage site**, alongside Shirakami-Sanchi. The reasons for its registration are its **valuable ecosystem, with vegetation vertically distributed from subtropical to subarctic zones**, and its **primeval forests of Yakusugi (Yakushima cedars) that are thousands of years old**.

It is one of Japan's rainiest regions, famously described by Fumiko Hayashi as "having rain 35 days a month," with annual precipitation reaching about 8,000 mm in mountainous areas. This abundant water has nurtured the Yakusugi and the moss-covered primeval forests. **Yakusugi refers to Japanese cedars over 1,000 years old**; those younger are distinguished as "kosugi" (young cedars).

## <span id="jomonsugi"></span>About Jomon Sugi

The symbol of Yakushima, **"Jomon Sugi,"** is one of Japan's largest Yakusugi cedars, estimated to be **2,170 to 7,200 years old** (with various theories). This giant tree, with a **trunk circumference of 16.4 meters and a height of 25.3 meters**, has become a symbol of Yakushima tourism since its **discovery in 1966**.

The trail to Jomon Sugi is a serious trekking route, covering **approximately 22 km round trip from Arakawa Trailhead and taking about 10 hours**. It is divided into a first half along an old railway track and a second half with a steep ascent, with **departure around 5 AM** being standard. For beginners, the accompaniment of a **mountain guide** is highly recommended. There are **private car restrictions (March to November)**, so bus use from Yakusugi Nature Museum and other locations is essential.

## <span id="shiratani"></span>Shiratani Unsuikyo and Princess Mononoke's Forest

**Shiratani Unsuikyo** is a valley about a 30-minute drive from Miyanoura. It is a **primeval forest of moss-covered woods and giant trees**, with the highlight being **"Mononoke's Forest" (Moss-covered Forest)**, which served as the **inspiration for the world of the film *Princess Mononoke***.

It doesn't require the same level of physical fitness as Jomon Sugi, and offers multiple routes such as the **Yayoi Sugi Course (approx. 2 hours), Bugyo Sugi Course (approx. 4 hours), and Taikoiwa Course (approx. 5 hours)**, making it an ideal spot for beginners to experience Yakushima, enjoyable for everyone from novices to experienced hikers. Even on rainy days, the moss shines even more beautifully, offering a different kind of charm.

## <span id="yakujinja"></span>Yaku Shrine (Yakushima's Oldest Shrine)

Located in Miyanoura, Yakushima, **"Yaku Shrine"** is the island's guardian shrine (sōchinju). It is known as the **"southernmost Shikinaisha (shrine listed in the Engishiki Jinmyocho) in Japan,"** and its enshrined deities include **Amatsuhiko Hohodemi no Mikoto** and other mythological gods.

Many visitors stop here before exploring Yakushima to pray for **safe travels and a successful pilgrimage**, and it has long been deeply revered as the island's spiritual pillar.

## <span id="yakusugiland"></span>Yakusugi Land and Kigen Sugi

**Yakusugi Land** is an **area where you can view Yakusugi cedars accessible by car**. It's a natural recreation forest at an altitude of about 1,000m, with courses of varying difficulty (**30-minute, 50-minute, 80-minute, and 150-minute** options), making it a popular spot where you can experience Yakusugi even without full climbing gear.

Nearby, **"Kigen Sugi"** is a giant tree estimated to be **3,000 years old**, and it's **located right next to the road**, making it accessible to everyone. It offers a precious "giant tree experience" for those who cannot undertake the hike to Jomon Sugi.

## <span id="point"></span>Key Pilgrimage Tips

-   **Jomon Sugi trekking** requires essential gear and physical fitness; a **guide is recommended**.
-   **Shiratani Unsuikyo** can be experienced in half a day, ideal for beginners to Yakushima.
-   Report your arrival on the island and pray for safety at **Yaku Shrine**.
-   As it's a rainy island, **rain gear and a waterproof backpack cover** are essential.
-   Walk with the mindset of **"borrowing from nature"** (take everything you brought with you back).
-   Absolutely **do not step on Yakusugi roots**, and avoid trampling on moss.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

**From Kagoshima Mainland:**
-   **High-speed ferry Toppy** (Kagoshima Port → Miyanoura Port and Anbo Port) approx. 2 hours
-   **Ferry Yakushima 2** (Kagoshima Port → Miyanoura Port) approx. 4 hours
-   **Airplane** (Kagoshima Airport → Yakushima Airport) approx. 40 minutes

**Island Transportation:** Rental cars and taxis are standard. Buses are infrequent and inconvenient for sightseeing.

**Parking:** Parking is available at major tourist spots. Private car restrictions are in place for a long period (March to November) at the Jomon Sugi trailhead (Arakawa Trailhead), so using the shuttle bus from Yakusugi Nature Museum is generally required.

## <span id="season"></span>Recommended Seasons and Times

**The fresh green season from April to June** and **autumn in October and November** are relatively easy for hiking. **The rainy season (mid-June to July) and typhoon season (August to September)** often make hiking difficult. In **winter**, there is snow in the mountainous areas, and traverses like Mount Miyanoura require snow mountain equipment. If you want to see **sea turtle egg-laying**, visit from May to July; for **hatching**, late July to September.

## <span id="manner"></span>Unique Yakushima Etiquette

1.  **At Yaku Shrine and other shrines, follow the two bows, two claps, one bow ritual.**
2.  **Absolutely do not step on Yakusugi roots** (to protect the bark).
3.  **Do not trample on moss or vegetation**, and stay on marked trails.
4.  **Take all your trash with you** (to protect the natural heritage).
5.  **Do not feed wild animals** (Yakushima deer and Yakushima macaques).
6.  **Carry a portable toilet and use it only in designated areas** (Jomon Sugi trail).

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can beginners hike to Jomon Sugi?**

A. It's a long-distance trek of **approximately 11 km one-way and 10 hours round-trip**, requiring **sufficient physical fitness and gear** regardless of prior hiking experience. For beginners, the accompaniment of a **mountain guide** is highly recommended. **Departing around 5 AM** is standard, and a headlamp is essential.

**Q2. Is Shiratani Unsuikyo worth visiting on its own?**

A. Yes, absolutely! The **moss-covered forest scenery encapsulates the essence of Yakushima**, and if you want to experience the world of Princess Mononoke, Shiratani Unsuikyo is the perfect choice. The **Bugyo Sugi Course (approx. 4 hours)** is an easy-to-walk trail for beginners, visiting Yayoi Sugi and Sanbon-ashi Sugi (Three-legged Cedar).

**Q3. What about rain precautions?**

A. Yakushima is extremely rainy, famously described as having **"rain 35 days a month."** Be sure to bring **full-fledged rain gear like Gore-Tex**, a **waterproof backpack cover**, **change of clothes**, and **quick-drying base layers**. Even on rainy days, the moss looks beautiful, offering a different kind of charm.

**Q4. What is the cost for a Jomon Sugi trekking guide?**

A. The average cost is **20,000 to 25,000 yen per person** (as of 2026). Prices vary for small-group guides and early morning departure plans. Choose a reputable guide service like **"YNAC (Yakushima Nature Activities Center)"** from a booking site.

**Q5. Can I see sea turtle egg-laying?**

A. Egg-laying can be observed at **Nagata Inaka Beach** from **mid-May to July**. **Observation tours** require advance reservations through the **Nagata Sea Turtle Conservation Council**. Flash photography is strictly prohibited; observe quietly from the designated distance.
