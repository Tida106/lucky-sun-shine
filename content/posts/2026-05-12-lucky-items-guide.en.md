## <span id="basics"></span>What are Good Luck Charms?

Good luck charms are a collective term for **items entrusted with wishes for good fortune, prosperity, health, and protection against evil.** In Japan, they have been deeply rooted in daily life since ancient times as **engimono (lucky talismans)**, influenced by Shinto, Buddhism, and Taoism (Feng Shui from China).

> **Note**: The effects of good luck charms are cultural and symbolic. They are not scientifically or medically guaranteed, and individual experiences of their effectiveness may vary.

### Three Roles of Good Luck Charms

-   **Omamori (Talismans)** — A spiritual anchor that eases the owner's anxiety and tension.
-   **Consciousness Switch** — A visual reminder of "how one wants to be."
-   **Symbol of Place** — Declares that a room, shop, or entrance is "a special place."

Rather than believing that "**the object itself possesses power**," a modern interpretation suggests that "**through the object, one's consciousness becomes aligned, leading to changes in behavior.**"

## <span id="types"></span>Characteristics of Each Type

### Wallets

**Traditionally known as the "gateway to financial fortune,"** wallets are the most common good luck charm. It is said that paying attention to their material, color, shape, and replacement timing can change your relationship with money.

| Feature | Key Point |
|---|---|
| Color | Financial luck: yellow, gold, brown tones; Savings: black, navy; Purification: white |
| Material | Cowhide, snakeskin (auspicious), cordovan (durability) |
| Shape | Long wallets are considered good for financial luck (bills are not folded) |
| Replacement Timing | "Haru-saifu" (spring wallet, associated with "expanding") and "Aki-saifu" (autumn wallet, associated with "bearing fruit") are auspicious |
| Lifespan | Approximately 1,000 days (3 years) is a guide for replacement |

→ For more details, refer to [How to Choose a Wallet for Money Luck](/blog/money-luck-wallet/).

### Omamori

**Omamori are talismans granted by Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples, serving as representations of deities or Buddhas.** It is strictly forbidden to open the contents of the pouch, and they should be handled with care.

| Type | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Kaiun-mamori | General good fortune |
| Yakuyoke-mamori | Aversion of disaster |
| Enmusubi-mamori | Good relationships, romance, marriage |
| Kinun-mamori | Business prosperity, financial luck |
| Gakugyo-mamori | Academic success, passing exams |
| Kenko-mamori | Recovery from illness, health and longevity |
| Kotsu-anzen-mamori | Protection during travel |

Having multiple omamori is generally **not an issue, as "the gods do not quarrel among themselves."** Traditionally, they are returned to the shrine or temple where they were received after about a year.

### Engimono (Lucky Talismans)

This is a general term for traditional Japanese **shapes and words** imbued with meanings of good fortune, often in the form of ornaments or small items.

| Engimono | Origin and Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shichifukujin (Seven Lucky Gods) | Seven deities of good fortune, for business, health, and longevity |
| Tsurukame (Crane and Turtle) | Symbols of longevity (crane lives for a thousand years, turtle for ten thousand years) |
| Maneki-Neko (Beckoning Cat) | Attracts customers and money luck (explained later) |
| Daruma Doll | "Nanakorobi Yaoki" (fall seven times, rise eight), goal achievement (explained later) |
| Kumade (Rake Talisman) | Rakes in good fortune, popular at Tori-no-ichi (Rooster Market) |
| Maneki-Hebi (Beckoning Snake / White Snake) | Financial luck, regeneration through shedding skin |
| Octagonal Mirror | A Feng Shui item that balances the energies of the eight directions |
| Dragon Ornament | Career advancement, power, financial fortune |

### Maneki-Neko (Beckoning Cat)

A **uniquely Japanese lucky charm** said to originate in the Edo period. The meaning differs based on which paw is raised.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right Paw Raised | Attracts financial luck and wealth (often male cats) |
| Left Paw Raised | Attracts people and customers (often female cats) |
| Both Paws Raised | Attracts both (though some avoid it, associating it with "giving up") |
| Color | Calico = good fortune, White = general luck, Black = warding off evil, Gold = financial luck, Pink = romance |

It is commonly placed near the cash register in shops and businesses. In homes, it can be displayed in the entrance or living room.

→ For more details on color-specific benefits, sizes, origins, placement, and memorial service rituals, refer to [Guide to Maneki-Neko: Colors, Paw Directions, Sizes, Placement, and Rituals](/blog/maneki-neko/).

### Daruma Doll

A **symbol of goal achievement.** Traditionally, you fill in the left eye (the viewer's right eye) when making a wish, and the right eye upon achieving it.

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red | Ward off evil, good health and longevity (most traditional) |
| White | Academic success, goal achievement |
| Gold | Financial luck, business prosperity |
| Pink | Romance, good relationships |
| Purple | Career advancement, good fortune |
| Black | Business prosperity (for business owners) |

Takasaki Daruma dolls from Gunma Prefecture account for about 80% of the national market, making it Japan's largest production area.

## <span id="display"></span>Basic Display and Usage

### Basic Placement

-   **Entrance** — A place to welcome good fortune, such as Maneki-Neko and Shichifukujin.
-   **Living Room** — The center where families gather, like dragon ornaments and crane and turtle.
-   **Bedroom** — Personal amulets, for restful sleep and health.
-   **Desk or Workspace** — For work luck, Daruma dolls, small Maneki-Neko.
-   **Kamidana (Household Shinto Altar)** — Omamori and sacred talismans.

### Feng Shui Tips for Placement

-   **West** — Financial luck (gold or yellow items)
-   **East** — Work and development luck (blue or green items)
-   **South** — Fame and popularity (red or reddish items)
-   **North** — Romance and health luck (white or pink items)

→ For more details, refer to [Feng Shui and Power Stone Basics](/blog/fengshui-powerstone-basics/).

### Display Etiquette

-   Place them in **a position higher than eye level** (treating them with respect, similar to deities or Buddhas).
-   **Avoid direct sunlight and humidity.**
-   **Clean them regularly** (dust is believed to impede the flow of energy).
-   **Replace broken or chipped lucky charms with new ones.**

### Inappropriate Display Methods (NG)

-   Directly opposite the entrance (considered inauspicious in Feng Shui).
-   Toilets or washrooms (wet areas).
-   Directly on the floor.
-   Placing them in a cluttered or disorganized area.

## <span id="dispose"></span>Disposal and Return Rituals

In Japan, it is traditional **to return good luck charms with gratitude once their purpose has been served.** It is considered good manners to avoid disposing of them with regular trash.

### How to Choose Where to Return Them

| Charm | Return Location |
|---|---|
| Omamori and talismans from shrines | The **old talisman collection box (kosatsu-nosho)** at the shrine where you received them |
| Omamori and talismans from temples | The temple where you received them |
| Engimono like Maneki-Neko and Daruma | The **old talisman collection box** at a shrine (they may not accept all types) |
| Takasaki Daruma dolls | Daruma Kuyo (Daruma memorial service) at Daruma-ji Temple |
| General engimono | **Dondo-yaki** (a traditional bonfire ritual, around January 15th) |

### Home Ritual for Disposal

The **minimum ritual** if returning them is difficult:

1.  **Wrap them in white paper or cloth.**
2.  **Sprinkle a pinch of salt.**
3.  **Express gratitude by saying, "Thank you very much."**
4.  **Dispose of them as burnable waste, separated from other trash.**

It is important to have the feeling of "**saying goodbye**" rather than simply "throwing away."

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is it okay to have multiple good luck charms?**

A. **Yes, it's perfectly fine.** In Shinto, it's believed that "the gods do not quarrel among themselves," so having multiple omamori and engimono together is not an issue. However, if you don't **focus on a specific "theme,"** your intentions might be dispersed, so **3 to 5 items** is a practical number.

**Q2. Do inexpensive plastic lucky charms also have an effect?**

A. The traditional view is that "**intention matters more than material**," but if you plan to display it for a long time, **wood, pottery, or genuine lucky charms** might evoke more affection. Shrine-granted items, regardless of material, are treated as sacred.

**Q3. Can I use second-hand or gifted lucky charms?**

A. Omamori and sacred talismans are **primarily intended for the person who received them.** Gifts from family or close friends are generally acceptable, but buying and selling them second-hand (e.g., on flea markets) is considered bad manners. **Engimono (like Maneki-Neko and Daruma dolls) are acceptable as gifts.**

**Q4. Should I dispose of a charm immediately after my wish comes true?**

A. **It's traditional to display it for about one year.** Rather than disposing of it the moment your wish comes true, it's customary in Japan to **continue cherishing it for another year** with gratitude. **The Dondo-yaki (bonfire ritual) of the following year** is a common timing.

**Q5. Can I use Feng Shui and Shinto good luck charms together?**

A. Yes. **Many Japanese households traditionally combine them.** The mixture of Feng Shui (Chinese origin), Shinto, and Buddhism is a characteristic feature of Japanese religious culture. It is recommended to choose items based on **the theme you wish to value.**

**Q6. Are power stones also considered good luck charms?**

A. Yes. **Bracelets, raw stones, and pendants** are all types of good luck charms. For more details, refer to [The Complete Guide to Choosing Power Stones](/blog/how-to-choose-powerstones/).

## The Editorial Team's View: The Idea That "Buying Brings Luck" Is Outdated

We've introduced five types of good luck charms, but finally, let the editorial team share our honest opinion. **The very notion that "buying things will automatically bring good luck" is actually the furthest from true good fortune** — that's our genuine feeling.

There are two reasons for this.

First, **the euphoria of shopping fades within a few days.** That "this will change my luck!" feeling right after acquiring a new wallet or Maneki-Neko often doesn't last even a week. What's truly important is **whether you can continue to treat that item with care even after the initial excitement has worn off.**

Second, **the act of buying itself is a "declaration of your will."** Separately from whether the item itself has power, the decision to "I want to be the kind of person who owns this wallet" itself becomes the starting point for changing your actions. That's why **one item you chose with conviction, even if inexpensive,** holds far more meaning than **an expensive one bought out of inertia.**

### Recommended Order for Acquiring Charms

| Priority | Item | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Wallet** | Touched daily, directly linked to your relationship with money |
| 2nd | **Omamori** (received at a shrine) | The experience of making an effort to acquire one at a significant juncture is meaningful |
| 3rd | **Maneki-Neko or Daruma Doll for the Entrance** | Serves as a "welcome home landmark" that you see upon returning |
| 4th and beyond | Engimono and ornaments | It's easy to keep accumulating them; focusing on a specific theme is important |

If you try to "get everything at once," you'll likely end up not developing affection for any of them. Try to dedicate **at least one year to genuinely engaging with just one item.**

### Common Misconceptions

-   ❌ "**More expensive lucky charms are more effective.**" → ✅ What matters is whether you can **place it somewhere you see every day.** Even a 100-yen Daruma can be meaningful if you "interact" with it daily.
-   ❌ "**Collecting multiple charms boosts your luck.**" → ✅ Cluttered lucky charms can have the opposite effect. The trick is to have **up to 3-5 items, and align them with a common theme.**
-   ❌ "**Disposing of them with regular trash is fine.**" → ✅ At the very least, wrap them in white paper and sprinkle a pinch of salt. The act of "saying goodbye" also **sets the tone for your relationship with the next lucky charm you welcome.**
