## Enmusubi and Shrines

"**Enmusubi**" (matchmaking and forging connections) is a broad concept that encompasses not only romantic love, but also **all kinds of encounters in life, including work, friendships, and family relationships**. In Shinto, the deities governing connections are diverse, including **deities of nation-building** (Okuninushi-no-Okami), **goddesses** (Munakata Sanjojin, Ichikishima-hime-no-Mikoto), and **married deities** (Izanagi and Izanami). Rooted in the **"love stories of the gods"** from the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, unique enmusubi beliefs have developed in various regions.

While the 10th month of the lunar calendar is called "Kannazuki" (the month without gods) elsewhere, in Izumo, it's known as "Kamiarizuki" (the month with gods)—it's believed that all the deities from across Japan gather in Izumo to **hold a meeting to forge connections for the coming year**. This is why Izumo Taisha is considered the headquarters for enmusubi.

> **Note**: The blessings of shrines carry cultural and symbolic significance. They do not guarantee the fulfillment or timing of your wishes.

## <span id="izumo"></span>Izumo Taisha (Shimane Prefecture)—The Headquarters for Matchmaking

The main enshrined deity, **Okuninushi-no-Okami**, is the protagonist of the Japanese myth of "**Kuni-Yuzuri**" (the Transfer of the Land) and a byword for the **"deity of matchmaking and relationships."** In mythology, it's said that he gained his power of matchmaking after receiving **Sakimitama Kushimitama** (spirits of good fortune and wonder).

- **Location**: Taisha-cho, Izumo-shi, Shimane
- **Highlights**: The grand shimenawa (sacred rope) at Kagura-den, Soga-no-Yashiro behind the main hall, and the sacred statue of divine compassion (Gojiai-no-Goshinzo).
- **Worship Feature**: The prayer ritual is **two bows, four claps, one bow** (different from general shrines).
- **Seasonal Festival**: Kamiari Festival (10th lunar month) — Legend of the matchmaking conference where deities from across Japan gather.

## <span id="meiji"></span>Meiji Jingu (Tokyo)—The Married Couple Camphor Trees in the City Center

Enshrining Emperor Meiji and **Empress Shoken**, this is Tokyo's largest sacred forest (chinju no mori). The **Meoto Kusu (Married Couple Camphor Trees)** towering in front of the main shrine are bound by a shimenawa rope and are cherished as **one of Tokyo's foremost power spots for matchmaking and relationships**.

- **Location**: Yoyogi, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo
- **Highlights**: Meoto Kusu, Kiyomasa's Well (Kiyomasa-no-Ido), and the 700,000 sqm sacred forest on the north approach.
- **Worship Feature**: Two bows, two claps, one bow (standard practice).
- **Recommendation**: Easily accessible as the **"closest sacred site for matchmaking"** for those living in Tokyo.

→ [Meiji Jingu Worship Guide: Tokyo's Largest Power Spot, Home to a 700,000 sqm Sacred Forest](/blog/meiji-jingu/)

## <span id="enoshima"></span>Enoshima Jinja (Kanagawa Prefecture)—Benzaiten and the Dragon Palace for Relationships

Enshrining the **Munakata Sanjojin (Three Goddesses of Munakata)** (Tagirihime-no-Mikoto, Ichikishima-hime-no-Mikoto, and Tagitsuhime-no-Mikoto), this is one of the **Three Great Benzaiten Shrines of Japan**. Its matchmaking beliefs, associated with its **connection to the Dragon Palace**, have been cherished since ancient times, and its location floating on the Shonan sea makes it a popular dating spot.

- **Location**: Enoshima, Fujisawa-shi, Kanagawa
- **Highlights**: Three main shrines—Hetsunomiya (with the Eight-Armed Benzaiten in Hoan-den), Nakatsunomiya, and Okutsunomiya—and the Dragon Love Bell (Ryuren-no-Kane).
- **Worship Feature**: A custom of praying for love by wishing for two names at the Dragon Love Bell.
- **Blessings**: Matchmaking, improvement in performing arts, and financial luck (Benzaiten).

→ [Enoshima Jinja Worship Guide: Shonan's Maritime Sacred Site Enshrining One of Japan's Three Great Benzaiten and the Munakata Sanjojin](/blog/enoshima-jinja/)

## <span id="aso"></span>Aso Jinja (Kumamoto Prefecture)—Marital Harmony from Takasago-no-Matsu

Enshrining **Takeiwatatsu-no-Mikoto** and the **Twelve Deities of Aso**, this is the Ichinomiya (chief shrine) of Higo Province. The **"Takasago-no-Matsu" (Pine of Takasago)** within the precincts, with its male and female pines standing side by side, is cherished as a symbol of **"marital harmony" and "fulfillment of good relationships."** It is also known for its **Romon gate being fully restored in 2023, seven years after the 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes.**

- **Location**: Ichinomiya-machi, Aso-shi, Kumamoto
- **Highlights**: One of Japan's Three Great Romon Gates (now restored), Takasago-no-Matsu, the Main Shrine (Shinden) and Hall of Worship (Haiden).
- **Worship Feature**: It is said that **walking around** the Takasago-no-Matsu **twice** will bring marital harmony.
- **Blessings**: Matchmaking, marital harmony, and bountiful harvests.

→ [Aso Jinja Worship Guide: The Ichinomiya of Higo Province With One of Japan's Three Great Romon Gates, Rebuilt After the Kumamoto Earthquakes](/blog/aso-jinja/)

## <span id="omiwa"></span>Omiwa Jinja (Nara Prefecture)—Japan's Oldest-Class Shrine for Relationships

Enshrining **Omononushi-no-Okami**, this is one of Japan's oldest-class shrines, with **Mt. Miwa itself as its sacred object (goshintai).** It preserves the ancient form of worship where **Mt. Miwa is directly revered without a main hall (honden)**, and prayers for matchmaking, inspired by a matchmaking myth (the legend of Hotatama-yorihime and the spindle), have been cherished since ancient times.

- **Location**: Miwa, Sakurai-shi, Nara
- **Highlights**: Hall of Worship (Haiden) (no main hall), Mt. Miwa climbing (tohai), Sai Jinja's sacred well (Yakui-do), and Meoto-iwa (Married Couple Rocks).
- **Worship Feature**: Nature worship of Mt. Miwa itself; mountain climbing (tohai) requires prior application.
- **Blessings**: Matchmaking, marital harmony, health, and warding off evil.

→ [Omiwa Jinja Worship Guide: Japan's Oldest-Class Shrine With Mt. Miwa as its Sacred Object](/blog/omiwa-jinja/)

## <span id="itsukushima"></span>Itsukushima Jinja (Hiroshima Prefecture)—Goddess of Beauty Floating on the Sea

A World Heritage site enshrining **Ichikishima-hime-no-Mikoto** and the **Munakata Sanjojin (Three Goddesses of Munakata).** Famous for its **Great Torii Gate** standing in the sea, it attracts worshipers from across the nation who pray for matchmaking to the **goddess of beauty.**

- **Location**: Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi-shi, Hiroshima
- **Highlights**: The Great Torii Gate in the sea, the shinden-zukuri style shrine buildings, and the scenery that changes with the tide.
- **Worship Feature**: During low tide, you can walk to the base of the torii gate.
- **Blessings**: Matchmaking, business prosperity, financial luck, and maritime safety.

→ [Itsukushima Jinja Worship Guide: Complete Guide to Miyajima's Floating World Heritage Power Spot](/blog/itsukushima-jinja/)

## <span id="togakushi"></span>Togakushi Jinja (Nagano Prefecture)—Ancient Legends of Kuzuryusha Shrine's Matchmaking

A sacred site for mountain asceticism (Shugendo) in Shinshu, **composed of five shrines.** In addition to **Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Mikoto** enshrined at Okusha and **Ame-no-Yagokoro-Omoikane-no-Mikoto** at Chusha, **Kuzuryu Okami**, enshrined at **Kuzuryusha Shrine**, has been worshipped since ancient times as the **"deity of matchmaking and toothaches."** The pilgrimage course visiting all five shrines to pray for matchmaking is a tradition.

- **Location**: Togakushi, Nagano-shi, Nagano
- **Highlights**: The cedar avenue on the Okusha approach, Kuzuryusha Shrine, and the three cedars at Chusha.
- **Worship Feature**: **Goshameguri (Five Shrine Pilgrimage)** visiting all 5 shrines (half to full day).
- **Blessings**: Matchmaking (Kuzuryu), good fortune (Okusha), and academic success (Chusha).

## Tips for Visiting Matchmaking Shrines

Here are traditional tips for a more thoughtful and effective visit to pray for relationships.

- **Focus on "gratitude and determination" rather than "wishing"**: This is a fundamental Shinto mindset. First, express gratitude for existing connections, then convey your determination for new ones.
- **Pray for "good connections" rather than a specific person**: It's said that wishing for "the best connections for oneself," rather than specifying a particular individual, ultimately attracts better relationships.
- **Prepare yourself before visiting**: Get ready in the morning and wear clean, neat attire. The very act of preparing yourself to appear before the deities is preparation for attracting good relationships.
- **Visit multiple times**: It's traditionally said that connections deepen not just from a single visit, but by **returning at key milestones**.
- **Express gratitude**: Don't forget to make an "Orei-mairi" (thank-you visit) after your wishes are granted—this is the proper way to invite further good connections.

## Matchmaking Amulets and Sacred Items

Representative amulets and sacred items available at major matchmaking shrines:

- **Enmusubi Mamori (Matchmaking Amulet)** (many, including Izumo Taisha and Meiji Jingu): A basic amulet to carry with you.
- **Heart-shaped Ema (Votive Tablet)** (Meiji Jingu, Hikawa Jinja, and others): Write your wish and dedicate it.
- **Red String Enmusubi Mamori** (Enoshima Jinja and others): A symbol of the "red thread of fate."
- **Meoto Mamori (Married Couple Amulet)** (one set for two people): For those with partners, praying for marital harmony.
- **Omikuji (Fortune Slip)** (Izumo Taisha has a unique omikuji without "good" or "bad" fortunes): Take it home as a precious message from the deities.

Traditionally, **amulets should be returned to the issuing shrine after about a year.** If you can't return it due to distance, it's acceptable to place it in the old amulet collection box at a nearby shrine.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Is it okay to "shrine-hop" (visit multiple matchmaking shrines)?**

A. **Absolutely fine.** Traditionally, pilgrimages to multiple shrines, such as "Sanjamairi" (visiting three shrines), have been common. However, visiting too many in one day can dilute your focus, so **up to three shrines a day** is a good guideline. The fundamental principle of Shinto is that shrines do not compete with each other.

**Q2. What if Izumo Taisha is too far to visit?**

A. There are **"bunshi" (branch shrines) of Izumo Taisha** located throughout Japan, such as **Tokyo Bunshi, Fukuoka Bun'in, and Hokkaido Bun'in.** If you cannot visit the main shrine, you can still worship the same Okuninushi-no-Okami at a branch shrine.

**Q3. Should I visit a shrine right after a heartbreak?**

A. It's a wonderful opportunity to express **"gratitude for past connections" and "determination for future good relationships."** It's okay to cry. It's said that the deities will warmly watch over your **"emotional reset."**

**Q4. What kind of attire should I wear for matchmaking prayers?**

A. **Clean, everyday clothes** are perfectly fine. There's no need to be overly formal, and some people choose **soft colors like pink or white.** Avoid excessive exposure; the basic rule is to wear **"something you wouldn't be ashamed to wear before the deities."**

**Q5. Is it okay to bring power stones when visiting a shrine?**

A. Yes. Many people bring relationship-boosting stones like **Rose Quartz** and **Morganite.** Common practices include holding them in your palm before passing through the torii gate, or placing them near your omamori (amulets) after worship. It's tempting to think that **the more matchmaking shrines you visit, the stronger your connections will become**, but our editorial team's observations suggest the opposite trend. **If you visit more than three shrines in one day, you might find yourself unsure of what you wished for at each one, ultimately leading to a blurred memory of your visit**—this is the honest feedback from those who've tried it.

The fundamental issue isn't about the intensity of blessings, but rather a decrease in the **quality of worship as a "practice of verbalizing your wishes."** This is also why Shinto has traditionally advocated for "**Ichisha Ichigan**" (one wish per shrine).

### Try Organizing Your Wishes in This Way

Before praying for relationships, try answering these three questions for yourself; it can transform the quality of your visit.

1.  **"What kind of connection do I want most right now?"** (Romantic? Work? Friendship? Family?)
2.  **"Am I ready to receive that connection?"** (Lifestyle, mindset, time)
3.  **"When that connection arrives, what am I prepared to change about myself?"** (Readiness to accept)

If you visit without clear answers, the deities may find it difficult to respond. When you approach a shrine **prepared to articulate your wishes in your own words**, the entire worship experience will transform.
