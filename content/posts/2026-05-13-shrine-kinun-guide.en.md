## The Relationship Between Financial Luck, Business Prosperity, and Shrines

Deities associated with **"financial luck"** include Benten (Benzaiten, one of the Munakata Three Goddesses, Ichikishimahime-no-Mikoto), Daikokuten (syncretized with Okuninushi-no-Okami), Ebisu-sama (Kotoshiro-nushi-no-Kami, Sukunabikona-no-Mikoto), and Inari Okami (Ukanomitama-no-Kami). These deities have been worshipped in various forms, blending Shinto and Buddhist beliefs over time.

Shrines dedicated especially to **"Benten (Benzaiten)"** are at the heart of business prosperity worship. The three shrines of **Enoshima, Itsukushima, and Munakata** are nationally renowned for financial luck, forming the lineage of the **"Three Great Benten Shrines of Japan."** Additionally, Inari worship has spread throughout the nation as a guardian deity for merchants, with **Fushimi Inari Taisha** as its head shrine, symbolizing thriving business.

> **Please Note**: The blessings of shrines carry cultural and symbolic meanings. They do not guarantee the fulfillment of wishes or specific financial outcomes.

## <span id="enoshima"></span>Enoshima Shrine (Kanagawa Prefecture) — One of Japan's Three Great Benten Shrines

Enshrining the **Munakata Three Goddesses**, this is one of **Japan's Three Great Benten Shrines**. **Benten (Benzaiten)** has been deeply revered by merchants since ancient times as a **goddess of wealth and the performing arts**. Enoshima became a famous spot for financial luck, sparking an **"Enoshima Pilgrimage"** boom in the Edo period.

-   **Location**: Enoshima, Fujisawa City, Kanagawa Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The three shrines of Hetsunomiya (with the Eight-Armed Benten statue in Hoanden), Nakatsumiya, and Okutsugu, and the White Dragon King Golden Purifying Water for money washing.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: You can directly offer prayers to the **Eight-Armed Benten statue** in Hoanden.
-   **Blessings**: Financial luck, business prosperity, improvement in arts and skills, matchmaking.

## <span id="itsukushima"></span>Itsukushima Shrine (Hiroshima Prefecture) — Goddess of Maritime Trade

This World Heritage site enshrines **Ichikishimahime-no-Mikoto** and the other **Munakata Three Goddesses**. The origin of **"Itsukushima"** is said to be "**Itsuki-shima** (an island where deities are worshipped and enshrined)." Taira no Kiyomori deeply revered it as a guardian deity of maritime trade, which led to its fame as a shrine for business prosperity.

-   **Location**: Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi City, Hiroshima Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The Great Torii Gate in the sea, the main shrine built in the Shinden-zukuri style, and Senjokaku.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: Revered by merchants since ancient times as a guardian for **maritime trade**.
-   **Blessings**: Business prosperity, financial luck, matchmaking, maritime safety.

## <span id="munakata"></span>Munakata Taisha (Fukuoka Prefecture) — Guardian Deity of Ancient Trade

This shrine is the general guardian of the sea, enshrining the **Munakata Three Goddesses** (Tagorihime-no-Kami, Tagitsuhime-no-Kami, and Ichikishimahime-no-Kami). **Okinoshima, the "Island of Gods,"** is a World Heritage site, and the goddesses have been deeply worshipped on a national scale since ancient times as **guardians of trade routes** with the continent. As the **head shrine of the Munakata Three Goddesses worship, alongside Enoshima and Itsukushima**, it's also famous for financial and wealth luck due to its **lineage of Benten worship**.

-   **Location**: Munakata City, Fukuoka Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The three shrines of Hetsunomiya, Nakatsumiya, and Okitsumiya, and the national treasures in the Shimpokan museum.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: The group of national treasures symbolizes **ancient wealth and authority**.
-   **Blessings**: Financial luck, wealth luck, business prosperity, international exchange, maritime safety.

## <span id="izumo"></span>Izumo Taisha (Shimane Prefecture) — Daikokuten and the God of Fortune

The main enshrined deity, **Okuninushi-no-Okami**, was **syncretized with "Daikokuten"** and has been deeply revered by merchants since ancient times as a **god of fortune and wealth**. The image of Daikoku-sama riding on rice bales is a representative figure of the **Seven Lucky Gods**, and Izumo Taisha serves as its de facto head shrine.

-   **Location**: Taisha-cho, Izumo City, Shimane Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The grand Shimenawa rope at Kaguraden, the Statue of Divine Benevolence, and Soga-no-Yashiro.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: Worship of Okuninushi-no-Okami (Daikokuten) as a **"deity who brings good fortune."**
-   **Blessings**: Financial luck, business prosperity, matchmaking, bountiful harvests.

## <span id="omiwa"></span>Omiwa Shrine (Nara Prefecture) — Financial Luck from Snake Deity Worship

**Omononushi-no-Okami** is regarded as the **Nigimitama (benevolent spirit)** of **Okuninushi-no-Okami** and is associated with **snake deity worship**. Since ancient times, **snakes** have been revered across Japan as **"messengers of the gods that bring wealth."** At Omiwa Shrine's **"Mi-no-Kamisugi" (Snake Deity Cedar Tree)**, offerings of eggs are continuously made.

-   **Location**: Miwa, Sakurai City, Nara Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: Mi-no-Kamisugi (Snake Deity Cedar Tree), the Haiden (worship hall, no main sanctuary), and Sai Shrine's medicinal well.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: Snake deity worship, with **visiting on the Day of the Snake (Mi no Hi)** believed to be an auspicious day for increasing financial luck.
-   **Blessings**: Financial luck, matchmaking, health, warding off evil.

→ [Omiwa Shrine Visiting Guide | One of Japan's Oldest Shrines, With Mount Miwa as its Sacred Body](/blog/omiwa-jinja/)

## <span id="kasuga"></span>Kasuga Taisha (Nara Prefecture) — Symbol of Fujiwara Clan Prosperity

This ancient shrine enshrines **Takemikazuchi-no-Mikoto** and the **four tutelary deities of the Fujiwara clan**, which supported the clan's immense power. The **Mantoro (Lantern Festival)**, where **3,000 lanterns** are lit, creates a fantastical atmosphere. Many people have visited since ancient times to pray for **business prosperity**, making it a renowned shrine for financial luck, alongside career advancement.

-   **Location**: Kasugano-cho, Nara City, Nara Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: 3,000 lanterns in the Chumon Gate and corridors, Mantoro (Setsubun and Obon), and the deer in Nara Park.
-   **Financial Luck Feature**: Symbol of Fujiwara clan prosperity, worshipped for both **career advancement and financial luck**.
-   **Blessings**: Business prosperity, good fortune in competition, career advancement, academic achievement.

## Tips for Praying for Financial Luck

Here are some traditional tips to help you make your prayers for financial luck and business prosperity more thoughtfully.

-   **Focus on "Prosperity and Integrity" rather than just "Profit"**: In Shinto, the basic mindset is to "**prosper through honest work**." Prioritize prayers for sincere effort over speculative wishes.
-   **Auspicious Osenbako (Offering Coin) Wordplay**: It's a tradition for financial luck prayers to offer coins with auspicious wordplay, such as 5 yen (go-en, meaning "good fortune/connections"), 45 yen (shiju go-en, meaning "constant good fortune"), or 125 yen (jubun go-en, meaning "ample good fortune"). Remember, **your feelings are more important than the amount**.
-   **Visit on the Day of the Snake (Mi no Hi) or Day of the Tiger (Tora no Hi)**: It's believed that visiting on these auspicious days for financial luck is especially effective.
-   **Visit at New Year, Setsubun, or the Start of a Fiscal Period**: Combine "**making a wish and expressing gratitude**" for the start of the year's business.
-   **Thanksgiving Visit (O-rei-mairi)**: Performing a "**thanksgiving visit**" after achieving results is a practice believed to invite the next wave of financial luck.

## Financial Luck Omamori (Charms) and Sacred Items

Representative omamori (charms) and sacred items available at major financial luck shrines:

-   **Shobai Hanjō Omamori (Business Prosperity Charm)** (many shrines): To be displayed in shops or offices.
-   **Kin-un Omamori (Financial Luck Charm)**: To be carried in your wallet.
-   **Zeniarai (Money Washing)** (Enoshima Shrine, Koami Shrine, etc.): A practice of purifying money with sacred spring water within the shrine grounds.
-   **Kumade (Rake)** (Tori-no-Ichi markets, etc.): An auspicious item for business prosperity.
-   **Omikuji (Fortune Slip)**: To be consulted as a reference for your financial fortune.

**Please remember to return your omamori to the shrine where you received them after about a year**, and replace old wallet charms with new ones.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Will my money immediately increase after visiting a financial luck shrine?**

A. Shrines are places for **"aligning your feelings and intentions,"** not for instantly increasing your wealth. It is traditionally believed that financial luck will eventually come as a result of strengthening your **"resolve to continue your work with integrity."**

**Q2. How much should I offer as an osenbako (offering coin)?**

A. There are **no strict rules regarding the amount**. While amounts with auspicious wordplay like **5 yen, 45 yen, or 125 yen** are often preferred, **what truly matters is your heartfelt intention**. Whether you pray with 10,000 yen or express gratitude with a single 5-yen coin, it is believed that the deities receive them equally.

**Q3. Should I spend money washed with Zeniarai immediately or save it?**

A. While practices vary depending on the tradition, the most common approach is to keep it in your wallet as "**seed money**" and spend any amount that accumulates, essentially "**circulating it quickly**." This is a way to respectfully treat money purified in the sacred grounds as a "**money magnet**" or "primer."

**Q4. Can I pray for financial luck related to "investments or lotteries" at a shrine?**

A. **Yes, it's possible**, but Shinto tradition has always placed more emphasis on "**prosperity gained through sincere labor**" than on "wealth acquired without effort." Instead of speculative prayers, it's more natural to pray for **"sharpened judgment and decision-making skills."**

**Q5. Is it okay to bring power stones when visiting a shrine?**

A. Yes, many people bring financial luck stones such as **citrine** and **rutilated quartz**. Common practices include holding them in your palm before passing through the torii gate or placing them near your omamori (sacred talismans) after your visit.
