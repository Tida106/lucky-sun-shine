## Characteristics of Chubu's Power Spots

Power spot shrines in the Chubu region are characterized by having **many high-altitude sacred sites** where **"Shinanokuni Mountain Worship," "Mount Fuji Worship," and "Shugendo Sacred Lands"** intersect.

- **Suwa Worship**: A pilgrimage to the four shrines (Kamisha and Shimosha) dedicated to Takeminakata-no-kami, famous for the Onbashira-sai festival.
- **Togakushi Worship**: A pilgrimage to five shrines rooted in the Ama-no-Iwato myth, deeply connected to Shugendo (mountain asceticism).
- **Mount Fuji Worship**: The head shrine of 1,300 Sengen Shrines, with the sacred peak itself as the primary deity.

These revered shrines are easily accessible by Shinkansen, Chuo Main Line, and Tokaido Main Line, making them perfect for **day trips or overnight stays from Tokyo and Nagoya**.

## <span id="suwa"></span>Suwa Taisha (Nagano Prefecture) — Shinano Province's Ichinomiya and Four-Shrine Pilgrimage

Enshrining **Takeminakata-no-kami**, Suwa Taisha is the **Ichinomiya (chief shrine) of Shinano Province**. It's composed of **four main shrines**: Kamisha Honmiya, Kamisha Maemiya, Shimosha Akimiya, and Shimosha Harumiya. It's also one of Japan's oldest shrines, famously known nationwide for the **Onbashira-sai (Pillar Festival)** held once every seven years!

-   **Location**: Suwa City, Chino City, and Shimosuwa Town, Nagano Prefecture (comprising 4 shrines)
-   **Highlights**: The intricate carvings at Kamisha Honmiya, the sacred Onbashira pillars, and the "Nemiri-no-Sugi" (Sleeping Cedar) at Shimosha Akimiya.
-   **Access**: From JR Chuo Main Line's "Chino" Station (for Kamisha) or "Shimosuwa" Station (for Shimosha), a walk or short taxi ride away.
-   **Blessings**: Victory in competitions, protection for agriculture, and national peace and security.
-   **Recommended Season**: For the four-shrine pilgrimage, late spring to early autumn is best. The year of the Onbashira-sai (once every 7 years) is truly special!

## <span id="togakushi"></span>Togakushi Shrine (Nagano Prefecture) — A Shugendo Sacred Site for a Five-Shrine Pilgrimage

This is a **Shugendo sacred site** composed of **five shrines**, rooted in the **Ama-no-Iwato myth**. Each shrine enshrines a different deity, such as **Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-mikoto at Okusha**, **Ame-no-Yagokoro-Omoikane-no-mikoto at Chusha**, and **Kuzuryu-Okami at Kuzuryu-sha**. The **cedar avenue leading to Okusha** is a breathtaking sight, highly popular among photography enthusiasts!

-   **Location**: Togakushi, Nagano City, Nagano Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The magnificent cedar avenue leading to Okusha, the three sacred cedars at Chusha, Kuzuryu-sha, and the charming Togakushi Soba villages.
-   **Access**: Approximately a 1-hour bus ride from JR Hokuriku Shinkansen "Nagano" Station.
-   **Blessings**: Good fortune (Okusha), academic success (Chusha), and matchmaking (Kuzuryu-sha).
-   **Recommended Season**: From fresh green foliage to autumn leaves. During winter, the Okusha approach can be quite snowy, so be prepared!

## <span id="fujisan"></span>Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha (Shizuoka Prefecture) — The Head Shrine of All Sengen Shrines in Japan

Enshrining **Konohana-no-Sakuya-bime-no-mikoto**, this is the **head shrine for over 1,300 Sengen Shrines nationwide**! It's built on a grand belief system, with **Mount Fuji itself** revered as the primary deity, and the **Okunomiya (inner shrine) at the summit of Mount Fuji** also being part of its sacred grounds. It has been deeply revered since ancient times as the **Ichinomiya of Suruga Province**.

-   **Location**: 1-1 Miyacho, Fujinomiya City, Shizuoka Prefecture
-   **Highlights**: The vibrant vermilion main hall, Wakutama Pond, and the Okunomiya at Mount Fuji's summit (accessible only in summer).
-   **Access**: Approximately a 10-minute walk from JR Minobu Line's "Fujinomiya" Station.
-   **Blessings**: Safe childbirth, matchmaking, warding off evil, and protection for Mount Fuji climbers.
-   **Recommended Season**: Spring for the cherry blossoms (around 500 trees on the grounds!) and the Mount Fuji climbing season (July to September).

## Model Itineraries for Exploring Chubu

Here are some recommended plans if you want to visit all three shrines:

### Classic Route: Nagano (Shinshu) 2 Days and 3 Nights

-   **Day 1**: Shinjuku → Limited Express on Chuo Line to Chino → Pilgrimage to Suwa Taisha Kamisha → Overnight stay at Shimosuwa Onsen.
-   **Day 2**: Shimosuwa Akimiya and Harumiya → Travel → Nagano Station → Bus to Togakushi Shrine → Enjoy Togakushi Soba → Overnight stay at Nagano Station.
-   **Day 3**: Continue with Togakushi's five shrines → Return to Tokyo.

### Mount Fuji and Suwa: 1 Day and 2 Nights

-   **Day 1**: Shinjuku → Gotemba → To Fujinomiya → Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha → Overnight stay around Fuji.
-   **Day 2**: Travel → Chino → Pilgrimage to Suwa Taisha Kamisha → Return to Tokyo.

### Solo Visit: Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha

-   **Day Trip from Tokyo**: Tokyo → Shinkansen to Shin-Fuji → Local train to Fujinomiya → Visit the shrine → Return to Tokyo (approx. half a day).

## Key Visiting Tips

Here are some common tips when visiting these three shrines in Chubu:

-   **Be Mindful of Altitude**: Togakushi is around 1,000m (3,280 ft), Suwa just under 800m (2,620 ft), and Mount Fuji ranges from 0m to 3,776m (12,388 ft). Be prepared for temperature differences and sudden weather changes!
-   **You can complete Suwa Taisha's Four-Shrine Pilgrimage in one day!**: If you collect Goshuin (shrine seals) from all four shrines, you'll receive a special **souvenir**.
-   **Togakushi requires approximately a 2km (1.2 mile) walk along the Okusha approach**: Comfortable walking shoes are highly recommended.
-   **Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha's Okunomiya (inner shrine) is open only in summer**: Outside of climbing season, please visit the main hall (Honmiya).
-   **Beware of snow and ice in winter**: Togakushi and Suwa are areas with heavy snowfall.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can the Four-Shrine Pilgrimage at Suwa Taisha be completed in one day?**

A. **Absolutely!** You can visit them in the order of **Kamisha Honmiya → Kamisha Maemiya → Shimosuwa Harumiya → Shimosuwa Akimiya**, which takes approximately **2 to 3 hours by car**. If you're using public transportation, it might take **half a day to a full day**, so a **car or taxi** is more efficient.

**Q2. How long does the Okusha approach at Togakushi take?**

A. It's about **30 to 40 minutes one way** (approximately 2km, with a cedar avenue and stairs). For a round trip, allow **1 to 1.5 hours**. **It can be dangerous during winter due to heavy snow**, so summer to autumn is highly recommended!

**Q3. Can I also visit the Okunomiya (inner shrine) at Mount Fuji's summit from Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha?**

A. **Yes, but only during the summer climbing season (July to September)**. Kusushi Shrine (Okunomiya) at the summit of Mount Fuji is part of the sacred grounds. If you plan to combine mountain climbing and shrine visiting, please ensure you have proper preparations and equipment.

**Q4. Are there any other famous shrines in the Chubu region?**

A. **Absolutely!** There are many other prominent shrines in the Chubu and Tokai regions, such as **Atsuta Jingu (Aichi), Ise Jingu (Mie and Kinki), and Nangu Taisha (Gifu)**. Sun-chan plans to introduce them on this site in due course!

**Q5. Can I bring crystals with me when visiting a shrine?**
