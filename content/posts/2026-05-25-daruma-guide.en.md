## What is a Daruma? | A "Nanakorobi Yaoki" Lucky Charm Originating from Bodhidharma

A Daruma doll is a traditional Japanese craft shaped like an **"okigaeri koboshi" (a tumbling doll that always gets back up)**, modeled after **Bodhidharma (Daruma Daishi), an Indian monk, in his zazen (seated meditation) posture**.

### Bodhidharma and the Legend of "Menpeki Kunen"

Bodhidharma was a Zen Buddhist monk from India in the 5th to 6th centuries. He traveled to China, where it is said he practiced **"Menpeki Kunen"** — meditating facing a wall for nine years. His indomitable spirit became a symbol of **"nanakorobi yaoki" (fall down seven times, get up eight)**, culminating in the form of the **"Daruma doll that gets up no matter how many times it falls"** in Edo-period Japan.

### Connection to Sericulture Beliefs

Especially in **Takasaki City, Gunma Prefecture**, sericulture farmers developed a custom of buying red Daruma dolls (red being a color for warding off evil) to pray for **"the safe growth of silkworms."** This is the origin of the current **"Takasaki Daruma,"** and Gunma Prefecture still accounts for **about 80% of the national share**, making it Japan's largest Daruma producing region.

### The Philosophy Behind Making Wishes with a Daruma

A Daruma doll is a lucky charm that supports the entire process of **"deciding on a wish, starting to work towards it, and achieving it."** It's not about buying it and forgetting about it; the original way to use it is to **place it where you can see it every day to remember your resolve.**

## The Eye-Painting (Me-ire) Ritual

When you think of a Daruma doll, you think of **eye-painting** — this ritual is the core act that transforms a Daruma from "just a figurine" into a "partner for wish-making."

### Basic Ritual

1.  **Clearly define your wish in your heart** (preferably write it down on paper).
2.  **Paint the "right eye of the Daruma" (the Daruma's left eye)** black with sumi ink (Bodhidharma's left eye = "A" sound = the beginning of all things).
3.  **State your resolve aloud at the moment of painting the eye**: "I will definitely achieve [your wish]."
4.  **Display it in a place you see every day** (during the wish-making period).
5.  **Once your wish comes true, paint the other eye (the Daruma's right eye)**: Say "Thank you" with gratitude.
6.  **A Daruma with both eyes painted should be offered for memorial service** at a shrine or temple.

### About the Sumi Ink and Brush for Eye-Painting

-   Sumi ink and a brush are often included with the Daruma.
-   If not included, **calligraphy sumi ink and a fine brush** can be used as alternatives.
-   **Oil-based markers** are NOT recommended (cannot be corrected, and considered disrespectful for a lucky charm).

### Timing for Painting Both Eyes

-   **Passing an exam → On the day the results are announced.**
-   **Winning an election → Immediately when the vote count is finalized** (a tradition for election Daruma).
-   **Achieving a goal → On the evening of achievement, in front of your family.**
-   **If a wish hasn't come true after one year → It's also a custom to paint both eyes with gratitude and then hold a memorial service.**

## How to Choose Without Failure and What to Avoid

### ✅ Checkpoints When Choosing

1.  **Decide "what you wish for" first before buying** — Match the Daruma's color to your wish.
2.  **Check its facial expression** — Each one is unique. Choose an expression that **makes you feel, "I want to spend a year with this one."**
3.  **Inspect the paint quality** — Cheaper items may have thin paint that fades quickly.
4.  **Check its balance** — Physically test if it falls over and stands back up ("nanakorobi yaoki" symbol).
5.  **Confirm if sumi ink and a brush for eye-painting are included** — If not, plan to prepare them separately.

### ❌ What to Avoid (NG Examples)

-   **Making wishes on multiple Daruma dolls simultaneously** — This can dilute your wishes. **One wish, one Daruma at a time.**
-   **Procrastinating the eye-painting with "I'll do it someday"** — The principle is to do it on the day you buy it.
-   **Leaving a Daruma with one eye unpainted for years** — Whether achieved or not, bring closure within a year.
-   **Painting the eye with a regular pen or oil-based marker** — This is considered disrespectful for a lucky charm.
-   **Hitting or kicking the Daruma** — Treating it roughly while trying to test "nanakorobi yaoki" is not allowed.
-   **Continuing to use a cracked or chipped Daruma** — Thank it and offer it for memorial service.

## Correct Way to Display and Use

### Basic Placement

-   **Slightly above eye level** — Gives a sense of being "watched over."
-   **Facing East or South** — Directions where positive energy (yang) flows.
-   **Next to your kamidana (household altar), if you have one** — As if reporting to the deities.
-   **Avoid direct sunlight** — Can cause fading.

### Recommended Placement by Type of Wish

| Wish | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| **Passing an Exam** | Back right corner of the study desk (visible from the desk, but out of reach). |
| **Business Opening or Prosperity** | Near the cash register in the store, or in front of the office. |
| **Health Prayer** | Near the bedroom entrance, or where the family sees it daily. |
| **Family Safety** | Towards the center of the living room, where the family gathers. |
| **Romantic Fulfillment** | By the bedside in the bedroom, or on top of the shoe cabinet at the entrance. |

### Display Etiquette

-   **If cracked or chipped, offer it for memorial service** and get a new one.
-   **Wipe it clean regularly** — It's believed that accumulating dust can cloud your wishes.
-   **Photos are OK before painting both eyes**, but after, treat it as sacred.
-   **Do not leave it with one eye unpainted for too long** — Daily sight of an "unachieved" wish is not good psychologically.

## Care and Disposal (Daruma Kuyo)

### Daily Care

-   **Dust with a soft, dry cloth**: Approximately once a month.
-   **Paint is susceptible to water** — Do not wipe with a wet cloth.
-   **If fading becomes noticeable** — Move it to a darker spot indoors.

### Daruma Kuyo (Memorial Service) Ritual

Daruma dolls with both eyes painted, or those that have been with you for a year, are traditionally returned through a **"Daruma Kuyo" (Daruma memorial service)** or **"Otakiage" (burning ritual)**.

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| **Daruma Kuyo Festival** | The **Daruma Kuyo Festival** at Takasaki Shorinzan Daruma-ji Temple (January 6th) is nationally famous. |
| **Dondo-yaki** | A fire festival held around January 15th at local shrines. |
| **Shrine's Old Talisman Collection Point** | Place it together with old talismans. Some shrines may not accept them, so check beforehand. |
| **The Shrine or Temple Where it Was Purchased** | Returning it to where it was bought is the original custom. |
| **Home Memorial Service (if unavoidable)** | Wrap it in white paper, add a pinch of salt, say "Thank you" with gratitude, then dispose of it separately from other trash. |

The essence of Daruma Kuyo is not **"throwing it away because the wish didn't come true,"** but rather **"thanking it for supporting you for one year"** and saying farewell with gratitude.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Do I have to paint the Daruma's eye immediately after buying it?**

A. **Within the same day is ideal.** If you buy it without a clear wish and leave it for many days, the solemnity of the "wish-making ritual" diminishes. It's recommended to make your wish clear in words during a quiet moment on the day you buy it, then paint the eye.

**Q2. Can I buy multiple Daruma dolls if I have multiple wishes?**

A. **It's not recommended.** The principle is one wish, one Daruma at a time. If you have multiple wishes, it's more effective for wish-making to **focus on "the most important one right now,"** and welcome the next Daruma after that wish has been granted. Having multiple mini Daruma as "charms" is a different usage and is acceptable.

**Q3. If my wish doesn't come true, should I still paint both eyes?**

A. **Either is OK,** but the traditional custom is to **paint both eyes out of gratitude and offer it for memorial service after one year.** If you view it as a way to "acknowledge your effort for a year, even if unfulfilled," it can help you feel at peace.

**Q4. Can a parent paint the eye on behalf of their child for exam success?**

A. **It's best if the person themselves paints it.** Wish-making is a ritual to give form to "one's own resolve," so it's meaningful for the individual to hold the brush. Parents should remember their role is to watch over, not to act on their behalf.

**Q5. Can I use a used or gifted Daruma doll?**

A. **Generally, it's not recommended.** Daruma dolls are tools for "individual wish-making," so it's believed that residual energy from the previous owner's wish may remain. However, receiving a **brand-new Daruma before eye-painting** as a gift is perfectly fine.

## Editorial Team's View: Daruma is a "Tool for Verbalizing Wishes"

To be honest, from the editorial team's perspective on Daruma dolls, **"being able to verbalize your wish when painting the eye" is far more important than "buying the Daruma doll itself."**

In fact, many people who hesitate to paint the eye are in a state of **"not having a clear wish yet."** This isn't a bad thing; it means **"the Daruma doll is pushing you to practice verbalizing your true wish."** You can't paint the eye if your wish is vaguely "I want to pass" or "I want to succeed." In the process of concretizing **"what, by when, and to what extent,"** your own resolve will also solidify.

### Editorial Team's Recommended "3 Questions Before Eye-Painting"

Before painting the eye, try writing down the answers to these questions:

1.  **"What do I want to achieve, and for what purpose?"**
2.  **"What will I start doing to achieve it?"** (Be specific about actions.)
3.  **"Even if I don't achieve it, what effort would make me feel 'it was worth it' a year from now?"**

If you can write down the answers to these three questions, go ahead and paint the eye without hesitation. If you can't, **postpone painting the Daruma's eye for a week** and use that time to think.
