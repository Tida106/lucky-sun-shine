## Introduction

Known as the "**Emperor's Stone**," Alexandrite is a rare gemstone that uniquely changes color from **green by day to red by night**. It was discovered in Russia's Ural Mountains in 1830 and named after the then-Crown Prince (later Emperor Alexander II). Its colors, **"the green and red of the Russian military uniform,"** led it to be cherished as a special stone by the Russian imperial family.

This article provides a comprehensive overview, from the origins of its gemstone meanings and mineralogical characteristics to the **mechanism behind its day-and-night color change**, its purported effects, care instructions, how to distinguish natural stones from synthetics and simulants, and frequently asked questions. It's structured to serve as a comprehensive guide for anyone wishing to deeply understand this "**stone of change and transformation**," which is counted among the world's three rarest gemstones.

## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Stone Name | Alexandrite |
| Japanese Name | Chrome Variety of Chrysoberyl (Kinryokuseki) |
| Mineral Species | Chrysoberyl |
| Crystal System | Orthorhombic |
| Main Producing Regions | Russia (Ural Mountains - largely depleted today), Brazil, Sri Lanka, East Africa |
| Mohs Hardness | 8.5 |
| Specific Gravity | 3.70 to 3.78 |
| Refractive Index | 1.745 to 1.755 |
| Color | Daylight: Blue-Green to Green / Incandescent Light: Red-Purple to Red |

Alexandrite forms when trace amounts of chromium are present in the mineral **Chrysoberyl**. The optical phenomenon known as "**color change**" occurs because its **absorption spectrum shifts between green and red**. In sunlight (daylight spectrum), green is selectively reflected, while in incandescent light (nighttime spectrum), red is selectively reflected.

With a Mohs hardness of 8.5, it is very durable, second only to sapphire (9), making it **suitable for everyday wear**. It also lacks cleavage, making it relatively resistant to impact. However, **top-quality Alexandrite (with a distinct color change from green by day to red by night)** is among the **world's three rarest gemstones**, characterized by a much lower market supply than even diamonds.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Meanings, History, and Cultural Background

The gemstone meanings of Alexandrite are "**Hidden Passion, Transformation, Premonition, and Good Fortune**."

Legend has it that Alexandrite was accidentally discovered in an emerald mine in the Ural Mountains on April 17, 1830, which was the **16th birthday** of Crown Prince Alexander Nikolaevich (later Emperor Alexander II) of Russia. The day after its discovery, a gemologist brought a green crystal home and observed it under candlelight, realizing it had "**turned into a red stone**." It was then presented to the imperial court as "**the stone that is green by day and red by night**."

Since the Russian military uniform at the time featured a **"combination of green and red,"** it became known as **"the stone symbolizing Russia itself"** and held special significance among the Russian imperial family and nobility. Until the Ural Mountains' mines were depleted in the late 19th century, it was the world's sole source.

Since the 20th century, deposits have also been found in Brazil, Sri Lanka, and East Africa, but stones rivaling the **vibrant color change of Uralian Alexandrite** are rare. Most Alexandrites on the market today come from Brazil and Sri Lanka, and the majority exhibit **only a slight color change**.

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing Methods and Care

Alexandrite is robust with a **Mohs hardness of 8.5** and is a versatile stone that **responds well to almost all cleansing methods**.

- **Running Water**: Briefly rinse with lukewarm water.
- **Placing on a Quartz Cluster or Tumbled Stones**: A classic overnight cleansing method.
- **Smudging with White Sage Smoke**: 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient.
- **Moonlight Bath and Sunlight Bath**: Place by a window for three days before and after a full or new moon. It is also resistant to sunlight.
- **Sound Bath with Tuning Fork or Crystal Tuner**: Within the range that does not damage metal settings.

Cleansing methods to avoid:

- **Burying in coarse salt for extended periods** (as it can discolor metal settings or plating).
- **Prolonged use of an ultrasonic cleaner** (can potentially expand cracks in stones with numerous inclusions).
- **Strong impacts** (While Mohs hardness 8.5 is robust, even untreated stones have limits).

For daily care, simply soak it in **lukewarm water with a mild, diluted detergent** for 5 minutes, gently scrub the entire setting with a soft brush, rinse thoroughly, and then wipe dry. If left with sebum buildup, its beautiful color change can become dull, so making it a **monthly habit** will maintain its beauty for a lifetime.

## <span id="fake"></span>Distinguishing Natural, Synthetic, and Similar Stones

- **Natural Alexandrite** exhibits a **distinct color-change effect**. The more vivid the shift from **"blue-green to reddish-purple,"** the higher its value.
- **Synthetic Alexandrite** has the same chemical composition as natural stone but can be identified by its **inclusion patterns**. It will be clearly marked "Synthetic" on a gemological report.
- **Synthetic Corundum (Synthetic Sapphire plus Chromium)**: An "**Alexandrite-like" synthetic stone** that has been on the market since the 1900s. Be cautious if the color change is too vivid or appears overly artificial.
- **Color Change Garnet**: A natural color-change stone. It is less expensive than Alexandrite, but its color-change pattern is different.
- **Color Change Sapphire**: Exhibits a blue to purple shift. It leans more towards blue than Alexandrite.
- The most reliable method is to choose stones **accompanied by a gemological report (from GIA, CGL, Gübelin, and others).**

It is not uncommon to encounter **Alexandrite that shows only a slight color change**. Most stones from Brazil and Sri Lanka do not display the dramatic color change of Uralian Alexandrite. If it is **clearly stated as "Alexandrite" on the gemological report**, it is genuine, but the **vividness of the color change can alter the price by several to dozens of times.**

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can Alexandrite be worn?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 8.5, it is sufficiently hard and durable to be **passed down through generations**. While the platinum and gold settings may show wear after 10 to 20 years, the stone itself can be enjoyed almost indefinitely if the **setting is regularly re-polished and re-worked**.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear it in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. For **untreated Alexandrite**, a short bath is generally fine, but chlorine in pools and sulfur in hot springs can discolor the metal setting. **During sports, there's a risk of the stone detaching from its setting due to impact**, so it's safer to **make a habit of removing it**. As a rare gemstone, **minimizing the risk of loss** is also crucial.

**Q3. Why does Alexandrite change color?**

A. Alexandrite possesses a unique optical property where its **absorption spectrum shifts between green and red**. Since sunlight (daylight) contains more green spectrum, it appears green, and since incandescent light (nighttime indoor light) contains more red spectrum, it appears red. Its appearance is determined by the **color temperature of the light source**, making it a pure physical phenomenon.

**Q4. Are Alexandrites from Brazil and Sri Lanka inferior?**

A. **Not at all**. While Uralian Alexandrite is indeed legendary and considered the pinnacle, **Brazilian Alexandrite (especially from Espírito Santo)** can exhibit excellent, vivid color changes. Sri Lankan stones are often **larger and have high transparency**, offering distinct charms depending on the origin. The **"color change evaluation" on a gemological report** is often more important than the country of origin.

**Q5. I'm not born in June, but can I still wear Alexandrite?**

A. Absolutely! Birthstones are merely **"stones with particularly good compatibility,"** and there's no problem for those born in other months who wish to resonate with Alexandrite's meanings of "transformation and hidden passion." It is a stone with good compatibility, regardless of birth month, for **those experiencing life transitions or working in professions with dual aspects.**

**Q6. My Alexandrite doesn't show much color change, is it still genuine?**

A. **Even with a slight color change, if it's stated as "Alexandrite" on the gemological report, it's genuine.** Many Alexandrites from Brazil and Sri Lanka show only a "subtle color shift" to the naked eye. The trick to choosing without regret is not to expect the **dramatic color change of Uralian Alexandrite** too much. Genuine Alexandrites definitely exist even in the **more accessible price range (around 100,000 to 500,000 JPY or similar dollar value).**
