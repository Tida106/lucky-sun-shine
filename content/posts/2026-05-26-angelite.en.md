## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Content |
|---|---|

| Refractive Index | 1.569-1.614 |
| Color | Pale sky-blue to light blue (sometimes with white inclusions) |

Angelite is a **pale blue to sky-blue variety of anhydrite (CaSO₄)**, a mineral whose blue color is attributed to **trace amounts of strontium**. It belongs to the same calcium sulfate family as **Selenite (CaSO₄·2H₂O)**, with the key distinction being its **lack of water molecules**.

The name "Angelite" is a **trade name or commercial name** and not its formal mineralogical designation. When blue anhydrite was discovered in Peru in **1987**, it was commercially named "Angelite" due to its **sky-like color** and its symbolism as a **"healing vibration reminiscent of angels."** It is important to understand that this is a modern trade name born from the **context of the New Age movement**.

With a Mohs hardness of 3-3.5, it is **relatively soft for a gemstone** and can be scratched by a fingernail. It is also **sensitive to water**, and prolonged immersion can cause surface degradation. The key to a long-lasting relationship with Angelite is to treat it more as a "**healing stone for display**" than as "**jewelry to wear**."

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care Methods

With a Mohs hardness of 3-3.5, Angelite is soft and **sensitive to water**, requiring delicate handling.

Acceptable Cleansing Methods:

-   **Place on a Quartz Cluster or Tumbled Stones**: The safest and most common method.
-   **Pass Through White Sage Smoke**: 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight Bath**: Place near a window for 3 days around the full moon.
-   **Sound Bath with a Tuning Fork or Crystal Tuner**: Effective if it does not touch metal.

Methods to Avoid:

-   **Water/Running Water** (Anhydrite readily absorbs moisture, leading to surface degradation).
-   **Coarse Salt** (may absorb moisture and damage the surface).
-   **Ultrasonic Cleaner** (risk of expanding cracks).
-   **Prolonged Direct Sunlight** (risk of fading for its pale blue color due to UV rays).
-   **Long-term Storage in Humid Places** (moisture can damage the crystal structure).

For daily care, simply **lightly wipe with a soft, dry cloth**. Never washing it with water is the absolute rule for a lasting relationship with Angelite.

## <span id="fake"></span>How to Distinguish Natural and Similar Stones

-   **Natural Angelite** has a **soft, pale sky-blue hue** and naturally exhibits **slight whitish mottling**.
-   **Confusion with Selenite**: Though both are calcium sulfates and similar in appearance, Selenite exhibits a **transparent to white fibrous luster**, while Angelite has a **uniform pale blue texture**.
-   **Confusion with Dyed Magnesite**: Magnesite dyed blue sometimes circulates as "Angelite-like." Can be distinguished by touch and specific gravity.
-   **Confusion with Howlite Turquoise (Dyed Howlite)**: Dyed howlite is a more vivid blue, while Angelite is a paler sky-blue—distinguishable by color.
-   **Glass Imitations**: Plastic and glass imitations also exist. Can be distinguished from genuine stone by specific gravity, coolness to the touch, and texture.
-   **Choosing one with a gemstone certificate is the most reliable method.**

The **essence of choosing Angelite** lies in its **soft, pale sky-blue color**. A blue that is too vivid or artificially uniform is a sign to suspect dyed or similar stones.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How many years can Angelite be worn?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 3-3.5, it is **relatively soft for a gemstone** and can be scratched by a fingernail. **If handled carefully, avoiding strong impact, friction, and water**, it can be enjoyed for several years. However, it is not a type of stone meant to be passed down through generations, like diamonds or sapphires. It is more realistic to approach it as a "**charm for a few years**."

**Q2. Is it okay to wear Angelite in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. Please consider **all of these to be 'not okay.'** As it is **fatally sensitive to water**, hot water, pools, hot springs, and sweat during sports—all will degrade Angelite. "**Keep it absolutely away from water**" is the cardinal rule for Angelite.

**Q3. Angelite and Selenite: Which is stronger for "angelic connections"?**

A. Both are spoken of as "**angelic healing stones**," but they are said to **have different personalities**:
-   **Angelite**: **Pale sky-blue**. Supports "**communication and dialogue with angels**."
-   **Selenite**: **Transparent moon goddess stone**. Possesses the "**power to purify other stones**."
The correct approach is to choose based on your purpose, and many enthusiasts even carry **both as a pair**.

**Q4. Can Angelite help one "talk to angels"?**

A. This is a widely discussed efficacy as **spiritual symbolism**, but it has **not been medically or scientifically verified**. Its true purpose is to be a **protective charm** that supports feelings such as "**desiring to feel watched over**" and "**wishing to have quiet moments looking up at the sky**."

**Q5. What is the difference between Angelite and Howlite Turquoise (Dyed Howlite)?**

A. **Angelite** is **natural pale sky-blue anhydrite**, while **Howlite Turquoise** is **white howlite that has been dyed blue**. They are entirely different minerals. They can be distinguished by **differences in color** (Angelite is paler, dyed howlite is more vivid) and **texture** (Angelite is uniform, dyed howlite often has a web-like pattern).

**Q6. What if my Angelite accessory chips or degrades due to water?**

A. In the world of power stones, it's traditionally interpreted as the stone "**acted as a proxy or shield**" for you. As it is a soft and delicate stone, chipping or degradation is not uncommon. It is common practice to return it to the earth with gratitude or carefully store it away in a drawer. Its **affordable price point** also makes it easy to welcome a new stone, which is a positive aspect of Angelite.
