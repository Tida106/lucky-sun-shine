Aragonite is a **calcium carbonate (CaCO₃)** mineral that shares the **same chemical composition as Calcite** but has a different crystal structure. This makes them **"polymorphs."** Even with the same chemical composition, their differing crystal systems (Aragonite is orthorhombic, Calcite is trigonal) result in significant differences in crystal form and physical properties.

Its name originates from the **"Aragon region of Spain,"** where it was discovered and named in 1797. The Japanese name, **"Arare-ishi (hailstone),"** comes from its crystal aggregates resembling snow pellets. **The most commonly distributed type is the "honey-colored (yellowish-brown) radial crystal" from Morocco,** with its unique **"Sputnik-type" (satellite-shaped)** formation, where **needle-like crystals radiate in multiple directions,** being prevalent in the market.

With a Mohs hardness of 3.5 to 4, it is considered **relatively soft for a gemstone,** easily scratched even by a fingernail. **It is not suitable for jewelry** and is primarily used as **raw stones, tumbled stones, or decorative pieces for the home.**

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care

Aragonite has a **Mohs hardness of 3.5 to 4,** making it soft, and it is **weak against acids due to its calcium carbonate composition,** requiring specific care.

OK Cleansing Methods:

-   **Placing it on a Clear Quartz cluster or tumbled stones**: The safest and most common method.
-   **Passing it through White Sage smoke**: 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight bathing**: Place it near a window 3 days before or after a full moon.
-   **Sound bathing with a tuning fork or crystal tuner**: Effective if it does not touch metal.

Cleansing Methods to Avoid:

-   **Running water or washing with water** (brief exposure is okay, but **prolonged immersion will degrade the surface**).
-   **Coarse salt** (being a soft stone, its surface can be scratched).
-   **Acidic substances** (calcium carbonate **dissolves in vinegar, lemon juice, or acidic detergents** — absolutely NOT recommended).
-   **Ultrasonic cleaners** (may cause cracks to expand).
-   **Prolonged exposure to direct sunlight** (colored Aragonite risks fading).

For daily care, **gently wipe it with a soft, dry cloth.** **Keeping acidic substances absolutely away** from it is the most important point for maintaining Aragonite's beauty for a long time.

## <span id="fake"></span>Distinguishing Natural Aragonite and Similar Stones

-   **Natural Aragonite (Sputnik type)** has a unique shape where **needle-like crystals radiate in multiple directions.** It can feel **surprisingly light** when held.
-   **Difference from Calcite**: They have the **same chemical composition (CaCO₃)** but different crystal systems:
    -   **Aragonite**: Orthorhombic, with **needle-like/radial crystals.**
    -   **Calcite**: Trigonal, with **rhombohedral crystals.**
    -   **Birefringence**: Calcite shows strong birefringence, while Aragonite shows almost none.
-   **Relationship with Pearls and Coral**: **Pearls, coral, and seashells also have a calcium carbonate crystal structure** — broadly classified as "Aragonite-like."
-   **Glass Imitations**: There are glass imitations that mimic the radial crystal structure. They can be distinguished from genuine stones by **weight, coolness to the touch, and texture.**
-   **Dyed Aragonite**: Some colored Aragonite pieces on the market are dyed. Distinguishable by the **naturalness of the color distribution.**
-   **Choosing with a certificate of authenticity** is the most reliable method.

The popularity of the **"Sputnik type"** stems from its visual appearance of **needle-like crystals radiating in multiple directions,** which perfectly aligns with the symbolism of **"grounding."** **Well-formed and larger crystals** are highly valued.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can I wear Aragonite?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 3.5 to 4, it is **relatively soft for a gemstone,** easily scratched even by a fingernail. **"Placing" it rather than "wearing" it** is the primary use, and **jewelry-grade Aragonite is rare.** If handled carefully, avoiding strong impacts, water, and acids, you can enjoy it for several years to a decade.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear it in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. **Absolutely NOT** for all of these. **Calcium carbonate dissolves in acidic substances,** so **sweat, sulfur from hot springs, chlorine from pools, and soap scum from baths** will all degrade Aragonite. **Acquiring it as a "display stone" rather than jewelry** is its intended use.

**Q3. Should I choose Aragonite or Calcite?**

A. They have the **same chemical composition (CaCO₃)** but are treated as distinct due to their **different crystal structures and symbolisms**:
-   **Aragonite**: **Sputnik-type needle-like radial crystals.** Strong symbolic connection to **grounding.**
-   **Calcite**: **Rhombohedral crystals.** Symbolizes **light amplification and purification.**
The correct choice depends on your **intended use,** and many admirers own both as a pair.

**Q4. Is Aragonite's "grounding effect" real?**

A. **It has not been scientifically or medically proven.** The mineralogical fact that **"calcium carbonate = same composition as earth and coral"** led to its widespread association with the symbolism of **"earth connection."** It's not that **"placing Aragonite automatically calms you,"** but rather that **"every time you see Aragonite, you are reminded to get your feet on the ground"** — that ritualistic use is its true way of engagement.

**Q5. Is it true that drinking "Aragonite water" makes you healthy?**

A. **Absolutely do not do that.** **Aragonite is soluble in water,** and it has no health benefits. While there is a modern spiritual practice of "crystal elixirs" (water infused with stones), it is a mineralogical fact that **calcium carbonate stones dissolve in water, so they should never be used for drinking.**

**Q6. What should I do if my Aragonite accessory chips or degrades from water?**

A. In the world of power stones, it's traditionally believed that the stone **"took the hit for you."** As it is a soft and delicate stone, chipping or degradation is not uncommon. It's generally handled by returning it to the earth with gratitude or carefully storing it in a drawer. This approach also beautifully aligns with its symbolism as **"a stone that returns to the earth."**
