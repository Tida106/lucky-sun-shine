Black Tourmaline is the **most iron-rich variety within the Tourmaline group**, with **Schorl** being its official mineralogical name. While it belongs to the **same Tourmaline group** as Pink Tourmaline and Green Tourmaline, its **overwhelmingly high iron content** gives it its deep, jet-black color.

A common characteristic of all tourmalines is their **pyroelectricity and piezoelectricity**—physical phenomena where they **generate an electric charge when heated or rubbed**. This property is the origin of its Japanese name "**Denkiseki**" (electric stone) and its traditional uses for "**warding off negative energies and dealing with electromagnetic fields**."

With a Mohs hardness of 7 to 7.5, it is sufficiently hard for daily use, and **it has no cleavage, making it quite resistant to impact**. It is widely available in various forms such as rough stones, tumbled stones, and bracelets. It is also popular as an entry-level gemstone, allowing you to start your authentic power stone journey at an accessible price range (from a few thousand yen).

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Properties and Historical and Cultural Background

Black Tourmaline's stone properties are "**Strong Protection, Purification, Grounding, and Endurance**."

The name "Tourmaline" itself comes from the Sinhala word (Sri Lanka) "**turamalli**, meaning 'stone of mixed colors'." Within this group, Schorl (black) derives its name from the German word "**Schorl**." Since ancient times, it has been known among **European miners** as a "stone that absorbs negative energies," and they had a custom of bringing it into the mine shafts.

In medieval Europe, **alchemists** kept Black Tourmaline in their laboratories as a "**stone to protect against evil spirits**." It was also believed that **shamans and sorcerers** wearing it during rituals could create a "**barrier that blocks the entry of negative energies**."

In 1703, a Dutch jeweler discovered the electrification phenomenon of heated tourmaline, calling it a "**mysterious stone that attracts ashes**," which caused a stir in the European scientific community. This became the origin of the Japanese name "Denkiseki" (electric stone) and modern legends such as "**a stone that generates negative ions**" and "**a stone that absorbs electromagnetic waves**."

Since the latter half of the 20th century, with the widespread adoption of **PCs, televisions, and smartphones**, the demand for Black Tourmaline as a "**protective stone for EMF shielding**" has surged. Today, it is one of the most widely circulated power stones, traditionally placed on **desks, in bedrooms, and at entrances** as a stone for warding off negative energies.

## <span id="fake"></span>Identifying Natural and Similar Stones

-   **Natural Black Tourmaline (Schorl)** typically has a **prismatic crystal structure** and often shows **vertical striations (grooves)**.
-   **Confusion with Morion (Black Quartz)**: Black Tourmaline is an electric stone (tourmaline), while Morion is a black variety of quartz (crystal) and a distinct mineral. They can be distinguished by their **crystal structure and the direction of their striations**.
-   **Confusion with Onyx (Black Onyx)**: This is a black variety of chalcedony and a distinct mineral. It can be distinguished by its **translucency and luster**.
-   **Confusion with black basalt and obsidian**: Obsidian is vitreous (glassy) and has a different luster than Black Tourmaline.
-   **Glass imitations**: There are also rough stone-like imitations. You can distinguish them from genuine stones by their **specific gravity, coolness to the touch, and the presence or absence of striations**.
-   Choosing a stone **accompanied by a gemological certificate** is the most reliable way.

"**The difference between Black Tourmaline and Morion (Black Quartz)**" is a frequently asked question:
-   **Black Tourmaline** = Tourmaline group (iron tourmaline). Characterized by **prismatic crystals and vertical striations**.
-   **Morion** = Black variety of quartz (crystal). Characterized by **hexagonal prismatic crystals**.

Both are said to offer protection from evil, but **they are entirely different minerals scientifically**.
