## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

Bloodstone is a variety of **chalcedony**, characterized by a **deep green base color with red spots of iron oxide** scattered throughout. Its official mineralogical name is **Heliotrope**, derived from the Greek words **"Helio = sun" and "Tropein = turning/facing,"** referring to ancient observations that **"when immersed in water and turned toward the sun, its red spots reflect."**

With a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, it is durable enough for daily wear and **resistant to impact due to its lack of cleavage**. It has been cherished since ancient times as a material for seals, cameos, and carvings. The number, size, and arrangement of its red spots vary greatly, giving each Bloodstone a unique charm, as **no two are exactly alike**.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Symbolism, History, and Cultural Background

Bloodstone's symbolism includes "**courage, devotion, decisiveness, and endurance**."

In ancient Babylonia and Egypt, it was prized since before recorded history as an "**offering stone to the gods**" and an "**amulet against evil**," even used in royal seals. In ancient Rome, it was carried into battle as a "**warrior's guardian stone**," with a widespread belief that "**those who wear it will not suffer fatal wounds**."

In medieval Christian tradition, it was believed to have been "**born when Christ's blood dripped onto green jasper during his crucifixion**," becoming a jewel of special significance in churches and monasteries as a "**symbol of Christ's blood**." From this tradition, it also earned the name "**Martyr's Stone**," and has been passed down to modern times as a symbol of "**devotion, sacrifice, and rebirth**."

Medieval European alchemists believed that "**wearing Bloodstone enhanced magical powers by borrowing the sun's energy**," and it frequently appeared in alchemical texts under the name Heliotrope (the stone that turns to the sun). Although it was recognized as a **March birthstone** only in the 20th century, its symbolism deeply resonates with the seasonal feeling of "**March = the beginning of spring = having the courage to embrace a new season**."

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care Methods

Bloodstone has a **Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7**, making it durable enough for daily wear, and it **responds well to many cleansing methods**.

-   **Placing it on a quartz cluster or in tumbled quartz chips**: A classic overnight cleansing method.
-   **Passing it through white sage smoke**: One to two minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight or Sunlight exposure**: Place it by a window for three days before and after a full or new moon. As its name Heliotrope suggests, it has a **deep connection with the sun**.
-   **Running water**: Short rinses with lukewarm water are fine.
-   **Sound bath with a tuning fork or crystal tuner**: Effective if careful not to touch metal.

Cleansing methods to avoid:

-   **Burying it in coarse salt for extended periods** (may scratch the surface).
-   **Long-term use of an ultrasonic cleaner** (may expand cracks at inclusions).
-   **Strong detergents or acidic substances** (may discolor the red iron spots).

For daily care, **gently wiping with a soft cloth** is fundamental. If sweat or sebum accumulates, quickly rinse it with lukewarm water and immediately wipe it dry.

## <span id="fake"></span>Identifying Natural and Similar Stones

-   **Natural Bloodstone** is characterized by the unique **arrangement, size, and color of its red spots**, meaning **"no two are exactly alike."**
-   **Dyed quartz or jasper**: Similar-looking dyed stones are on the market. Distinguish them by the **gradation of color and the realism of the spots**.
-   **Green Jasper**: Similar to Bloodstone's base color, but any stone **without red spots** is jasper.
-   **Blood Jasper**: Sometimes difficult to distinguish clearly, and trade names can cause confusion.
-   **Glass imitation**: There are also glass imitations that replicate the pattern. Can be distinguished from genuine stones by **specific gravity, coolness to the touch, and texture**.
-   The most reliable way to ensure authenticity is to choose stones **with a certification**.

The balance between the "**clarity of the red spots**" and the "**depth of the green base color**" is essential when choosing Bloodstone. Traditionally, it is best to select stones where there is a **beautiful contrast between the base color and the spots**, rather than those with too many or too few spots.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can Bloodstone be worn?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, it's durable enough for daily wear and **can be passed down through generations**. Its history of being used in **seals and cameo carvings** since ancient times demonstrates its ability to last for hundreds of years with proper care. While the platinum or gold setting may wear down in 10-20 years, **reworking the setting** periodically allows you to enjoy the stone for a long time.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear Bloodstone in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. **Short baths are generally fine**, but chlorine in pools and sulfur in hot springs can discolor the metal setting. **Strong detergents and acidic substances** may also discolor the iron content in the red spots, so it's safest to **avoid contact with chemicals**. For sports, there's a risk of the setting dislodging due to impact, so **developing the habit of taking it off** will ultimately make it last longer.

**Q3. What's the difference between Bloodstone and Jasper?**

A. **Bloodstone** is **green chalcedony (microcrystalline quartz) with red spots** scattered throughout. **Jasper** is **opaque quartz rich in impurities**, coming in various colors like green, red, brown, and yellow. The classic definition of Bloodstone is "**green jasper plus red spots**," but there is also a trade name "**Blood Jasper,**" and some individual stones can be difficult to distinguish clearly.

**Q4. Can I wear Bloodstone if I'm not born in March?**

A. Absolutely! A birthstone is merely a stone with **"particularly good compatibility,"** and there is no problem for individuals born in other months who wish to resonate with Bloodstone's symbolism of "courage and decisiveness." It is a stone that pairs well, regardless of birth month, for **those facing important decisions or engaged in selfless work**.

**Q5. Why is Bloodstone also called "Heliotrope"?**

A. **Heliotrope** is derived from the Greek words **"Helio = sun" and "Tropein = turning/facing,"** meaning "**sun-turning stone**." Ancient texts record an observation that "**when Bloodstone is immersed in water and turned toward the sun, its red spots reflect and tint the water red.**" From this property, it came to be known as "Sun's Blood" and "sun-turning stone." **Heliotrope is the formal mineralogical name**, while Bloodstone is the traditional and jewelry trade name.

**Q6. What should I do if my Bloodstone accessory chips?**

A. In the world of crystals, there's a tradition of interpreting this as the stone "**acted as a substitute for you**." While a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7 is quite durable, strong impacts can cause chipping. Since **chipped stones can often be recut through polishing**, first consult the store where you purchased it or a trusted jewelry repair shop.

| Evaluation Factor | Importance |
