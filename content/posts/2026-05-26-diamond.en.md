## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Stone Name | Diamond |
| Japanese Name | Kongoseki (金剛石) |
| Mineral Species | Native element mineral: Carbon (C) |
| Crystal System | Isometric (Cubic) |
| Main Production Areas | South Africa, Botswana, Russia, Australia, Canada |
| Mohs Hardness | 10 (Highest for natural minerals) |
| Specific Gravity | 3.52 |
| Refractive Index | 2.417 |
| Color | Colorless to pale yellow, Fancy colors (pink, blue, yellow, green, and more) |

Diamond is a **crystal of pure carbon (C)**; in terms of composition alone, it's made of the same element as pencil lead (graphite). The difference lies in the arrangement of atoms. The crystal structure, formed only under high temperature and pressure at depths of over 150km below the Earth's surface, gives it a **Mohs hardness of 10, the highest for any natural mineral**. Its refractive index of 2.417 is top-tier for a gemstone, and it's this optical property that allows it to intensely disperse light, creating "**fire**" depending on the cut.

However, "hard" and "unbreakable" are not the same thing. Due to its **cleavage**, a strong impact from a specific angle can cause it to chip instantly. Making it a habit to remove your ring during baths, washing dishes, and sports is basic etiquette for ensuring its longevity.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Language, History, and Cultural Background

The gemstone language of diamond includes "**eternal bonds, indomitable spirit, purity, and sanctity**." Its etymology comes from the ancient Greek word "**adamas**," meaning "**unconquerable**," derived from its overwhelming hardness, believed to be unbreakable.

In ancient India, diamonds were mined and worshipped from around the 4th century BCE, treated as symbolic stones adorning royal crowns. In medieval Europe, knights wore them as "**amulets for courage**." It is said that Archduke Maximilian I of Austria gave the first **diamond engagement ring** to Mary of Burgundy in the 15th century, leading to the widespread culture of "engagement stone equals diamond."

De Beers' "**A Diamond is Forever**" campaign (1947) in the 20th century firmly established the modern image of "eternal love equals diamond" worldwide. While this context was popularized through marketing, the symbolism itself, backed by its physical permanence of Mohs hardness 10, represents an intrinsic meaning humanity has recognized since before myths.

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care Methods

Diamond is a **versatile stone compatible with almost all cleansing methods**.

-   **Running Water**: A quick rinse with lukewarm water is fine for a short time.
-   **Placing on Quartz Cluster or Tumbled Stones**: A classic overnight cleansing method.
-   **Passing through White Sage Smoke**: 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight and Sunlight Bathing**: Place by a window 3 days before and after a full or new moon. It is also resistant to sunlight.
-   **Sound Bath with Tuning Fork or Crystal Tuner**: Within the range that does not damage the metal setting.

Cleansing methods to avoid:

-   **Burying in coarse salt for extended periods** (as it can cause discoloration of the metal setting or plating).
-   **Prolonged operation of an ultrasonic cleaner** (the stone itself may be fine, but if there are many inclusions (internal flaws), cracks could expand).
-   **Strong impact** (potential for chipping along cleavage planes. This is the biggest caution for physical care).

For daily care, simply soak it in **lukewarm water with a diluted mild detergent** for 5 minutes, scrub the back of the setting with a soft brush, rinse thoroughly, and wipe dry. If sebum stains accumulate, the beautiful fire will dull, so making it a monthly habit will help maintain its sparkle for a lifetime.

## <span id="fake"></span>How to Distinguish Natural Diamonds, Synthetic Diamonds, and Simulants

-   **Natural diamonds** usually have microscopic inclusions (internal flaws). "**Too perfect transparency**" is actually a point to suspect synthetic or simulant stones.
-   **Synthetic diamonds (lab-grown)** are chemically and structurally identical to natural diamonds, true carbon crystals. Their price is about 30-50% of natural diamonds. They are clearly marked as "Laboratory-Grown" on GIA grading reports.
-   **Moissanite**: The highest quality simulant. Its refractive index is higher than diamond, and its fire is stronger, so if the light dispersion feels "too flashy," it might be moissanite.
-   **Cubic Zirconia (CZ)**: With a refractive index of 1.95, its fire is weaker, and its higher specific gravity makes it appear slightly smaller for the same carat weight.
-   **White Topaz and White Sapphire**: Hardly exhibit any fire (rainbow flashes).
-   Choosing stones with a **grading report** (from GIA, CGL, and more) is the most reliable method.

Synthetic diamonds are not "fakes" but rather "**diamonds grown in a laboratory from the same material as natural ones**." As a sensible option for those who want to achieve high 4C grades while keeping costs down, they have rapidly gained popularity in the engagement ring market in recent years.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How many years can a diamond be worn?**

A. A Mohs hardness of 10 means it won't wear down during a human lifetime, and its greatest strength is its ability to be **passed down through generations**. However, the platinum or gold setting may wear down in 10-20 years. By regularly having the setting re-worked or re-polished, the stone itself can be enjoyed semi-permanently.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear it in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. A short bath is okay, but it's generally safer to remove it considering the **risk of impact** and the dulling of its fire by soap residue. Chlorine in pools and sulfur in hot springs can discolor the metal setting, and sports carry the risk of strong impacts causing cleavage fractures. Developing the **habit of removing it** will ultimately make it last longer.

**Q3. Do synthetic diamonds (lab-grown) have no "effects"?**

A. As a mineral, it is a carbon crystal completely identical to natural ones, so the majority of enthusiasts believe its gemstone language and symbolic meaning are the same as natural diamonds. If you prioritize "**the billions of years Earth took to create it**," choose natural. If you prioritize "**the balance between 4C and budget**," choose lab-grown. The choice depends on your values.

**Q4. If someone didn't receive an engagement ring, is it okay to buy one for themselves?**

A. In recent years, more and more people are choosing solitaire diamond necklaces or rings as a "**self-reward diamond**." Rather than just being a stone for engagement, choosing to welcome it as a "**stone of commitment to one's own life**" fully resonates with the diamond's gemstone language of "indomitable spirit and purity."

**Q5. Is it unlucky if a diamond accessory chips or falls out of its setting?**

A. In the world of power stones, there's a tradition of viewing it as having "**taken a hit on your behalf**." If it merely fell out of the setting, it can usually be repaired. If it chipped, it can often be re-cut by polishing (though the carat weight will decrease). So, the practical first step is to consult the original store or a trusted jewelry repair shop.

**Q6. I'm not born in April, but can I still wear a diamond?**

A. Of course! Birthstones are merely "**stones with especially good compatibility**." There's no problem at all for those born in other months who wish to resonate with the diamond's gemstone language of "eternal bonds and indomitable spirit." In fact, for April-born individuals, there's the joy of cherishing it as "**their month's stone**," and for those born in other months, the joy of "**welcoming it as a stone of life's resolve**"—both are wonderful ways to connect with it.

## <span id="team-view"></span>Editorial Team's Perspective: Separating "Investment Value" and "Amulet Value"

The way diamonds are discussed often conflates "**asset value**" and "**symbolic value**." Our editorial team recommends clearly separating these two when evaluating them.

| Perspective | Natural Diamond | Lab-Grown Diamond | Solitaire CZ / Moissanite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset Value (Resale) | ◎ Value retention over decades | △ Resale market still immature | × Almost zero |
| Rarity as a Mineral | ◎ Formed by Earth over billions of years | × Can be mass-produced in weeks | × Synthetic stone |
| Symbolic Value (Vow, Amulet) | ◎ Classic | ◎ Chemically identical | ○ Depends on "sentiment" |
| Cost Performance | △ High cost | ◎ 30-50% cheaper for the same grade | ◎ Significantly cheaper |

In other words—

-   If you want to "**preserve it as an asset or pass it down through generations**," natural is the only choice.
-   If you "**only want the symbolic value and wish to allocate your budget to 4C grades**," then lab-grown is for you.
-   If you prioritize "**everyday sparkle and casual wearability**," then moissanite or CZ are also reasonable options.

It's not about which is "correct," but the first step to making a choice without regret is to articulate **why you are welcoming this stone into your life**.

### Common Misconceptions

-   ❌ "**Diamonds don't depreciate in value**" → ✅ Small stones under 1 carat are usually less than half their purchase price in the resale market. Asset value only applies to higher grades like **1 carat or more, D color, IF clarity, and with a GIA grading report.**
-   ❌ "**Bigger carat means better**" → ✅ The balance of the 4Cs (Carat, Color, Clarity, Cut) is essential. A good cut determines the fire, so a **well-cut 0.3ct diamond can sparkle more brilliantly than a poorly cut 1ct one.**
-   ❌ "**Synthetic diamonds are fakes**" → ✅ Chemically identical to natural ones. They are **legitimate synthetic gemstones** explicitly marked as "Laboratory-Grown" on grading reports.
