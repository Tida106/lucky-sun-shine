This article provides a thorough overview, from the origins of its gemstone meanings and biological characteristics to the **differences between Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian, and Freshwater types**. It's designed to be a definitive guide for those who not only admire pearls as a cherished jewel but also wish to deeply understand them as **"a stone that accompanies life's milestones."**

## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Stone Name | Pearl |
| Japanese Name | 真珠 (Shinju) |
| Mineral Species | Organic Gemstone (Calcium Carbonate + Conchiolin) |
| Crystal System | Orthorhombic (Aragonite crystal structure) |
| Main Producing Regions | Japan (Akoya), Australia (South Sea), Tahiti (Tahitian), China (Freshwater) |
| Mohs Hardness | 2.5 to 4.5 (Extremely soft) |
| Specific Gravity | 2.65 to 2.85 |
| Colors | White, cream, pink, gray, black, gold, and various others |

Pearls are formed within **Akoya oysters, Pinctada maxima (South Sea oysters), Pinctada margaritifera (Tahitian black-lip oysters), and freshwater mussels (such as Hyriopsis cumingii)**. They are created when calcium carbonate (aragonite) and conchiolin (protein) are layered around an irritant. As a gemstone where **the biological process itself crystallizes**, pearls are truly unique, distinct from mineral gemstones.

With a Mohs hardness of 2.5 to 4.5, pearls are extremely soft for a gemstone, and their biggest weakness is their vulnerability to **sweat, cosmetics, perfume, and acidic substances**. For daily wear, it's essential to develop the habit of "**wiping pearls after wearing them**," and the basic etiquette is to **remove them before water activities, applying makeup, or exercising.**

### Types of Pearls

- **Akoya Pearls**: Traditional Japanese cultured pearls, typically 6-9mm in diameter, known for their elegant white to pinkish luster.
- **South Sea Pearls (White Lip Pearls)**: Produced in Australia and Indonesia, these are large pearls, 10-18mm, in white to golden hues.
- **Tahitian Pearls (Black Lip Pearls)**: From Tahiti, typically 8-15mm, with colors ranging from green to peacock, gray, and black.
- **Freshwater Pearls**: Primarily from China, these pearls come in a wide variety of shapes and are more affordable. Examples include baroque, rice, and button shapes.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Gemstone Meanings and Historical/Cultural Background

The gemstone meanings of pearl are "**purity, health, longevity, wealth, and vibrant beauty**." Its etymology is derived from the **Latin word 'perna,' meaning bivalve**.

In ancient Rome, the legend of **Queen Cleopatra famously dissolving a pearl in vinegar and drinking it** is known as a symbol of wealth and power (regardless of its actual scientific veracity, it's a widely known cultural story). In medieval Europe, pearls were called "**Tears of the Moon**" and "**Tears of Mermaids**," treated as sacred jewels offered to the goddesses of the moon and sea.

In Japan, pearls have been celebrated as "**shiratama**" (white jewels) since the **Kojiki and Manyoshu eras**, and they continue to hold a special place as **formal jewelry for ceremonial occasions** (weddings, funerals, graduation ceremonies, etc.) today. In 1893, **Kokichi Mikimoto successfully cultivated Akoya pearls for the first time in the world**, leading to the widespread availability of pearls that were previously only obtained through natural harvesting. Japan is still known as a producer of **the world's highest quality Akoya pearls**.

The British Royal Family, including **Queen Elizabeth, Princess Diana, and Princess Catherine**, have consistently worn pearls at ceremonial events, maintaining their status as "**the Queen's jewel**."

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care

Pearls are **extremely delicate** gemstones. Many common crystal cleansing methods are NOT suitable, so please learn these specific care instructions.

OK Cleansing Methods:

-   **Gently wipe with a dry, soft cloth**: The safest and almost only method for daily cleansing.
-   **Pass through white sage smoke**: For 1-2 minutes (without direct contact).
-   **Moonlight bath**: Place near a window 3 days before or after a full moon. Pearls have a deep connection to the moon.

Absolute NG Cleansing Methods:

-   **Washing with water or running water**: Conchiolin dissolves, and the surface luster will be lost.
-   **Coarse salt**: Damages the surface and permanently removes the luster.
-   **Ultrasonic cleaners**: Risk of internal conchiolin detaching.
-   **Prolonged exposure to direct sunlight**: Causes discoloration and dehydration.
-   **Cosmetics, perfumes, hairsprays**: Always apply makeup and style hair *before* putting on pearls.

Daily care boils down to: "**Always wipe your pearls with a dry, soft cloth after wearing them.**" If sweat, sebum, or cosmetics are left on them, the surface luster will be permanently lost within 5-10 years. For long-term storage, keep them in a **silk or soft cloth pouch**, in a **place where they won't touch other jewelry**.

## <span id="fake"></span>Distinguishing Genuine, Cultured, and Imitation Pearls

-   **Genuine Pearls (Natural and Cultured)**: When gently rubbed against your teeth, they feel "**gritty**" (due to their layered nacre structure).
-   **Imitation Pearls (Fake Pearls)**: Glass or plastic coated on the surface. When rubbed against your teeth, they feel "**smooth**."
-   **Cultured and Natural**: Over 99% of pearls are cultured. **Cultured pearls involve inserting a nucleus into the oyster to grow**, while **natural pearls form completely spontaneously**. Natural pearls are rare, costing millions of yen (hundreds of thousands of dollars) per piece.
-   **Surface Texture**: Genuine pearls are **not perfectly spherical and have slight irregularities (growth lines)**.
-   **Weight**: Plastic imitations are noticeably light. Glass imitations are slightly heavier than genuine pearls.
-   The most reliable way is to choose pearls **with a grading report (e.g., CIBJO, Japan Pearl Promotion Society)**.

The common misconception that "**cultured pearls are fake**" is incorrect; **cultured pearls are genuine pearls**. Because real nacre layers form inside the Akoya oyster, their chemical composition and luster are identical to natural pearls. Due to the balance of price and availability, almost all pearls in modern circulation are cultured.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can pearls be worn?**

A. If handled correctly, they can be passed down for **decades, even generations**. However, they are vulnerable to sweat, cosmetics, water, and friction, so the habit of "**always wiping them dry after wearing**" is an absolute condition for longevity. It's recommended to have necklaces **restrung by a specialist every 5-10 years** to prevent loss due to thread breakage.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear them in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. **Absolutely not for any of these activities**. Hot water, chlorine, sweat, and friction are all major enemies of pearls. The basic rule is to **always remove them before bathing, exercising, or applying makeup**. The traditional Japanese jewelry etiquette of "**put on last, take off first**" is precisely a wisdom to protect pearls.

**Q3. What is the etiquette for wearing pearls at ceremonial occasions?**

A. **Basic Rules**:
-   **White Pearls**: Acceptable for both celebratory and mourning occasions (versatile).
-   **Black Pearls**: Primarily for mourning (though acceptable for some celebratory events, regional differences apply).
-   **Size**: 7-8mm is acceptable for both; 9mm and larger are more for celebratory events; 6mm and smaller are more for mourning.
-   **Strands**: For mourning, **only a single-strand necklace** is appropriate (double strands can imply 'misfortune piling up,' making them unsuitable).
-   **Earrings/Piercings**: Single stud types are acceptable for both.

**Q4. What if pearls yellow or lose their luster?**

A. If the damage is minor, **cleaning and reprocessing at a specialized store** can sometimes restore them. For severe damage, complete restoration is difficult, but it may be possible to **re-sort and re-string the pearls for a necklace** to maintain their appearance. Consult specialists like **Mikimoto or Tasaki**.

**Q5. Can I wear pearls even if I'm not born in June?**

A. Of course! They are an **essential item for ceremonial occasions** and a piece of jewelry to own for life, regardless of your birth month. They also serve as a **family heirloom passed from mother to daughter**, cherished as a gemstone that accompanies women throughout all stages of life.

**Q6. Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian, Freshwater – which should I choose?**

A. **It depends on your purpose**:
-   **Formal wear for ceremonial occasions**: Akoya (traditional Japanese, elegant).
-   **Large and glamorous**: South Sea (large, impactful presence).
-   **To express individuality**: Tahitian (peacock green and other unique colors).
-   **Affordable for versatile daily use**: Freshwater (baroque shapes, casual wear).

## <span id="team-view"></span>Editorial Team's View: Luster (Teri) and Nacre Thickness ('Maki') are Essential

A common misconception when choosing pearls is to **select them based only on size and color**. In reality, the most crucial factors determining a pearl's value are its **"teri" (luster) and "maki" (nacre thickness)**.

| Evaluation Factor | Importance |
|---|---|
| Teri (Luster) | ◎ Most Important. Whether it has a **mirror-like reflection**. |
| Maki (Nacre Thickness) | ◎ Important. Thicker nacre means better durability and luster. |
| Blemishes (Bumps/Dimples) | ○ The fewer, the better, but zero is rare. |
| Color | ○ Subject to preference. Pinkish hues are popular; greenish hues are rare. |
| Shape | △ Round is classic, but baroque shapes can also have value as unique pieces. |
| Size | △ Larger pearls are more expensive but depend on the use (around 8mm is versatile). |

In other words:

-   **"Teri" is more important than "size."**
-   **"Maki" is more important than "color."**
-   **"Shape" depends on the occasion** (round for ceremonial events, baroque also fine for casual wear).

### Common Misconceptions

-   ❌ "**Cultured pearls are fake**" → ✅ Cultured pearls are **genuine pearls**. Over 99% are cultured, but their chemical composition is identical to natural pearls.
-   ❌ "**Larger pearls are more luxurious**" → ✅ **Teri and maki** are the essence. A high-quality 8mm pearl can be more valuable than an ordinary 10mm pearl.
-   ❌ "**Black pearls for funerals**" → ✅ **White pearls are acceptable for both celebratory and mourning occasions**. Black pearls are primarily for mourning, but regional differences apply. **White pearls are the most versatile.**
