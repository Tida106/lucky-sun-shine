## <span id="why-blue"></span>Why Stones for Interpersonal Relationships Tend to be Blue-Hued

Many stones traditionally associated with interpersonal luck are overwhelmingly **blue-hued**—ranging from **pale blue, light blue, turquoise blue, to light green (colors associated with the throat chakra)**. This is not a coincidence, but deeply rooted in the history of cultures worldwide, where **blue has symbolized sincerity in words, calmness, and peace**.

- The **Fifth Chakra (Throat Chakra)** is considered an energy center of blue that corresponds to **communication and honest expression**.
- In ancient Egypt, **lapis lazuli and turquoise** were cherished as stones that supported the words of royalty.
- In modern spirituality, **"Angel Stone (Angelite)" and "Caribbean Sea (Larimar)"** are seen as symbols of calm communication.

From a color psychology perspective, **blue tones are known to regulate the parasympathetic nervous system and calm the heart rate**. This makes them visually fitting choices not only as amulets but also as colors that support the prerequisites for good interpersonal relationships: **"calmly facing others" and "choosing words carefully."** On the other hand, **pink-hued stones (Rose Quartz)** and **green-hued stones (Jade, Chrysocolla, Amazonite)** are cherished as stones that support interpersonal luck in a different way, symbolizing **"compassion, gentleness, and nurturing femininity."**

## <span id="care"></span>Care and Purification Methods

Many stones for interpersonal relationships **range broadly from Mohs hardness 3 to 7, and each has specific vulnerabilities**:

| Stone        | Main Vulnerabilities                             |
| :----------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| Aquamarine   | Vulnerable to sudden temperature changes         |
| Angelite     | **Soft (Mohs 3 to 3.5)**, **critically vulnerable to water** |
| Larimar      | **Blue color fades in direct sunlight**, no prolonged water exposure |
| Rose Quartz  | Risk of fading with prolonged direct sunlight exposure |
| Amethyst     | Purple color fades in direct sunlight            |
| Turquoise    | **Vulnerable to water, salt, and cosmetics**     |
| Jade         | Risk of cleavage fracture from strong impact     |
| Chrysocolla  | **Soft and vulnerable to water and acids**       |
| Amazonite    | Blue-green color fades in direct sunlight        |

Common safe purification methods include **"placing them on a quartz cluster or in tumbled quartz," "passing them through white sage smoke," and "moonlight bathing."** Especially **Angelite, Turquoise, Larimar, and Chrysocolla dislike water**, so please avoid purification by running water.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. If I wear a stone for interpersonal relationships, will my workplace relationships improve?**

A. **It cannot be guaranteed to improve them**. The effects of crystals are **cultural and symbolic meanings** and do not guarantee specific improvements in relationships, resolution of workplace or friendship troubles, or successful reconciliation. **"By carrying an amulet, positive actions might increase, and as a result, the situation might change"** — expecting such an indirect effect is a realistic way to approach them.

**Q2. Which stone helps improve relationships with people I don't get along with?**

A. Traditionally, **Aquamarine, Rose Quartz, and Jade**, among others, are spoken of as symbols of **"harmony in interpersonal relationships."** However, **this does not guarantee that relationships with difficult people will necessarily improve**. Their true purpose is not to change others, but to serve as an amulet that helps you adjust your own mindset. Depending on the situation, **creating distance or consulting a third party** might be important options.

**Q3. Are there any stones to deal with workplace troubles or harassment?**

A. **"No"** is the honest answer. Serious workplace troubles and harassment are **not issues to be solved by stones, and consulting professional organizations such as internal counseling services, labor unions, labor standards inspection offices, or lawyers** should be the top priority. While **Amethyst and Morion** are spoken of as symbols that "ward off bad relationships and negative energy," this refers to **"an amulet that protects one's own mind,"** and they are **not a substitute for practical action.**

**Q4. Is it okay to give stones for interpersonal relationships to children as gifts?**

A. **It depends on the intention**. Giving them as a gift with the sentiment **"I hope you get along well with your friends" or "I wish you to foster wonderful relationships"** is a lovely choice. However, the "effect of making friends" **should not be emphasized to children**. Explaining it as **"something to cherish as an amulet"** will lead to a healthier approach for the child. For **small children, be careful of choking hazards** and do not leave them unattended.

**Q5. Can I get the same stone for my partner or family members to make "pair stones"?**

A. **Yes, you can**. There's a tradition worldwide of having **matching Rose Quartz, Jade, or Turquoise, for example,** as a way to express **"wishing for each other's happiness."** However, they should not be held with the intention of controlling the other person; rather, they should be exchanged as **symbols of a shared desire to cherish your relationship**, which is the healthy way to use them.

**Q6. What if I feel that "my relationships aren't improving even though I have a stone for interpersonal relationships"?**

A. **Stones are "symbols of action," not "action itself."** The true process involves **developing an awareness of "wanting to change how I interact with others"** by having an interpersonal relationship stone, and then translating that into specific actions (honest communication, actively listening to others, re-evaluating boundaries, consulting experts if needed). Always remember that an amulet is a catalyst, but action is up to you.
