## <span id="why-color"></span>Why Career and Competitive Luck Stones Tend to Be "Golden-Brown, Red, and Blue"

Stones traditionally associated with career and competitive luck largely fall into four color categories: **golden-brown (decision-making and focus), red (passion and victory), blue (intellect and calmness), and black (protection and willpower).** This isn't mere coincidence; it reflects the **four distinct facets of professional situations: decision-making, action, protection, and intellect.**

- **Golden-Brown Tones (Tiger's Eye, Rutilated Quartz)**: Decision-making ability to discern the essence, and focus.
- **Red Tones (Ruby, Garnet, Carnelian)**: Passion, victory, and active drive.
- **Blue Tones (Lapis Lazuli, Sodalite)**: Calmness, intellect, and honest judgment.
- **Black Tones (Onyx, Hematite)**: Protection, willpower, and grounding.

From a color psychology perspective, **golden-brown enhances focus, red stimulates active energy, blue calms the parasympathetic nervous system, and black stabilizes willpower.** Thus, this combination of colors makes perfect sense, not only as talismans but also visually, to support **"you when it matters most"** from four different directions.

## <span id="ruby"></span>② Ruby | "Queen of Gems": Victory and Passion

**July's birthstone,** Ruby, is one of the **four most precious gemstones in the world.** Known as the **"Queen of Gems" and the "Stone of Victory and Passion,"** it has a history as a **warrior's protective stone.** As a symbol of **competition, challenge, and crucial moments,** it remains a supreme choice, even today selected for the jewelry of figures like Queen Elizabeth and the Princess of Monaco.

- **Mohs hardness of 9** — second only to diamond, making it durable enough for everyday wear.
- **"Pigeon's Blood"** is the rarest and most prized color.
- Ideal as a protective jewel for **crucial days or years of challenge.**

## <span id="care"></span>Care and Purification Methods

Many career and competitive luck stones have a **Mohs hardness of 6 to 9,** making them sufficiently durable for daily use, but they do have **individual vulnerabilities:**

| Stone          | Main Weaknesses                                                |
|----------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tiger's Eye    | Strong detergents and coarse salt can damage surface stripes.  |
| Ruby           | Glass-filled treated items are vulnerable to heat and detergents. |
| Garnet         | Sensitive to rapid temperature changes.                        |
| Carnelian      | Risk of fading with prolonged direct sunlight exposure.        |
| Onyx           | Prone to chipping from strong impacts.                         |
| Lapis Lazuli   | **Vulnerable to water and salt** (risk of discoloration).      |
| Hematite       | **Rusts with moisture** (avoid storage in high humidity).      |
| Sodalite       | Blue color may fade with direct sunlight.                      |
| Pietersite     | Be cautious of the amphibole fiber direction.                  |

Common **safe purification methods** include **"placing on a crystal cluster or smaller tumbled stones,"** "**passing through white sage smoke,**" and "**moon bathing.**" Especially for **Lapis Lazuli and Hematite, which dislike water,** avoid purification with running water.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Which is the "strongest" stone for boosting career and competitive luck?**

A. **There is no single "strongest" answer.** Culturally, Tiger's Eye is often called the "absolute classic for business settings," and for crucial competitive moments, Ruby is considered supreme. However, **the stone you can cherish and connect with is truly the strongest for you** — this is the essence of choosing crystals. Please make your selection based on a comprehensive consideration of symbolism, budget, and personal preference.

**Q2. If I wear a career luck stone, am I guaranteed a promotion or to pass an exam?**

A. **No, that cannot be guaranteed.** The effects of crystals are **cultural and symbolic in nature,** and they do not guarantee any specific promotion, passing of exams, victory, contract closure, or qualification acquisition. **"By carrying a talisman, positive actions may increase, and as a result, the situation might change"** — expecting such an indirect influence is the realistic way to approach them.

**Q3. Which stones are recommended for students taking exams?**

A. Traditionally, **Sodalite (thought and conviction)** and **Fluorite (the genius stone, concentration)** are cited as the **"two major classics among exam stones."** Lapis Lazuli is also popular as a symbol of intellect. A lightweight use, such as **"placing a tumbled stone in a pencil case,"** is suitable for students. However, remember that they **do not guarantee success,** and **there is no talisman more effective than studying itself.**

**Q4. Should I separate stones for work and for competitive situations?**

A. **Either approach is fine.** Many enthusiasts differentiate their use, choosing **Tiger's Eye or Lapis Lazuli for "everyday work" and Ruby or Garnet for "crucial competitive days."** On the other hand, the style of continuously wearing **"the one stone that brings you the most peace of mind"** every day is equally effective. **The method you can sustain** is what matters most.

**Q5. My Hematite has rusted. What should I do?**

A. Hematite has a **tendency to rust when exposed to moisture.** You can prevent this by **storing it with a desiccant.** If it has already rusted, it's realistic to **gently wipe it with a soft cloth and consider replacing it** depending on its condition. There's also a traditional ritual of gratitude, where you might see it as having "taken your place" or absorbed negativity on your behalf.

**Q6. What if I feel like I have a career luck stone, but I'm not seeing results?**

A. **Stones are "symbols of action," not "action itself."** The true process is that carrying a career luck stone **awakens an awareness of "wanting to change my situation"** — and this awareness then leads to concrete actions (skill development, job searching, qualification acquisition, communication improvement). Remember the premise: the talisman is a catalyst, but action is up to you.
