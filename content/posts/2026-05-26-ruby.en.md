## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Content |
|---|---|

| Japanese Name | Kogyoku (Crimson Gem) |
| Mineral Species | Corundum |
| Crystal System | Trigonal |
| Primary Sources | Myanmar (Mogok region), Mozambique, Thailand, Sri Lanka, Madagascar |
| Mohs Hardness | 9 (Second only to diamond) |
| Specific Gravity | 4.00 |
| Refractive Index | 1.762〜1.770 |
| Color | Vivid Red to Deep Crimson (Chromium is the coloring agent) |

Ruby is the **same corundum mineral as sapphire**. It gets its red color from trace amounts of chromium within aluminum oxide (Al₂O₃). Corundum has an extremely high **Mohs hardness of 9**, second only to diamond, making it ideal for daily wear. It also lacks cleavage, which means it's resistant to breaking from impact – another strength of ruby.

The most prized color is known as "**Pigeon's Blood**," a **pure, deep crimson with a slight purplish tinge**. Traditionally, rubies from Myanmar's (formerly Burma) Mogok region have been considered the finest, but in recent years, **high-quality rubies from Mozambique** have also gained high acclaim in the international market.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Lore, History, and Cultural Background

Ruby's stone lore includes "**Passion, Victory, Blazing Love, and Vitality**." Its etymology comes from the Latin word "**rubeus = red**," which is the root of names like "Ruby" in English and "Rubis" in French, and other languages across Europe.

In ancient Indian Sanskrit, it was called "**Ratnaraj (King of Gems)**" and treated as the highest-ranking stone adorning kings' crowns. In Hinduism, it was revered as the **stone of the sun god Surya**, considered to embody the sun's power, and used for offerings to deities.

In medieval Europe, it was carried into battle as a "**warrior's guardian stone**," and a widespread legend claimed it would "**protect its wearer from harm**." It's even said that warriors in the Burmese kingdom would **incise their skin and insert rubies** to gain protection on the battlefield.

From the 16th century onwards, European royalty and nobility exclusively cherished ruby as "**the most noble red**." Even today, rubies are frequently used in royal jewelry, such as pieces worn by **Queen Elizabeth and the Princess of Monaco**.

## <span id="care"></span>Cleansing and Care

Ruby has a **Mohs hardness of 9**, making it extremely robust and a versatile stone that **responds well to almost all cleansing methods**.

-   **Running Water**: A brief rinse with lukewarm water is fine.
-   **Placing on Quartz Cluster or Tumbled Stones**: A classic overnight cleansing method.
-   **Smudging with White Sage Smoke**: 1 to 2 minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight Bath and Sunlight Bath**: Place by a window 3 days before and after a full or new moon. It is also resistant to sunlight.
-   **Sound Bath with Tuning Fork or Crystal Tuner**: Within a range that doesn't damage metal settings.

Cleansing methods to avoid:

-   **Burying in coarse salt for extended periods** (as it can discolor metal settings or plating).
-   **Ultrasonic cleaner** (Rubies with **glass filling treatment** may have the internal glass removed. Always confirm the presence of treatment at the time of purchase.)
-   **Strong impact** (While corundum is hard, there are limits even for untreated stones.)

For daily care, simply soak it in **lukewarm water with a diluted neutral detergent** for 5 minutes, gently brush the entire setting (even the back), rinse thoroughly, and then dry with a soft cloth. If left with sebum stains, its beautiful deep red can appear dull, so making it a **monthly habit** will ensure its sparkle lasts a lifetime.

## <span id="fake"></span>How to Distinguish Natural, Synthetic, and Treated Stones

-   **Natural rubies** often show internal "**silk**" (needle-like rutile inclusions). Even top-tier Pigeon's Blood quality rubies typically exhibit minute inclusions.
-   **Synthetic rubies (flux method and Verneuil method)** have the same chemical composition as natural ones but can be identified by **gas bubbles or curved growth lines**. They are explicitly labeled "Synthetic" on gemological reports.
-   **Heat treatment** is a standard practice for the majority of rubies in circulation. Its impact on value is limited.
-   **Glass filling treatment (lead glass filling)** is a process used to fill surface fractures. Since it significantly lowers the value, always confirm its presence at the time of purchase.
-   **Similar stones**: Red spinel (same red color but not corundum), red garnet (different refractive index), red tourmaline, and others.
-   Choosing rubies with a **gemological report (from GIA, CGL, Gübelin, etc.)** is the most reliable approach.

Synthetic rubies are not "fakes," but rather "**legitimate synthetic gemstones grown in a laboratory from the same materials as natural ones**." They are widely available in the jewelry market as a reasonable option for those who want to enjoy a vivid red color at a more affordable price.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can a ruby be worn?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 9, it is the second hardest natural mineral after diamond. It boasts durability that can be **passed down through generations**. While the platinum or gold settings may show wear after 10-20 years, the stone itself can be enjoyed indefinitely with **periodic re-setting or polishing** of the mounting.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear ruby in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. For **untreated or only heat-treated rubies**, a short bath is generally okay. However, chlorine in pools and sulfur in hot springs can discolor the metal settings. For **glass-filled rubies**, heat and detergents can cause the glass to come out, so it's safest to **remove them completely**. Always confirm the treatment status at the time of purchase.

**Q3. What's the difference between Pigeon's Blood ruby and other rubies?**

A. "**Pigeon's Blood**" refers to a **pure, deep crimson with a slight purplish tinge**. Authoritative gemological laboratories like Gübelin Gem Lab and SSEF (Swiss Gemological Institute) certify only the finest rubies from Myanmar and Mozambique that meet specific color criteria. Prices can be 3 to 10 times higher than regular rubies, making them a **top-tier investment class**.

**Q4. Can I wear ruby even if I'm not born in July?**

A. Absolutely! Birthstones are simply **"stones that are particularly compatible,"** and there's no issue for those born in other months who wish to resonate with ruby's stone lore of "passion and victory." For **business owners, sales professionals, managers, athletes**, and other professions with **many competitive moments**, ruby is a compatible stone regardless of their birth month.

**Q5. What if my ruby accessory comes out of its setting?**

A. In the world of crystals, there's a tradition of viewing this as the stone "**taking its place for you**" or acting as a protector. Since corundum is very hard, the stone itself rarely breaks. If it has **merely come out of its setting, it can often be repaired**, so first consult the store where you purchased it or a trusted jewelry repair shop.

**Q6. Do synthetic rubies have no "effects"?**

A. As a mineral, synthetic rubies are the same corundum crystal as natural ones, so the majority of enthusiasts believe their **stone lore and symbolic meaning are identical to natural rubies**. If you value "**the millions of years the Earth took to create it**," then natural is for you. If you prioritize "**vibrant red and cost-effectiveness**," then synthetic is a good choice. The decision comes down to your personal values.

| Color State | Assessment |
|---|---|
