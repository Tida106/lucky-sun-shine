Sardonyx is a microcrystalline quartz aggregate called **chalcedony**. It is a variety where **"sard (reddish-brown chalcedony)" and "onyx (banded chalcedony)" are integrated**. As its etymology suggests, its most distinctive feature is its layered **red and white stripes**, and its value is determined by the clarity of the banding and the thickness of its layers.

With a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, it is durable enough for daily wear and, having **no cleavage, it is resistant to impact**. It has been cherished since ancient times as a material for seals (sealing stamps) and **cameo carvings**, precisely because of its ease of handling. **Cameo carvings that leverage its stripes** are an ancient traditional technique where the white upper layer is carved away to reveal the red lower layer, allowing for a unique expression exclusive to Sardonyx.

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Meanings and Historical/Cultural Background

Sardonyx's stone meanings are "**Marital Harmony, Friendship, Happy Married Life, and Sincere Promises**." Its etymology comes from the combination of the Greek words "**Sardis (an ancient city in Asia Minor)**" and "**Onyx (claw)**," a historical name for banded agate from Sardis that became established over time.

In ancient Rome, it was one of the most beloved materials for **seal stones**. When sealing letters or contracts with wax, the family crests or images of gods carved into Sardonyx were pressed into the wax as a **"token of a sincere promise."** A legend even arose that "**Sardonyx would not stick to the wax of one who lied**," positioning it as a symbol of honesty.

Sardonyx is also said to have been used in the **"altar of the Temple of Jerusalem"** in the Old Testament, indicating its historical significance as a **special stone in Judeo-Christian cultures**. During the Renaissance in Europe, the **"custom of using Sardonyx for wedding and engagement rings"** became popular among the nobility, establishing its meaning of **"Marital Harmony."**

## <span id="care"></span>Purification and Care

Sardonyx has a **Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7**, making it durable enough for daily wear and **compatible with most purification methods.**

-   **Placing it on a quartz cluster or in tumbled stones**: A standard overnight purification method.
-   **Passing it through white sage smoke**: One to two minutes is sufficient.
-   **Moonlight or Sunlight Bath**: Place it by a window three days before or after a full or new moon. It is relatively resistant to sunlight.
-   **Running Water**: Briefly rinsing it with lukewarm water is acceptable for short periods.
-   **Sound Bath with a Tuning Fork or Crystal Tuner**: Effective if not touching metal.

Purification methods to avoid:

-   **Burying it in coarse salt for extended periods** (potential for surface damage)
-   **Prolonged use of an ultrasonic cleaner** (potential for cracks to expand at stripe boundaries)
-   **Prolonged exposure to direct sunlight for dyed items** (risk of fading)

For daily care, **gently wiping it with a soft cloth** is fundamental. If sweat or sebum accumulates, quickly rinse it with lukewarm water and dry it immediately. For **cameo carvings**, dirt tends to collect in the engraved grooves, so gently clean them with a soft toothbrush.

## <span id="fake"></span>How to Distinguish Natural, Dyed, and Similar Stones

-   **Natural Sardonyx** features **natural contrast in its banding**, with **subtle variations** in layer thickness and color intensity.
-   **Dyed Sardonyx**: Many commercially available pieces undergo dyeing treatments to enhance color. This is a **standard treatment** with limited impact on value.
-   **If the contrast is "unnaturally vivid" or the "layers are perfectly uniform,"** suspect color enhancement.
-   **Similar Stones**: **Carnelian** (same chalcedony, but solid color without stripes), **Agate** (striped, but different colors), **Black Onyx** (solid black chalcedony).
-   **Glass Imitations**: There are also glass imitations that mimic banding. These can be distinguished by **specific gravity, coolness to the touch, and texture.**
-   Choosing a stone **with a certificate of authenticity** is the most reliable method.

The "difference between Sardonyx and Carnelian" is a common question:
-   **Carnelian** = The solid reddish-brown version of Sardonyx (no stripes).
-   **Sardonyx** = Carnelian with white stripes layered within it.
Minerally, they are both chalcedony; the distinguishing factor is the **presence or absence of stripes**.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How long can Sardonyx be worn?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7, it is durable enough for daily wear and has the resilience to be **passed down through generations.** **Sardonyx cameo carvings** have a long tradition in Europe as heirloom jewelry **passed "from grandmother to grandchild,"** and with proper care, it's not uncommon for them to be used for 100 years or more.

**Q2. Is it okay to wear it in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. **Brief baths are generally okay**, but chlorine in pools and sulfur in hot springs can discolor the metal settings. **Color-enhanced items** may fade with heat or strong detergents, so **it's safest to remove all jewelry.** During sports, there's a risk of impact causing settings to come loose, so **making a habit of removing it** will ultimately help it last longer.

**Q3. What's the difference between Sardonyx and Carnelian?**

A. **Minerally, they are both chalcedony**, but **Sardonyx** refers to the variety with **white stripes** in layers, while **Carnelian** refers to the **solid reddish-brown** variety without stripes. **Cameo carving** is a traditional technique that utilizes Sardonyx's stripes to carve out upper and lower layers, creating designs that cannot be achieved with Carnelian.

**Q4. I'm not born in August, but can I still wear Sardonyx?**

A. Absolutely! Birthstones are simply **"stones that are particularly compatible"** with certain months. Anyone born in other months who resonates with Sardonyx's meanings of "marital harmony and friendship" can certainly wear it without any issue. It's a stone that is highly compatible with all individuals who wish to **nurture a marriage or long-term partnership.**

**Q5. Is dyeing treatment on Sardonyx problematic?**

A. It is a **standard treatment widely used in the market**, and its impact on value is limited. The treatment will be specified on a certificate of authenticity. It's important to understand that **"dyed does not mean fake"** and to enjoy the **beautiful banding at an accessible price** with this realistic approach. **Completely untreated pieces** are rare and significantly higher in price.

**Q6. How do I choose a Sardonyx cameo carving?**

A. Evaluate it based on three points: **precision of carving, contrast of stripes, and overall balance**:
-   **Precision of carving**: Is the facial expression and hair flow intricately carved?
-   **Contrast of stripes**: Is the boundary between white and red clear, making the carving stand out?
-   **Overall balance**: Does the figure fit well within the frame, and are the neck and shoulder lines natural?
Cameos made by traditional Italian and German workshops are expensive but hold value as **works of art that can be passed down through generations.**
