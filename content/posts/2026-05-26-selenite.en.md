## <span id="basic"></span>Basic Information and Mineral Data

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Stone Name | Selenite |
| Japanese Name | Tōsekkō (Transparent Gypsum) |
| Mineral Species | Gypsum |
| Crystal System | Monoclinic |

| Mohs Hardness | 2 to 2.5 (Extremely soft) |
| Specific Gravity | 2.30 to 2.33 |
| Refractive Index | 1.520 to 1.530 |
| Color | Colorless and transparent to white and milky white (fibrous crystals have a silky luster) |

Selenite is a transparent variety of the mineral **gypsum**, with a **chemical composition of CaSO₄·2H₂O (hydrated calcium sulfate)**. It is a "**hydrous mineral**" with **water molecules incorporated into its crystal structure**, which is the fundamental reason for its **extreme sensitivity to water**.

With a Mohs hardness of 2 to 2.5, it is so soft that it **can be scratched by a fingernail**, placing it among the most delicate gemstones. **Fibrously grown crystals have a silky luster, resembling "satin spar" or "fiber optics,"** which is the essence of Selenite's mystical appearance.

Selenite's most significant characteristic is its purported **"rare ability to purify other power stones."** While **quartz clusters and tumbled chips are common purification tools**, Selenite is regarded as a stone with **"even stronger purifying power,"** used by many healing practitioners as a **"tool to place other stones upon for purification."**

## <span id="meaning"></span>Stone Meanings, History, and Cultural Background

In ancient Greece and Rome, it was revered as "**the stone of the Moon Goddess Selene**" and is said to have been used in **rituals aligned with the phases of the moon**. In ancient Egypt, it was sometimes **placed inside sarcophagi**, symbolizing "**a stone that guides souls to the moon**."

In medieval Europe, it was likened to "**angel wings**" and placed on **altars in monasteries and churches**, considered "**a stone that protects sacred spaces**." **Shamans and mediums** believed that wearing Selenite during rituals helped them **"maintain a connection with higher dimensional beings."**

In modern New Age movements and the healing world, it has been rediscovered as **"one of the most powerful purifying stones,"** and its use as a **"tool to purify other stones by placing them on top"** has become widespread. **Forms other than jewelry for purification tools**, such as "**Selenite plates," "Selenite wands," and "Selenite domes,"** are abundantly available, which is a unique characteristic of Selenite.

## <span id="care"></span>Purification Methods and Care

Selenite has a **Mohs hardness of 2 to 2.5**, making it **extremely soft and fatally vulnerable to water**, thus requiring special care.

Acceptable Purification Methods:

- **Pass it through white sage smoke**: For 1 to 2 minutes. **The safest method that does not use water.**
- **Moonlight bath**: Place it by a window for 3 days around the full moon. A traditional purification fitting for **the Moon Goddess's stone**.
- **Sound bath with tuning forks or crystal tuners**: Effective if not allowed to touch metal.
- **Generally no purification needed**: Selenite itself is **"a rare stone that possesses the power to purify other stones,"** thus its own purification is considered **largely unnecessary**.

**Absolutely Not** Recommended Purification Methods:

- **Water or running water** (It has a dissolving property in water, so its surface will cloud white, and prolonged immersion will cause it to disintegrate.)
- **Coarse salt** (Absorbs moisture and destroys the surface.)
- **Ultrasonic cleaner** (Expands cracks and penetrates moisture.)
- **Long-term storage in humid places** (Moisture destroys the crystal structure.)

For daily care, simply **wipe it gently with a soft, dry cloth**. **Never wash it with water**. During **rainy seasons and periods of high humidity**, it is ideal to store it with a desiccant, such as silica gel.

## <span id="fake"></span>Distinguishing Natural Selenite and Similar Stones

- **Natural Selenite** has a **silky luster** and its **fibrous crystal structure** is visible to the naked eye.
- **Same Gypsum Group**: Names vary by shape, such as **Satin Spar (fibrous)**, **Desert Rose (rose-like)**, and **Fishtail (fish-tail like)**.
- **Glass imitations**: There are also Selenite-like imitations made of plastic and glass. You can distinguish them from genuine Selenite by their **coolness, weight, and silky luster**.
- **Alabaster (Snowflake Gypsum)**: An opaque variety from the same gypsum group. It can sometimes be mistaken for an ornamental stone.
- **Confusion with Quartz and Crystal**: Highly transparent quartz and Selenite can sometimes be visually confused, but they are easily distinguishable by their **hardness (quartz 7, Selenite 2.5)**. If it can be scratched with a fingernail, it's Selenite.
- Choosing one **with a gem identification certificate** is the most reliable option.

The "**essence when choosing Selenite**" is its **silky luster**. The **"soft flow of light woven by crystals,"** which cannot possibly be replicated by plastic imitations, is the greatest charm of natural Selenite.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. How many years can I wear Selenite?**

A. With a Mohs hardness of 2 to 2.5, it is **among the softest gemstones** and **not suitable for wearing**. While some pieces are made into jewelry, such as pendants, its extreme vulnerability to sweat, water, and friction makes it more practical to use it as a **"tool to place" or "ritualistic implement" rather than "a gemstone to wear."**

**Q2. Is it okay to wear it in the bath, pool, or during sports?**

A. **Absolutely not for any of them**. Due to its **water-soluble nature**, baths, pools, hot springs, and sweat during sports — all will destroy Selenite. **"Keep it away from all water"** is the absolute rule for Selenite.

**Q3. Why can Selenite purify other stones?**

A. While **no scientific mechanism has been proven**, it is **traditionally said in the healing community that "Selenite is a rare stone that does not retain negative energy itself."** Mineralogically, its characteristic as a **hydrous mineral with water molecules incorporated into its crystal structure** might be layered with the symbolism of **"always maintaining a fresh state."** The original way to interact with it is to enjoy its **symbolism as a protective charm.**

**Q4. What is the specific method for purifying other stones with a Selenite plate?**

A. Simply **"place the stones you wish to purify on a Selenite slab (plate) overnight."** Specifically:
1. Place the Selenite slab in a **dry and clean location**.
2. Arrange the power stones you want to purify (bracelets, pendants, tumbled stones, etc.) on top of it.
3. Leave them there **overnight (approximately 6 to 12 hours)**.
4. The next morning, retrieve the purified stones.
This is a safe purification method for **delicate stones sensitive to water, salt, and sunlight (such as Rose Quartz and Amethyst)**, and many enthusiasts keep it on hand.

**Q5. Are Selenite and Satin Spar different?**

A. They are **different forms within the same gypsum group**. **Selenite** is a general term for transparent to translucent gypsum, **Satin Spar** refers to those with a **"fibrous crystal structure,"** and **Desert Rose** refers to those with a **"rose-like shape."** **Mineralogically, all are gypsum**, but in the healing world, the general term "**Selenite**" is often used.

**Q6. What if my Selenite accessory breaks or dissolves in water?**

A. In the world of power stones, there's a tradition of viewing it as having "**taken your place or sacrificed itself**." Selenite, in particular, is often thanked for "**taking on the purification burden of other stones**." It's common to return it to the earth with gratitude or carefully store it in a drawer. You can also see this as a turning point to welcome a new stone and resume your connection.
