## <span id="five-aspects"></span>Five Aspects to Consider for 'Compatibility'

Power stone 'compatibility' is not determined by a **single rule**; rather, it's traditionally understood as **being judged multi-dimensionally from several perspectives.** Here are five key aspects:

| Aspect         | Content                                            | Example                             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **① Purpose**  | Combine stones that support the same wish          | Money luck stones / Love luck stones |
| **② Color**    | Similar colors, complementary colors, chakra alignment | Group by blue shades / Balance with complementary colors |
| **③ Five Elements** | Balance of Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water      | Green (Wood) x Red (Fire) for mutual generation |
| **④ Chakra**   | Corresponding colors for the seven chakras         | Mindful of the balance from 1st to 7th |
| **⑤ Hardness** | Whether stones physically scratch each other       | Mindful of differences in Mohs hardness |

These five aspects are not about **"only one being correct"**; instead, judgment is made by combining them. Let's introduce each one in turn.

## <span id="by-purpose"></span>① Matching by Purpose

The **most straightforward and practical criterion** is the "matching by purpose" approach. By combining stones that support the same wish, it's believed that **"your focus becomes clearer."**

-   **Money Luck Stones**: Citrine and Rutilated Quartz
-   **Love Luck Stones**: Rose Quartz and Moonstone
-   **Work Luck Stones**: Tiger's Eye and Lapis Lazuli
-   **Protection Stones**: Morion and Black Tourmaline

However, it's said that stacking **"three or four stones for the same purpose"** can disperse the symbolic energy, so starting with one or two stones is the classic approach.

## <span id="by-color"></span>② Matching by Color

This approach is based on **color psychology and traditional color theory.** Grouping by **similar colors creates calmness; combining complementary (opposite) colors provides balance; and assembling multiple colors adjusts all chakras.** There are three main approaches:

| Combination Type | Impression               | Example                                             |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Similar Colors** | Calm and unified         | Aquamarine x Turquoise x Lapis Lazuli (blue tones) |
| **Complementary Colors** | Balance and contrast     | Rose Quartz (pink) x Aventurine (green)             |
| **All Chakra Colors** | Overall energy adjustment | Collect the seven colors: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, purple |

**"Grouping by similar colors"** is the easiest introductory approach to avoid mistakes. It also creates visual harmony as jewelry, making for a visually pleasing selection.

## <span id="by-gogyo"></span>③ Matching by the Five Elements and Eastern Philosophy

The **traditional Chinese philosophy of the "Five Elements" (Gogyo)** — a way of understanding the world through the cycle of five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. Power stones can also be aligned with the Five Elements through their colors and symbolism, and there's a tradition of judging combinations based on relationships called **"Soshō" (mutual generation) and "Sōkoku" (mutual overcoming).**

| Five Elements | Corresponding Color | Example Stones                        |
| ------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Wood**      | Green, blue-green   | Jade, Aventurine, Amazonite           |
| **Fire**      | Red, purple         | Ruby, Garnet, Amethyst                |
| **Earth**     | Yellow, brown       | Citrine, Tiger's Eye, Amber           |
| **Metal**     | White, gold         | Clear Quartz, Pearl, Pyrite           |
| **Water**     | Black, deep blue    | Morion, Onyx, Lapis Lazuli            |

**Soshō (Mutually Generative Combinations)**: In the order of Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal → Water → Wood
-   Example: **Jade (Wood) x Garnet (Fire)** = Symbolizing Wood generating Fire

**Sōkoku (Balancing Combinations)**: Wood ↔ Earth, Fire ↔ Metal, Earth ↔ Water, Metal ↔ Wood, Water ↔ Fire
-   Example: **Garnet (Fire) x Pyrite (Metal)** = A relationship where Fire melts Metal = A combination for significant change

However, the Five Elements are not an **"absolute rule,"** but one cultural concept. It's not about assuming **"Sōkoku = bad,"** but rather interpreting it flexibly according to your purpose – that's the true way to use it.

This approach originates from **Indian Yoga philosophy** and the "Seven Chakras" — energy centers believed to be located along the midline of the body. By combining stones with colors corresponding to each chakra, it aims to **"balance the overall energy."**

| **2nd (Sacral)**       | Lower abdomen   | Orange              | Carnelian, Orange Calcite         |
| **1st (Root)**         | Base of spine   | Red, black          | Garnet, Hematite, Morion          |

While there's a traditional practice of **"collecting all seven colors,"** it's not practical for beginners. A lighter approach, such as **"focusing on the area you're currently interested in (e.g., blue tones for the throat chakra),"** is perfectly sufficient.

## <span id="by-hardness"></span>⑤ Hardness Differences and Physical Compatibility

The most practical and crucial aspect is **"Mohs hardness difference."** When a harder stone touches a softer one, the softer stone can get scratched. This physical phenomenon means extra care is needed, especially for accessories like bracelets where stones come into contact.

| Mohs Hardness | Example Stones                                 |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 10            | Diamond                                        |
| 9             | Ruby, Sapphire                                 |
| 8〜8.5        | Emerald, Morganite, Alexandrite, Topaz         |
| 7〜7.5        | Clear Quartz, Amethyst, Citrine, Rutilated Quartz, Tourmaline, Garnet |
| 6.5〜7        | Jade, Tiger's Eye, Carnelian, Pietersite       |
| 5〜6          | Turquoise, Lapis Lazuli, Moonstone, Opal       |
| 3〜4          | Pearl, Amber, Angelite, Aragonite, Howlite     |
| 2〜2.5        | Selenite                                       |

**Guideline**: Combinations with a **hardness difference of 3 or more are highly likely to scratch softer stones.** Examples:
-   ❌ **Diamond (10) x Pearl (3-4)**: Pearl is easily scratched
-   ❌ **Clear Quartz (7) x Selenite (2.5)**: Selenite is easily scratched
-   ✅ **Clear Quartz (7) x Amethyst (7)**: Same hardness, safe

Keeping the **"hardness difference within 2 if threaded on the same bracelet"** is a practical rule for physical compatibility.

## <span id="ng-examples"></span>Combinations Generally 'Best Avoided'

Here are a few combinations traditionally and practically considered **"less compatible."** However, please consider these as cultural references, not **"absolute rules."**

### NG Example ①: Stacking Too Many Powerful Stones

Wearing three or more stones described as having **"strong energy,"** such as Rutilated Quartz, Ruby, or Moldavite, simultaneously can disperse their energy and blur your intention as a charm. **"One to two powerful stones at most"** is the traditional sentiment.

### NG Example ②: Combinations with Opposing Symbolism

For example:
-   **Morion (protection, blocking) and Rose Quartz (love, acceptance)**: A conflict between "closing" and "opening"
-   **Onyx (severing ties) and Inca Rose (reconnecting with past relationships)**: A conflict between "releasing" and "holding on"

Such combinations with **"opposite symbolic directions"** can create conflicting desires within your heart, blurring your focus as a charm.

### NG Example ③: Physical Contact with Too Large a Hardness Difference

As mentioned in the previous section, mixing stones with a Mohs hardness difference of 3 or more in a bracelet carries a realistic risk of scratching softer stones.

### NG Example ④: Storing Water, Salt, or Acid-Sensitive Stones Together When Wet

Pearls, Turquoise, Lapis Lazuli, Howlite, and Aragonite, among others, are sensitive to moisture. Storing these stones in the same case in a humid place can accelerate their deterioration due to mutual sweat or moisture.

### NG Example ④: Combinations for 'Rebounding Curses' or 'Harming Specific Individuals'

In traditional amulet beliefs, combinations intended to harm others have long been said to **"bring adverse effects back upon oneself."** Charms are meant to be **"a support for one's own heart," not "tools to control or attack others"** — this fundamental principle remains true today.

## <span id="practical-tips"></span>Practical Tips for Combining Stones

After organizing the theory, I've summarized five practical tips to keep in mind for your daily interactions with stones:

### Tip ①: Start with the 'Two-Stone Rule'

For your first combination, stick to two stones. Once you get used to it, add a third. This **"gradual deepening from simple to complex"** is the classic style for long-lasting enjoyment.

### Tip ②: Value 'Visual Harmony' Too

For jewelry, **visual harmony** is also an important factor. Combinations where **"colors clash" or "volumes are mismatched"** carry the risk of you simply **"not wanting to keep wearing them,"** even before considering them as charms. Choose combinations that feel good for you to look at.

### Tip ③: Change Combinations as Your Purpose Changes

The charms you need change with different stages of life. **Rose Quartz and Morganite** during a period of considering marriage, **Tiger's Eye and Pietersite** during a job hunt — regularly reviewing **"the combination you need right now"** is the true way to engage with them.

### Tip ④: Be Mindful of 'Physical Compatibility During Storage'

Be mindful of hardness differences not only when wearing them, but also when storing them in a jewelry box. Softer stones (such as Pearl, Selenite, Howlite) should be stored in individual cloth pouches for longevity.

### Tip ⑤: 'Add Clear Quartz if You're Undecided'

**Clear Quartz (Crystal)** is considered compatible with all stones, and **"add Clear Quartz if you're undecided"** is a traditional golden rule. When a combination feels unbalanced, adding Clear Quartz is often said to bring overall calm.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Are there any stones that should absolutely never be combined?**

A. **There are no absolute 'no-go' combinations.** The "combinations best avoided" introduced in this article are **cultural and symbolic references**, not **absolute rules.** If you cherish a combination, **it can work even if it goes against traditional compatibility** — this is the essence of choosing power stones.

**Q2. Does combining stones double their effects?**

A. **It's not accurate to say they double.** The effects of power stones are **cultural and symbolic in nature**, and there's no scientific way to numerically measure an increase or decrease in effects due to combinations. You can expect a psychological effect of **"making your intentions clearer,"** but this also varies from person to person.

**Q3. Should I really avoid 'Sōkoku' combinations in the Five Elements?**

A. **There's no absolute need to avoid them.** The Five Elements represent an **ancient Chinese philosophical worldview**, and in modern power stone culture, they are treated as **"one point of reference."** The modern approach is to view **"Sōkoku as combinations that emphasize balance,"** and **interpret them flexibly according to your purpose.**

**Q4. What's the ideal number of stones for a bracelet?**

A. **There's no absolute rule for the number of stones**, but traditionally, **"two to three stones"** are considered classic. Buddhist rosaries traditionally have **108 beads**, which is a religious number with special meaning. For **everyday bracelets**, **"mixing 2 to 3 types of stones that suit your purpose"** tends to create a lasting balance.

**Q5. Is it okay to wear stones for different purposes (e.g., money luck stones and love luck stones) together?**

A. **It's perfectly fine.** **"Having multiple wishes simultaneously"** is a natural human feeling, and **wearing money luck stones and love luck stones together doesn't mean their symbolism will clash.** However, if you **mix three or more different purposes**, there's a risk that **"what you're wishing for right now" might become unclear**, so it's important to **"be mindful of your priority wishes."**

**Q6. Is it okay to combine stones I've had for a long time with newly purchased ones?**

A. **No problem at all.** Stones you've **"cherished for many years"** hold **invaluable sentimental value**, and combining them with new stones won't cause symbolic clashes. In fact, using **your old stones as "the established core of your charm collection"** and adding new stones around them is a style that encourages long-lasting engagement.
