## <span id="what-is-kouten"></span>What is a "Healing Crisis"?

The "healing crisis" is a concept used in the spiritual world and some alternative therapies. It's sometimes explained as a belief that "temporary discomfort can occur as the body adjusts to new energies."

You might have seen explanations like, "Feeling sluggish when you first start wearing a stone is a healing crisis," at a store where you bought stones or on a stone information website.

**However, this is neither a medical term nor a scientifically proven phenomenon.** There is no medical concept equivalent to a "healing crisis," and there's no basis for connecting physical discomfort with "it's because of the stone, but it's a sign that things will get better."

## <span id="why-dangerous"></span>Why is Believing in a "Healing Crisis" Dangerous?

### ① Risk of Overlooking a Real Illness

Feeling "sluggish," experiencing "headaches," "insomnia," or "a depressed mood"—these can also be signs of various illnesses that can occur regardless of stones.

Important illnesses requiring early detection and treatment, such as anemia, thyroid disorders, depression, infections, and internal organ diseases, could be left untreated if you think, "It's just a healing crisis, so I'll wait and see."

The biggest risk is missing the opportunity to receive necessary medical care by attributing physical discomfort to "the stone."

### ② Enduring by Believing "It Will Get Better if I Bear With It"

If there's an explanation that "a healing crisis is temporary," people tend to endure painful symptoms, thinking, "It will get better if I wait a little longer."

Ignoring and enduring signs of illness can cause symptoms to worsen. **If your body feels "distressed," it's a sign to seek medical attention.**

### ③ Cases Where It's Used to Prevent Returns and Complaints

Unfortunately, it's said that there are cases where, in response to situations like, "I've felt unwell since buying the stone, so I want to return it," the explanation given is, "That's a healing crisis; please continue to wear it for a little longer."

Not all sellers do this, but please be aware that the term "healing crisis" can sometimes be used to alleviate consumer anxiety. If you have physical discomfort, it's crucial to confirm your condition medically before considering how to handle the stone.

## <span id="correct-actions"></span>Three Correct Actions for Physical Discomfort

### Action ①: Go to the Doctor First

This is the top priority. No matter why you're feeling unwell, **physical discomfort is your body's signal.** Even if the timing coincides with when you started wearing a stone, it's impossible to definitively say the stone is the cause, and it's overwhelmingly more likely that the stone is not the cause.

For those hesitating to see a doctor:

- Persistent sluggishness or fatigue → Internal Medicine
- Headaches, insomnia → Internal Medicine, Neurology, or Psychosomatic Medicine
- Depressed mood, severe anxiety → Psychosomatic Medicine or Psychiatry
- Skin abnormalities (on areas where the stone was touched) → Dermatology

Even if you think, "It might not be serious enough for a hospital...," if bothersome symptoms persist for several days, it's worth seeing a doctor.

### Action ②: Try Taking Off the Stone

In parallel with seeking medical attention, trying to remove the stone can be effective.

If your condition improves after taking off the stone, it's possible that the stone wasn't suitable for you (this includes physical reasons such as material allergies or skin irritation). Conversely, if your condition doesn't change even after removing the stone, the cause lies elsewhere.

"Taking off the stone and observing" is a supplementary check to be done after receiving medical care. If your condition stabilizes by removing it, **there's no need to force yourself to keep wearing it.**

### Action ③: Don't Force Yourself to Wear It If It Doesn't Feel Right

When considering a stone as an "item for enjoyment," if you continue to feel anxious, heavy, or down while wearing it, **there's no reason to keep wearing that stone.**

I understand feelings like, "It was expensive" or "I chose it with such care." However, if wearing it makes you feel heavy-hearted, forcing yourself to keep it defeats the purpose. There's absolutely no problem with considering letting it go.

## <span id="real-reasons"></span>The Real Reasons Why Starting a Stone and Physical Changes Coincide

The experience of "my physical condition changed around the same time I bought a stone" often means that "unrelated changes occurred simultaneously."

**Factors that often coincide with this timing:**

-   **Seasonal changes**: Spring and autumn are times when the autonomic nervous system can easily become imbalanced, making people more prone to physical discomfort.
-   **Increased stress**: Psychological burdens such as starting a new life, changing jobs, or changes in relationships can manifest physically.
-   **Changes in lifestyle**: The period when you feel "I want to change something," which might lead you to buy a stone, is often also a time when your entire lifestyle is changing.
-   **Placebo and nocebo effects**: By consciously thinking, "It might be because of the stone," your attention to your physical condition increases, making you more prone to noticing discomfort.

This isn't to say that "stones have no effect at all," but rather that **it's important not to easily attribute the cause of physical changes to stones.** If you're anxious because you don't know the cause, seeking confirmation from a medical institution is all the more a shortcut to peace of mind.

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q. How long does a "healing crisis" typically last?

A. Since "healing crisis" is not a medical term, there's no basis for stating that it "ends in XX days." **If physical discomfort persists for more than a few days, it's highly likely to be some form of physical anomaly, not a healing crisis.** Please see a doctor without enduring the symptoms.

---

### Q. Are there ways to alleviate a "healing crisis"?

A. We do not recommend trying to alleviate a "healing crisis" with stones. If you are experiencing physical discomfort, **addressing it medically is fundamental.** While practicing basic self-care like rest, hydration, and a balanced diet, if symptoms persist, please see a doctor.

---

### Q. My condition improved after I took off the stone. Does this mean the stone wasn't right for me?

A. There could be several reasons. These include allergies to the material, skin irritation (including metal allergies), or psychological factors such as feeling relieved once the anxiety of "it might be the stone's fault" disappeared. In any case, if your condition stabilizes by not using that stone, **there's no need to force yourself to keep wearing it.**

---

### Q. Can I "return" a stone I've started wearing?

A. If you are considering returning a stone due to physical discomfort, first check the store's return policy. If your return is denied with only the explanation "It's a healing crisis, please wait and see," you can also consult a consumer center (phone number: 188). If you have physical discomfort, please prioritize seeing a doctor.

---

### Q. Can stones cause physical discomfort?

A. Stones themselves do not typically cause physical discomfort directly. However, allergic reactions to metal components, skin friction, or psychological pressure (such as the belief "this stone might not be compatible with me") can affect your physical condition. Please consult a medical institution for any concerning symptoms.
