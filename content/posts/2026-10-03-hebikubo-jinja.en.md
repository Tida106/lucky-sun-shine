Hebikubo Shrine isn't just a surface-level money luck shrine. This entire area was once blessed with abundant natural springs and was called "Hebikubo" (literally "snake hollow"), and a deep reverence and faith in water and snakes has been carefully nurtured here since ancient times.

There's a **legend of the Dragon God**, who answered solemn prayers for rain during the great drought that struck the Musashi region in 1322 (the Genko era of the Kamakura period), bringing blessed rain to save the town. There's also the **legend of the White Snake's homecoming**, in which a White Snake deity, having lost its dwelling due to the changing times, appeared in a local village elder's dream, wishing to "return to its original home." These stories have been carefully passed down through the warm love and faith of the local people.

Hebikubo Shrine is home to two great powers: the **White Snake deity, who sheds its skin and is reborn**, and the **Dragon God, who overcomes trials to ascend to the heavens**. ## 🌿 The Power of "Shedding and Rebirth" Found Within the Shrine Grounds! Spots for Resetting Your Past and Opening Up Your Luck

A snake's "shedding of its skin" is a symbol of "rebirth and resetting"—casting off your old self and being born anew. Here, let's introduce the spots that will help elevate yourself and free you from career worries or a cluttered mind!

### Nade-Shirohebi (The Petting White Snake)
* **【Features】:** Waiting for you just past the torii gate is a beautiful, smooth stone statue of two White Snake deities gently wrapped around a golden sacred jewel. You can pray while petting it directly with your hands.
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** From the snake's habit of regularly shedding its skin, it has long been believed, in animism and Shinto alike, to hold powerful spiritual energy for "purification, rebirth, and recovery from illness." The two snakes also represent a devoted married couple (the larger one female, the smaller one male), and are said to bring blessings for household harmony and good relationships, as well as for "rekindling a lost connection" by mending a once-broken bond.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** Let go of the regrets and worries built up from your past by gently petting this White Snake deity, as if shedding your own skin! The golden sacred jewel also holds a prayer for world peace, so if you touch it with a feeling of gratitude, your heart and body will feel refreshingly light and renewed ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Official Website (Shrine Grounds Guide)](https://hebikubo.jp/)

### Hebikubo Ryujinja (Hebikubo Dragon God Shrine)
* **【Features】:** Located deep within the shrine grounds at the "Motomiya" (original shrine), this is a solemn area enshrining an impressive White Dragon statue measuring 8 meters long, guarded by 7 White Snake statues surrounding it.
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** This enshrines the Dragon God who, during the great drought of 1322, brought rain through a 7-day fasting rain prayer performed by the priest Homitsu. According to the shrine's tradition, "when a White Snake deity completes pure ascetic training and reaches the pinnacle of evolution, it is promoted to its eighth form: the White Dragon." The transformation of the snake (mi, the zodiac sign) into the dragon (tatsu) is a play on words meaning **"mi ga tatsu" (to establish oneself, success in life, business prosperity, and the fulfillment of great wishes)**, carrying a powerful prayer and belief.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** The story of a small White Snake deity steadily evolving into a great Dragon God through unwavering effort is inspiring just to witness! If you're thinking "I want to break through my current wall!" or "I want to achieve something big in my business or goals!", make a firm resolution in front of the White Dragon. The powerful energy of "establishing yourself" will surely push you forward ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Official Website (Shrine Grounds Guide)](https://hebikubo.jp/)

### Guchi Tsubo (The Complaint Jar)
* **【Features】:** A large, unglazed clay jar quietly placed within the shrine grounds. You can open the lid and pour out what's in your heart into it.
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** This is a practical space for releasing the anger, sadness, and complaints (impurities) that build up in daily life, cleansing your mind before approaching the pure presence of the deities.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** Before telling the deities your wishes and gratitude, start by doing some spring cleaning of the heart! Pour out whatever's been weighing on you that you can't tell anyone else into the jar with a decisive "there!", then snap the lid shut and seal it away. With a heart that feels refreshed and light, your hands pressed together will more easily receive the deities' warm blessings ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Official Website (Shrine Grounds Guide)](https://hebikubo.jp/)

## 💰 Improve Your Flow and Attract Abundance! Money-Washing and Good Luck Charms

Hebikubo Shrine's money luck prayers aren't simply about increasing your wealth. At their core is a "virtue of circulation": by purifying your money and sending it back out into the world with gratitude, even greater abundance is said to circulate back to you.

### Zeni-mawashi and Zeni-arai (White Snake Seed Coins and the Coin-Linking Ritual)
* **【Features】:** At the shrine office, you receive a "White Snake seed coin," place it on a stone mill, and turn it clockwise three times in the "zeni-mawashi" (coin-turning) ritual. Afterward, you place the seed coin and your own money in a basket and rinse it clockwise three times in the "Shirohebi Shimizu" spring water, in the "zeni-arai" (money-washing) ritual. The purified seed coin is carefully kept in your wallet, and returned to the "zeni-tsunagi" pole with gratitude on your next visit.
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** This is a ritual of purifying money with the sacred spring water of the White Snake deity, who serves as a messenger of Benzaiten, the goddess of wealth and the arts. Money is living energy that stagnates if it doesn't flow. This is based on the "philosophy of money luck circulation": by sending it back out into the world in a good way with gratitude, even greater abundance circulates back. The clockwise motion symbolizes the smooth flow of the universe and time.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** Sun-chan's style is to silently say, "Thank you for coming to me. I'll use you carefully to make everyone happy," while washing the money! If you use your purified money to joyfully support your own growth or the people important to you, it will come back to you as waves of happiness many times over. Treasure your purified seed coin as a "lucky magnet" in your wallet ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Official Website (Shrine Grounds Guide)](https://hebikubo.jp/)

### Yumemi Fuda (Dream-Vision Talisman)
* **【Features】:** A special talisman for money luck and rebirth, granted only during the New Year period (January 1–11) or on "Mi no Hi" (Day of the Snake) festival days.
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** This remarkably rare talisman actually contains a real shed skin from the "Iwakuni White Snake," a nationally designated natural monument. It's said to condense the White Snake deity's boundless vitality, regenerative power, and financial fortune. Unlike ordinary charms, it doesn't need to be returned after one year, and can be carried in your wallet for a lifetime. Its name comes from the legend of the White Snake deity once appearing in a local village elder's dream.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** A charm containing an actual shed skin from the White Snake deity feels like a true miracle! Just tucking it gently into your wallet will not only attract money luck, but will always support your own "power to be reborn again and again." If you visit on a Day of the Snake, be sure to check for it at the amulet office ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Amulet (Omamori) Guide](https://hebikubo.jp/omamori/)

### Mi-Kuji (Snake Fortune) and the Tsuchinoto-mi Festival Day
* **【Features】:** Festival days held every 12 days on the "Day of the Snake," and once every 60 days on "Tsuchinoto-mi Day," along with an adorable ceramic white snake fortune-telling charm (omikuji).
* **【Deep Background and Origins】:** In the twelve zodiac signs, the "Mi" (snake) is considered a messenger of Benzaiten. In particular, "Tsuchinoto-mi Day," when the "Tsuchinoto" of the ten heavenly stems (the earth energy that nurtures and gives birth to metal, in the five elements) overlaps with "Mi," is called the single most auspicious day for money and financial luck among all of Benzaiten's festival days. Vows made and money purified on this day are said to especially receive the blessings of the gods and buddhas.
* **【Sun-chan's Top Pick Explanation】:** The "Mi-Kuji" with its adorably round eyes can be offered anywhere you like on the shrine grounds after reading your fortune, or you can take it home as good luck interior decor! The shrine grounds on Tsuchinoto-mi Day are as lively as a festival, and you can feel the power of the White Snake deity and Dragon God even more strongly ☀️
* **【Official Info】:** [Hebikubo Shrine Official Website (Shrine Grounds Guide)](https://hebikubo.jp/)
