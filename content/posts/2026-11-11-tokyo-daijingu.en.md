## Introduction

Tokyo Daijingu is a shrine about a five-minute walk from Iidabashi Station. It was founded as a place to pray toward the Grand Shrine of Ise, so it is affectionately called **"Tokyo's Ise Shrine"**. It is especially popular with women as a **shrine for love and matchmaking**.

This guide covers **its history, deities, the beginning of Shinto weddings, matchmaking charms, visiting tips and access**.

## <span id="history"></span>History

The shrine was founded in **1880 (Meiji 13)** as a place in Tokyo to worship toward the Ise Shrine, after approval by Emperor Meiji. It originally stood in Hibiya and was called **Hibiya Daijingu**.

In **1928 (Showa 3)** it moved to its present location in Iidabashi and was called **Iidabashi Daijingu**. After World War II it took its current name, **Tokyo Daijingu**.

## <span id="deity"></span>Deities and Matchmaking Blessings

The enshrined deities are the same as those of the Ise Shrine: **Amaterasu-Sumeomikami, Toyouke-no-Okami and Yamatohime-no-Mikoto**. The shrine also honors the **Three Deities of Creation** (Ame-no-Minakanushi, Takamimusubi and Kamimusubi).

The Three Deities of Creation are said to oversee the creation of all things and "musubi" (binding together), which is why the shrine is believed to bring blessings for good relationships. It is also one of the "Five Shrines of Tokyo".

## <span id="wedding"></span>Japan's First Shinto Wedding

In **1900 (Meiji 33)**, the wedding of the Crown Prince (later Emperor Taisho) was held in the Kashikodokoro sanctuary in the Imperial Palace. In commemoration, Shinto weddings for the general public are said to have begun in **1901**. This is why the shrine is known as **the first shrine in Japan to hold Shinto weddings**.

Shinto weddings are still held here today, with dances performed by shrine maidens.

## <span id="amulet"></span>Charms, Fortunes and Goshuin

Many visitors come to pray for good relationships, and the shrine's offerings reflect that.

- **Matchmaking charms**: There are many designs. The "Suzuran" (lily-of-the-valley) charm, named after the flower's meaning, is said to be popular.
- **Love fortunes (koi-mikuji)**: Fortune slips with advice about love, including versions in English.
- **Goshuin**: Available 9:00–17:00.

Offerings and prices may change, so please check at the shrine office on the day.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

1. **Visit on a weekday morning.** Weekends and holidays tend to be crowded, and queues can form in the precinct.
2. **Purify at the water pavilion before the main hall.** See our [shrine etiquette guide](/en/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).
3. **Pray to the same deities as at Ise.** The shrine has long been a place for those who cannot travel to Ise.
4. **Offer thanks along with your wish.** A shrine is considered a place to greet the deities and express gratitude.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

- About 5 minutes on foot from **JR Chuo-Sobu Line Iidabashi Station, West Exit**
- About 5 minutes from **Tokyo Metro Yurakucho/Namboku Line Iidabashi Station, Exit B2a**
- About 5 minutes from **Tokyo Metro Tozai Line / Toei Oedo Line Iidabashi Station, Exit A4**
- From JR Tokyo Station, about 20 minutes with a change at Ochanomizu

There is no parking for visitors, so please use public transport.

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q1. What kind of shrine is Tokyo Daijingu?

It was founded in 1880 as a place in Tokyo to worship toward the Ise Shrine, and is called "Tokyo's Ise Shrine". It is known as the first shrine in Japan to hold Shinto weddings and is popular for matchmaking.

### Q2. What are the opening hours?

The gate is open 6:00–21:00, charms and fortunes are available 8:00–19:00, and goshuin 9:00–17:00. Hours may change, so check the official website.

### Q3. How can I avoid crowds?

Weekends and holidays are crowded. A weekday morning is best.

### Q4. How do I get there from the station?

It is about 5 minutes on foot from any exit of Iidabashi Station. The JR West Exit and Metro Exits B2a and A4 are convenient.

### Q5. Is there an admission fee?

Visiting is free. Charms and goshuin require a separate offering.
