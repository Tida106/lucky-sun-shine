## Introduction

Hakuto Shrine in Tottori City is **known as the setting of the myth of the Hare of Inaba** (the White Rabbit). It enshrines the rabbit from the Kojiki and is loved as a shrine for good connections, because the rabbit is said to have helped bring Okuninushi and Princess Yakami together. It is also certified as a "Sanctuary of Lovers".

Three things worth knowing before you go:

- **The grounds are free to enter**, and the shrine office is said to be open about 9:00-16:00.
- **It is a rabbit-spotting walk.** Lanterns, charms and ema plaques all feature rabbits.
- **White Rabbit Coast is right in front**, with the roadside station "Shinwa no Sato Shirousagi" next door, so shrine, sea view and a meal fit into one stop.

## The Myth of the Hare of Inaba

In the Kojiki, a white rabbit on the Oki islands tricks crocodiles (sometimes read as sharks) into lining up so it can cross to the mainland, and is skinned as punishment. Misled by passing gods, it suffers more, until Okuninushi arrives last and teaches it the proper remedy.

The rabbit later helped Okuninushi win Princess Yakami, which is why the shrine has been revered for **matchmaking**. Where the myth's island lies today is uncertain; the Oki theory is one of several.

## What You May Take Away (Traditional Benefits)

These are traditional beliefs, not guarantees.

- **Good connections and romance**, tied to the Okuninushi and Yakami story.
- **A reminder of kindness**: the tale of a hurt rabbit being helped.
- **Walking a mythical setting**, with shrine and coast in one visit.
- **Charming rabbit-themed charms and ema**.

## Mitarashi Pond, Musubi Stone & White Rabbit Coast

- **Mitarashi Pond**: said to be where the rabbit washed its wounds; its water level is traditionally said never to change.
- **Musubi stone**: a stone charm; tradition says tossing it onto a torii brings good connections. Many visitors simply take it home. Check that no one is nearby.
- **White Rabbit Coast**: white sand and the Sea of Japan, a famed sunset spot, with the island linked to the myth offshore.
- **Roadside station**: local products and meals after your visit.

## Model Route (Approx. Times)

1. Walk the approach from the parking lot (about 10 min)
2. Pray at the hall and visit the charm counter (10-15 min)
3. Pond and musubi stone (about 10 min)
4. Coast and roadside station (15-30 min)

Allow **about 40 minutes to 1 hour** in total.

## Tips

- The shrine office closes around 16:00, so go earlier for charms.
- Wear comfortable shoes; the coast can be windy.
- Summer beach season can be crowded; mornings are easier.

## Access

- **Train/bus**: JR Tottori Station, then Hinomaru Bus about 40 min to "Hakuto Jinja-mae"; buses are infrequent, so check the timetable.
- **Car**: about 20 min from Tottori IC, using the shared free lot.

## Shrine Etiquette

1. Bow before the torii.
2. Walk to the side of the path.
3. Two bows, two claps, one bow.
4. Toss the musubi stone only when no one is near.
5. Do not touch or climb the rabbit statues.

See the [shrine visiting basics](/blog/shrine-visit-basics/) for more.

## FAQ

**How long should I plan?** About 40-60 minutes, longer with the coast.

**Is there parking?** A shared free lot with the roadside station, reportedly.

**Can I go alone?** Of course; many come for the rabbit story.
