## Introduction

Izanagi Jingu on Awaji Island, Hyogo, is **the shrine linked to Japan's creation myth**. According to the *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*, the god Izanagi, after finishing the creation of the islands, spent his remaining days at Taga on Awaji, the first island he created. The shrine is said to have begun at the site of that retreat, and it is the only shrine in Hyogo with the title "Jingu."

Three things worth knowing before you go:

- **Visiting is free and the grounds are open freely.** The amulet office is reported to be open about 9:00-17:00.
- **The "Married Couples' Camphor Tree" is known as a place to pray for a happy marriage, good relationships and safe childbirth.** It feels easy to stand quietly there, whether you come with a partner or alone.
- **It is a day trip from Kobe and Sannomiya**, so it combines well with a drive or sightseeing around Awaji Island.

## The creation myth and history

The *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* record that Izanagi and Izanami created Awaji and the other islands. After completing this work, Izanagi is said to have handed rule to Amaterasu and built a **"kakurinomiya" (retreat)** at Taga on Awaji to live out his days.

A mausoleum was built on the site of that dwelling, and the shrine is said to have grown from there. The current main hall was moved to the mausoleum mound in the Meiji era; before that the area was a sacred, off-limits place. It is often called "Japan's oldest shrine" and is part of the Japan Heritage story "Awaji, the Island of Creation."

## What visitors hope to gain (traditionally said)

These are commonly told associations, not guaranteed effects.

- **Marriage and relationships:** with two deities enshrined and the Married Couples' Camphor Tree on the grounds, couples and people hoping for new connections visit.
- **Safe childbirth and children:** the tree is also known among those praying for these.
- **Reporting a new start to the god of beginnings:** some people visit at turning points such as a new life stage.
- **Feeling ancient Japan on foot:** the "Sun Path Marker" connects the sun's movement with famous shrines.

## The Married Couples' Camphor Tree and the Sun Path Marker

Do not miss the **Married Couples' Camphor Tree**. It is said to be about 900 years old and is a Hyogo Prefecture natural monument. Two trees are said to have grown into one, and it was recorded in Edo-period gazetteers as an entwined camphor. It is regarded as a sacred tree housing Izanagi and Izanami, and the small Iwakusu Shrine beside it enshrines their child Hiruko.

The other highlight is the **Sun Path Marker**, a circular monument showing how the sun's movement relates to famous shrines: Ise's Inner Shrine due east, Watatsumi Shrine on Tsushima due west, and others toward the solstice sunrise and sunset directions.

## Suggested route and timing

Times are rough estimates.

1. **Walk the approach and large torii** (about 10 min)
2. **Front gate and worship hall** (about 10 min)
3. **Camphor tree and Iwakusu Shrine** (about 10 min)
4. **Sun Path Marker and the amulet office** (about 10-15 min)

**Allow about 40 minutes to 1 hour in total.** Morning is a good time, especially if you combine it with Awaji sightseeing.

## Tips

- **Go early in the morning** for a quieter visit.
- **The amulet office closes at 17:00.** Aim to arrive before 16:00 for goshuin or amulets.
- **Wear comfortable shoes**; the grounds are gravel.
- **Buses are infrequent**; check the return time in advance.
- **January 15:** a rice-gruel divination festival is known; check the official site for the latest schedule.

## Access

- **Train and bus:** from JR Maiko or Sannomiya, take an express bus onto Awaji, then an Awaji Kotsu bus (Nishiura line) to "Izanagi Jingu-mae" and walk a short distance. Direct express buses from Sannomiya and Maiko are also reported; check the operators for times.
- **By car:** parking is available, and it is handy for touring the island.

## Basic etiquette

1. Bow before passing through the torii.
2. Walk near the edge of the approach, as the center is said to be for the gods.
3. Purify your hands and mouth at the water pavilion.
4. Bow twice, clap twice, bow once.
5. At the camphor tree, do not damage it or step into its roots; pray quietly.

## FAQ

**How long should I allow?** About 40 minutes to 1 hour for the shrine, camphor tree and Sun Path Marker.

**Is there an admission fee?** The grounds are reported to be free to enter.

**Can I visit alone?** Of course. Many come to report a new start, not only for relationships.

**Can I combine it with other Awaji sights?** Yes; it is part of the Japan Heritage story "Awaji, the Island of Creation."
