## Introduction

Kifune Shrine sits beside the **Kibune River, the source of the Kamo River**, in the mountains north of Kyoto. It enshrines the **water deity Takaokami-no-Kami** and has long been called the head shrine of water deities. Famous for the lines of vermilion stone lanterns along its approach, it is also one of Kyoto's most popular **matchmaking shrines**. This guide covers **the history, the three-shrine pilgrimage (Hongu, Okumiya and Yui-no-Yashiro), the water fortune slips, the dragon hole, access and FAQ** in one page.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

The exact founding date of Kifune Shrine is unknown, but records are said to show the buildings were **rebuilt in 677**, so it is considered an old shrine with over 1,300 years of history. A legend says that **Tamayorihime-no-Mikoto travelled up the Yodo, Kamo and Kibune rivers in a yellow boat and enshrined a water deity here**.

Because the Kibune River is the source of the Kamo River and the shrine lies almost due north of the Kyoto Imperial Palace, it was venerated by successive imperial courts as **the guardian of the capital's water**. Prayers were offered for rain in droughts and for the rain to stop in long wet spells. It is also said that **Emperor Saga offered live horses for these prayers**, which later led to the custom of the votive plaque, the ema.

## <span id="sansha"></span>How to Visit the Three Shrines

Kifune Shrine has **three shrines** spread along the Kibune River: **Hongu, Yui-no-Yashiro (Nakamiya) and Okumiya**.

| Shrine | Features |
|---|---|
| Hongu | Enshrines Takaokami. Sacred spring water and the water fortune slip |
| Yui-no-Yashiro | The matchmaking shrine. Knotted wish letters |
| Okumiya | Said to be the original site. The dragon hole and the boat-shaped stone |

Visiting all three in the order **Hongu → Okumiya → Yui-no-Yashiro** is said to be best. The walk from Hongu to Okumiya takes **about 10 minutes** along a quiet riverside path.

## <span id="okumiya"></span>Okumiya, the Dragon Hole and the Boat-Shaped Stone

Beneath the main hall is said to be a large hole called the **"dragon hole" (ryuketsu)**, known as a place of strong energy. Please do not peer into it, and simply pray quietly. Beside the hall is the **"boat-shaped stone" (funagata-ishi)**, said to be where **Tamayorihime's yellow boat was hidden under stones**. It is also revered by people connected with ships and the sea.

## <span id="yui"></span>Yui-no-Yashiro and Matchmaking

Yui-no-Yashiro, between Hongu and Okumiya, enshrines **Iwanagahime-no-Mikoto, a deity of good relationships**, and has been known for matchmaking since the Heian period. A well-known story says that the **poet Izumi Shikibu prayed here to reconcile with her estranged husband, and her wish came true**. A poem monument for her stands on the grounds.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- Visit the three shrines in the order **Hongu → Okumiya → Yui-no-Yashiro**
- Try the **water fortune slip** at Hongu
- Do **not peer into the dragon hole** at Okumiya; pray quietly
- Stone steps can be slippery in rain, so wear **comfortable shoes**
- It gets crowded in the river-terrace season and in autumn, so go **early**

## <span id="access"></span>Access

By train and bus: take the **Eizan Electric Railway Kurama Line to Kibuneguchi Station**, then **Kyoto Bus Route 33** to the Kifune stop, and walk about 5 minutes.

Opening hours are roughly **6:00-18:00 from December 1 to April 30 and 6:00-20:00 from May 1 to November 30** (the amulet office is 9:00-17:00). They change during light-up periods, so please check the official site.

## <span id="season"></span>Best Time to Visit

**Fresh green maples (May-June) and autumn leaves (November)** are especially beautiful. Summer is cooler than central Kyoto and is the river-terrace season. **Early morning** is quiet for walking all three shrines. In winter, snow and ice can make the steps slippery.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, not the center
3. Purify at the water basin: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand
4. Bow twice, clap twice, bow once
5. Take turns when drawing sacred water
6. Stay quiet near the dragon hole and the halls

If you are unsure about the basics, see our [Complete Guide to Shrine Etiquette](/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## <span id="faq"></span>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. In what order should I visit the three shrines?**

A. Generally **Hongu → Okumiya → Yui-no-Yashiro**. The shrines are spread along the river, so wear comfortable shoes.

**Q2. How much is the water fortune slip?**

A. About **200 yen**. Prices can change, so please check on site or on the official site.

**Q3. What time can I visit?**

A. From 6:00. Closing time varies by season: around **18:00 in winter and 20:00 in the regular season**.

**Q4. Can I visit Kifune and Kurama together?**

A. A **ridge trail** connects the Okunoin of Kurama-dera with Kifune Shrine, and it is a popular hike. Wear suitable clothing and allow plenty of time.

**Q5. Can I go on a rainy day?**

A. Yes, but steps and mountain paths are slippery. Kifune in the rain has deep greens and a quiet charm.

## Summary

- Kifune Shrine is an old shrine of a water deity beside the **Kibune River, source of the Kamo River**
- The main deity is **Takaokami**; Yui-no-Yashiro enshrines the matchmaking deity **Iwanagahime**
- The basic route is **Hongu → Okumiya → Yui-no-Yashiro**
- Highlights are the **water fortune slip** and the **dragon hole and boat-shaped stone**
- Reach it from **Kibuneguchi Station** by Kyoto Bus or on foot
