## Introduction

Chichibu Shrine stands in the town center of Chichibu City, Saitama, as the **chief guardian shrine of the Chichibu region**. Together with Mitsumine Shrine and Hodosan Shrine it is counted as one of the **Three Shrines of Chichibu**, and it is known for the **vividly colored carvings of its main hall** and the **Chichibu Night Festival**, inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.

Because it is within walking distance of the station, it is an easy first stop in Chichibu. This article covers its history, deities and blessings, the carving highlights, the festival, access and nearby spots.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

According to tradition, the shrine began when **Chichibuhiko no Mikoto**, governor of the ancient Chichibu region, enshrined his ancestral deity **Yagokoro Omoikane no Mikoto**, in the reign of Emperor Sujin. In 2014 it marked 2,100 years since its founding.

In the medieval period, devotion to the Buddhist Myoken Bosatsu was incorporated, and the shrine was for a time called "Myoken-gu." After the separation of Shinto and Buddhism in the Meiji era, Myoken was replaced by Ame-no-Minakanushi no Kami, and Prince Chichibu Yasuhito was later enshrined, forming today's four deities.

The present halls are said to have been donated by Tokugawa Ieyasu in 1592. They are **Gongen-zukuri**, with the main hall, offering hall and worship hall combined in one structure, and renovation work is said to have been completed in September 2024. For more on this style, see [How to Tell Shrine Architecture Styles Apart](/blog/shrine-architecture-styles/).

## <span id="benefit"></span>Blessings

Chichibu Shrine is said to bring the following blessings.

- **Academic success and exam passing** (Omoikane is regarded as a deity of wisdom)
- **Business prosperity**
- **Good relationships**
- **Family safety and safe childbirth**
- **Protection from misfortune and from directional ill luck**
- **Traffic safety and long, healthy life**

## <span id="carving"></span>Main Hall Carvings

The great appeal of Chichibu Shrine is the **richly colored carvings** that surround the main hall. Some are said to be the work of the master carver **Hidari Jingoro**, and each side has a different theme. Walk once around the hall and match each direction with its name.

- **Tiger of Fertility and Child-Rearing** (front)
- **Three Energetic Monkeys** (west side)
- **Owl of the Pole Star** (north side)
- **Chained Dragon** (east side)

"Hokushin" means the North Star. The owl is said to face the main hall with its body while its head turns due north, and it is closely tied to Myoken worship, the veneration of the North Star and Big Dipper.

## <span id="yomatsuri"></span>Chichibu Night Festival

The shrine's annual festival is the **Chichibu Night Festival**, held every year on **December 2 and 3**. It is counted among Japan's three great float festivals, alongside Kyoto's Gion Festival and Takayama's festival, and the float events and kagura of the Chichibu festival are inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.

The highlight is the night of December 3. Six floats, both canopy floats and stage floats, are pulled through the city to follow the procession of the deity toward the Otabisho, and fireworks light the winter sky. Because it becomes extremely crowded, allow plenty of time.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- After praying at the Haiden, **walk once around the main hall** to see the carvings
- It is easier to find them by **matching direction and name**
- Plan on about **30 minutes to 1 hour** for worship and viewing the carvings
- Goshuin fees and reception hours can change, so **check the official website beforehand**
- On the festival days (December 2-3), the grounds and surroundings are very crowded

## <span id="access"></span>Access

By train: about 3 minutes on foot from **Chichibu Station** (Chichibu Railway), or about 15 minutes on foot from **Seibu-Chichibu Station** (Seibu Chichibu Line).

By car: about 40 minutes from Hanazono IC on the Kan-etsu Expressway (depending on traffic).

**Parking**: free parking for worshippers is available (about 30 spaces).

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Spots to Visit

- **[Mitsumine Shrine](/blog/mitsumine-jinja/)** — One of the Three Shrines of Chichibu, high in the mountains at about 1,100 m and known for Oin-sama wolf worship
- **Hodosan Shrine** — One of the Three Shrines of Chichibu, in Nagatoro
- **Nagatoro Gorge** — Famous for river rafting
- **Waraji Katsudon** — A Chichibu local specialty, good for lunch after your visit

If you combine it with Mitsumine Shrine, visiting Chichibu Shrine in town first and then heading up to the mountain shrine is an easy order to follow.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow once before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, as the center is the deity's path
3. Purify at the temizuya: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand, then the ladle
4. **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once**
5. Look at the carvings quietly, so as not to disturb other visitors

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q. What are the highlights of Chichibu Shrine?

The carvings around the main hall (the Chained Dragon, Three Energetic Monkeys, Owl of the Pole Star and the Tiger of Fertility and Child-Rearing) and the December Chichibu Night Festival are the best known.

### Q. How long does a visit take?

Plan on about 30 minutes to 1 hour for worship and a walk around the main hall carvings.

### Q. What are the Three Shrines of Chichibu?

This is the collective name for Chichibu Shrine, Mitsumine Shrine and Hodosan Shrine. Many people visit them together.

### Q. Can I take photos?

Photography rules can differ by time and place. Please follow posted notices and the official website, and avoid disturbing other visitors.

- Chichibu Shrine is the **chief guardian shrine of the Chichibu region** and one of the Three Shrines of Chichibu
