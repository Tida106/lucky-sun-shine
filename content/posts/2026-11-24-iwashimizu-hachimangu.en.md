## Introduction

Iwashimizu Hachimangu is an old shrine on **Mt. Otokoyama** in Yawata, Kyoto Prefecture. Standing in the **southwest "ura-kimon" direction** of the ancient capital Heian-kyo, it has been revered as a protector of the city, and today it draws worshippers seeking **protection from misfortune**. Its vermilion halls are designated National Treasures.

It is said to have begun when the deity Hachiman was invited from Kyushu's [Usa Jingu](/blog/usa-jingu/), and the two shrines remain closely linked. This article covers its history, highlights and access.

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

The monk Gyokyo is said to have received an oracle from Hachiman while praying at Usa Jingu in Kyushu, telling him that the deity would move to Mt. Otokoyama near the capital to protect the nation. Hachiman was accordingly welcomed to the mountain in 859, and the halls were completed in 860 by order of the court.

The shrine received deep devotion from the imperial court and later became the clan shrine of the Minamoto warrior family. For centuries it was a site of combined Shinto and Buddhist worship until the separation of the two in the Meiji era. In 2016 the main hall and related buildings were designated National Treasures.

## <span id="deities"></span>Deities and Blessings

The three deities are Hachiman, that is **Emperor Ojin**, his mother **Empress Jingu**, and **Himemegami**. Hachiman has long been revered as protector of Kyoto and the imperial family. Blessings often mentioned include:

- **Protection from misfortune and good fortune**
- **Protection of the nation and success in contests**
- **Safe childbirth and child-rearing**

### The National Treasure Main Hall

The main hall is in **Hachiman-zukuri**, with two buildings joined front and back, and is said to be a lavish structure decorated with animal carvings and more. For this style, see [How to Tell Shrine Architecture Styles Apart](/blog/shrine-architecture-styles/).

### Kora Shrine and Tsurezuregusa

The subsidiary Kora Shrine at the foot of Mt. Otokoyama appears in *Tsurezuregusa*, in the well-known story of a monk from Ninna-ji who mistook it for the main shrine and went home after praying there.

### The Edison Monument

The grounds hold a monument to Thomas Edison, recalling the account that links his light bulb research with the bamboo of Yawata. Commemorative festivals are held around February and October each year.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- From Keihan **Iwashimizu-Hachimangu Station**, the cable car makes the climb easy
- From the cable car's top station, the halls are about **a 5-minute walk**
- The **Iwashimizu Festival** on September 15 is known as an imperial-envoy festival
- Allow about **1 to 1.5 hours** for worship and a stroll

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train: from Keihan Main Line **Iwashimizu-Hachimangu Station**, take the approach cable car for about 3 minutes to Hachimangu-sanjo Station, then walk about 5 minutes.

By car: about 5 km, or roughly 10 minutes, from Oyamazaki IC on the Meishin Expressway. Parking is available, but fees and operations may change in busy seasons.

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow once before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, as the center is the deity's path
3. Purify at the temizuya: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand, then the ladle
4. **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once**
5. Stay quiet and avoid disturbing other visitors

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q. What blessings does it offer?

As a guardian of Kyoto's ura-kimon direction, it is best known for protection from misfortune.

### Q. How do I get up the mountain?

The approach cable car from Keihan Iwashimizu-Hachimangu Station is the easiest way. There is also a walking approach.

### Q. How is it related to Usa Jingu?

It is said to have begun when Hachiman was invited from Usa Jingu in Kyushu.
