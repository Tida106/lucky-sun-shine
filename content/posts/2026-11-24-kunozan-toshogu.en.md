## Introduction

Kunozan Toshogu stands on Mt. Kuno in Shizuoka and is regarded as **the original of the Toshogu shrines** that enshrine Tokugawa Ieyasu across Japan. According to his will, Ieyasu is said to have first been buried on Mt. Kuno. Its halls, decorated in black lacquer, gold and vivid colors, were designated National Treasures in 2010.

The halls are in **Gongen-zukuri**, said to be the prototype for Toshogu shrines such as the one at Nikko. This article covers its history, highlights and access. For the style itself, see [How to Tell Shrine Architecture Styles Apart](/blog/shrine-architecture-styles/).

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

Tokugawa Ieyasu spent his later years in Sunpu (Shizuoka) and died in 1616. In keeping with his will that his remains be buried on Mt. Kuno, he is said to have been laid to rest there first. In 1617 the second shogun, Hidetada, founded Kunozan Toshogu, and Ieyasu is said to have been re-enshrined at Nikko in the same year.

The halls are said to have been built by Nakai Masakiyo, and are reported to have been repaired repeatedly over some 400 years to protect their lacquer coating. Behind the main hall stands a mausoleum, whose stone pagoda is said to have replaced an earlier wooden structure in the time of Iemitsu.

## <span id="deities"></span>Deities and Blessings

The deity is **Tokugawa Ieyasu**, founder of the Edo shogunate. Blessings often mentioned include:

- **Good fortune and career success**
- **Success in contests**
- **Protection from misfortune**

## <span id="highlight"></span>Highlights of the Grounds

### The Vividly Colored National Treasure Halls

The Gongen-zukuri halls, with main hall, stone chamber and worship hall joined as one, are fully lacquered and adorned with gold fittings and carvings.

### The Mausoleum

Behind the main hall, up about 40 stone steps from the mausoleum gate, stands the mausoleum said to hold Ieyasu's remains, marked by a stone pagoda about 5.5 m high.

### The Museum

The museum on site is said to hold more than 2,000 items, including Ieyasu's everyday belongings and the weapons and armor of successive shoguns.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- Viewing the halls requires an admission fee (about 500 yen for adults; subject to change)
- A combined ticket with the museum is available
- Climbing from the foot of the mountain means **1,159 steps**, so allow your strength
- Allow about **1.5 to 2 hours** for worship and viewing

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Ropeway: the **Nihondaira Ropeway** is the convenient way up Mt. Kuno. After getting off, climb about 100 steps and pass through the Romon gate.

On foot: you can also climb the 1,159-step main approach from the foot of the mountain.

Please check current operations and fees in the official guidance beforehand.

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Spots to Visit

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow once before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, as the center is the deity's path
3. Purify at the temizuya: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand, then the ladle
4. **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once**
5. Stay quiet and avoid disturbing other visitors

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q. What kind of shrine is Kunozan Toshogu?

It enshrines Tokugawa Ieyasu and is regarded as the original of all Toshogu shrines.

### Q. Are the halls a National Treasure?

Yes. The main hall, stone chamber and worship hall were designated National Treasures in 2010.

### Q. How do I get there?

The Nihondaira Ropeway is convenient. You can also climb the stairway from the foot of the mountain.
