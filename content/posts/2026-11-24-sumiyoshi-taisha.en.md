## Introduction

Sumiyoshi Taisha in Sumiyoshi Ward, Osaka, is the **head shrine of about 2,300 Sumiyoshi shrines** across Japan. Osaka residents affectionately call it "Sumiyossan," and more than two million people are said to visit for the New Year each year.

The halls of its Hongu (main shrines) are in the ancient **Sumiyoshi-zukuri** style and are designated National Treasures. This article covers its history, highlights and access. For the style itself, see [How to Tell Shrine Architecture Styles Apart](/blog/shrine-architecture-styles/).

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

According to tradition, Sumiyoshi Taisha began in 211, when Empress Jingu, ruling after her husband's death, enshrined the three Sumiyoshi sea deities. They are said to have been born when Izanagi purified himself in the sea after returning from the underworld, and one theory identifies them with the three stars of Orion's belt.

When founded, the shrine stood close to the shore, but silting and land reclamation pushed the coastline westward. The Hongu were once rebuilt every 20 years, a custom that ended by the early 19th century, and the present halls are said to date from 1810.

## <span id="deities"></span>Deities and Blessings

The shrine enshrines the sea deities, the **three Sumiyoshi deities**, and **Empress Jingu**, one in each of the four Hongu. Blessings often mentioned include:

- **Safe voyages and protection at sea**
- **Protection from misfortune and family safety**
- **Business prosperity** (known for the "Hatsutatsu-mairi" visits)

## <span id="highlight"></span>Highlights of the Grounds

### Sumiyoshi-zukuri Halls

Sumiyoshi-zukuri is a simple style with straight gabled roofs, dating from before elaborate architecture arrived from the Asian continent, and is regarded as a model of Japanese shrine architecture. Vermilion pillars, white walls and straight roofs are its hallmarks.

### The Arched Bridge (Sorihashi)

The grounds hold a steeply arched bridge. Its vermilion railings are reflected in the pond below, the bridge itself is stepped, and a pair of komainu sit at its foot. It is also known as the setting of a short story by Kawabata Yasunari, and a monument to him stands nearby.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- It is common to pray **in order, starting from the First Hongu**
- Notice that the four Hongu **face the sea (west)**
- New Year visits are extremely crowded, so allow plenty of time
- Allow about **1 hour** for worship and a stroll

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train: a short walk from **Sumiyoshitaisha Station** on the Nankai Main Line. Sumiyoshi-torimae Station on the Hankai Tramway is also nearby.

By car: please check parking availability and fees in the official guidance beforehand.

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Spots to Visit

- **Arched Bridge**: a landmark of the grounds
- **Usa Jingu**: head shrine of Hachiman shrines. See [our Usa Jingu article](/blog/usa-jingu/)
- **Iwashimizu Hachimangu**: Kyoto's old shrine against misfortune. See [our Iwashimizu Hachimangu article](/blog/iwashimizu-hachimangu/)

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow once before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, as the center is the deity's path
3. Purify at the temizuya: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand, then the ladle
4. **Bow twice, clap twice, bow once**
5. Stay quiet and avoid disturbing other visitors

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q. What kind of shrine is Sumiyoshi Taisha?

It is an old Osaka shrine regarded as the head of all Sumiyoshi shrines, worshipped as a shrine of sea deities.

### Q. What is Sumiyoshi-zukuri?

An ancient style of hall with straight gabled roofs, seen in the National Treasure Hongu.

### Q. Is it crowded at New Year?

Yes. More than two million visitors are said to come, so it is very crowded.
