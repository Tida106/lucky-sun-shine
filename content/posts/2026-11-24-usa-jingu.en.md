## Introduction

Usa Jingu in Usa City, Oita, is the **head shrine of the Hachiman shrines**, which are said to number in the tens of thousands across Japan. Visitors traditionally pray at both the **Upper Shrine**, with its vermilion halls, and the **Lower Shrine** at the foot of the hill. The halls of the Upper Shrine are regarded as the prototype of the distinctive **Hachiman-zukuri** architectural style.

This article covers its history, deities, blessings, highlights, its unusual way of praying and access. For more on the style itself, see [How to Tell Shrine Architecture Styles Apart](/blog/shrine-architecture-styles/).

## <span id="history"></span>History and Origins

According to the shrine's records, the deity Hachiman first appeared near Mt. Ogura in 571, and the first hall is said to have been built on the hilltop where the Upper Shrine now stands in 725.

The shrine is also known for the tradition that Hachiman helped with the casting of the Great Buddha of Todai-ji in the Nara period. It is regarded as an early example of Shinto-Buddhist syncretism, and faith in Hachiman spread to shrines across Japan.

The Lower Shrine is said to have been founded around a century after the Upper Shrine, in the early 9th century. After the separation of Shinto and Buddhism in the Meiji era, Usa Jingu became a purely Shinto shrine.

## <span id="deities"></span>Deities and Blessings

The same three deities are enshrined in three halls at both the Upper and Lower Shrines.

- **First Hall: Hachiman Okami (Emperor Ojin)**: regarded as a guardian of the nation and imperial family
- **Second Hall: Himegami**: a collective name for three goddesses, associated with safe sea travel
- **Third Hall: Empress Jingu**: said to bring safe childbirth

Blessings often mentioned include good fortune, traffic safety, safe childbirth and learning.

### The Three Halls in Hachiman-zukuri

The three halls of the Upper Shrine are a leading example of **Hachiman-zukuri**, in which two buildings are placed one behind the other with their roofs joined, and they are designated National Treasures. Visitors see only the outside, but the gutter running between the front and rear roofs is a feature rarely found at other shrines.

### The Lower Shrine and the Inner Shrine

The Lower Shrine at the foot of the hill enshrines the same three deities. About 6 km to the south, on Mt. Omoto, stands the Inner Shrine.

## <span id="point"></span>Visiting Tips

- Pray with **two bows, four claps and one bow** (four claps instead of the usual two)
- Pray at **each of the three halls**, not only the central one
- Visit the **Lower Shrine** as well as the Upper Shrine (a local saying holds that without the Lower Shrine, you have prayed only halfway)
- Allow about **1 to 2 hours** for both shrines
- The Upper Shrine is reached by stone steps, so wear comfortable shoes

## <span id="access"></span>Access

Train and bus: about 10 minutes by bus from **Usa Station** on the JR Nippo Line, then about a 10-minute walk (please check current schedules).

By car: the shrine is close to the center of Usa, with parking around the grounds. It can be crowded in busy seasons.

## <span id="nearby"></span>Nearby Spots to Visit

- **Lower Shrine**: the foothill sanctuary, best visited together with the Upper Shrine
- **Inner Shrine (Mt. Omoto)**: about 6 km south of the main grounds
- **Treasure Hall**: displays portable shrines and old documents
- **Usa karaage**: a local specialty dish

For more shrines on Kyushu, see [Shrines of Kyushu and Okinawa](/blog/shrine-kyushu-guide/).

## <span id="manner"></span>Basic Visiting Manners

1. Bow once before passing through the torii
2. Walk along the edge of the approach, as the center is the deity's path
3. Purify at the temizuya: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand, then the ladle
4. At Usa Jingu, **bow twice, clap four times, bow once**
5. Pray quietly at each of the three halls

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

### Q. What kind of shrine is Usa Jingu?

It is an old shrine in Oita regarded as the head shrine of all Hachiman shrines in Japan.

### Q. How many times do I clap?

At Usa Jingu the custom is two bows, four claps and one bow.

### Q. Should I visit both the Upper and Lower Shrines?

Yes. A local saying holds that visiting only one is only half a visit, so both are recommended.
