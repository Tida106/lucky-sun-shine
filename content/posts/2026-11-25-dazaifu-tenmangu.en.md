## Introduction

**Dazaifu Tenmangu** in Dazaifu, Fukuoka, is a shrine built over the grave of Sugawara no Michizane, the deity of learning (known as Tenjin). It is regarded as the head shrine of the roughly 12,000 Tenmangu and Tenjin shrines across Japan, and is counted among Japan's three great Tenjin shrines together with Kitano Tenmangu in Kyoto and Hofu Tenmangu in Yamaguchi. About 10 million people are said to visit each year, making it one of Kyushu's best-known shrines.

This guide covers the history, the highlights of the grounds, the plum season and annual events, visiting tips, and access for first-time visitors.

## History: A Shrine Built over Michizane's Grave

Sugawara no Michizane (845 to 903) was a scholar and politician of the Heian period. He was sent from Kyoto to Dazaifu in exile in 901 and died here in 903. According to tradition, his disciple Ajisake no Yasuyuki then built a mausoleum shrine at his grave, which became the origin of the shrine.

Later, Michizane came to be honored as a "deity of learning," and Tenmangu shrines spread across the country. Dazaifu Tenmangu is regarded as their head shrine.

The present main hall is said to have been rebuilt in 1591 by Kobayakawa Takakage. It is a splendid example of Momoyama-period architecture and is designated an Important Cultural Property. A major restoration, the first in 124 years, began in 2023 and took about three years; it is said to have been completed in 2026. Please check the official website for what can be visited.

## Blessings (as traditionally said)

- **Academic success and exam prayers**, through Tenjin, the deity of learning
- **Protection from misfortune**, praying for safe days
- **Sincerity**, valuing a heart that does its best

These blessings are cultural and symbolic, and no result is guaranteed. Many visitors find it helpful to report their efforts so far and give thanks.

## Highlights of the Grounds

### The Sacred Bull

Michizane is said to have been born in the Year of the Ox, and many bull statues have been dedicated in the grounds. It is said that rubbing the head of the sacred bull inside the entrance brings wisdom, and its head and horns have been polished smooth by visitors.

### Shinji Pond and the Arched Bridge

In the middle of the grounds lies Shinji Pond, shaped like the character for "heart." Three bridges cross it, said to represent the past, present and future. The arched bridge with vermilion railings stands out against the green trees and the water, and is a popular photo spot.

### The Flying Plum (Tobiume)

The sacred tree to the right of the main hall, as you face it, is the "Tobiume" (flying plum). According to legend, when Michizane composed a poem lamenting his parting from the plum tree at his Kyoto residence, the tree longed for its master and flew to Dazaifu in a single night.

## Plum Season and Annual Events

Dazaifu Tenmangu is famous for its plums. The grounds are said to hold about 6,000 plum trees of around 200 varieties, which bloom in red and white from late January to early March.

Main events are listed below. Dates change from year to year, so please check the official announcement.

- **Usokae ritual** (January 7): worshippers swap carved wooden bullfinches, turning last year's misfortunes into luck
- **Kyokusui no En** (first Sunday of March): people in Heian-period costume compose poems before a floating sake cup reaches them
- **Shinko-shiki Taisai** (late September): a grand procession carrying Michizane's spirit by portable shrine, the shrine's largest festival
- **Zankiku no En** (late November): a ceremony remembering Michizane's love of chrysanthemums

## Visiting Tips

1. Bow lightly before the torii gate
2. Purify your hands and mouth at the water basin
3. At the main hall: two bows, two claps, one bow
4. Ema, shuin stamps and charms come after your prayer

The full etiquette is explained in [Shrine Visit Basics: Etiquette, Dress and Manners](/en/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## The Approach and Umegae Mochi

An approach of about 400 meters leads from Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station to the shrine. More than 80 souvenir shops and tea houses line the way, and the smell of the local specialty, umegae mochi (a grilled rice cake with sweet bean paste), drifts through the air. Many visitors enjoy snacking along the road after their visit.

## Access

- About 30 minutes by train from Nishitetsu Fukuoka (Tenjin) Station, then about 5 minutes on foot from Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station
- About 40 minutes by car from Hakata
- By car: about 6 km (15 minutes) from Dazaifu IC on the Kyushu Expressway; paid parking is available (about 1,000 spaces)

It gets very crowded at New Year, in exam season and during the plum season, so allow plenty of time.

## FAQ

### Q. How is Dazaifu Tenmangu different from Kitano Tenmangu?

Both enshrine Sugawara no Michizane and are counted among Japan's three great Tenjin shrines. Dazaifu Tenmangu stands over Michizane's grave in Fukuoka, where he died. Kitano Tenmangu is in Kyoto.

### Q. How long does a visit take?

Walking from the approach to the main hall while seeing the highlights takes about 10 to 20 minutes. Allow 1 to 2 hours if you want to pray and snack along the approach at a relaxed pace.

### Q. Is there an admission fee?

The grounds are free. Some facilities, such as the treasure hall, charge a fee.

## Summary

Dazaifu Tenmangu is the head shrine of Tenjin worship, built over Michizane's grave. Its sacred bull, the arched bridge over Shinji Pond, the Romon gate, the main hall and the plum season give you plenty to enjoy. Whether you pray for your studies or simply give thanks, visit with a calm heart.
