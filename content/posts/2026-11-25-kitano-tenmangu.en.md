## Introduction

**Kitano Tenmangu** in Kamigyo Ward, Kyoto, enshrines Sugawara no Michizane, the deity of learning. It is regarded as the head shrine of the roughly 12,000 Tenmangu and Tenjin shrines across Japan, and is affectionately called "Kitano no Tenjin-san." Many students visit to pray for exam success.

This guide covers the history, blessings, highlights, the plum and autumn seasons, the monthly fair on the 25th, and access for first-time visitors.

## History

According to tradition, the shrine began in 947 when the spirit of Sugawara no Michizane was enshrined here. Michizane was a Heian-period scholar and politician. After he was exiled and died, a series of disasters struck the capital, and he was enshrined to calm his spirit.

Over time, he came to be honored as a "deity of learning," and as temple schools spread in the Edo period, Tenjin shrines appeared across the country. Kitano Tenmangu is regarded as the birthplace of Tenjin worship, and is counted among Japan's three great Tenjin shrines together with Dazaifu Tenmangu (Fukuoka) and Hofu Tenmangu (Yamaguchi).

## Blessings (as traditionally said)

- **Academic success and exam prayers**, through Michizane, the deity of learning
- **Progress in the arts and culture**, as a deity of learning and culture
- **Protection and wishes**, as a place for daily gratitude and prayer

These blessings are cultural and symbolic, and no result is guaranteed. Many visitors find it helpful to report their efforts so far and give thanks.

### The Worship Hall and Main Hall (National Treasure)

The current halls are said to have been built in 1607 by Toyotomi Hideyori, and are known as a splendid example of Momoyama-period architecture. The worship hall and main hall are joined by a connecting room, and are designated National Treasures.

## Plum and Autumn Leaves

Kitano Tenmangu is a famous plum-viewing spot, tied to Michizane's love of plum trees. About 1,500 trees are said to bloom from late February to mid-March. The Baika-sai plum festival on February 25 is linked to the anniversary of Michizane's death.

In autumn, the grounds are also known for their foliage. About 350 maples are said to grow around the historic Odoi earthwork, and the peak is usually mid-November to early December. The maple garden charges an admission fee, and dates and hours change each year, so please check the official announcement.

## The Monthly "Tenjin-san" Fair on the 25th

Michizane's birthday is said to be June 25 and the anniversary of his death February 25, so a fair is held on the 25th of every month. Stalls selling antiques, used clothing and more line the approach, and it gets very lively. The shrine's parking lot is closed on the 25th, so public transport is recommended.

## Visiting Tips

1. Bow lightly before passing through the torii
2. Purify your hands and mouth at the water pavilion
3. Make two bows, two claps and one bow at the hall
4. Ema and goshuin come after your prayer

For the full steps, see [Shrine Visit Basics](/en/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## Access

- About 35 minutes from Kyoto Station on Kyoto City Bus route 50, getting off at Kitano Tenmangu-mae
- About 5 minutes on foot from Kitano-Hakubaicho Station (Keifuku Electric Railway)
- By car, about 30 minutes from Meishin Expressway Kyoto-minami IC (parking available, but closed on the 25th)

It gets very crowded during exam season, in the plum and autumn seasons, and on the 25th, so allow extra time.

## FAQ

### Q. How is Kitano Tenmangu different from Dazaifu Tenmangu?

Both enshrine Sugawara no Michizane and are counted among the three great Tenjin shrines. Kitano Tenmangu is in Kyoto, and Dazaifu Tenmangu is in Fukuoka.

### Q. When should I visit to pray for exam success?

There is no fixed timing. Visit whenever you feel ready, with gratitude and resolve. It is very crowded just before exams, so an earlier visit can be easier.

### Q. Is there an entrance fee?

Visiting the grounds is free. Some areas, such as the maple garden, charge a fee.

## Summary

Kitano Tenmangu is the head shrine of Tenjin worship and enshrines the deity of learning. Its National Treasure halls, the Sanko-mon gate, and the plum and autumn scenery make it a rewarding visit. Whether you pray for your studies or simply give thanks, visit with a calm mind.
