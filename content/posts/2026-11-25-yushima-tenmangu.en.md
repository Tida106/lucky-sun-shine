## Introduction

**Yushima Tenmangu** in Bunkyo Ward, Tokyo, is widely known as **Yushima Tenjin**. It enshrines Sugawara no Michizane, the deity of learning, and students from all over Japan visit to pray for exam success. It is counted among the three great Tenjin shrines of the Kanto region, and in early spring about 300 plum trees bloom in its grounds.

This guide covers the history, blessings, highlights, plum festival and access for first-time visitors. "Yushima Tenmangu" is the official name and "Yushima Tenjin" is the popular name; both refer to the same shrine.

## History

According to the shrine's tradition, it was founded in 458 by imperial command to enshrine Amenotajikarao no Mikoto. In 1355, Sugawara no Michizane was enshrined here as well, at the request of local people.

Ota Dokan is said to have restored the shrine later, and Tokugawa Ieyasu is said to have donated land to it in the Edo period. Along with Kannoji in Yanaka and Meguro Fudo, it was known as one of the "Three Tomi" of Edo, where lotteries called tomikuji were held.

## Blessings (as traditionally said)

- **Academic success and exam prayers**, through Michizane, the deity of learning
- **Good fortune in contests**, linked to Amenotajikarao no Mikoto, a deity of great strength
- **Relationships and success**, which also draws many working adults

These blessings are cultural and symbolic, and no outcome is guaranteed. Many visitors find it helpful to report their efforts so far and give thanks, rather than only asking for success.

## Highlights of the Grounds

### The Cypress Main Hall

The current halls were rebuilt in 1995 using cypress throughout. Under the broad, curved roof you will find colorful carvings and golden fittings.

### The Bronze Torii and Approach

A bronze torii stands at the entrance of the approach. Its green patina gives it a calm look, and the main hall can be seen beyond. The grounds also have a stone once used to post notices about lost children and a monument related to Izumi Kyoka's novel *Onna Keizu*.

## Visiting Tips

1. Bow lightly before passing through the torii
2. Purify your hands and mouth at the water pavilion
3. Make two bows, two claps and one bow at the hall
4. Ema and goshuin come after your prayer

For the full steps, see [Shrine Visit Basics](/en/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## Plum Festival and Chrysanthemum Festival

The shrine has been a plum-viewing spot since the Edo period. The Bunkyo Plum Festival is usually held from early February to early March, when about 300 plum trees bloom. A chrysanthemum festival is also held in November. Dates and details change every year, so please check the official announcement.

## Access

- About 2 minutes on foot from Yushima Station (Tokyo Metro Chiyoda Line), Exit 3
- About 5 minutes from Ueno-hirokoji Station (Ginza Line), Exit A4
- About 8 minutes from Okachimachi Station (JR), North Exit

It gets very crowded during exam season and the plum festival, so allow extra time.

## Nearby

Ueno Park and Shinobazu Pond are close by and nice for a stroll after your visit.

## FAQ

### Q. Are Yushima Tenmangu and Yushima Tenjin the same?

Yes. Yushima Tenmangu is the official name, and Yushima Tenjin is the popular name.

### Q. When should I visit to pray for exam success?

There is no fixed timing. Visit whenever you feel ready, with gratitude and resolve. It is very crowded just before exams, so an earlier visit can be easier.

### Q. Can I get a goshuin?

Yes, the shrine is said to offer them. Please check the hours and format on site or in the official announcement.

## Summary

Yushima Tenmangu (Yushima Tenjin) is one of Tokyo's leading shrines to the deity of learning. Its cypress halls, the stroking ox and plum season make it a rewarding visit. Whether you pray for your studies or simply give thanks, visit with a calm mind.
