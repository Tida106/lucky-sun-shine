## Introduction

Dewa Sanzan Shrine stands on Mt. Haguro in Tsuruoka, Yamagata, and it is **one of Tohoku's leading sacred sites of mountain worship and Shugendo**. Mt. Haguro, Mt. Gassan and Mt. Yudono are together called "Dewa Sanzan" (the Three Mountains of Dewa), and the Sanshin Gosaiden on the summit of Mt. Haguro enshrines the deities of all three.

Three things worth knowing before you go:

- **You can pray to all three mountains at the summit of Mt. Haguro.** Gassan and Yudono close under snow in winter, so the Sanshin Gosaiden on Haguro is the place you can visit all year round.
- **The approach of 2,446 stone steps from the Zuishinmon gate to the summit is famous.** The climb is said to take around an hour, so sturdy footwear matters.
- **You do not have to walk.** A bus or car takes you close to the summit, so you can choose a plan to match your time and energy.

## <span id="history"></span>History and the meaning of the three mountains

"Dewa Sanzan" is the collective name of Mt. Haguro (414 m), Mt. Gassan (1,984 m) and Mt. Yudono (1,504 m). According to tradition, about 1,400 years ago Prince Hachinoko, a son of the 32nd emperor Sushun, climbed Mt. Haguro and sensed the deity Haguro Gongen, which is said to be how it began.

Each mountain is said to carry its own meaning:

- **Mt. Haguro**: the present. A mountain for praying for happiness in this life.
- **Mt. Gassan**: the past. A mountain for praying for the peace of those who have passed.
- **Mt. Yudono**: the future. A mountain for praying for rebirth.

Visiting all three is called a "journey of rebirth." In the Edo period it was said, "Ise in the west, Oku-mairi in the east," and many people are said to have made the pilgrimage.

## <span id="benefit"></span>What people visit for

Blessings listed for the shrine include business prosperity, good relationships, traffic safety, family well-being, protection from misfortune, safe childbirth and long life. These are traditional introductions and not a promise of results.

A distinctive point is that **many people value the time spent walking up itself**: climbing the steps one at a time and settling the mind in the quiet of the cedar avenue.

## <span id="approach"></span>Walking the approach (Zuishinmon to the Sanshin Gosaiden)

Here is the flow if you walk.

1. **Zuishinmon gate**: the sacred area of Dewa Sanzan begins here. Beyond the gate, the stone-step approach starts.
2. **The National Treasure five-story pagoda**: a pagoda about 29 m high among the cedars, said to be the oldest pagoda in the Tohoku region. It stands just before the first slope, so it is a good first stop.
3. **The stone steps (First to Third Slopes)**: 2,446 steps over about 1,700 m. Pictures such as sake cups and gourds are carved here and there, 33 in all. It is said that finding every one makes a wish come true.
4. **Ninosaka tea house**: a rest stop just past the Second Slope. Many people stop for its famous chikara-mochi rice cakes.
5. **Sanshin Gosaiden**: a large thatched-roof shrine hall at the summit. The roof is introduced as about 2.1 m thick, and the interior is fully lacquered. The deities of all three mountains are enshrined together.
6. **Kagami-ike pond and the bell tower**: the pond in front of the hall has long been revered as a mysterious pond. The bell tower and great bell on the east side are also worth a look.

## <span id="omamori"></span>Goshuin, charms and annual events

Goshuin and charms are said to be available around the Sanshin Gosaiden at the summit. Hours and items can change, so it is safest to check before you go.

The best-known annual event is the **Shorei-sai**, held from New Year's Eve into the early hours of New Year's Day. It is a signature festival of Mt. Haguro and is designated an Important Intangible Folk Cultural Property. Its core is said to be a ritual in which two yamabushi (mountain ascetics), who have spent 100 days in seclusion, compete over which one is favored by the deity. Many people visit around the New Year.

## <span id="course"></span>Model routes and timing

### Walking up to the summit (about 2–3 hours)

The classic route is Zuishinmon → pagoda → stone steps → Ninosaka tea house → Sanshin Gosaiden → bus down. The climb is said to take about one hour, and the descent about 40 minutes.

### Taking the bus to the summit (about 1–1.5 hours)

Take the bus to the summit and visit the Sanshin Gosaiden and Kagami-ike pond. On the way back, walking down the steps toward the pagoda lets you enjoy the approach even in a short time.

## <span id="point"></span>Tips for a smooth visit

- **Wear comfortable shoes.** The long stone steps can be slippery on rainy days or in morning dew. Sandals and heels are best avoided.
- **Bring drinks.** There are few shops along the steps. In summer in particular, carry extra water.
- **Check the return bus time first.** Buses are not frequent, so decide how long to stay at the summit.
- **Check the season for Gassan and Yudono.** They close under snow, so the visiting period is limited.
- **Leave energy for the descent.** Some people find going down harder on the knees, so plan within your means.

| Method | Details |

Parking is near the summit past the toll road, and spaces are said to be limited. It gets crowded around New Year and long weekends, so arriving early is recommended.

## <span id="around"></span>Nearby stops

- **Ideha Cultural Museum**: exhibits the history and culture of Dewa Sanzan. Visiting before you climb is said to make the highlights easier to follow.
- **Shojin ryori at a shukubo**: around Mt. Haguro, some temple lodgings serve shojin ryori (Buddhist vegetarian cuisine). It is known as a rare chance to taste yamabushi culture.
- **Mt. Gassan and Mt. Yudono**: the other two mountains. Many people plan them for another day, according to season and stamina.

## <span id="manner"></span>Shrine etiquette

1. Bow once before passing through a torii or the Zuishinmon gate.
2. The center of the path is said to be for the deity, so walk to the side.
3. Purify at the water pavilion: left hand → right hand → mouth → left hand.
4. Pray with **two bows, two claps, one bow**.
5. Along with your wish, thank the deity for your daily life.
6. If you see yamabushi or people in training, pass quietly and keep photography modest.
7. At Yudonosan Shrine, it is customary not to photograph the sacred area or describe it in detail to others. Follow the guidance on site.

If you are unsure about etiquette, see [Shrine Visit Basics](/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

**Q1. How long does the stone-step climb take?**
A. From Zuishinmon to the summit, about an hour going up is a guide. Including breaks and photos, allow 2–3 hours in total.

**Q2. Can I visit without walking?**
A. Yes. A bus or car takes you close to the summit, and you can pray at the Sanshin Gosaiden.

**Q3. Can I visit in winter?**
A. The Sanshin Gosaiden on Mt. Haguro is said to be open in winter. Snow and ice make the steps slippery, so if you walk you need proper gear and a weather check.

**Q4. Should I also visit Gassan and Yudono?**
A. The Sanshin Gosaiden on Haguro enshrines the deities of all three, so visiting it alone is already a pilgrimage. If you have time and stamina, some people also continue to Gassan and Yudono.

**Q5. Can I get goshuin?**
A. It is said to be available. Hours can change, so please check beforehand.

**Q6. Is it crowded at New Year?**
A. Many people are said to visit, together with the Shorei-sai from New Year's Eve into New Year's Day. Please check public transport and road conditions beforehand.
