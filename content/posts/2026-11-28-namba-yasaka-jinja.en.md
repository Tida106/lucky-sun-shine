## Introduction

Namba Yasaka Shrine is within walking distance of the busy Namba district of Osaka, and it is known for **a giant shrine stage shaped like a lion's head, the "Shishiden" (Lion Hall)**. You walk through a residential street, and suddenly a lion with its mouth wide open appears. Many first-time visitors are said to stop in their tracks.

Three things worth knowing before you go:

- **The star is the Lion Hall.** The lion head is about 12 m high, 11 m wide and 10 m deep, and is said to swallow bad luck and invite winning luck.
- **It is known for winning luck, exam success and business prosperity.** People often visit before exams or important moments at work.
- **Admission is free, and about 30 minutes is a good estimate.** It is easy to fit in between sightseeing in Namba and Dotonbori.

## <span id="history"></span>History and the Lion Hall

The shrine has long been called "**Naniwa Shimo-no-Miya**" and has been loved as the guardian shrine of the Namba area. The founding date is not clear, but it is said to have been known by the Enkyu era (1069–1073) as a shrine to Gion Gozu Tenno. One tradition says it began when Gozu Tenno appeared and calmed an epidemic in the time of Emperor Nintoku.

It was originally a combined Shinto-Buddhist site with a temple and many sub-temples. After the Meiji-era separation of Shinto and Buddhism the temple declined, and in 1872 the shrine became a village-rank shrine. After the buildings were lost in wartime, **the current main hall was completed in 1974**.

The **Lion Hall** was built beside it, to the west of the main hall, at the same time. Its theme is the local lion dance that has long been used to ward off evil, and it is described as an attempt to create a shrine symbol that would surprise people in Osaka. Deities are enshrined inside, and the front is a **stage** for offering kagura and lion dances.

## <span id="benefit"></span>What people visit for

These are things commonly said about this shrine, not guarantees of results.

- **Winning luck:** the big mouth is said to swallow bad luck and hardship and bring victory.
- **Study and exam success:** students and people facing exams are said to visit.
- **Business prosperity:** some visit at career turning points, since the lion is said to invite business luck.
- **Protection from misfortune and relationships:** as a shrine to Susanoo-no-Mikoto it is known for warding off bad luck, and many come for the love fortune slips.

A good way to think of it: a place to tell the lion your resolve before something important, and ask it to give you a push.

### The main hall

Next to the Lion Hall, **the main hall is a calm, mostly white building**. In contrast to the flashy Lion Hall, it lets you pray quietly. Start by giving thanks and sharing your resolve here.

### Water pavilion and the grounds

Rinse your hands and mouth at the water pavilion before going on. Although the shrine is in the middle of the city, the grounds are tidy and easy to switch your mood in, even for a short visit.

## <span id="omamori"></span>Fortune slips, charms and goshuin

The amulet office is said to open **9:00–16:50**. The following are examples introduced in past articles; **types and offering amounts can change**.

- **Love fortune slip (koi-mikuji):** a carp-shaped slip. "Koi" can mean both "love" and "carp," so it is chosen by people hoping for love and good connections. Some come with a carp-shaped "koi-koi" charm.
- **Other slips:** sea bream, folding fan and daruma-shaped slips have also been introduced.
- **Charms:** a lion-head charm against misfortune and a school-bag charm for study and safe commuting have been introduced.
- **Goshuin:** said to be available.

Some **ema (votive plaques)** are printed with two lion-dance heads side by side, a lively design for a shrine with a Lion Hall.

## <span id="event"></span>Annual events

### Tug-of-war ritual (every third Sunday of January; Osaka City Intangible Folk Cultural Property)

This ritual is based on the story of Susanoo-no-Mikoto defeating the eight-headed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi at the upper Hi River in Izumo, relieving people's hardship and opening the way for farming and industry. The official guide says it is held **in the morning on the third Sunday of January** each year. (The same page also describes it as a New Year custom of the 14th, so please check the latest notice before you go.)

The ritual is introduced as follows.

1. **Tsuna-uchi-hajime:** making of the rope begins.
2. **Tsuna-hikidashi:** the rope is brought out.
3. **Hikiai:** the great rope is shaped like the serpent, and teams pull against each other toward that year's lucky direction.
4. **Offering:** the rope is enshrined before the deities, with prayers to ward off illness and disaster, for the bounty of sea, river and mountain, steady livelihoods, family safety and business prosperity.

Following old tradition, the rope is made into **a great rope with eight heads and eight tails**. Its origin is unclear, but since it appears in Edo-period printed books it is thought to have continued for a very long time. It is also introduced in the "Settsu Meisho Zue" as the tug-of-war of Gozu Tenno of Naniwa village.

### Summer festival and boat procession (July)

The summer festival is described as including a boat procession on the Dotonbori River. Dates and content can vary by year, so please check the official site if you plan to go.

### New Year visit

Crowds are expected from January 1 to 3. To see the Lion Hall in peace, it is best to avoid those days.

## <span id="course"></span>Model route and timing

Times are only a guide.

1. **Pass the torii and look up at the Lion Hall** (about 5–10 min)
2. **Purify at the water pavilion and pray at the main hall** (about 5–10 min)
3. **Fortune slips, charms and goshuin at the amulet office** (about 10 min)
4. **Walk around the grounds and view the Lion Hall from the side and back** (about 5–10 min)

Plan for **about 30 minutes in total**. If you want to take photos or choose items slowly, allow a little more.

## <span id="point"></span>Tips for a smooth visit

- **Weekday mornings are best:** weekends and holidays bring more visitors. Late weekday mornings are said to be calmer.
- **Mind the amulet office hours:** it opens at 9:00, so walk the grounds first if you arrive early.
- **Bring cash:** credit cards are reportedly not accepted.
- **Photo manners:** people gather in front of the Lion Hall. Take turns and do not block those praying.
- **It is in a residential area:** please walk quietly around the shrine.

## <span id="access"></span>Access

- **Train:** about 6 minutes on foot from Namba Station (Osaka Metro Midosuji, Yotsubashi and Sennichimae lines). From Nankai Namba Station it is about 8–10 minutes on foot, and from JR Namba Station about 7 minutes. It is also within walking distance of Daikokucho Station (Osaka Metro).
- **Car:** reportedly no dedicated parking. Please use nearby paid lots.

A route through Namba Parks and then south is introduced as easy to follow.

## <span id="around"></span>Nearby shrines and stops

There are several luck-related shrines in the same Naniwa ward. Please check each official guide before visiting.

- **Imamiya Ebisu Shrine:** a shrine for business prosperity loved as "Ebisu-san." The Toka Ebisu festival from January 9 to 11 draws huge crowds. It is right by Nankai Imamiya-Ebisu Station.
- **Okuninushi Shrine (Shikitsu Matsunomiya):** known as "Kizu no Daikoku-san," with stone mouse guardians in the grounds.
- **Dotonbori and Hozenji:** classic Namba sightseeing spots. A food walk after your visit pairs well.

## <span id="manner"></span>Shrine etiquette

1. Bow once before passing through the torii.
2. Walk at the side of the approach, since the center is said to be for the gods.
3. Purify at the water pavilion: left hand, right hand, mouth, left hand.
4. Pray with **two bows, two claps, one bow**.
5. Give thanks as well as making a wish.
6. When taking photos in front of the Lion Hall, take care not to block those praying.

If you are unsure about etiquette, see [Shrine Visit Basics](/blog/shrine-visit-basics/).

## <span id="faq"></span>FAQ

**How long does a visit take?** About 30 minutes. Allow a little more if you take photos or choose items slowly.

**Is there an admission fee?** Admission is free. Fortune slips, charms and goshuin require a separate offering.

**Can I get a goshuin?** It is said to be available. The amulet office hours are about 9:00–16:50, so please check beforehand.

**When do the lion's eyes light up?** They are described as lighting up at the summer festival and New Year. Please check the official site for details.

**Is there parking?** Reportedly there is no dedicated parking. Going by train is recommended.

**When is the tug-of-war ritual held?** The official guide says the morning of the third Sunday of January each year. It may vary by year, so please check the official site for the latest information.
