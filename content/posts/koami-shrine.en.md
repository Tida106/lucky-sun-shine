## 1. The Miraculous Legend of Protection That Survived Every Disaster

### Lucky Charms and the All-Zelkova-Wood Main Hall — Absolute Protective Power That Overcomes Any Crisis
* **What makes it special?** Koami Shrine is beloved as a shrine of "strong luck and protection from misfortune" thanks to three historic miracles.
    * **The Great Kanto Earthquake miracle:** During the 1923 earthquake, the head priest carried the shrine's sacred object to the nearby Shin-Ohashi Bridge and prayed. While surrounding bridges collapsed, Shin-Ohashi alone remained standing, saving the lives of the many people who had fled there.
    * **The WWII miracle:** During the war, soldiers who received a departure blessing and charm from Koami Shrine before heading to the battlefield are recorded to have all returned home safely.
    * **The Tokyo air raids miracle:** Even as the surrounding neighborhood burned during the 1945 Tokyo air raids, the beautiful all-zelkova-wood main hall and kagura stage, built in 1929, survived completely unscathed.
* **Where does this power come from?** The shrine's history stretches back roughly 1,000 years to the Heian period. In 1466, when a plague swept through the area, enshrining the deity of Inari brought the outbreak to an immediate end. A deep, ongoing wish to protect the local community from disaster has run through the shrine's history ever since, and it has proven its power by protecting both people and its buildings through earthquake after earthquake, war after war. That track record is exactly why it's still so deeply trusted today.
* **Sun-chan's recommendation:** I wholeheartedly recommend this shrine to anyone facing a major life challenge, or feeling anxious about an unlucky year or a big change in their life. No matter how fierce the storm, this shrine's warm, gentle light will protect you and give you the courage to keep moving forward.

## 2. Where Finance Meets Faith: The Tokyo Money-Washing Benten

### The Tokyo Money-Washing Benten and Its Sacred Well — An Oasis for Cleansing Money with Gratitude
* **What makes it special?** At the "money-washing well" on the grounds, you can place coins — or even real 10,000-yen notes! — in a bamboo basket and rinse them in the pure water. Money washed here is called "seed money," and keeping it safely in your wallet is said to attract new wealth and abundance.
* **Where does this power come from?** The shrine enshrines Ichikishima-hime-no-kami, better known as Benzaiten, goddess of wealth and the arts. Washing money isn't just about making more of it — it carries a deeper meaning: cleansing away the tiredness and attachment that build up as money circulates through the world, and restoring it to its original, pure energy of abundance. Because the shrine sits right next to Nihonbashi Kabutocho, Tokyo's financial district, many securities professionals, businesspeople, and executives stop by on their lunch break to center themselves and circulate their money with a spirit of gratitude.
* **Sun-chan's recommendation:** Perfect for anyone hoping to boost their money luck, wanting a more positive mindset at work, or simply wanting to say thank you to the money that comes their way. Gently washing your money tends to leave your mind feeling just as refreshed and clear.

## 3. Adorable Charms and Delicate Carvings Worth Seeing

### Mayudama Omikuji and the Ascending & Descending Dragon Carvings — Craftsmanship and a Bond That Lasts
* **What makes it special?** On the grounds you'll find "mayudama omikuji" — fortune slips tucked inside real silkworm cocoons, in soft shades of white, pink, and yellow that are simply adorable. Look up at the main hall and you'll also see stunning wood carvings of an ascending dragon and a descending dragon, crafted by a master artisan in 1929 — breathtaking in their detail.
* **Where does this power come from?** The cocoon used for the mayudama omikuji is made from a single, beautiful strand of silk thread — symbolizing the wish that your connection with the divine will be as fine, strong, and long-lasting as that thread. Even within its small grounds, the shrine has carefully preserved Japanese traditional craftsmanship and a spirit of gratitude, which is how these carvings and its unusual pentagonal kagura stage have survived to this day.
* **Sun-chan's recommendation:** Hugely popular with anyone hoping for a warm, meaningful connection, or anyone who just wants a peaceful escape from a busy day. The moment you hold one, you can feel the warmth of nature and the artisan's care.
