## 1. You Don't "Buy" an Omamori? (Nature's Subscription and the Loop of Gratitude) ☀️

When choosing an omamori at a Shinto shrine, it is all too easy to say, "I am going to buy an omamori." However, shrines actually consider it improper to say "buy." The traditional, polite way to phrase it is to "receive" (ukeru or sazukaru) an amulet 🌿
An omamori is not merely merchandise sold in a store. Instead, it is a precious vessel where you temporarily hold and look after the warm power and protection of the kami (deities). That is why in Japanese, omamori are not counted as ordinary objects with counters like "one piece or two pieces." Instead, they are reverently counted as "one body, two bodies" (ittai, nitai), just as if welcoming living, sacred beings ✨
And then there is the term "hatsuhoryo" (the shrine offering or fee paid when receiving an omamori). Long ago, this custom began when people harvested the first, most precious rice grains of the season (hatsuho) and gratefully offered them to the kami, saying, "Thank you, dear deities, for blessing us with another bountiful harvest!" 🌾
In other words, hatsuhoryo is not a "price tag" for shopping. It is a heartfelt gift of "thank you" for the abundant blessings we receive from nature 💙
When we offer our hatsuhoryo with genuine gratitude, the kami accept our sincere hearts and send their protective blessings back to us in the form of an omamori... Doesn't this feel just like **"nature's subscription (a loop of gratitude)"**? ☀️
Rather than "paying money to get an item," you are choosing to participate in a gentle cycle of circulating grateful energy. When you think about it this way, the omamori resting in your hand becomes so much more precious and beloved, doesn't it? ✨

## 2. Why Must You Never Open It? (Sacred Sparks and the Secret Diplomatic Pouch) ✨

Have you ever wondered, "What is actually inside an omamori?" Yet, since ancient times, we have always been told, "Never peek inside the pouch." There is actually a deeply romantic and mystical reason for this rule 💙
Inside the pouch rests a tiny piece of wood or sacred washi paper called a "naifu," where a divine division of the kami resides 🌿
You might wonder, "Can divine power really be divided up like that?" To understand this, picture **a candle's flame** 🕯️ When you light a new candle from a single large flame, does the original flame shrink or disappear? Not at all! In fact, the room only fills with more bright, radiant light ☀️
Omamori work in the very same way. From the grand light present at the shrine, an authentic **"sacred spark"** has been shared specifically for the palm of your hand ✨ Therefore, carrying an omamori with you is just like holding hands with a "portable deity" who walks beside you wherever you go 💙
Furthermore, the cord tied tightly at the top of the pouch (often tied in a wish-granting knot known as kano-musubi) acts as a powerful barrier or sanctuary, overflowing with the mystical energy of new life and happiness 🌿
Think of it like **a secret diplomatic pouch** that connects two nations 💼 Just as a diplomatic pouch is sealed tight with the utmost security, an omamori pouch is a tiny sacred sanctuary perfectly protected by pure prayers. If you open the bag, the negative, chaotic energy of the outside world can slip inside, breaking that beautiful, protective barrier.
"Believing in and cherishing the unseen kami who is always right there beside you" — that innocent, tender mindset is the ultimate password that keeps your omamori's protective barrier strong ☀️

## 3. The Meaning of the Otakiage Ritual After One Year (A Sign of Gratitude to the Kami) 🌿

After cherishing your omamori for about a year, you might think, "Is it time to return it?" It is often said that an omamori's blessing lasts for about a year, but this never means that "it has an expiration date" ✨
Throughout this entire year, your omamori stayed right by your side, gently absorbing daily stress, anxious thoughts, and unseen misfortunes on your behalf 💙
That is why, once a year has passed, we bring the omamori back to a shrine for a ritual called "otakiage" (a sacred ceremonial burning with purifying fire). This is not simply throwing it away. It is a heartwarming story of returning the divine presence that protected you for a full year back to the main deity amidst purifying flames, accompanied by our deepest heartfelt thanks ☀️
"Receive the blessing, spend time together, and return it with deep gratitude." By honoring this beautiful, endless circle of harmony, your heart and your personal fortune will always remain fresh and filled with clean, positive energy ✨
Expressing your gratitude to your old omamori as you return it, and welcoming a fresh, new light at your next milestone... With each step you take, your luck and vitality will keep upgrading to a whole new level 💙
