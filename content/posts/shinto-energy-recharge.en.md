## 1. Why There Are No Sacred Texts or Strict Rules ☀️ ~The Myriad Gods Born from Gratitude to Great Nature~

Many religions around the world often have "absolute sacred texts" like the Bible or the Quran, and "strict precepts" that must be followed. However, Japanese Shinto has neither such sacred texts nor a founder (the person who established the religion)! ✨

That's because, since ancient times, Shinto spontaneously emerged from the hearts of Japanese people who felt gratitude and appreciation for the blessings of nature and their daily lives 🌿.
The rain that helps crops grow, the warm sun that shines down, the beautiful mountains and seas… Finding gratitude in all the workings of great nature is a gentle worldview called "animism."

As the phrase "Yaoyorozu no Kami" (eight million gods, or myriad gods) suggests, it's believed that gods (objects of gratitude) dwell not only in the sun, mountains, and rivers, but in everything around you 💙. Shinto's origin is not about being bound by rules, but about feeling "thank you for the abundant blessings right here, right now."
It's precisely because there are no strict teachings like "You MUST do this!" that when you visit a shrine, you're enveloped in a sense of inexplicable peace and warmth ☀️.

## 2. The True Meaning of Temizuya (Purification Fountain) ✨ ~Not About Being Wicked, But Fully Recharging and Resetting 'Tired Energy'!~

When you visit a shrine, you purify your hands and mouth at the temizuya (purification fountain) within the grounds, right? Have you ever wondered, "Do I need to cleanse myself because I did something bad?"
Well, that's actually a big misunderstanding! ✨

Academically speaking, "Kegare" (穢れ) in Shinto does not refer to moral evil or sin. Instead, it means **"Kigare" (気枯れ)**, which is "a state where your energy has run out from daily efforts, and your heart feels a little tired."
When you live diligently every day, it's natural for anyone to have their energy deplete and their heart feel weary, right? Shinto absolutely does not blame you for that 💙.

And "Harai" (purification rituals) and cleansing at the temizuya are not meant as punishment. Rather, by coming into contact with fresh natural elements (water and wind), they are a **"habit of fully recharging and resetting your depleted energy to 100%!"**

The moment you touch the pure water at the temizuya is, so to speak, "a quick charge time for your heart" ⚡️✨ It's a wonderful system where nature's power fills your energy, as if saying, "You worked so hard this week, great job!" 💙
